1. "1"C't DEPUTY FOREIGN MINISTER BURDULI MET WITH US 
NOVEMBER 27 TO PRESS GEORGIA'S CASE FOR A DECISION AT THE 
OSLO MINISTERIAL AND U.S. FLEXIBILITY ON SEVERAL POINTS 
WE HAD EXCISED FROM THE GEORGIAN DRAFT. MUCH ALONG THE 
LINES OF REF A, BURDULI EXPRESSED A DESIRE TO REACH AN 
ACCOMMODATION WITH THE U.S. ON THE DRAFT'S SPECIFICS. 
UNLESS OSLO MINISTERS APPROVE A DECISION, HOWEVER, 
GEORGIA WILL BE FORCED UNHAPPILY TO ADOPT "RADICAL" 
POSITIONS IN OSCE, SUCH AS "BRACKETING" PROPOSALS THAT 
ARE IMPORTANT TO OTHER DELEGATIONS. AT AN OSLO 
PREPARATORY MEETING THAT SAME DAY, 53 DELEGATIONS 
APPROVED THE GEORGIAN DRAFT TEXT WITH MINOR 
MODIFICATIONS, LEAVING THE U.S. ISOLATED IN OPPOSITION TO 
ANY REFERENCE TO A POSSIBLE OSCE OFFICE IN THE GALI 
DISTRICT. END SUMMARY. 
2. -te9- GEORGIAN DEPUTY FOREIGN MINISTER BURDULI, 
ACCOMPANIED BY GEORGIAN OSCE AMBASSADOR MIKELADZE, MET 
WITH US NOVEMBER 27 TO PRESS THE CASE FOR A DECISION ON 
GEORGIAN ISSUES AT OSLO AND TO URGE THE U.S. TO SHOW 
GREATER FLEXIBILITY ON SPECIFIC POINTS IN THE GEORGIAN 
DRAFT TEXT. BURDULI UNDERSCORED THE IMPORTANCE OF A 
MINISTERIAL DECISION TO GEORGIA. THE COPENHAGEN 
MINISTERIAL HAD PRODUCED ONLY A CHAIRMAN'S SUMMARY 
STATEMENT, BUT GEORGIA HAD RELUCTANTLY AGREED IN THE 
INTEREST OF BEING COOPERATIVE WITH KEY OSCE PLAYERS. BUT 
GEORGIA HAD GAINED NOTHING FROM THIS COMPROMISE, AND IT 
HAD SENT A SIGNAL TO ABKHAZIA THAT THE OSCE WAS 
DISINTERESTED -- A SIGNAL WHICH ONLY ENCOURAGED 
DEVELOPMENTS SUCH AS THE MAY VIOLENCE IN GALI. 
3. tet BURDULI STRESSED THAT THE OSLO MINISTERIAL MUST 
SEND A STRONG SIGNAL OF OSCE INVOLVEMENT IN GEORGIA 
THROUGH ADOPTING A DECISION. FAILURE TO DO SO WOULD 
FORCE GEORGIA TO RESORT TO "RADICAL" STEPS, SUCH AS 
BRACKETING ISSUES IMPORTANT TO OTHER DELEGATIONS. THIS 
WOULD BE AN EXTREMELY PAINFUL STEP FOR GEORGIA TO TAKE, 
AS IT UNDERSTANDS WELL THE VALUE OF COOPERATION, BUT 
UNDER THE CIRCUMSTANCES IT WOULD HAVE TO USE ALL THE 
TOOLS AT ITS DISPOSAL.
