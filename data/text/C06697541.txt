FOR AT LEAST THE NEXT TEN DAYS. THE CANADIAN MILITARY 
BLAMES THE STATE DEPARTMENT FOR THE MISUNDERSTANDING, 
CLAIMING CANADIAN CONCERNS ABOUT THE CHOPPERS WERE KNOWN 
TO THE U.S. MILITARY. 
--444 MR. SMITH MADE IT CLEAR HE PERSONALLY FOUND NO 
FAULT WITH THE STATE DEPARTMENT. THE CANADIAN MILITARY, 
HOWEVER, CLAIMED IT COULD NOT COMPLETE ITS MISSION IN 
HAITI WITHOUT THE LIFT PROVIDED BY THE CHOPPERS. 
-4g4-- FOLLOWING THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S PREVIOUS 
CONVERSATION WITH MR. SMITH TWO HOURS EARLIER, THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY HAD SPOKEN WITH DEPUTY DEFENSE 
SECRETARY WHITE, AMBASSADOR SWING AND GENERAL 
SHALIKASHVILI AND HAD CONVEYED SMITH'S EARLIER JUDGMENT 
THAT UNMIH COULD TOLERATE A SHORT GAP IN MEDIUM-LIFT 
HELICOPTERS. 
-Hi-- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SUGGESTED CANADA'S MINISTER OR 
DEPUTY MINISTER OF DEFENSE CONTACT HIS U.S. COUNTERPART 
TO REACH AN UNDERSTANDING. HE ADDED, HOWEVER, THAT 
SHOULD SECRETARY OF DEFENSE PERRY AVID GENERAL 
SHALIKASHVILI BE UNWILLING TO HALT THE DEPARTURE OF THE 
CHOPPERS, UNMIH WILL HAVE TO ACCEPT AN APPROXIMATE 
TEN-DAY PERIOD WITHOUT HELICOPTER SUPPORT. 
-*S}- MR. SMITH ACCEPTED RESPONSIBILITY FOR THE 
CONFUSION, AND EXPRESSED HIS APOLOGIES TO THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY. HE ADDED THAT, ON THE POLITICAL SIDE, THE 
CONFUSION,OVER THE HELICOPTERS IS NOT INTERPRETED IN 
CANADA AS A LACK OF U.S. SUPPORT. 
(U) THE CALL ENDED AT 1757 EST.
