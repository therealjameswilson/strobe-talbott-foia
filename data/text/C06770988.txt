,SUMMARY 
1. -(44 DURING A JANUARY 14 MEETING ON SOUTH ASIA WITH 
CHINESE MFA ARMS CONTROL DEPARTMENT DIRECTOR GENERAL SHA 
ZUKANG, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT OUTLINED OUR DIPLOMATIC 
EFFORTS WITH INDIA AND PAKISTAN. WHILE WE CONTINUED TO 
PURSUE OUR LONG-TERM OBJECTIVE OF UNIVERSAL ADHERENCE TO 
THE NPT, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID WE WERE ALSO FOCUSING ON 
NEAR-TERM GOALS (ADHERENCE TO THE CTBT, RESTRAINT ON 
DEVELOPMENT AND DEPLOYMENT OF BALLISTIC MISSILES, 
SUSPENSION OF FISSILE MATERIAL PRODUCTION, AND 
STRENGTHENING OF EXPORT CONTROLS) . IN THIS •CONTEXT, HE 
URGED CHINA TO CONSIDER POSITIVELY JOINING OTHER STATES 
THAT HAVE TESTED NUCLEAR WEAPONS IN MAKING INDIVIDUAL 
PARALLEL DECLARATIONS THAT THEY WILL OBSERVE MORATORIA ON 
FISSILE MATERIAL PRODUCTION.

DIPLOMATIC EFFORTS, 
4. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT SAID HE WANTED TO TELL SHA 
ABOUT OURDIPLOMATIC EFFORTS WITH INDIA AND PAKISTAN, AS HE 
HAD DONE REGULARLY WITH THE AMBASSADORS OF P-5 COUNTRIES IN 
WASHINGTON. IT WAS IMPORTANT TO DO SO BECAUSE THE P-5 
COUNTRIES HAVE A SPECIAL RESPONSIBILITY AND BECAUSE OUR 
EFFORTS WERE WITHIN THE CONTEXT OF THE P-5 JUNE COMMUNIQUE. 
AS HE APPROACHED THE SIGHTH ROUND OF TALKS WITH THE INDIANS 
AND PAKISTANIS LATER THIS MONTH, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID 
IT WAS AN APPROPRIATE MOMENT TO ASSESS OUR EFFORTS TO DATE. 
5.   CONSISTENT WITH THE COMMUNIQUE AND PRESIDENTS 
CLINTON AND JIANG'S JOINT STATEMENT ON SOUTH ASIA, THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID OUR LONG-TERM GOAL REMAINED UNIVERSAL 
ADHERENCE TO THE NPT. IN THIS REGARD, HE EMPHASIZED THAT 
NOTHING IN OUR DIALOGUE WITH INDIA AND PAKISTAN CONCEDED 
ANY POSSIBLITY OF THE TWO COUNTRIES BECOMING DE JURE OR DE 
FACTO NUCLEAR WEAPON STATES (NWS) UNDER THE NPT. WE HAVE 
MADE CLEAR TO THE TWO COUNTRIES THAT THEY WOULD NOT BE 
ENTITLED TO ANY BENEFITS ACCORDED TO NPT PARTIES, SUCH AS 
NUCLEAR COOPERATION., UNLESS THEY RENOUNCED NUCLEAR WEAPONS. 
OUR EFFORTS WERE AIMED AT CONVINCING INDIA AND PAKISTAN 
THAT IT WAS IN THEIR INTEREST EVENTUALLY TO ADHERE TO THE 
NPT.

FISSILE MATERIAL MORATORIUM 
7. +G4- IN THIS CONTEXT, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID WE HOPED 
TO ACHIEVE A MEETING OF MINDS ON GETTING INDIA AND PAKISTAN 
TO ACCEPT A MORATORIUM ON FISSILE MATERIAL PRODUCTION. A 
MORATORIUM NOW WOULD ALLOW PAKISTAN TO LOCK IN THE CURRENT 
SITUATION WITH INDIA. IF .NOT CAPPED AT.THE CURRENT LEVEL, 
HOWEVER, HE SAID PAKISTAN WILL FIND ITSELF GREATLY 
DISADVANTAGED IN OPEN COMPETITION WITH INDIA. 
8. -(-G-)- WHILE BOTH INDIA AND PAKISTAN PREVIOUSLY HAD 
INDICATED DIFFICULTY IN MAKING UNILATERAL PLEDGES, THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY SUGGESTED THAT A WILLINGNESS ON THE PART 
OF EACH OF THOSE COUNTRIES WHICH HAD TESTED TO ANNOUNCE 
MORATORIA ON FISSILE MATERIAL PRODUCTION WOULD MAKE IT VERY 
DIFFICULT FOR THE TWO COUNTRIES TO REFUSE. PAKISTAN WOULD 
SIGN IF INDIA AGREED TO A MORATORIUM. INDIA WOULD 
POSITIVELY CONSIDER IF CHINA SIGNED. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
EXPLAINED THAT SUCH A MORATORIUM WOULD BE COORDINATED AND 
WOULD NOT APPLY UNTIL ALL COUNTRIES THAT HAD TESTED HAS 
AGREED, SO THERE WOULD BE NO DANGER OF LOSING LEVERAGE OVER 
INDIA. HE ALSO NOTED THAT SUCH A MORATORIUM WOULD NOT 
IMPLY NWS STATUS TO INDIA OR PAKISTAN BECAUSE IT WOULD BE 
BASED ON UNILATERAL DECLARATIONS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
URGED CHINA TO CONSIDER FAVORABLY PARTICIPATION IN THIS 
PROPOSAL. A/S INDERFURTH ADDED THAT INDIA HAD ALREADY 
INDICATED THAT IT WOULD CONSIDER MULTILATERAL INITIATIVES 
ONCE FMCT NEGOTIATIONS GOT UNDERWAY IN GENEVA.
