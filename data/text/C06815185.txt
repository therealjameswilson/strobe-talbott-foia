DEAR YEVGENIY MAXIMOVICH:
JIM COLLINS RELAYED TO ME YOUR QUESTION ABOUT
WHETHER THE U.S. HAD CHANGED ITS POSITION WITH
REGARD TO THE LINKAGE BETWEEN NEGOTIATIONS ON
FURTHER REDUCTIONS IN OFFENSIVE STRATEGIC
WEAPONRY UNDER START IIIAND NEGOTIATIONS ON
STRATEGIC DEFENSE. THE SHORT ANSWER IS, YES.
ID LIK: TO USE THIS LETTER TO LAY OUT WHAT
CHANGE WEVE MADE AND WHY.
WHEN OUR LEADERS AND MINISTERS SETTLED ON BASIC
COMPONENTS OF START IIIIN HELSINKI IN 1997, THE
UNITED STATES AND RUSSIA AGREED THAT START III
NEGOTIATIONS WOULD BEGIN ONCE START II ENTERS
INTO FORCE. SUBSEQUENTLY, WE AGREED THAT START
IIINEGOTIATIONS WOULD BEGIN ONCE RUSSIA RATIFIED
START II,WITHOUT WAITING FOR ENTRY INTO FORCE.
THIS IS THE PERIOD TO WHICH YOU REFER IN YOUR
MESSAGE TO ME.
THAT COMMON VIEW WAS RECORDED PUBLICLY IN A JOINT
STATEMENT ISSUED BY OUR PRESIDENTS DURING THEIR
SUMMIT IN MOSCOW IN SEPTEMBER 1998. THEY MET A
MATTER OF DAYS AFTER NORTH KOREA FIRED A LONG-
RANGE, THREE-STAGE MISSILE WE CALL THE TAEPO
DONG-1r WHICH DEMONSTRATED THAT NORTH KOREA HAS
THE TECHNOLOGY TO BUILD MULTI-STAGE MISSILES.
WITH THIS TECHNOLOGY, A LARGER NORTH KOREAN
MISSILE, THE TAEPO DONG-2., COULD POSE A NUCLEAR
THREAT TO THE UNITED STATES.
QUITE SIMPLY, THAT DEVELOPMENT CAST A SHADOW OVER
THE GLOBAL STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENT; ID EVEN SAY IT
"CHANGED" THE STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENT. PRESIDENT
CLINTON ADDRESSED THIS ISSUE AT SOME LENGTH IN
THE SPEECH HE DELIVERED ON SEPTEMBER I AT
GEORGETOWN UNIVERSITY WHEN HE ANNOUNCED HIS
DECISION WITH REGARD TO NMD.
THERES PRECEDENT FOR OUR COUNTRIES ADJUSTING
THEIR POSITION ON TREATIES TO TAKE ACCOUNT OF NEW
REALITIES. YOU WILL RECALL THAT RUSSIA CITED
CHANGES IN THE STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENT, IN
PARTICULAR THE UNSTABLE SECURITY SITUATION IN THE
CAUCASUS, IN PROPOSING MAJOR CHANGES TO THE CFE
FLANK LIMITS. THE UNITED STATES AND ALL OTHER
PARTIES AGREED IN 1995 AND AGAIN IN 1999 TO MAKE
SUBSTANTIAL ADJUSTMENTS TO THE ORIGINAL CFE
TREATY TO GIVE RUSSIA FLEXIBILITY TO MEET WHAT IT
FELT WAS A NEW THREAT TO ITS SECURITY.
