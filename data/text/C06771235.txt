3. 4.64--UKUN PERMREP GREENSTOCK AND UK AMBASSADOR MEYER HAD 
A BRIEF DROP-BY MEETING WITH THE DEPUTY SECRETARY IN 
ADVANCE OF MEETING WITH U/S PICKERING (SEPTEL). THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY BEGAN BY ASKING GREENSTOCK FOR HIS VIEWS 
ON THE RUSSIAN-PROPOSED DRAFT SECURITY COUNCIL RESOLUTION 
ON KOSOVO THAT HAD BEEN INTRODUCED ON MARCH 25. (SEE REFS 
A AND B. THE RESOLUTION WAS DEFEATED ON NARCH 26 BY A 12-
3 VOTE.) GREENSTOCK EXPLAINED THAT RUSSIAN PERM REP 
LAVROV HAD PUT FORWARD THE DRAFT RESOLUTION UNDER 
INSTRUCTIONS, WITH LITTLE ELABORATION OR ANIMATION. 
GREENSTOCK BELIEVED THAT LAVROV HAD SENT CLEAR SIGNALS 
THAT THE RUSSIANS WANTED TO WORK CONSTRUCTIVELY IN THE 
SECURITY COUNCIL WITH THE UK AND THE U.S. ON ISSUES OTHER 
THAN IRAQ AND THE BALKANS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY, PLEASED 
TO HEAR TABLING OF THE RESOLUTION HAD NOT BEEN 
CONTENTIOUS, ADVISED THE TWO THAT THE SECRETARY HAD JUST 
HAD A GOOD, BUT POINTED, CONVERSATION WITH FM IVANOV. 
4. 4.S..4- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ASKED GREENSTOCK TO PUT ON 
"HIS FORMER POLDIR HAT " TO PROVIDE HIS OWN READ ON THE 
KDSOVO CRISIS. GREENSTOCK REPLIED THAT MORE "MEDIUM-TERM" 
THINKING WAS NEEDED. MILOSEVIC WAS IN "A BELEAGUERED 
MOOD, " LISTENING TO VERY FEW PEOPLE. DOING A DEAL NOW WAS 
WORSE THAN WHAT WAS HAPPENING WITH THE AIRSTRIKES. 
GREENSTOCK BELIEVED THAT MILOSEVIC WAS AS DIFFICULT AND 
RECALCITRANT AS EVER - "HE WON'T GIVE IN." MILOSEVIC 
COULD LOSE HALF HIS BATTLEFIELD EQUIPMENT AND DEFENSES, 
BUT HE RETAINED SUPPORT FROM THE SERBAIN POLITICAL 
STRUCTURES. "HE'LL LAST THIS OUT AS LONG AS POSSIBLE; 
AND, IF HE CAN LAST THIS OUT, HE WILL HAVE TOLD US OFF AND 
HE'LL STILL HAVE KOSOVO." GREENSTOCK BELIVED THAT 
MILOSEVIC WAS MAKING A MISTAKE, ESPECIALLY WITH REGARD TO 
THE REPORTED POGROMS AND IN BEING COMPLETELY UNRESPONSIVE 
TO HOLBROOKE AND THE AMERICANS HE CLAIMED TO SO ADMIRE. 
5. 4S4 THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ADVISED THAT HE HAD JUST COME 
FROM A BRAINSTORMING SESSION WITH THE SECRETARY, NSC 
ADVISOR BERGER, AND OTHERS. FRAGMENTS OF INTELLIGENCE 
REPORTS HAD CONFIRMED ANECDOTAL INFORMATION ABOUT REPORTED 
ATROCITIES, BUT CONCLUSIONS WERE LARGELY BASED ON PATTERNS 
OF PAST SERBIAN BEHAVIOR. HE RECALLED THAT, IN BOSNIA, 
HARD INTELLENGE HAD LAGGED BEHIND EVENTS, AND NOTED THAT 
THE PREDICTED BAD WEATHER WOULD INHIBIT OVERHEAD 
OBSERVATIONS OF WHAT WAS HAPPENING ON THE GROUND. 
NONETHELESS, THERE WAS ENOUGH INFORMATION FROM NGOS AND 
THE MACEDONIAN AND ALBANIAN BORDERS TO SUGGEST THERE WAS A 
BAD SITUATION ON THE GROUND. 
6. +64 AMBASSADOR MEYER INTERJECTED THAT WHILE ONE MIGHT 
DISCOUNT TIME AND PACE OF AIRSTRIKES, MILOSEVIC COULD 
ACCELERATE HIS ADVANCE AND CREATE A PUBLIC RELATIONS 
PROBLEM FOR THE ALLIES. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY COUNTERED 
THAT INCREASED ACTIVITY BY MILOSEVIC COULD ALSO BE USED TO 
STRENGTHEN THE RATIONALE FOR HAVING LAUNCHED THE NATO 
AIRSTRIKES - WHICH COULD THEN BE EXPANDED TO ASK WHY THE 
ALLIES WERE NOT DOING MORE. 
7. +Si- MEYER AND GREENSTOCK JOINTLY ASKED ABOUT U.S. 
WILLINGNESS TO STICK THE CAMPAIGN OUT, WITH MEYER NOTING 
THERE WAS NOTHING TO SUGGEST THUS FAR THAT ANY INITIAL 
"WOBBLINESS" BY THE GREEKS AND THE ITALIANS WAS GAINING 
MOMENTUM. MEYER ADVANCED A PERSONAL VIEW THAT THERE 
APPEARED TO BE DIFFERING LEVELS OF PUBLIC SUPPORT AMONG 
THE ALLIES, ALTHOUGH NONE SEEMED TO BE UNDER SEVERE 
PRESSURE FROM THEIR PUBLICS. REACTIONS SO FAR SEEMED TO
