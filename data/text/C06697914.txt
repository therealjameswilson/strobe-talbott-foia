1. Classified by John F. Keane, Charge d'affaires, 
Reason: 1.5 (d). 
RELEASE IN FULL 
2. Summary: The May 30-31 visit of Deputy Secretary of 
State Strobe Talbott showcased U.S. support for 
Venezuela's recent economic measures. In public and 
private, the Deputy Secretary acclaimed the "Agenda 
Venezuela" and assured U.S. endorsement of robust 
economic assistance from the international financial 
institutions for a seriousVenezuelan adjustment program. 
The message was carried by heavy local media coverage and 
got through. The visit also advanced our objectives on 
Haiti (support for UNMIH extension and Opening the door 
to more Venezuelan concessions to Haiti in conjunction 
with the San Jose facility), as well as on narcotics 
cooperation. • Caldera was vocal about Venezuela's 
problems with Colombia, and Colombia's internal crisis 
appears to weigh heavily on his mind. Helms-Burton 
sticks in Foreign Minister Burelli's nationalist craw, 
and he appears to favor a joint Latin American response. 
End summary. 
MEETING WITH PRESIDENT CALDERA 
3. ECONOMIC REFORM: Caldera expressed gratitude for USG 
public statements in support of Venezuela's economic 
reform efforts, adding that he hoped the U.S. would 
assist Venezuela at the IMF, World Bank and IDB. The 
Deputy Secretary congratulated Caldera on the wisdom and 
courage of his recent economic policy decisions and 
assured him of U.S. support in the IFIs. He also 
commended Caldera for his hemispheric leadership on anti-
corruption and informed him that he would sign the OAS 
anti-corruption convention at the Panama OASGA. 
4. HAITI: The Deputy Secretary thanked the Venezuelan 
Government for its support on Haiti in the past, while 
emphasizing that Haitian democracy depends in large 
measure on continued outside assistance. He proposed 
that Venezuela consider expanding the benefits of the San 
Jose facility to allow Haiti access to petroleum on 
concessionary terms. Caldera, unfamiliar with the issue, 
turned to Foreign Minister Burelli, who replied that 
Haiti was the first beneficiary of the San Jose facility 
to have its outstanding debt refinanced by Venezuela, 
suggesting that Venezuela had already done enough. The

Deputy Secretary commented that there was more that could 
be done within the context of the San Jose accord and 
suggested that he and Burelli pursue some ideas during 
their bilateral later that day. While on Haiti, Talbott 
also requested Venezuelan support for an extension of the 
mandate for UNMIH, noting that an extension has been 
sought by President Preval. Caldera indicated that he 
thought Venezuela would concur if an extension is the 
Haitian Government's wishes. 
5. COLOMBIA: Colombia was very much on Caldera's mind. 
He expressed grave concern about Colombian drug 
traffickers and guerrillas operating on Venezuelan soil 
and then finding refuge on the Colombian side of the 
border. Caldera said he would like to see ,a 
-constitutional solution to Colombia's internal crisis. 
Nevertheless, he predicted Samp'er will ride out the storm 
until the end of his term, but his government will be 
rendered ineffectual by an opposition that is 
increasingly powerful, thereby exacerbating problems for 
Venezuela. 
6. The Deputy Secretary replied that the U.S. respects 
Colombia's sovereignty, and believes a solution must be 
transparent, fair and consistent with the rule of law. 
Talbott stressed that our laws require that we modulate 
our assistance to Colombia in accordance with our 
assessments of Colombia's seriousness in combatting 
narcotic trafficking. The U.S. shares the doubts of many 
informed and well-meaning Colombians about the 
impartiality of the Colombian Chamber of Deputies' 
Accusations Committee and the likely flawed outcome of 
the process in the Chamber. 
7. Acting Assistant Secretary Jeffrey Davidow reiterated 
that the U.S. wants to work with the many honorable 
Colombians who are concerned about the impact of 
narcotics trafficking on their society, just as we 
welcome opportunities to cooperate with venezuela against 
narcotics and narcoguerrillas, and we seek ways to foster 
US-Venezuelan-Colombian cooperation on this mutual 
problem. The U.S. will oppose any resolution of the 
Colombianpoliticalcrisiswhichisextra-constitutional, 
he added, but at the same time there is evidence that 
Samper's campaign accepted 
trafficker financing, which has influenced Colombian 
government decision-making. Thus, the crisis in Colombia 
is a challenge to Colombian democracy. Caldera wound up 
the discussion of Colombia by noting that he and many of 
Samper's other friends have urged him to resign for the 
good of Colombia, but Samper is not listening.

8. NARCOTICS COOPERATION: Caldera stressed his concerns 
about narcotics flowing over the border, as well as the 
actions of Colombian narcoguerrillas on Venezuelan soil, 
and the need for the Venezuelan security forces to pursue 
them "right up to the border", which prompts protests 
from the local Colombian authorities (sic.). (Comment: 
Caldera did not acknowledge that Venezuelan forces are 
practicing "hot pursuit", but one can infer that from his 
remarks about Colombian protests. End Comment.) He 
indicated that Venezuela stands ready to collaborate 
fully with the U.S. on counterdrug programs, but is 
resource poor and will welcome any assistance the U.S. 
can provide, alluding to the visit two months ago by 
Secretary of Defense Perry. Talbott' replied that 
Secretary Perry was very pleased with what he saw during 
his visit and had worked out with the Venezuelan Minister 
of Defense a specific program of cooperation, which is 
being implemented. However, U.S. ability to respond 
effectively, the Deputy Secretary continued, depends on 
Venezuela developing a plan integrating police/armed 
forces cooperation. (Comment: Caldera did not appear to 
grasp the Deputy Secretary's point, but it was repeated 
and understood later, during the bilateral with Burelli; 
see below.) 
9. U.S. SUPPORT: The meeting ended with a REITERATION BY 
the U.S. side that the principal message to the President 
of Venezuela was that he could count on the support of 
President Clinton in the IMF and elsewhere as he presses 
ahead with economic reform. (U.S. participants in 
addition to Talbott, the Charge and Davidow were NSC 
Senior Advisor James Dobbins, D Executive Assistant 
Victoria Nuland, and D Special Assistant Geoff Pyatt. 
Caldera was accompanied by Burelli, Ambassador to the 
U.S. Pedro Luis Echeverria, Vice FonMin AMBASSADOR MILOS 
Alcalay, and the FonMin's number three, Ambassador 
Edmundo Gonzalez.) 
MEETING WITH FON MINISTER MIGUEL ANGEL BURELLI RIVAS

Rio Group meetIng, Burelli noted that the focus was on 
developing a coordinated Rio Group. response to Helms-
Burton (H-B). One possibility ls the adoption of'
national legislations as an "antidote" to H-B, and

ECONOMIC TEAM PROMOTES REFORM AND FREE TRADE 
12. The key members of the GOV's Economic Team, Minister 
of Finance Luis Raul Matos Azocar, Minister of Planning 
(CORDIPLAN) Teodoro Petkoff, Minister of Development and 
President of the Foreign Trade Institute Freddy Rojas 
Parra, and Minister of Agriculture Raul Alegrett, met 
with Deputy Secretary Talbott and his party over 
breakfast. The Economic Team reinforced Venezuela's 
commitment to neo-liberal economic reforms under an IMF 
Stand-by Agreement and were pleased with Deputy Secretary 
Talbott's message of support and praise from the Clinton 
Administration for the GOV's economic policy shift. 
Taking the lead, Finance Minister Matos revealed that the 
GOV would sign a letter of intent with IMF Executive 
Director Camdessus in Caracas on Monday, June 3. Matos 
briefed on reform measures implemented thus far, and'he 
and Petkoff reaffirmed plans to downsize Venezuela's 1.2 
million civil service.

DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT MEETING WITH OIL INDUSTRY REPS 
15. The Deputy Secretary received a general summary of 
developments in the petroleum sector from representatives 
of the Ministry of Energy and Mines, PDVSA, and U.S. oil 
companies." Deputy Minister of Energy Evanan Romero 
outlined how the energy sector will lead economic 
recovery. PDVSA Vice President Graf explained that 
foreign investment is 40-45 percent of the total needed 
to meet plans to raise production from three to five 
million barrels per day by 2006. In ten years, marginal 
fields should produce 450,000 barrels per day, 
Apertura/profit sharing arrangements 500,000 bpd and 
strategic associations 300,000 bpd, he said. Though it 
provides only 100,000 direct jobs, petroleum production 
contributes 24 percent to the GNP and its cascade creates 
30 percent more. The PDVSA reps were confident of quick 
Congressional approval for the model contract needed to 
finalize foreign investment agreements.

professionalism and said proximity to the United States,

PRESS COVERAGE OF THE VISIT 
19. The visit of Deputy Secretary Talbott received 
extensive and prominent press coverage on Saturday, June 
1. His press conference at Miraflores was also covered 
by the evening news program of Venevision (Channel 4, 
leading network, nation-wide coverage) on Friday night. 
Focus of coverage in print press and television was on 
U.S. support for the "Agenda Venezuela" program of 
economic reform measures. Headline in "E1 Universal" 
(conservative leading daily, circulation 160,000), for 
example, was: "Clinton offers support to assure success 
of Agenda venezuela."
