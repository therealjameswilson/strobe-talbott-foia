2. SUMMARY: IN AN EXCEPTIONALLY FRANK AND CORDIAL 
MEETING PRESIDENT PEREZ BALLADARES AND DEPSEC 
TALBOTT LAID OUT THEIR GOVERNMENTS' RESPECTIVE 
POSITIONS ON POST-1999 MILITARY BASING. BOTH AGREED 
THAT THE PRINCIPAL GOAL IS TO ESTABLISH A MATURE, 
HEALTHY BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP FOR THE NEW CENTURY: 
MILITARY BASING IS NOT THE SINE QUA NON OF THAT 
RELATIONSHIP, NOR ARE THE TWO MUTUALLY 
CONTRADICTORY. PEREZ REPEATED HIS GOVERNMENT'S 
INTEREST IN SHARING WITH US HOWARD AFB, AND PERHAPS 
SOME ASSOCIATED FACILITIES, FREE OF CHARGE, AS AN 
INTERNATIONAL ANTINARCOTICS CENTER, BUT WAS CLEAR IN 
STATING THAT HE COULD JUSTIFY THE USE OF OTHER 
BASES TO THE PANAMANIAN PUBLIC, ONLY IF THERE IS 
COMPENSATION. TALBOTT MADE CLEAR THAT COMPENSATION 
WOULD BE INCONSISTENT WITH CURRENT US PRACTICE IN 
COUNTRIES IN WHICH WE HAVE MILITARY FACILITIES AND 
CONTRARY TO THE NEW STRUCTURE WE SEEK WITH PANAMA. 
PRESIDENT PEREZ BALLADARES ENTHUSIASTICALLY ACCEPTED 
THE OFFER OF COOPERATION TO ENCOURAGE INVESTMENT IN 
THE REVERTED AREAS. TALBOTT GAVE THE PANAMANIAN 
SIDE A TWO PAGER OUTLINING THE ECONOMIC BENEFITS TO 
PANAMA OF CONTINUED US MILITARY BASING HERE. END 
SUMMARY.

TRANSFER OF THE CANAL WERE PROCEEDING SMOOTHLY:

REMAINED TO BE RESOLVED, BUT THEY WOULD BE. PANAMA 
WAS AWAITING A BLUE RIBBON REPORT FROM THE ARMY 
CORPS OF ENGINEERS WHICH MIGHT RECOMMEND TECHNICAL 
(INFRASTRUCTURE) IMPROVEMENTS THAT COULD NECESSITATE

4. TALBOTT NOTED THAT PRESIDENT CLINTON WOULD BE 
ANNOUNCING THIS WEEK THE NOMINATION OF PANAMANIAN 
CITIZEN ALEMAN AS THE NEW ADMINISTRATOR OF THE 
PANAMA CANAL COMMISSION. HE THEN SUGGESTED A 
DISCUSSION OF POST-1999 MILITARY RASING AND MADE 
CLEAR THAT HE WAS EXPRESSING THE VIEWS OF THE USG, 
INCLUDING THE PERSPECTIVE OF PRESIDENT CLINTON. HE 
NOTED THAT BOTH COUNTRIES MUST GET THEIR PRIORITIES 
STRAIGHT: ABOVE ALL ELSE IS THE NEED TO CREATE A 
HEALTHY, MATURE US-PANAMA RELATIONSHIP FOR THE NEXT 
CENTURY: THE US ALSO SEES ADVANTAGES FOR BOTH 
COUNTRIES TO A CONTINUING MILITARY PRESENCE AFTER 
1999. IF THAT GOAL CAN BE RECONCILED WITH THE 
OVERARCHING PRIORITY OF MUTUAL BENEFIT AND RESPECT, 
THAT WOULD BE IDEAL. 
5. PRESIDENT PEREZ BALLADARES RESPONDED THAT 
MAINTAINING AND DEEPENING THE "SPECIAL RELATIONSHIP" 
IS CRITICAL: A CONTINUING MILITARY PRESENCE IS A 
SECONDARY ISSUE. PANAMA NEEDS A STRATEGIC ALLIANCE 
WITH THE US WHICH WOULD BE EXPRESSED DIFFERENTLY 
THAN IN THE PAST: "WE SHOULD GO FROM AN UP/DOWN 
RELATIONSHIP TO AN UP/UP ONE," HE SAID. HE RECALLED 
THAT WHEN HE HAD MET WITH PRESIDENT CLINTON LAST 
SEPTEMBER, THE TWO HAD DECIDED TO EMBARK ON INFORMAL 
DISCUSSIONS ON THE MILITARY PRESENCE ISSUE. THOSE 
HAD TAKEN PLACE AND PANAMA'S POSITION REMAINED 
UNCHANGED: FOR A CONTINUED MILITARY PRESENCE TO BE 
SELLABLE TO THE PANAMANIANS THERE MUST BE CLEAR 
BENEFITS THAT WOULD ACCRUE, TO THEM. IN RELATION TO 
ANTINARCOTICS, HE SAID, WE HAVE A SHARED INTEREST 
AND FOR THAT REASON WE COULD DEDICATE HOWARD AFB TO 
AN EFFORT THAT WOULD BE MULTINATIONAL IN CHARACTER, 
EVEN IF MANAGED BY THE U.S. MILITARY. THE OTHER 
BASES, HOWEVER, ARE ASSETS OF PANAMA THAT MUST 
PROVIDE DIRECT COMPENSATION TO THE PEOPLE. 
AFTER 1999, THE BASES ARE OURS, PEREZ BALLADARES 
SAID. WE COULD NOT GIVE THEM AWAY TO ANYONE 
WITHOUT COMPENSATION. T 
HAT WOULD BE IMPOSSIBLE, HE 
CONTENDED. 
6. TALBOTT RESPONDED THAT WE WOULD CERTAINLY NOT 
WANT PANAMA TO HAVE US STAY UNLESS IT WAS SEEN TO BE 
IN PANAMA'S SELF INTEREST THAT WE DO SO: THAT WOULD 
BE INCONSISTENT WITH THE FUNDAMENTALS OF THE 
RELATIONSHIP. OUR PRESENCE MUST BE ROOTED IN 
PANAMA'S NATIONAL INTEREST. THE NO-COMPENSATION 
POSITION IS NOT STONEWALLING NOR A TACTICAL 
NEGOTIATING DEVICE, BUT RENT WOULD BE INCONSISTENT 
WITH THE NEW RELATIONSHIP WE ALL WANT. IT DOES NOT 
FORM PART OF OUR MILITARY PRESENCE IN KOREA, JAPAN, 
SECTION 02 OF 02 PANAMA 2747 
OR GERMANY; IN FACT THOSE COUNTRIES EFFECTIVELY 
COMPENSATE THE U.S. FOR CONTRIBUTING TO THEIR 
DEFENSE. WE ARE NOT TRYING TO IMPOSE ARTIFICIAL 
DEADLINES, BUT WE THINK THAT THE LONGER THE ISSUE 
GOES WITHOUT RESOLUTION, THE MORE OF A HOT POTATO IT 
WILL BECOME; ALSO, A TIMELY DECISION IS NECESSARY 
FOR BOTH COUNTRIES' PLANNING PURPOSES. 
7. PRESIDENT PEREZ BALLADARES RETURNED TO THE NEED 
TO CREATE A STRATEGIC RELATIONSHIP FOR THE 21ST 
CENTURY COMPOSED OF MANY ELEMENTS, INCLUDING 
COMMERCE. HE REPEATED HIS INTEREST IN JOINT

PARTICIPATION IN THE DRUG WAR, BUT NOTED THAT "I 
REALLY DON'T SEE ANY POSSIBILITY OF DOING ANYTHING 
ELSE WITH THE REST OF THE MILITARY INSTALLATIONS" 
(I.E. OTHER THAN HOWARD). "IT IS BETTER TO BRING 
THIS ISSUE OUT IN THE OPEN," IN ORDER TO GET ON 
WITH DEVELOPING THE NEW RELATIONSHIP. TALBOTT THEN 
SOUGHT TO FURTHER CLARIFY PEREZ BALLADARES' 
COMMENTS. HE ASKED WHETHER HOWARD WOULD HAVE TO 
HAVE ITS USES LIMITED SOLELY TO COUNTER NARCOTICS. 
"NO," RESPONDED THE PRESIDENT, OTHER ACTIVITIES 
COULD TAKE PLACE THERE, ASSUMING THAT 90 PER CENT OF 
ITS MISSION WAS THE DRUG WAR. HE REPEATED THAT HE • 
WAS WILLING TO MAKE HOWARD AND SURROUNDING 
FACILITIES AVAILABLE, FREE OF COST TO THE US FOR THE 
MULTINATIONAL DRUG EFFORT, BUT, OF COURSE, THE US 
WOULD HAVE TO MAINTAIN THE FACILITIES AT US EXPENSE. 
IN RELATION TO OTHER INSTALLATIONS, SUCH AS CLAYTON, 
AND SHERMAN, "THESE FACILITIES WILL BELONG TO US, 
AND IF YOU HAD TO CREATE THEM FROM GROUND ZERO IN 
ANOTHER COUNTRY, YOU WOULD HAVE TO SPEND A LOT OF 
MONEY TO ACQUIRE, RENT OR BUILD THEM. WE ARE NOT 
ASKING FOR COMPENSATION FOR A SERVICE, BUT RATHER 
FOR PAYMENT FOR PANAMANIAN PROPERTY THAT COULD BE 
USED FOR OTHER ECONOMICALLY BENEFICIAL PURPOSES." 
8. TALBOTT REPEATED THE POINT ABOUT OUR NO-
COMPENSATION POLICY BEING RASED ON OUR CONCEPT OF 
THE MATURE RELATIONSHIP WE HAVE WITH OTHER ALLIES 
AND WOULD LIKE TO SEE REPLICATED HERE. NCLARTY 
NOTED THAT PRESIDENT CLINTON REMEMBERS HIS PLEASANT 
MEETING WITH PRESIDENT PEREZ BALLADARES AND HE TOO 
IS KEEPING HIS EYE ON CREATING A NEW RELATIONSHIP 
FOR THE NEXT CENTURY. DAVIDOW ASKED THE PRESIDENT 
TO CLARIFY WHAT HE MEANT BY HOWARD'S FACILITIES. THE 
PRESIDENT RESPONDED THAT HE WAS REFERRING TO 
AIRSTRIPS, HANGARS, AND HOUSING. THE PRESIDENT 
SEEMED WILLING TO ENTERTAIN THE POSSIBILITY OF A 
RENT-FREE ARRANGEMENT ON HOUSING, ALTHOUGH IT WAS 
NOT CLEAR WHETHER HE WOULD INCLUDE CLAYTON IN SUCH 
AN ARRANGEMENT. (COMMENT: THE PANAMANIAN POSITION 
TO DATE HAS BEEN TO INSIST ON RENT FOR HOUSING AT 
CLAYTON.) DAVIDOW ALSO TOLD THE PRESIDENT OF THE 
RECENT CONVERSATION BETWEEN SECRETARIES PERRY AND 
KANTOR ABOUT HELPING PANAMA ATTRACT INVESTMENT. 
PEREZ BALLADARES RESPONDED ENTHUSIASTICALLY AND 
URGED AMBASSADOR ARIAS TO FOLLOW UP ON ALL ISSUES 
WITH DAVIDOW WHEN HE RETURNS TO WASHINGTON. AS THE 
MEETING ENDED, AT ARIAS' REQUEST, TALBOTT HANDED 
OVER A TWO PAGE DOCUMENT SU=MARIZING OUR VIEW OF 
POTENTIAL ECONOMIC BENEFITS TO PANAMA FROM A POST-, 
1999 MILITARY PRESENCE, I.E. ABOUT $200 MILLION PER 
ANNUM.
