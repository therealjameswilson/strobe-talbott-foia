SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY'S MEETING WITH LATVIAN STATE 
SECRETARY RIEKSTINS 
RELEASE IN FULL 
DECLASSIFIED]
Classified by EUR DAS Ronald D. Asmus for reasons para. 1.5 
(b) and (d) 
1. -ft4---Summary: Deputy Secretary Talbott met January 21 
with Latvian State Secretary Maris Riekstins to discuss the 
NATO summit, social integration, commercial investment and 
relations with Russia. Both agreed,on a "Madrid Plus" 
Washington Summit outcome, although the Deputy Secretary 
said there could be no precommitments or predesignation for 
NATO membership. Riekstins said Latvia is producing a new 
agenda to promote social integration but said western 
financing would be needed. He explained that encouraging 
increased U.S. investment is a top priority of the new 
Latvian government, and Amb. Kalnins added a request for a 
U.S. marketing study to help attract a flagship U.S. 
investment. On relations with Russia, Riekstins said 
Latvia was subject to discriminatory economic actions by 
Russia. The Deputy Secretary underscored that Russia's 
relations with the Baltics area is a litmus test of the 
seriousness of its reform. The Deputy Secretary concluded 
by saying the U.S. will increasingly differentiate in its 
policy towards the Baltic states, but that it would be a 
positive differentiation, not discrimination or breaking 
the synergy of the joint Baltic Charter approach. Other 
Latvian participants included Arab. Ojars Kalnins, MFA 
officials Rihards Mucins and Ivars Pundurs, and DCM Einars 
Semanis. Other USG participants were EUR DAS Ronald Asmus, 
D Staff John Bass, and desk officer Damian Leader 
(notetaker) . End Summary. 
2. Riekstins began by reviewing the accomplishments 
of the past year and laying out Latvia's agenda for 1999. 
Latvia hopes the Washington Summit will produce a -"Madrid 
Plus" outcome and referred the Deputy Secretary to a non-
paper for the details. On Social integration, the 
government would have a new plan formulated by May 1, but 
will need additional foreign assistance to put the plan 
into action. Rikstins said he looked forward to working 
with the U.S. on the Economic Bilateral Working Group, and 
in particular, on how to attract a flagship U.S. 
investment. He suggested the U.S. could do a Kievenaar-
style study to assist potential U.S. investors. Riekstins 
said he had indications that Russia is not ready to 
reestablish a dialogue with Latvia, and cited a non-paper 
given to Sweden that complains of economic discrimination 
against ethnic Russians in Latvia and Estonia.. Russia, he 
said, ignores international evaluations when judging 
Latvia's performance.

3. The Deputy Secretary replied that we support a 
"Madrid Plus" result from the Washington Summit and want to 
convey a sense of momentum in the process, especially for 
deserving aspirant countries, including Latvia. He 
cautioned that we could not support predesignation or 
preselection of candidates in the communique, adding that 
we need to reach a common understanding of that gray area 
between guaranteed membership and a closed door. On social 
integration, the Deputy Secretary expressed his great 
admiration for the Latvian government's handling of the 
closely-fought referendum on citizenship and naturalization 
last year. That Latvia approved OSCE-compliant citizenship 
and naturalization law has great implications for its 
relations with the West and Russia, as well as 
domestically. On U.S. investment, the Deputy Secretary 
said that Amb. Holmes has made that a priority but there 
was more that Latvia could do to make it attractive to U.S. 
companies. ye encouraged Latvia to use the upcoming 
Economic Bilateral Working Group to inject more energy into 
attracting investment. 
4. (C)  The Deputy Secretary reiterated that we view 
Russian policy towards the Baltics as one of the litmus 
tests of Russian performance on reform. This includes its 
attitude toward the NATO aspirations of its independent 
neighbors. The Deputy Secretary remarked that leaders of 
the Caucasus republics envied the Baltics for the 
Partnership Commission and look to the Baltics as models of 
where they want to be. He asked Riekstins whether Latvia 
had much contact with the Caucasus states and, when told 
they did not, encouraged Latvia to find ways to work with 
the CIS states on common problems. 
5. (C) The Deputy Secretary then asked Riekstins who he 
sensed was running Baltic policy in Moscow. Riekstins said 
he though Deputy FM Avdeyev was in charge. He said FM 
Birkays met with FM Ivanov at the Oslo OSCE meeting and 
came away feeling the meeting was positive. But, speaking 
later, Avdeyev took a harder line, saying the citizenship 
referendum was only the first step. DAS Asmus added that 
after the Oslo meeting Titov told him that Russia was 
changing its tone with Latvia, but not the substance of its 
Baltic policy. The Deputy Secretary said that the U.S. 
wants to give Russia its proper weight in U.S.-Baltic 
relations: we don't want to ignore it, but it should not 
become a grand obsession. We need to get the tone and 
proportionality right, he added. 
6. (444- The Deputy Secretary said the U.S. wants to get 
away from lumping the Baltics together, and move towards a

positive form of differentiation, one that did not 
discriminate or break the synergy of working to promote 
their common destiny with the West. Riekstins replied that 
European support for the region is diminishing. The EU has 
blocked Latvia from beginning accession negotiations and 
been even less helpful with Lithuania. That harms Baltic 
cooperation. Riekstins added that it is focusing now on 
areas, like agriculture, where it can put itself into a 
better position with the EU. 
7. The Deputy Secretary replied that Latvia has been 
the most disadvantaged by negative differentiation. The EU 
has tapped Estonia, Brzezinski wants Lithuania in NATO, and 
that leaves Latvia in third place. The referendum proved 
pessimists wrong and has given positive energy to Latvia's 
integration with the West. When we speak of 
differentiation, we want to base it on real merit. Not all 
are equally strong in all areas. We want to use the 
Partnership Commission to suit the specific needs of each 
country as well as our common agenda. 
8. Amb. Kalnins then said that Latvia's number one 
goal now is attracting U.S. investment, and hoped to do so 
by the Partnership Commission in May. What is needed is a 
marketing survey to identify promising areas. The Deputy 
Secretary replied that he did not know if we could do.that 
since the private sector doesn',t act on USG 
recommendations. "Build it, and they will come" is the 
best advice for attracting investors. Riekstins concluded 
the meeting by mentioning the Secretary's promise to 
President Ulmanis'that, if the referendum passed, she would 
visit Riga.
