I enclose a copy of our correspondence in the summer 
of 1992. I still adhere to our target date that the 
world can achieve a "single, global authority" by 1997, 
or, allowing for some slippage, before the turn of the 
century. 
The reason for my optimism is that, although you 
would never know it from the media, the United Nations 
has now, for the first time ever, willy-nilly, entered 
into a serious constitutional debate. I enclose a 
memorandum of December 13 and a U.N. press release of 
December 3 as documentation. 
I also enclose our new brochure on the Binding Triad 
system for global decision-making. The last brochure 
went to press just before the Berlin Wall came down, 
but I am convinced that the Binding Triad concept is at 
least as valid now as it was then. 
I am in regular touch with some of the leading 
players in this "open-ended Working Group to consider 
all aspects of the question of equitable representation 
on and increase in the membership of the Security Coun-
cil, and other matters'related to the Council." In 
fact, we have already started to draft a set of about 
half a dozen amendments to the U.N. Charter that would 
transform the world body into a "single, world authori-
ty," and hope to have the first draft done within a 
couple of months. 
I would really like to talk to you about this. I 
will be in Washington from February 3-6 for the Third 
Global Structures Convocation: Human Rights, Global 
Governance, and Strengthening the United Nations, and 
wonder if we could meet during that period. (Brochure 
enclosed.) Either I could come to your office, we 
could meet for lunch, or, alternatively, our board of 
directors would be delighted to invite you as guest of
