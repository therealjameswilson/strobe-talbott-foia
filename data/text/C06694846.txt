2. SUMMARY. OVER A TWO-HOUR LUNCH HOSTED BY PRESIDENT 
BEDIE FOR THE DEPUTY SECRETARY OCTOBER 25, THE US AND 
IVORIAN PARTICIPANTS REVIEWED THE REGIONAL RESPONSE TO 
THE SITUATION IN LIBERIA, THE FUTURE ROLE OF ECOWAS, 
THE SITUATION IN NIGERIA, COTE D'IVOIRE'S OWN SUCCESS 
IN NATIONAL INTEGRATION, ITS FUTURE ROLE AS A 
PARTICIPANT IN INTERNATIONAL PEACE-KEEPING OPERATIONS, 
AND US PARTICIPATION IN COTE D'IVOIRE'S ECONOMIC 
RECOVERY. THE IVORIANS ASKED FOR USG HELP WITH 
REFUGEES, AND ASSISTANCE FOR IVORIAN TROOPS GUARDING 
THE BORDER. BEDIE USED A MAP TO EXPLAIN TO THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY WHY, IN HIS VIEW, COTE D'IVOIRE COULD NOT 
PARTICIPATE MILITARILY IN PEACE-KEEPING OPERATIONS IN 
LIBERIA--OUT OF CONCERN THAT THE ETHNIC LINKS BETWEEN 
THE PEOPLES ON THE TWO SIDES OF THE BORDER COULD RESULT 
IN A SPILLING OVER OF THE CONFLICT INTO COTE D'IVOIRE. 
UNDERSTANDING THE RISK OF INFLAMING ETHNIC TENSIONS, 
AND THE COMPLEXITY OF THE PROBLEM, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
ASKED INSTEAD FOR INCREASED DIPLOMATIC ACTIVITY BY COTE 
D'IVOIRE IN SUPPORT OF ECOWAS EFFORTS NOW BEING LED BY 
PRESIDENT RAWLINGS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ALSO NOTED 
THAT HE HAD TOLD THE MINISTER OF DEFENSE, EARLIER IN 
THE DAY, OF USG WILLINGNESS TO HELP TRAIN A MODEST 
NUMBER OF IVORIAN OFFICERS IN PEACE-KEEPING OPERATIONS, 
HOPING THAT AT SOME FUTURE TIME COTE D'IVOIRE WOULD BE 
ABLE TO PARTICIPATE ELSEWHERE (OTHER THAN LIBERIA) IN 
SUCH OPERATIONS. AT THE END OF THE LUNCHEON, THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY, RESPONDING TO AN IDEA ADVANCED BY THE 
PRIME MINISTER, SAID THAT THE USG WOULD ORGANIZE, IN 
COTE D'IVOIRE, A SEMINAR ON NATIONAL INTEGRATION, TO 
ENABLE COTE D'IVOIRE TO SHARE WITH ITS NEIGHBORS THE 
SECRET OF ITS SUCCESS IN ACHIEVING A STABLE AND UNIFIED 
COUNTRY. THE ATMOSPHERE WAS EXTREMELY CORDIAL 
THROUGHOUT. END SUMMARY.
