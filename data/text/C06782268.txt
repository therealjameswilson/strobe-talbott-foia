1. +e..-4- SUMMARY: On October 6, Acting Secretary Talbott met 
with Bulgarian Deputy Foreign Minister Dimitrov to discuss 
the crisis in Kosovo, NATO enlargement and Bulgaria's 
language dispute with Macedonia. Talbott underscored the 
seriousness of the situation in Kosovo and our willingness 
to use force if necessary to secure Milosevic's compliance 
with UNSCR 1199. Dimitrov indicated that Bulgaria was 
prepared to consider requests for support from NATO for 
any operation in Kosovo, but emphasized that the GOB 
needed to know as quickly as possible what assistance NATO 
might require. Dimitrov urged NATO to avoid a repeat of 
Madrid at the 1999 Washington Summit, urging that any 
communique language mentioning aspirants by name include 
Bulgaria. He also stressed the importance of a robust 
Open Door package to pro-reform forces in Southeast 
Europe. Talbott noted that the U.S. shares many of the 
same goals as Bulgaria vis-a'-vis the Open Door, but did 
not endorse any of Dimitrov's specific proposals. Talbott 
urged the GOB to make a concerted effort to resolve its 
language dispute with Macedonia after that country's 
October 18 elections. Dimitrov stressed the GOB's 
readiness to engage on the issue regardless of the outcome 
on October 18. Dimitrov also raised the timing of an 
official visit by Prime Minister Kostov to the U.S., 
underscoring the GOB's interest in having the visit take 
place before the April 1999 Washington Summit. END

Bulgaria's Ambassador to the U.S., Philip Dimitrov, 
accompanied DEM Dimitrov. Deputy Assistant Secretary

attended the meeting.

Talbott underscored that a diplomatic resolutionto the

Bulgaria to play an active role supporting international 
efforts to resolve the crisis, particularly in the OSCE. 
4. 4e4-Dimitrov thanked the Acting Secretary for providing 
U.S. views on Kosovo and asked that the U.S. continue to 
consult as the crisis unfolds. He said that the GOB was 
prepared to discuss how it might directly support a NATO 
operation in Kosovo, but asked that the Alliance make 
clear as early as possible what support it would require 
from Bulgaria. The GOB understood that NATO might require 
overflight rights and, following a ceasefire, the right of 
transit to facilitate delivery of humanitarian aid and 
other supplies to a NATO force in Kosovo. The GOB was 
prepared to consider these requests, but required 
sufficient time to meet- its constitutional obligations and 
initiate the formal decision-making process before acting. 
The GOB also wanted time to lay the political groundwork 
for a decision to provide direct support to NATO, Dimitrov 
said. Talbott said that the U.S. would cooperate by 
helping to identify specific means of military support 
required from Bulgaria. This would be conveyed through 
Ambassadors and specific NATO channels. 
NATO ENLARGEMENT 
5. --ect The crisis in Kosovo made clear the importance of 
strengthening the stable democracies in Southeast Europe 
as the best means of containing "the contagion," Dimitrov 
opined. From Bulgaria's perspective, it is particularly 
important to develop an enhanced partnership with NATO. 
We also. want to deepen our partnership with the U.S. 
beyond the current Southeast Europe Action Plan and the 
U.S.-Bulgaria Bilateral Work Plan, he said. After 
concluding a three-year loan agreement with the IMF, the 
government is now able to finalize a budget that will 
allow the GOB to push military reform, which he 
characterized as the weakest element of Bulgaria's • 
application for NATO membership. Dimitrov noted that 
Prime Minister Kostov will meet with the North Atlantic 
Council (NAC) on October 7. He asked that Ambassador 
Vershbow make a clear statement about the positive nature 
of Bulgaria's reform efforts and note U.S. support for 
these efforts. The GOB hopes that U.S. support will 
influence other NATO members. Dimitrov hoped that the 
U.S. could also provide additional assistance. 
Specifically, he asked that the U.S. assign a resident 
advisor to the Ministry of Defense. He also urged the 
U.S. to undertake this fall its promised study (the

6. --(7e4 Turning to the question of when Bulgaria might join 
NATO, Dimitrov noted that the GOB "is realistic" about its 
immediate prospects. The GOB understands that there is a 
reluctance to issue new invitations at the 1999 Washington 
Summit. Their greatest fear is a repeat of Madrid, 
specifically the mention of Romania and Slovenia, but not 
Bulgaria, in the Washington communique, he said. Dimitrov 
also expressed concern about the potential impact of a 
decision to admit Slovenia only. In Bulgaria's view, NATO 
had to do more than simply reaffirm its Open Door policy 
in Washington. If the communique would cite potential 
future members by name, the GOB believes Bulgaria should 
be mentioned. If no country is cited by name, Dimitrov 
suggested that the communique include language along the 
lines of the following: "the stable, democratic countries 
of Southeast Europe are the natural focus of further 
enlargement, if they continue to perform adequately on the 
way to membership." Dimitrov also proposed that NATO 
develop a mechanism to delineate between aspirants and 
non-aspiring PEP countries. A credible Open Door had to 
include substantive programs that allow countries likes 
Bulgaria to develop closer links to NATO, he said. 
7. 4G4-Speaking personally, Talbott noted that there is an 
emerging consensus within the Alliance that it would be 
premature to issue new invitations in 1999. While he 
could not endorse all the specifics of Dimitrov's Open 
Door proposals, he noted that the general thrust of the 
Bulgarian proposals reflects U.S. thinking. Specifically, 
Talbott underscored the importance of developing an Open 
Door package that highlights the progress countries like 
Bulgaria have made towards membership and encourages them 
to continue the reforms necessary to become a NATO member. 
We want to give Bulgaria a vote of confidence, he said. 
Talbott pledged that the U.S. will consult regularly with 
Bulgaria and other partners between now and the 1999 
Washington Summit to ensure that our views are understood. 
MA6EDONIA LANGUAGE DISPUTE 
8. —(-e* Talbott briefly addressed Bulgaria's language 
dispute with Macedonia, which he characterized as an issue 
of particular interest to him. He recognized that both 
parties were essentially in a holding pattern until after 
the October 18 Macedonian elections, but stressed the need 
for both sides to seek to resolve the issue afterwards. 
As the larger of the two parties, Bulgaria enjoyed a 
certain political advantage over Macedonia, Talbott 
observed. But Bulgaria's position also entailed a moral 
obligation to lead. Resolving the dispute would advance 
Bulgaria's interests and burnish its reputation as a good 
international citizen, Talbott said. It would help 
Macedonia cope with an extraordinarily difficult situation 
and, just as importantly, represent a defeat for the 
forces and attitudes in Southeast Europe epitomized by men 
like Milosevic. The U.S. will take the lead in praising 
the GOB if it can resolve the language dispute, Talbott

9. 4-e4-Dimitrov was quick to einphasize Bulgaria's interest 
in resolving the language dispute. The central 'issue is 
Macedonia's desire to identify a Macedonian "national 
minority" in Bulgaria, not the characterization of the 
Macedonian language, he said. The GOB has no intention of 
waiting for a change in the Macedonian government before 
reengaging on the language issue. We are willing to move 
forward with negotiations regardless of the results of 
Macedonia's elections, Dimitrov said. There is nothing to 
the "myth"(that the Bulgarian government would prefer to 
negotiate with the non-communists because they promise 
greater flexibility than the current Macedonian 
leadership, he said. 
KOSTOV VISIT 
10. -ec.-}-Dimitrov expressed the GOB's strong interest in 
moving forward with an official visit by Prime Minister 
Kostov to the United States. A key to the visit's success 
would be the level at which Kostov was received. The GOB 
hoped that a visit might be possible in late January or 
early February 1999. Dimitrov underscored Kostov's 
interest in coming to the U.S. prior to the April 1999 
Washington Summit.
