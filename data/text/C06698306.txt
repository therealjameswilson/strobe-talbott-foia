1. summary. during a june 24 working lunch with the 
acting secretary, uzbekistan president karimov requested 
usg assistance and cooperatiori in building a civil 
society. he pronounced himself a "changed" man from his 
1993 discussions on such issues with the acting 
secretary. "our quarrels with you three years ago are 
history." karimov also criticized moscow's efforts to 
create "supra-national structures" in the cis, and 
predicted that russian pressure on uzbekistan would 
continue regardless of who wins the july 3 election. he 
said uzbekistan would not join the cis customs union. he 
asked for usg support for central asian regional 
cooperation, noting that gou initiatives were being 
miscast as attempts to doMinate the region. end summary. 
human rights 
2. the acting secretary recalled his discussions 
with karimov in tashkent in 1993 and noted that, as he had 
said then, the usg places great importance on uzbekistan's 
independence and sovereignty, and stands ready to assist 
the gou's efforts to build a civil society. karimov 
replied that three years ago he had discussed "without 
pleasure" the issues of civil society and democracy, "but 
now i've changed." he said uzbekistan society's values 
and mentality had also changed, under usg influence. 
uzbekistan needed the cooperation and assistance of the us 
in the democratization sphere. "dur quarrels with you 
three years ago are history," he declared, adding "i 
remember with gratitude your criticism."

steps, such as its cooperation with the osce and 
international human rights ngo's. he urged karimov to 
move ahead on introducing greater press freedom, and 
suggested that karimov use his june 26 press conference at 
the national press club to emphasize the importance of a 
free press. shattuck said he also hoped that karimov "in 
the near term" would allow domestic ngo's to register and

. — role of CIS 
5. I't$ the acting secretary asked for karimov's views on 
the cis. karimov replied that, following the collapse of 
the soviet union, he still sees the cis as necessary to 
solve "disintegration problems" in a democratic and 
civilized way. otherwise, the alternative could be 
yugoslav style violence. however, uzbekistan 
categorically rejected "supra-national structures," which 
would lead to the reconstitution of the ussr, and a 
reduction in the sovereignty of individual states. 
karimov said he opposed the development of a political or 
military union. he predicted that russian pressure on 
uzbekistan would continue regardless of who wins the july 
3 election. he also criticized the recent agreement 
between russia, belarus, kazakstan and krygyzstan. russia 
was trying to convince everyone that the agreement 
protected each country's sovereignty, but it was •not 
true. uzbekistan was not going to join the customs union 
or the treaty of four. karimov said he saw the customs 
union as an attempt to deny uzbekistan economic 
sovereignty and independent access to the world market.
