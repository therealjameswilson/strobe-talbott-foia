SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S AUGUST 27 MEETING 
WITH CANADIAN ACTING FOREIGN MINISTER SMITH: HAITI 
1. CLASSIFIED BY: THOMAS G. WESTON, CHARGE D'AFFAIRES. 
REASON: 1.5 (B) AND (D) 
2. SUMMARY: DURING THEIR AUGUST 27 MEETING, DEPUTY 
SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT AND CANADIAN ACTING FOREIGN 
MINISTER GORDON SMITH EXCHANGED VIEWS ON HAITI, INCLUDING 
CANADIAN CONCERNS ABOUT THE RECENT DEPLOYMENT OF THE 82ND 
AIRBORNE DIVISION, THE NEED TO COORDINATE PRESSURE ON THE 
IFISI AND THE ISSUE OF A FUTURE UN MANDATE. (SEE SEPTELS 
ON OTHER ISSUES.) END SUMMARY 
3. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT, ACCOMPANIED BY AMBASSADOR 
AT LARGE FOR THE NEW INDEPENDENT STATES JAMES COLLINS, 
EXECUTIVE ASSISTANT ERIC EDELMAN, CHARGE TOM WESTON AND 
EMBASSY ESTCOUNS LYSYSHYN (NOTETAKER) DISCUSSED HAITI 
DURING HIS AUGUST 27 MEETING WITH ACTING FOREIGN MINISTER 
GORDON SMITH. KATHRYN MCCALLION, ADM FOR LATIN 
AMERICA/CARIBBEAN, MICHAEL PHILLIPS, ADM FOR USA, PAUL 
DUBOIS, DIRECTOR GENERAL FOR EUROPE, AND RALPH LYSYSHYN, 
DIRECTOR GENERAL FOR INTERNATIONAL SECURITY ALSO 
PARTICIPATED. 
4. SMITH OPENED THE DISCUSSION BY EXPRESSING CONCERN 
ABOUT THE RECENT DEPLOYMENT OF THE 82ND AIRBORNE DIVISION 
IN HAITI. FIRST, THE CANADIANS WERE "SURPRISED" ABOUT 
THE DEPLOYMENT AND THE LACK OF ADVANCE CONSULTATIONS. 
SECOND, HE WANTED TO KNOW IF THE DEPLOYMENT REPRESENTED 
AN "Implicit CRITICISM" OF CANADA'S LOWER PROFILE METHOD 
OF OPERATIONS. SMITH ADDED THAT, BASED ON RECENT GOC 
CONSOLATIONS WITH THE UN IN NY, THE CANADIANS ARE

5. D/S TALBOTT RESPONDED THAT IN TERMS OF THE PROCESS, 
THE CANADIANS SHOULD NOT BE CAUGHT BY "SURPRISE," AND HE 
WOULD TAKE STEPS TO SEE THAT SOME KIND OF "AUTODEFAULT" 
SYSTEM IS PUT IN PLACE TO ENSURE THAT THERE ARE NO FUTURE 
SURPRISES, EITHER IN OTTAWA OR ON THE GROUND IN HAITI, 
AND THAT SIM/LAR ACTION DOES NOT OCCUR WITHOUT PRIOR 
DIALOGUE. SMITH AND ADM MCCALLION EXPRESSED APPRECIATION 
FOR THAT ASSURANCE. 
6. TURNING TO SUBSTANCE, AND THE QUESTION AS TO WHETHER 
IT WAS "RIGHT" TO HAVE A HIGHLY VISIBLE MILITARY PRESENCE 
IN HAITI, D/S TALBOTT RESPONDED AFFIRMATIVELY, NOTING 
U.S. INTENTION TO INCREASE THE VISIBILITY OF THE U.S. 
MILITARY THROUGH SHIP VISITS AND ADDITIONAL LAND-BASED 
ACTIVITIES. SMITH THEN NOTED THAT CANADA HAD A DIFFERENT 
CONCEPT OF UN OPERATIONS, ADDING THAT SUCH A STRONG 
PHYSICAL SHOW OF DETERRENCE WAS NOT TYPICAL OF UN 
OPERATIONS. MCCALLION ASKED AGAIN IF THE 82ND AIRBORNE 
ACTION WAS AN IMPLICIT CRITICISM OF THE CANADIAN PROCESS, 
NOTING THE CANADIAN PRACTICE OF FEWER LOW-.KEY DAYTIME 
OPERATIONS AND QUICK RAPID BACK-UP TO THE POLICE AT 
NIGHT. IN RESPONSE, D/S TALBOTT ACKNOWLEDGED THAT THERE 
HAD BEEN AN "EXPLICIT" CRITICISM OF THE CANADIAN PROCESS 
BASED ON CONCERN THAT "KEEPING HEADS DOWN INVITES THE 
KIND OF ACTION THAT WE SEEK TO DETER." MCCALLION 
ACKNOWLEDGED THIS HAD BEEN A PROBLEM BECAUSE OF THE 
ABSENCE OF RULES OF ENGAGEMENT AT THE BEGINNING OF THIS 
MANDATE. 
7. TALBOTT THEN WENT ON TO PRAISE THE EXCELLENT JOB THAT 
CANADIAN CIVPOL WERE DOING ON THE GROUND, AND ON THE NEED 
FOR MORE "HANDS ON" MONITORING OF HNP, ADDING THAT WE 
WERE TRYING TO HELP WITH THE JUSTICE DEPARTMENT. 
MCCALLION ADDED THAT THE FRENCH LEADERSHIP OF CIVPOL HAD 
BEEN A "DISASTER" BECAUSE OF DIFFERING APPROACHES TO 
POLICING TASKS AND THE CIVPOL MANDATE. THE DEPARTURE OF 
A PARTICULARLY DIFFICULT FRENCH HEAD WAS LEADING TO MORE 
ACTIVISM. SHE WENT ON TO STRESS THE "FRAGILITY" OF THE 
SITUATION AND THE ABSENCE OF FINANCIAL SUPPORT. IT WAS 
NECESSARY TO GET MONEY INTO THE SYSTEM, TO PAY THE 
POLICE, AND TO FUND PENSIONS FOR THE SOLDIERS. CANADA 
WAS MAKING IT AN ISSUE AT THE HIGHEST LEVEL, PRESSING THE 
IMF AND WORLD BANK. D/S TALBOTT AGREED WITH THE NEED TO 
MOVE ON THIS PROBLEM. WHILE THE RECENT HAITIAN 
PARLIAMENTARY ACTION IN THIS AREA WAS LATE AND
