2. Summary: on august 26, bulgarian foreign minister 
georgi pirinski met with acting secretary stobe talbott 
for 45 minutes to discuss bilateral issues. FM pirinski 
was spending two days in washington, before attending the 
democratic national convention in chicago at the 
invitation of the national democratio institute. Pirinski 
gave a clear presentation, hitting broadly the themes of 
regional (balkan) security and cooperation, the economic 
situation, and european security. he then touched on 
relations with FYROM and turkeyl and promised to move 
forward on bilateral issues such as intellectual property 
right enforcement and visa policy for u.s. residents in 
bulgaria. The Acting Secretary noted the link between 
bulgarian commitment to economic reform and u.s. support 
for bulgaria before the ifi's, and pressed for completion 
of a bilateral assistance agreement. pirinski stated his 
election to the presidency would ensure a more stable 
government capable of carrying out reforms. end of 
summary. 
3. FM pirinski thanked the u.s. for its support of the 
sofia conference. he urged the U.S. to support bulgarian 
efforts to emphasize regional cooperation in 
infrastructure development, arguing that such projects did 
not have to be grandiose or expensive. He stated his 
support for the south balkan development initiative and 
the soUtheastern europe cooperation initiative (support he 
echoed in various fora in Washington). - 
4. the fm preemptively recognized the general perception 
that commitment to economic reform did not run very deep 
in bulgaria. he blamed the delays in implementing reforms 
on frequent elections which had created a political 
climate where structural reform was impossible. now, 
reforms were inevitable and there wasa broad consensus 
for reform. 
5. Pirinski argued that bulgaria was westward looking and 
eager to continue and intensify contacts with nato (moving 
beyond pfp exercises and into involvement/contacts with 
nato itself). In its internaldecision making with regard 
to nato membership, bulgaria was examining what is in its

6. the fm touched on a few other issues. he noted that 
while the gob tries to have balanced and friendly 
relations with its neighbors, thereare areas of tension. 
he noted the language issue that affects bulgaria's 
relationship with FYROM, and frictions with turkey, 
including those over boundaries in the black sea and 
turkey's blockage of wto membership for bulgaria. he 
noted that visas for u.s. residents in bulgaria were a 
problem that he hoped would be resolved soon. he stated 
his determination that progress would continue on the.ipr 
front. Pirinski said the two countries should move 
forward with the treaty to avoid double taxation. he 
noted that bulgaria had a problem with "governance"; the 
changes of 1989 and sanctions-busting activities had 
created fertile grounds for "illicit businesses." he 
hoped that increased investment and economic growth would 
rechannel those energies into legal activities. 
7. touching upon the presidential elections, pirinski 
said that the udf wanted to use a presidential victory to 
press for early parliamentary elections. he said that if 
the opposition was successful in achieving new elections, 
or even if it used the presidency as a forum to whip up 
dissent, he feared a return to the deadlock that 
characterized earlier governments (implying that economic 
reform would be jeopardized). 
8. after congratulating the fm on the success of the 
sofia conference, acting secretary talbott noted that U.S. 
support of bulgaria before the ifi's was contingent on 
continued reform. he strongly stressed, in conjunction 
with economic reform, the need to complete the bilateral 
assistance agreement, which has been pending for four 
years. he noted specifically the issue of the endowment 
for the American University in Bulgaria. 
9. on nato, Mr. talbott stressed that while people 
referred to "groups" of new members, or blocs, nato would 
make its decisions on new entrants on a case by case 
basis, and the process would be transparent and ongoing. 
he noted that this process of expansion will be carried 
out in tandem with the development of a special 
rito-russia relationship. finally, he ended by noting the 
bipartisan support for a successful transformation of the 
cee countries.

assistance agreement, as well as starting up talks on the
