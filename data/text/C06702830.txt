8. -EG4--SANTER CITED REINFORCEMENT OF EU STRUCTURES, 
INCLUDING DEVELOPMENT OF A TRUE COMMON FOREIGN AND 
SECURITY POLICY, AS ANOTHER KEY ELEMENT OF EUROPEAN 
INTEGRATION AND CALLED THIS AN OBJECTIVE OF THE ONGOING 
INTERGOVERNMENTAL CONFERENCE (IGC) SCHEDULED TO END IN 
JUNE. THIS WOULD MAKE THE EU A BETTER PARTNER FOR THE 
U.S. HE UNDERSCORED THE IMPORTANCE HE ATTACHED TO 
INTEGRATING THE WESTERN EUROPEAN UNION (WEU) INTO THE 
EU, BUT NOT IN THIS IGC. 
9.4;4— THE SECRETARY SAID U.S.-EU DIFFERENCES WERE 
RELATIVELY SMALL BUT, UNFORTUNATELY, CONSULTING WITH 
THE EU AS AN INSTITUTION CONTINUED TO BE COMPLICATED 
AND OFTEN FRUSTRATING. SHE NOTED THAT DURING HER FOUR 
YEARS AT THE UN, SHE CONSISTENTLY ENCOUNTERED 
DIFFICULTIES IN HOLDING PRODUCTIVE, TIMELY 
CONSULTATIONS WITH THE EU. TOO OFTEN, THE MOMENT FOR 
DECISIVE ACTION WAS PASSED BY THE TIME THE EU HAD A 
COMMON POSITION. WE NEEDED A BETTER MECHANISM, SHE 
SAID, TO PERMIT CONSULTATIONS WITH THE EU AND ITS 
MEMBER STATES WHILE EU POSITIONS WERE BEING DEVELOPED. 
10. SANTER AND BRITTAN ACKNOWLEDGED THAT THIS WAS 
AN IMPORTANT PROBLEM. SANTER HOPED THE IGC WOULD 
IMPROVE EU DECISION-MAKING. BRITTAN URGED THE U.S. TO 
MAKE MORE USE OF CONTACTS WITH THE COMMISSION TO GET 
READOUTS OF MEMBER STATE DIFFERENCES AS POSITIONS WERE 
BEING DEVELOPED AND ADDED THAT BOTH SHORT AND LONG-TERM 
SOLUTIONS WERE NEEDED.

SETTLEMENT.
