1.am_writing to enlist your assistance in establishing a 
telemedicine-satellite link between the Texas Children's Hospital 
in my home of HOUston and health care professionals in oil-rich 
Tyumen Province in Western Siberia. Such a satellite hook up 
would be a cost-effective way to supply vital clinical 
consultation and health.care education to an area of Russia that 
is both desperate for medical information and crucial to the 
stability of the Russian economy. This initiative is 
particularly timely in light of your upcoming meeting with 
Russian President Boris Yeltsin. 
Texas Children's Hospital has been instrumental in providing 42 
tons of donated medical supplies and equipment and pediatric 
medical assistance to the province of Tyumen through Project 
HEARD, Houston Emergency Aid for Relief and Disaster, a non-
profit organization founded by Texas Children's Hospital and 
private business in March of 1992. To complement their current 
plans to airlift additional medical relief to Tyumen this summer, 
Project HEARD hopes to provide health care professionals in 
Tyumen with training in the use and applications of the donated 
medical supplies through a telemedicine satellite link. 
There is currently a NASA telemedicine demonstration project 
slated to begin in mid-June between four U.S. medical facilities, 
including the Texas Medical Center in Houston, and a single 
facility in Moscow, Russia. If you were to indicate your support 
to NASA for coordinating these telemedicine projects, Tyumen and 
Moscow could receive pediatric medical programming at a minimal 
cost, as the infrastructure for a telemedicine exchange between 
Texas Children's Hospital and Tyumen is already largely in place. 
Russia has donated the use of a former military satellite and 
associated earthstations to the current "Spacebridge" project,
