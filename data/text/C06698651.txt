NATO and European Security 
The Deputy Secretary outlined the USG's views of 
a future security architecture for Europe that would have 
NATO serving as the basis for the continent's collective 
security. Citing the fact that European states desiring 
NATO membership are in varying states of readiness for 
joining, Horbulyn suggested that a process of developing 
ties first with the EU, then the WEU, then going on 
eventually to NATO membership might provide a model for 
bringing new states into NATO.

problems may have temporarily halted Russia's ability to 
act on this issue.

deployment would be a political disaster for the Kuchma

destroyed in the nearest future.)

NATO/Ukraine, NATO/Russia Charters 
11. J,C) Citing Ukraine's active role in PFP, exercise 
Peace Shield," and its agreement in principle to 
establish a NATO information center in Kiey, Horbulyn 
said that whenever Ukraine achieves progress with NATO, 
Russia reacts strongly. He said that if Russia decides 
that nuclear weapons could be redeployed on its western 
border, it will look for a forceful way to respond, most 
likely by increasing political and economic pressure on 
Ukraine. In that eventuality, Ukraine would need some 
form of guarantees that its interests would be 
protected. He suggested that a NATO/Ukraine charter, 
signed in conjunction with agreement on a NATO/Russia 
charter would be the most stabilizing approach.

Ukraine's Relationship with NATO 
12. Vpr The Deputy Secretary said that he, the 
President, Mr. Lake and the Secretary, have acknowledged 
Ukraine's leading role in central and eastern Europe. It 
follows then that NATO must have a steadily strengthening 
relationship with Ukraine as it expands. The 
relationship must include that possibility that Ukraine 
might at some future date decide to join NATO. That 
decision is for Ukraine to make. In the meantime, the 
Deputy Secretary cited two things NATO will not do: 1) , 
put pressure on Ukraine to apply for membership in NATO, 
and 2)• accept anyone else's attempt to exclude Ukraine 
from eligibility for membership in NATO. 
NATO Expansion and Russia 
13. kdl The Deputy Secretary noted that this was far 
from an abstract expression of policy.-He said one 
Russian official recently said that Russia wants it 
understood that the Baltic states and Ukraine could never 
be allowed to join NATO. He said that the U.S. has made 
very clear that such a position is unacceptable on its 
face and that. a Russian position on NATO that includes 
that proviso would "go noWhere." The Germans, French and 
British have conveyed the same message to Russia. 
14. ,4,e) The Deputy Secretary said that the President 
and the Secretary believe it would for NATO and Russia to 
develop a positive, constructive relationship. This was 
' the logic behind the Secretary's Stuttgart speech and the 
idea of a charter with Russia. The concept is not simply 
to calm Russian fears, but to ensure that Russia respects 
the right of every country to make its ownb decisions 
vis-a-vis NATO -- particularly Ukraine. 
15. .kel The Deputy Secretary said it was clear that 
each country would proceed at its own pace in developing 
its relationship with NATO, but it was essential that the 
process be transparent. He stressed that NATO's door 
must be open and never exclude the Baltic states, Ukraine 
or any other democratic country. He said the U.S. would 
fight to maintain an open process.
