. Summary:, 
2. 44;-)- Deputy Secretary Talbott met with new Canadian 
Deputy Foreign Minister Gaetan Lavertu October 27 to 
discuss multilateral and bilateral issues. (AA/S 
Gutierrez, OAS Ambassador and U.S. Summit Coordinator 
Lauredo, and Lavertu had a separate, impromptu discussion 
of hemispheric issues while waiting for the Talbott-
Lavertu meeting to begin.) Lavertu pushed for a 
Presidential bilateral visit to Canada before the Quebec 
City Summit of the Americas in April and brought up 
Canadian concerns regarding softwood lumber and in-transit 
preclearance. Lavertu said that agreement for a North 
Korean diplomatic mission to Canada would be announced 
soon. Talbott offered to recommend that the new President 
consider promptly the Canadian proposal for an early 
Presidential bilateral visit to Ottawa. Talbott briefed 
Lavertu on the Secretary's trip to North Korea and her 
impressions of Kim Jong I1's strategy for his country. 
Talbott expressed our strong view that the U.S. and Chile, 
as part of the Summit troika, have a more active role in 
agenda planning for the Quebec Summit and that a broader 
Executive Committee, made up of all past hosts, replace 
the troika in planning for future Summits. Talbott asked 
for Canadian support on both items. Gutierrez reviewed 
U.S positions regarding Peru and on Plan Colombia, and 
asked for continued Canadian engagement in the region. 
Lauredo thanked Lavertu for Canada's leadership in Peru. 
Talbott and Lavertu compared notes on the critical state 
of the Middle East Peace Process. Talbott and Lavertu 
agreed that, without credible opposition participation, 
upcoming,Haitian elections were unlikely to strengthen 
democracy there. Talbott also suggested continued contact 
on Russia, as Putin would visit Ottawa in December. End 
Summary.

Bilateral Presidential Visit/Summit of the Americas: 
6. -K-4- Lavertu said that the Canadians hoped that our 
new president would respect tradition and make his first 
post-election foreign visit to Canada, as had his 
predecessors. Lavertu said that Ottawa proposed that our 
new president plan to come to Canada well in advance of 
the Summit of the Americas scheduled for Quebec City in 
April for a bilateral visit, at least a one-day event. On 
the Summit of the Americas, Gutierrez and Lauredo said 
.that the U.S. would like the troika to play a more active 
role in the agenda planning process for the Summit. Our 
proposal for a broader Executive Committee to replace the 
troika for future summits was based on our desire to 
contribute to planning efforts for those events and ensure 
continued U.S. participation. Lavertu undertook to 
discuss tHis issue with Canada Summit Sherpa Marc Lortie 
on his returN to Ottawa. Kergin said that Canada was 
"still feeling its way" on timing for future summits, 
i.e., should they be triennial or biennial, etc. Canada 
still had not worked out what planning structure would be 
best -- troika, expanded troika, or some other solution. 
Amb. Lauredo said that the U.S. agreed that the host 
country should remain "first among equals" on the troika, 
but hoped that Canada would consider a process whereby 
future decisions would be made by consensus - with the 
U.S. and Chile as full and equal partners, not as 
"advisors" to the chair.

Bilateral Relations: 
8. 4.ert-Lavertu noted that within the next few months 
there would be new governments with new mandates in both 
Washington and Ottawa, and said that we should be looking 
closely at how to manage the.dual transition and to 
facilitate linkage of the two administrations. Lavertu 
repeated his proposal that our new president plan a 
bilateral visit to Canada well in advance of the Quebec 
Summit of the Americas. Lavertu said that such a visit 
would be a good opportunity to take stock of what is 
happening in the relationship, building on past successes. 
Lavertu said that it would be an opportunity to revisit 
the Canada-U.S. Partnership (CUSP) Forum, which had done 
good work, but on which more remained to be done. He 
noted that it would be a perfect occasion to review 
bilateral and multilateral-issues and Look at NAFTA. The 
Summit of the Americas was clearly an important 
multilateral issue for discussion. In short, Lavertu 
concluded, there was plenty to talk about. 
9. -H91- Lavertu then raised two specific bilateral 
issues. On the softwood lumber issue, he repeated the 
Canadian proposal that both countries appoint a panel cf 
"wise men" to tackle and de-politicize the softwood lumber 
agreement negotiations. On the preclearance issue, he 
urged prudence and restraint in resolving remaining 
differences and predicted that an agreement could be 
reached "within a matter of days." 
10. -4.G4- Deputy Secretary Talbott said that working with 
his Canadian counterparts over the past seven years had 
been a source of great professional and personal 
satisfaction to him. Talbott noted that the integration 
of the Office of Canadian Affairs into the Bureau of 
Western Hemisphere Affairs had worked well for the U.S. 
and that he hoped it would work well for Canada also. 
Talbott observed that U.S.-Canada issues tended to be 
concrete, technical, and closely related to the growing 
economic ties between us. 
11. -ee-)— Talbott said that the U.S. would consider the 
Canadian proposal of an early visit of the new President 
to Canada. He offered to leave the recommendation that 
the new President give prompt and proper consideration to 
the idea. On preclearance, Talbott agreed that the issue 
was still to be resolved and turned to WHA/CAN Director 
Saxton, who briefed on the latest developments in the 
negotiations.

Korea: 
13. 4.€4- Regarding the Korean Peninsula, Lavertu said 
that Ottawa had come to agreement with Pyongyang on

and that the agreement would be announced soon. He noted

Lavertu asked for the U.S. view of the situation as The

especially the possibility of a presidential visit to 
North Korea. 
14. -(-C--)- Talbott said that The Secretary's intense 
exposure to North Korea during her trip increased by 10-
fold her experience on the important issues related to the 
Peninsula. Talbott described North Korea's political
system as a grotesquely hierarchical and totalitarian one. 
Kim Jong IL was able to make important decisions right on 
the spot with The Secretary, even on highly technicaL 
issues. The North Korean leader, moreover, appears to be 
a highly intelligent and functional person, not someone 
disconnected from the real world. Kim Jong IL understands 
that his country is in deep, deep trouble. His opening to 
the U.S. and Canada is aimed at rescuing his country from 
total economic collapse. His strategy appears to be to 
build up his,ballistic missile capability to dangerous 
proportions and then ask what we are willing to trade for 
it. 
15. —eel- Talbott said that no decision has yet been inade 
regarding a possible Presidential visit to North Korea. 
The Secretary may be ready to make a recommendation on 
this by the end of next week. She believes that there is 
"something there to work with." Talbott noted that she 
had debriefed the South Koreans and Japanese, and that our 
challenge is to move ahead patiently without jeopardizing 
the interests of our allies.
