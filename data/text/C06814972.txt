Heading: STATE 72708 IMMEDIATE
SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY'S MEETING WITH UKRAINIAN DEFENSE
MINISTER KUZMUK
CLASSIFIED BY ROSS WILSON, PRINCIPAL DEPUTY, S/NIS.
REASONS: 1.5 (B,D),
1. Q{ SUMMARY. IN A MARCH 31 MEETING WITH THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY, UKRAINIAN DEFENSE MINISTER KUZMUK HIGHIGHTED THE
POSITIVE POLITICAL DEVELOPMENTS THAT ARE NOW ENABLING THE
GOVERNMENT TO MOVE FORWARD ON REFORM. HE EMPHASIZED THE
GOU'S FIRM COMMITMENT TO ADVANCING ITS INTEGRATION INTO
EUROPEAN AND EURO-ATLANTIC STRUCTURES AND ADVANCING
DEMOCRATIC AND MARKET REFORMS. THE GOVERNMENT WAS MAKING
SOME DIFFICULT BUT NECESSARY DECISIONS, SUCH AS THE
PRESIDENT'S DECREE ON CLOSING CHORNOBYL. URKAINE WOULD
NEED ASSISTANCE IN FINDING COMPENSATING SOURCES OF ENERGY
AND ADDRESSING THE SOCIAL PROBLEMS OF PEOPLE LIVING IN HE
AREA. IFI SUPPORT FOR REFORMS WAS IMPORTANT. WITHOUT IT,
RUSSIAN PRESSURE WOULD INCREASE. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY
REAFFIRMED U.S. COMMITMENT TO STRENGTHENING U.S.-UKRAINIAN
RELATIONS, OUR DESIRE TO RESCHEDULE PM YUSHCHENKO'S VISIT,
AND TO FIND WAYS TO MAKE EVEN CLEARER U.S. SUPPORT FOR
UKRAINE AND ITS REFORM AGENDA. KUZMUK WAS ACCOMPANIED BY
RELEASE IN
FULL
DECLASSIFIED
UKRAINIAN AMBASSADOR HRYSHCHENKO. ALSO PARTICIPATING ON
THE U.S. SIDE WERE S/NIS PRINCIPAL DEPUTY ROSS WILSON,
EUR/PRA DIRECTOR DEBRA CAGAN, EUR/WST DIRECTOR MARY
WARLICK, OSD/RUE DIRECTOR ED PUSEY AND KATHLEEN DOHERTY,
SPECIAL ASSISTANT, D. END SUMMARY.
2 ~THE DEPUTY SECRETARY WELCOMED KUZMUKI NOTING HIS
DESIRE TO REAFFIRM USG SUPPORT FOR STRENGTHENING U.S.—
UKRAINIAN RELATIONS, ESPECIALLY AT THIS TIME. WE WERE
AWARE OF RECENT QUESTIONING IN UKRAINE REGARDING THE U.S.
SUPPORT FOR THE UKRAINIAN GOVERNMENT AND ITS REFORM
EFFORTS. IT'S TRUE THERE WERE A NUMBER OF COMPLEX ISSUES
THAT NEEDED TO BE RESOLVED AND SOME UNFORTUNATE PRESS
COVERAGE OF THOSE ISSUES. HOWEVER, THE FUNDAMENTALS OF OrJR
BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP REMAINED VERY STRONG. WE REGRETTED
THE RECENT POSTPONEMENT OF THE PRIME MINISTER'S VISIT TO
WASHINGTON AND HOPED VERY MUCH TO RESCHEDULE,'HIS IN THE
NEAR FUTURE. WITHIN THE USG, THERE HAD BEEN A NUMBER OF
MEETINGS THIS WEEK TO DISCUSS WAYS IN WHICH WE COULD
REINFORCE OUR RELATIONSHIP WITH UKRAINE. IT WAS IMPORTANT
THAT THE REST OF THE WORLD KNOW OF OUR SUPPORT. KUZMUK'S
VISIT TO WASHINGTON WAS VERY TIMELY. IT WAS A REMINDER
THAT OUR BILATERAL MILITARY RELATIONSHIP WAS AN IMPORTANT
PART OF OUR BROADER RELATIONS.
3. ~KUZMUK RESPONDED THAT HIS MEETINGS WITH SECRETARY
COHEN, ON A WIDE RANGE OF TOPICS, HAD BEEN VERY USEFUL. HE
HAD MET WITH PM YUSHCHENKO BEFORE LEAVING KIEV AND HAD
ASKED HIM DIRECTLY WHETHER THERE WAS ANY BASIS TO THE
RESERVES MISMANAGEMENT ALLEGATIONS. THE PM HAD ASSURED HIM
THAT IFI CREDITS RECEIVED BY UKRAINE WERE USED ONLY FOR
THEIR INTENDED PURPOSE, NOT TO FINANCE ANY POLITICAL
CAMPAIGNS. THE NATIONAL BANK HAD OFFERED TO HAVE AN AUDIT
CONDUCTED OF ITS BOOKS AND WAS PREPARED FOR ANY RESTULS.
THIS WAS THE HONEST POSITION OF THE GOVERNMENT. NOW WAS A
CRITICAL TIME IN UKRAINE. KUZMUK NOTED THREE MAJOR
POLTICIAL DEVELOPMENTS: THE ELECTION OF KUCHMA LAST FALL,

WHICH WAS ALSO A WIN FOR DEMOCRACY IN UKRAINE; THE
APPOINTMENT OF A REFORM MINDED PM WITH MODERN VIEWS WHO HAD
FORMED A GOVERNMENT OF LIKE-MINDED PEOPLE; AND, THE
CREATION OF A MAJORITY COALITION IN THE RADA, SUPPORTIVE OF
THE GOVERNMENT. A NUMBER OF IMPORTANT LAWS, BLOCKED FOR
YEARS, WERE NOW BEING APPROVED, INCLUDING THE NATO SOFA
AGREEMENT.
4. ~ THESE THREE DEVELOPMENTS HAD PRODUCED A NUMBER OF
SIGINIFICANT RESULTS, INCLUDING THE ADOPTION OF A BALANCED
BUDGET FOR THE FIRST TIME IN 8 YEARS. THE GOVERNMENT WAS
COMMITED TO LIVING WITHIN ITS MEANS AND ACTING IN AN HONEST
AND RESPONSBILE MANNER TO EARN THE PUBLIC'S CONFIDENCE,
WHILE NOT BEING AFRAID TO MAKE TOUGH DECISIONS. SECRETARY
ALBRIGHT'S LATEST OP-ED PIECE, WHICH HAD JUST APPEARED IN
THE UKRAINIAN PRESS, HAD RIGHTLY NOTED THAT UKRAINE'S
SUCCESS DEPENDED NOT ONLY ON ACTIONS TAKEN BY THE
GOVERNMENT, BUT ON THE TRUST THE UKRAINIAN PEOPLE HAD IN
THE GOVERNMENT. UKRAINE WAS ALSO COMMITED TO ADVANCING ITS
INTEGRATION INTO EUROPEAN AND EURO-ATLANTIC STRUCTURES,
STRENGTHENING ITS RELATIONS WITH NATO AND ADVANCING
DEMOCRATIC AND MARKET ECONOMIC REFORMS. LAND REFORM WAS
ALSO BEING ENACTED; COLLECTIVE FARMS WERE BEING ABOLISHED
AND LAND RETURNED TO THE PEOPLE. FULFILLING THIS BROAD
AGENDA WOULD BE A LONG PROCESS, BUT UKRAINE WAS REALIZING
THE FIRST SIGNS OF STABILIZATION.
5. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY EXPRESSED APPRECIATION FOR
KUZMUK'S STATEMENTS AND NOTED THE USG SENSED AND SAW THESE
FIRST SIGNS OF PROGRESS, TOO. WE WERE CERTAIN THE PRIME
MINISTER UNDERSTOOD THE IMPORTANCE OF PRESERVING THE
INTEGRITY OF THE INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS AND
THE IMPORTANCE OF THE SUPPORT THEY PROVIDE FOR COUNTRIES.
UNDER SECRETARY LARSON WOULD BE IN KIEV NEXT WEEK FOR
MEETINGS. WE LOOKED FORWARD TO A POSITIVE RESULT OF THE
FIRST AUDIT AND GETTING THAT PARTICULAR ASPECT OF OUR
RELATIONSHIP BACK ON TRACK. THE U.S. HAD TAKEN PRESIDENT
KUCHMA'S APPOINTMENT OF YUSHCHENKO AS AN EXTRAODINARLIY
POSITIVE SIGNAL OF KUCHMA'S INTENTION TO INTENSITY
DEMOCTRACTIC AND ECONOMIC REFORMS IN URKAINE. THE U.S.
WOULD LOOKi FOR WAYS TO MAKE IT EVEN CLEARER THAT WE SUPPORT
UKRAINE AND ITS REFORM AGENDA.
6. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY NOTED HIS APPRECIATION FOR
KUZMUK'S COMMITMENT TO PRESERVING SECURITY IN EUROPE AND
STRENGTHENING UKRAINE'S RELATIONSHIP WITH NATO. HE ALSO
EXPRESSED APPRECIATION FOR UKRAINE'S RATIFICATION OF THE
SOFA, WHICH WOULD FACILITATE COOPERATION BETWEEN UKRAINE
AND NATO. IT WAS CLEAR UKRAINE WAS TAKING STEPS TO
CONTRIBUTE TO THE SECURITY IN THE REGION, THROUGH ITS
PARTICIPATION IN KFOR AND ITS POSSIBLE PARTICIPATION IN THE
MULTINATIONAL INTERCEPTION FORCE. UKRAINE WAS
STRATEGICALLY IMPORTANT, AND ITS SUCCESS WOULD AUGUR WELL
FOR THE REGION AS A WHOLE. THE U.S. HAD BOTH POLITICAL AND
SECURITY INTERESTS IN UKRAINE'S SUCCESS.
7.~THE U.S. HAD TWO OVERARCHING CONCERNS, HOWEVER: AN
INTERNAL CONCERN THAT UKRAINE MAKE GENUINE, SUSTAINED
PROGRESS ON ECONOMIC REFORM (WHICH KUCHMA AND YUSHCHENKO
NOW SEEMED COMMITED TO); AND, AN EXTERNAL CONCERN ABOUT THE
COURSE THAT RUSSIAN FOREIGN POLICY, WILL TAKE IN THE REGION.
DURING THE COURSE OF THIS YEAR, THE U.S. ADMINISTRATION
WOULD MAKE USE OF OPPORTUNITIES TO MAKE CLEAR TO THE
RUSSIANS ITS OBLIGATIONS REGARDING RESPECT FOR THE
SOVEREIGNTY, INDEPENDENCE, TERRITORIAL INTEGRITY AND RIGHTS

OF OTHER STATES IN 'HE REGION. THIS OBLIGATION WOULD BE
CRITICAL TO RUSSIA'S OWN STATED DESIRE TO INTEGRATE INTO
THE WEST. AND NO RELATIONSHIP IN THE REGION WAS MORE
IMPORTANT THAN THAT BETWEEN UKRAINE AND RUSSIA.
8. ~KUZMUK SAID HE WAS IMPRESSED WITH THE DEGREE TO
WHICH OUR VIEWS COINCIDED ABOUT UKRAINE. THE GOU WAS IN A
DIFFICULT POSITION TODAY. DECISIONS IT WAS TAKING WERE
UNPOPULAR, BUT NECESSARY. KUCHMA'S DECREE ON CLOSING
CHORNOBYL HAD BEEN A VERY DECISIVE ONE. ALTERNATIVE
SOURCES OF ENERGY WOULD NEED TO BE FOUND, HOWEVER,
INCLUDING FINANCIAL SUPPORT FOR COMPLETING THE R4/K2
REACTORS. IT WAS ALSO IMPORTANT TO CONSIDER THE FATE OF
PEOPLE IN THE CHORNOBYL AREA, INCLDUING 12,000 WORKING AT
THE PLANT, WHO WOULD LOSE THEIR JOBS. UKRAINE COULD NOT DO
ALL OF THIS ON ITS OWN. THE COAL INDUSTRY IN UKRAINE,
WHICH NEEDED TO BE RESTRUCTURED, WAS ANOTHER EXAMPLE OF A
BAD SOVIET LEGACY; FIVE MILLION PEOPLE WORKED IN THIS
SECTOR AND UKRAINE WOULD NEED TO FACE THIS PROBLEM.
UKRAINE WAS COUNTING ON IFI SUPPORT FOR THESE EFFORTS AND
WITHOUT IT PRESSURE FROM RUSSIA WOULD INCREASE. THE CLOSER
UKRAINE MOVED TOWARD THE WEST, THE MORE PRESSURE RUSSIA
APPLIED IN TERMS OF LIMITING GAS AND OIL SUPPLIES AND
CLOSING ITS MARKETS TO UKRAINIAN PRODUCTS. UKRAINE
SUSPECTED THAT THE NEW RUSSIAN. LEADERSHIP WOULD CONTINUE
SUCH PRAGMATIC POLICIEIS. AT THE SAME TIME, UKRAINE HAD
CHOSEN ONE PATH AND IT WOULD NOT CHANGE.
9. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID HE UNDERSTOOD'THE
PRESSURES UKRAINE WAS FACING, AS DID THE PRESIDENT, VICE
PRESIDENT AND SECRETARY, WHO WAS PREPARED TO INVEST
ADDITIONAL ENERGY IN UKRAINE IN THE MONTHS AHEAD. NEWS OF
THE ANNOUNCEMENT ON CHORNOBYL CLOSURE HAD REACHED US IN THE
MIDST OF A HIGH-LEVEL INTERAGENCY MEETING THIS WEEK ON
U.S.-UKRAINE RELATIONS, ATTENDED BY NSC DEPUTY JIM
STEINBERG AND OTHERS. WE UNDERSTOOD THE IMPORTANCE OF IFI
SUPPORT FOR UKRAINE IN PURSUING MACROECONOMIC REFORMS AND
FINANCIAL STABILIZATION. IT WOULD BE ESPECIALLY IMPORTANT
TO ATTRACT THE PRIVATE SECTOR, HOWEVER, AND TAKE STEPS TO
IMPROVE THE FOREIGN INVESTMENT CLIMATE IN UKRAINE, REFORMS
WE UNDERSTOOD KUCHMA AND YUSHCHENKO WERE COMMITED TO. WITH
RESPECT TO THE CHALLENGES OF CHORNOBYLg THE COAL 'INDUS TRY/
AND OTHER PROBLEMS WHICH WERE INHERITANCES OF THE'SOVIET
ERA, IT WAS IMPORTANT FOR UKRAINE TO REMAIN COMMITTED TO
FINDING UKRAINIAN SOLUTIONS. IT WAS ALSO IMPORTANT THAT
UKRAINE HAD MADE AN IRREVOCABLE DECISION REGARDING ITS
RELATIONS WITH THE WEST AND EUROPE. THE U.S. WOULD
RECIPROCATE WITH ITS SUPPORT AND A COMMITMENT TO HELP
UKRAINE SUCCEED. KUZMUK AGREED THAT THE SOVIET PAST SHOULD
NOT BE AN EXCUSE FOR UKRAINE'S PROBLEMS, BUT A LESSON NOT
TO ALLOW THAT EXPERIENCE TO HAPPEN AGAIN.
