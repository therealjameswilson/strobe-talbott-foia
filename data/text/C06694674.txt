2. SUMMARY: MEETING WITH THE DEPUTY SECRETARY OCTOBER 
20, FCO POLITICAL DIRECTOR NEVILLE-JONES VOICED BRITISH 
DESIRE TO WORK CLOSELY WITH US TO BRIDGE THE SUBSTANTIVE 
GAP ON A UNSC RESOLUTION ON THE BOSNIA ARMS EMBARGO AND 
STRESSED THE IMPORTANCE OF INITIATING DISCUSSIONS ON A 
SPECIFIC TEXT AS QUICKLY AS POSSIBLE. SHE UNDERSCORED 
HMG'S WILLINGNESS TO BROADEN THE RESOLUTION. AT THE 
SAME TIME NEVILLE-JONES MADE CLEAR THAT HMG COULD ACCEPT 
NEITHER AUTOMATICITY NOR A STATEMENT THAT THE EMBARGO 
HAD BEEN A MISTAKE, AND SHE REITERATED BRITISH 
INSISTENCE THAT A RESOLUTION PROVIDING FOR LIFT ALSO 
PROVIDE FOR PRIOR WITHDRAWAL OF UNPROFOR. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY AGREED THAT WE NEED ULTIMATELY TO FIND AN 
OUTCOME WE CAN ALL ACCEPT, AND IF POSSIBLE AVOID ANY ROW 
AMONG CONTACT GROUP MEMBERS IN THE PROCESS. HE MADE 
CLEAR WE NEEDED TO GO IN WITH A "HARD" RESOLUTION. 
3. FURTHER ON BOSNIA, NEVILLE-JONES STRESSED THAT IT

CONCERNED THAT WORK PRESS AHEAD ON A PLAN FOR UNPROFOR 
WITHDRAWAL. SHE SUGGESTED THAT A PULL-BACK OF BRITISH 
TROOPS COULD BEGIN IRRESPECTIVE OF THE OUTCOME ON THE 
ARMS EMBARGO, IF THERE WAS A STRONG DOMESTIC REACTION TO 
BOSNIAN SERB "PINPRICK" ATTACKS ON THE TROOPS. 
4. NEVILLE-JONES SAID THAT THE UK WAS AGREEABLE TO AN 
EARLY DISCUSSION ON NATO EXPANSION AS LONG AS THIS 
AVOIDED SETTING CRITERIA FOR ADMISSION, ESTABLISHING 
TIMETABLES, AND IDENTIFYING CANDIDATE MEMBERS. THE 
FOCUS SHOULD BE ON DEFINING THE NATO "ACQUIS," AND ON 
EXPLAINING TO INTERESTED COUNTRIES PRECISELY WHAT 
MEMBERSHIP IN THE ALLIANCE ENTAILED. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY EXPRESSED FULL AGREEMENT WITH THIS APPROACH.

NEVILLE-JONES URGED THAT THE QUAD MECHANISM BE
