SUBJECT: ACTING SECRETARY BRIEFS BALTICS ON ACTION PLAN 
1. (U) CLASSIFIED BY CAROL VAN VOORST, DIRECTOR EUR/NB. 
REASONS: 1.5 (B) AND (D). 
2._ SUMMARY. In a meeting August 29 with representatives 
of the three Baltic embassies, Acting Secretary Talbott 
provided an overview of the interagency-cleared Baltic 
Action Plan outlining a three-track approach to U.S. 
relations with Latvia, Lithuania, and Estonia. Noting 
that the plan would be briefed in more detail by a USG 
team prepared to visit the region in September, the Acting 
Secretary described it as a strategy designed to promote 
the integration of the Baltics into the West by fostering 
the development of institutional links, regional 
cooperation, peaceful relations with neighbors; and a 
deepening bilateral relationship with the U.S. Ambassador 
Ojars Kalnins (Latvia), Charge Lauri Lepik (Estonia), and 
Charge Stasys Sakalauskas (Lithuania) attended for the 
Baltic missions; Ambassador at Large Collins, EUR DAS 
Wayne, D staffer Goldberg, and EUR/NB director van Voorst 
(notetaker) joined the Acting Secretary. End summary. 
3. The Acting Secretary opened by explaining that he 
wanted to provide the Baltic governments with an initial 
sketch of the 'latest U.S. thinking on the Baltic dimension 
of our overall European security policy. For some time 
the USG had been considering how best to promote the 
integration of the Baltics into the western community; we 
were now ready to consult with the Baltics and others on a 
three-track approach to move us towards this objective. 
U.S. ambassadors in the Baltics would follow up the Acting 
Secretary's consultation with briefings in Vilnius, Riga, 
and Tallinn. An interagency team planned to visit the 
region in September for more detailed discussions with the 
Baltic governments. 
4. The Acting Secretary then outlined the highlights of 
action plan's three tracks. The objective of Track 1, 
which covered multilateral and regional activities, was to 
ensure that the Baltics make progress towards and 
ultimately achieve success in their aspirations to join 
Western security and economic institutions. He assured 
the representatives the U.S. would find every occasion to 
reaffirm and make good our position that NATO should be 
open to all interested states in a self-selecting process 
that was inclusive rather than exclusive. Talbott said 
the USG would give no credence to any attempt to rule out 
a country or a group of countries as future members. In

5. Talbott said the U.S. appreciated the Baltic 
aspiration for membership in the EU and would use our good 
relations with key Union members to press vigorously for 
expeditious membership for qualified Baltic applicants. 
The Acting Secretary noted that he had already raised this 
point in his discussions with the leading EU states. 
6. Pointing to the OSCE's role as a forum for regional 
discussion and conflict resolution, Talbott said the U.S. 
would continue to support the resident missions in Riga 
and Tallinn and; in the OSCE itself, would energize 
discussions with regional players -- including the 
Russians -- on practical steps to implement and advance 
OSCE goals in the Baltics. 
7. The Acting Secretary noted that an important subset of 
Track 1 was promotion of more intense regional cooperation 
as a fundamental way to increase security and confidence 
in the Baltic littoral area. The U.S. would therefore 
strongly support regional cooperation initiatives such as 
those under development in the Baltic Sea States Council. 
For our part the U.S. would, despite budgetary 
difficulties, continue our effort to contribute to the 
Baltic Peacekeeping Battalion (BaltBat). We would also 
work with other donors and the Baltic states themselves to 
rationalize military assistance efforts, improve the 
national defense capability of the Baltics, promote NATO 
interoperability of their commands, and increase 
intra-Baltic military cooperation. The U.S. intended to 
push ahead on implementation of the regional airspace 
initiative (RAT). 
8. Under Track 2, Talbott said the U.S. would do 
everything possible to stabilize the region by fostering 
normal relations between Russia and the Baltics. The USG 
would use its good offices to work towards a practical 
resolution to border disputes. The Acting Secretary 
stated that should Russia -- rhetorically or otherwise 
try to bully or intimidate the Baltics, the U.S. would 
continue to speak up and would find an appropriate way to 
discourage Moscow from activities impinging on Baltic 
sovereignty and territorial integrity. 
9. Under Track 3, the USG proposed an intensified program 
of bilateral activities supporting Baltic sovereignty, 
expanding our security relationship, increasing trade and 
investment, and countering crime and corruption. Talbott

10. The U.S. would also stress the intensification of our 
bilateral security links with the Baltics, Talbott said. 
He observed that we were expanding the IMET program for 
the Baltic states and were also developing a modest arms 
relationship to help the Baltic defense forces meet their 
legitimate needs. The U.S. would schedule additional 
joint exercises with Baltic units, and would continue to 
develop mil-to-mil contacts and the relationship between 
the Department of Defense and the Baltic defense 
establishments. 
11. To help the Baltics move ahead with economic reform 
and development, the administration would seek to raise 
the level of U.S. trade and investment in the Baltics by 
mobilizing the resources of agencies such as the Commerce 
Department, OPIC, TDA, and Exim. Talbott said the USG 
would also push forward aggressively to complete the 
required investment and taxation treaties. 
12. Concluding the roundup of bilateral activities under 
Track 3, the Acting Secretary characterized organized 
crime in the Baltics as a scourge and said the U.S. would 
establish an FBI presence in the region to help the Baltic 
governments deal with this growing concern.
