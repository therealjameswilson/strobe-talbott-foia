1. Classified by EUR/SCE Director Swigert. Reason 1.5 (d). 
2. Summary. FYROM ForMin Frckovski presented his views 
on the South Balkans in an 10/1 meeting with Deputy 
Secretary Talbott. Frckovski characterized relations with 
neighbors as generally positive but expressed concern over 
organized crime emanating from Russia through Bulgaria. 
He encouraged the U.S. to keep pressure on the FRY to 
begin talks on Kosovo. He noted the importance of the 
U.S. remaining engaged in Albania to help build democracy 
during a difficult transitional period. End summary. 
3. Also attending the 35 minute meeting for FYROM were 
Ambassador to the U.S. Ljubica Acevka and Foreign Ministry 
Head of Department Petre Mitev. 
Foreign Policy Priority: Relations With Neighbors 
4. Frckovski stated good neighborly relations are his 
main priority. His country occupies an important position 
at the crossroads of a north-south and east-west 
corridor. The latter route has fallen into disuse and it 
is now time to revive it while maintaining the former. 
FYROM has free trade agreements with Slovenia, Bosnia, 
Croatia, and the FRY, and'wants to sign such agreements 
with Bulgaria and Albania. .This would make FYROM unique 
in the region. 
Bulgaria: Good Relations But Concern About Russia 
5. Frckovski said relations with Bulgaria were good but 
expressed concern over Russian influence. Sofia still 
loOks towards Moscow while FYROM has a western 
orientation. Of primary concern is Russia's export of 
organized crime and money laundering through Bulgaria and 
Serbia. This can hamper economic integration,

Rest of Relationship 
6. Frckovski characterized relations with Greece as 
"quite good," and cited the transparent border and growing

7. The name issue, Frckovski continued, is a Greek 
internal problem. He noted that 82 countries have 
recognized his country, two-thirds of which use the,
constitutional name. Even some countries which recbgnize 
the country under the provisional name "The Former 
Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia" (Great Britain, France and 
Germany) do not use it when signing bilateral agreements, 
and instead use "Macedonian Government" and equivalent 
constructs. 
8. Deputy Secretary Talbott stated that Cyrus Vance's 
efforts under U.N. auspices presented the best channel for 
resolving the name issue. He encouraged Frckovski to 
continue support for these talks. Talbott noted that the 
significant progress that Athens and Skopje have made thus 
far coupled with a new Greek Government gives rise to 
optimism that there can be a solution to the name issue. 
Albania: U.S. Presence Important 
9. Frckovski stressed that a strong U.S. presence in 
Albania is critical in keeping it moving towards democracy 
during a difficult transitional period. Political 
relations are generally smooth with the "slight" exception 
of the issue of ethnic Albanians in FYROM. 'He added that 
FYROM is willing to discuss this with Albania. Albania's 
Prime Minister is expected to visit by the end of the year 
and sign several agreements, including one on mutual 
recognition of diplomas. 
FRY: Improving Relations But Still Need For Caution 
10. Relations with the FRY are generally positive, 
according to Frckovski. The FRY's recognition df FYROM's 
continuity might set a precedent for other former Yugoslav 
republics. The FRY ispressing for more integrated 
economic ties but FYROM wants to move more slowly. 
11. Note: In a conversation with EUR/SCE Director 
Swigert before the Talbott meeting, Frckovski was 
optimistic that the FRY-FYROM border commission could 
resolve most outstanding issues within six months. The

Kosovo: Talks Must Begin; Hardliners Must Be Neutralized 
12. Frckovski stressed the importance of getting 
Milosevic and Rugova to start talks. The U.S. needs to 
keep pressure on Milosevic. Hardliners in Kosovo also 
must be marginalized. 
13. Talbott responded that the U.S. has stressed to the 
FRY (most recently to ForMin Milutinovic the day before) 
that several things have to be done for Washington to 
normalize with Belgrade -- normalization within the FRY 
(particularly treatment of ethnic minorities and Kosovo), 
cooperation with the Haugue Tribunal, and normalization 
with neighboring countries (particularly the former 
Yugoslav republics and progress on successor state 
issues). 
Russia: Concern Over Strong Russian Presence 
14. Frckovski stated that FYROM wants to complete the 
transition to a market-based multi-ethnic democracy. 
Russia can be a threat to this goal. Stability needs to 
be consolidated in FYROM before it can be comfortable 
dealing with the Russians. He added that the Russian 
embassy in Skopje is by far the largest, including two 
diplomats who were expelled from the U.S. 
15. The meeting concluded with both sides expressing that 
bilateral relations between Washington and Skopje are 
excellent.
