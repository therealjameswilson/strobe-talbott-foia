I am replying to your July 20 letter to Secretary 
Christopher, concerning Russian non-payment to your client's 
firm of debts incurred by the Soviet Union.-
Payment arrearage problems are a serious issue and one on / 
which we place a high priority in our official contacts with 
the Russians. At the same time, we do not believe it would be 
apppropriate to link it directly to assistance. We believe it 
'is essential to go forward with humanitarian and technical 
assistance programs dedicated to helping the Russian pecple 
undertake the massive task of transforming their country into a 
free and democratic society with a market economy. Ensuring 
that this transformation is a success is vital to.our own 
national interest. 
Payment arrearage problems with the former Soviet Union 
began about four years ago and increased with the deMise of the 
U.S.S.R. As early as 1989, U.S. companies reported delays in 
payments on invoices for deliveries to Soviet organizations on 
open account. Most arrearages resulted from the former Soviet 
Bank for Foreign Economic Relations, Vneshekonombank (VEB), 
failing to authorize-payment from accounts held by Soviet 
foreign trade organizations. The collapse of VEB further 
complicated matters. 
The Russian Government says it lacks- the funds( to settle 
such debts at this time. Russia acknowledges arrearages to 
U.S. companies, asserts that they will be paid, and is 
reviewing possible short-term alternatives, such as the 
issuance of bonds.

Senior U.S. officials of the Commerce and State Departments 
have repeatedly raised payment arrearages to U.S. firms with 
their Russian counterparts. We are pleased to report that some 
progress in.obtaining payment for U.S. companies has been 
made. We recognize, however, that much more needs to be done, 
and will continue to use our official contacts with the Russian 
government to urge that arrears owed to U.S. firms be paid. We 
reiterated our concerns on this score during bilateral 
consultations with the Russians on July 20. The Russian side 
responded that arrears to three U.S. firms would soon be paid 
and that at least partial payment of other arrears would be 
considered. 
I assure you that we will continue to give this issue the 
high priority it warrants on the U.S.-Russian trade agenda 
until all U.S. companies have been paid. If we can be of help 
on other matters, please do not hesitate to contact us again.
