February 9. 1993 
Dear Strobe, 
The world has changed almost beyond recognition from that ex-
citing month we spent together in China, Siberia, and Moscow 
in 1988. Now you are assuming a richly earned and deseerved 
position that I am sure will result in a more constructive 
and effective U.S. policy toward the entire C.I.S. and 
Baltics. Congratulations. 
Although I am now in a very different sector, I believe we 
continue to share a common interest in doing what we can to 
help assure that the states of the former Soviet Union evolve 
toward democracy and capitalism, rather than fall back into 
authoritarianism and statism. I suspect you have heard, but 
if not please forgive my own lack of communication. In Sep-
tember, 1990, after 26 years, I took early retirement from 
Ohio State and from the Kettering Foundation. Since' October, 
1990, I have been Director Eastern Europe for KELLOGG 
Germany. In this poisiten, I have begun distribution of our 
products in Poland, Hungary, and in the Czech and Slovak Re-
publics. My most important work, however, continues to be 
building new KELLOGG production facilities and companies in 
the former Soviet Union. 
As a first step, before the end of this year, we will open a 
plant in Riga, Latvia, to produce KELLOGG'S Corn Flakes and 
Frosted Flakes. It is my intention to market these products 
as broadly as possible in the Baltics, Russia and Ukraine. 
However, my initial focus will be on the Baltics, St. 
Petersburg, and Moscow. In fact, our plan is to begin 
deliveries to these markets from Riga in November of this 
year, and this leads me to the purpose of my letter to you.

The biggest problem I face in developing this market is de-
veloping efficient and reliable distribution, initially in 
the above major markets. While I am investigating a number 
of Russian and Baltic companies, as you know as well as I, 
the required motivation, organizational skills and capital 
are hardly likely to be found today in any wholly-owned 
C.I.S. company. 
Therefore, I am seeking to identify and make contact with as 
many as possible of the major American and European producers 
of high-turnover, consumer products, whether food or 
household goods. My initial objective is simply to exchange 
experience in the development of the C.I.S. markets. 
My hope is to identify one or more companies who have them-
selves begun to develop distribution capabilities for their 
own products and who might be interested in discussing ' 
cooperation in the distribution and logistic areas with KEL-
LOGG. 
Perhaps this is way away from your current responsibilities 
and interests. On the other hand, I know that you, and I, in 
your former work, had many contacts with leading American 
firms, including producers of consumer goods. 
In short, any leads you could provide me in this area would 
be very highly and personally appreciated. 
In any case, I feel more assured about the future of our re-
lations with the Baltics and C.I.S. knowing that you are 
playing such a key role at the political level. Congratula-
tions and let's keep in touch. 
S erely yours,
