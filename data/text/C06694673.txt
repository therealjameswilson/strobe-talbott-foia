SUBJECT: THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S OCTOBER 19 MEETING WITH 
FCO MINISTER OF STATE DAVID DAVIS 
1.  CONrIDENTIAL ENTIRE TEXT. 
2. SUMMARY: THEDEPUTY SECRETARY OUTLINED FOR MINISTER 
OF STATE DAVIS THE ADMINISTRATION'S APPROACH TO NATO 
EXPANSION. HE NOTEDTHAT A NUMBER OF QUESTIONS NEED TO 
BE THOUGHT THROUGH BEFORE WE CAN ADDRESS THE WHO AND 
WHEN OF THE EXPANSION ISSUE. HE URGED THAT NATO 
EXPANSION BE SEEN WITHIN THE CONTEXT OF CREATION OF A 
BROAD EUROPEAN SECURITY ARCHITECTURE THAT WOULD ALSO 
EMBRACE CSCE, AND THAT IN PUBLIC WE AVOID RULING OUT 
RUSSIAN MEMBERSHIP IN THE ALLIANCE. DAVIS INDICATED 
THAT HMG THINKING ON NATO EXPANSION WAS VERY CLOSE TO 
OUR OWN. ON ENLARGEMENT OF THE EU, DAVIS STRESSED THE 
MAGNITUDE OF THE PROBLEMS TO BE OVERCOME AND-SUGGESTED 
THAT ACCESSION OF A FIRST TRANCHE OF CENTRAL EUROPEAN 
STATES MIGHT BE AS MUCH AS TEN YEARS AWAY. END SUMMARY. 
3. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY MET FOR FORTY MINUTES OCTOBER 
19 WITH FCO MINISTER OF STATE DAVID DAVIS, WHOSE BRIEF 
COVERS EUROPEAN ISSUES. DISCUSSION FOCUSED ON THE 
QUESTIONS OF NATO AND EUROPEAN UNION EXPANSION. 
NATO EXPANSION 
RELEASE IN FULL 
DECLASSIFIED 
Declassification Authority: Geoffrey W. Chapman, Senior Reviewer, 
A/GIS/IPS 5/8/2019 
4. IN RESPONSE TO DAVIS' EXPRESSION OF CONCERN THAT THE 
.S. MIGHT BE MOVING FORWARD TOO QUICKLY AND TOO PUBLICLY
ON NATO EXPANSION, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY NOTED THAT THE 
PRESIDENT HAD REACHED A DECISION THAT NATO SHOULD EXPAND 
BUT THAT WE WERE STILL IN THE INITIAL STAGES OF PUTTING 
TOGETHER A CONCEPTUAL APPROACH TO GUIDE THE PROCESS. WE 
NEEDED FIRST TO THINK THE ISSUE THROUGH AND GET INTO 
CLOSE CONSULTATIONS WITHIN NATO. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
POINTED OUT THAT BRINGING NEW MEMBERS INTO THE ALLIANCE . 
WAS NOT THE SIMPLE STEP SOME WOULD IMAGINE. COMPLICATED 
ISSUES SUCH AS NUCLEAR WEAPONS DEPLOYMENT AND TROOP 
STATIONING NEEDED TO BE THOUGHT THROUGH, AND PROSPECTIVE 
NEW MEMBERS NEEDED TO BE ABLE TO CARRY OUT THE 
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES THAT CAME WITH 
MEMBERSHIP. WE ALSO HAD TO HAVE IN MIND A CLEAR CONCEPT 
OF THE PURPOSE AND DIRECTION OF THE ALLIANCE IN THE 
POST-COLD WAR ERA. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY MADE CLEAR THAT 
WE WERE NOT YET PREPARED TO TALK ABOUT THE POSSIBLE-
TIMING OF EXPANSION, GIVEN THE HOST OF OTHER QUESTIONS 
THAT HAD TO BE ADDRESSED FIRST. DAVIS AGREED THAT IT 
WAS PREMATURE TO TALK ABOUT TIMETABLES.

INELIGIBLE GIVEN LIKELY RUSSIAN REACTIONS. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY STRESSED THE IMPORTANCE OF NOT PUBLICLY RULING 
OUT ANY PFP COUNTRIES, INCLUDING POSSIBLE RUSSIAN 
MEMBERSHIP, EVEN THOUGH WE COULD AGREE PRIVATELY THAT 
THIS WAS A VERY LONG WAY OFF. YELTSIN HAD TOLD US THAT 
RUSSIA COULD LIVE MUCH MORE EASILY WITH NATO EXPANSION 
IF IT WERE NOT EXPLICITLY EXCLUDED. MOREOVER, A POLICY 
OF NEVER SAYING NEVER WOULD MAKE IT EASIER FOR US TO 
GAIN RUSSIAN COOPERATION IN AREAS WHERE WE WISHED TO 
WORK TOGETHER. DAVIS SAID THAT HMG FULLY CONCURRED WITH 
THIS APPROACH, AND WAS INDEED SENSITIVE TO RUSSIAN 
REACTIONS TO POSSIBLE NATO MEMBERSHIP FOR FORMER WARSAW 
PACT COUNTRIES. 
6. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY URGED THAT WE LOOK AT NATO 
EXPANSION IN THE BROADER CONTEXT OF A NEW OVERALL 
SECURITY ARCHITECTURE FOR EUROPE. THE CSCE SUMMIT IN 
BUDAPEST, HE NOTED, WOULD BE A GOOD OPPORTUNITY TO 
ESTABLISH THIS BROADER CONTEXT. IN ADDITION TO 
EXPANSION OF THE ALLIANCE, WE NEEDED TO FOLD IN THE 
CONOMIC INTEGRATION OF THE CONTINENT AS WELL AS A MORE 
COMPREHENSIVE POLITICAL-SECURITY TRACK BUILT AROUND 
CSCE. THIS "SECOND TRACK" WOULD FOCUS ON INTEGRATING 
THE COUNTRIES THAT WOULD PROBABLY NOT BE EARLY 
CANDIDATES FOR NATO MEMBERSHIP. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
TOOK NOTE OF DISCUSSION OVER THE POSSIBILITY OF AN . 
ALLIANCE WITH A SMALL "A" BETWEEN NATO AND RUSSIA TO 
SUPPLEMENT THE "SECOND TRACK;" BUT THIS RAISED THE 
QUESTION OF SIMILAR ALLIANCES WITH OTHER FSU COUNTRIES. 
7. IN RESPONSE TO DAVIS' QUESTION, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
SAID THAT WE BELIEVE THERE SHOULD BE NO DIRECT LINKAGE 
BETWEEN NATO AND EU EXPANSION. THERE SHOULD OF-COURSE_-_:----

8. DAVIS STRESSED THAT EXPANSION OF THE EU WOULD.BE 
FRAUGHT WITH PROBLEMS AND WOULD, IN THE BRITISH VIEW, 
TAKE LONGER THAN GENERALLY ANTICIPATED. HE NOTED THAT 
THE EXISTING EU MEMBERS WOULD FIRST NEED TO_RESOLVE A____ 
NUMBER OF DIFFICULT ISSUES AMONG THEMSELVES,: NOT THE- -- 
LEAST BEING REVISION OF THE COMMON AGRICULTURAL POLICY._ 
WHILE SOME CENTRAL EUROPEAN STATES HELD OUT THE HOPE OF 
ACCESSION BY THE YEAR 2000, HE THOUGHT IT WOULD TAKE A - 
GOOD FIVE YEARS LONGER BEFORE THE FIRST TRANCHE WAS 
ADMITTED. HE TOOK NOTE OF STEPS UNDERWAY TO DRAW THE 
CENTRAL EUROPEAN STATES CLOSER TO THE EU PENDING FORMAL 
MEMBERSHIP, INCLUDING A BRITISH-ITALIAN INITIATIVE FOR 
COOPERATION ON FOREIGN POLICY.
