2.—(err) BEGIN SUMMARY. IN A PRIVATE MEETING WITH RUSSIAN DEPUTY PRIME MINISTER VALENTINA MATVIENKO ON THE MARGINS OF THE CONFERENCE ON WOMEN AND DEMOCRACY IN REYKJAVIK, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT RAISED THE CURRENT SITUATION IN CHECHNYA, ASKED FOR MATVIENKO'S PERSPECTIVE, AND REITERATED U.S. CONCERNS ABOUT: 
-THE NEED TO RELY ON POLITICAL DIALOGUE IN RESOLVING THE CONFLICT; -THE TOLL IT IS TAKING BOTH WITHIN RUSSIA AND ON ITS RELATIONS WITH NEIGHBORING COUNTRIES SUCH AS GEORGIA AND AZERBAIJAN; AND -RISING CONCERNS WITHIN THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY ABOUT RUSSIA POLICY IN CHECHYNA AND ITS IMPACT ON RUSSIAN INTERNAL DEVELOPMENTS AND EXTERNAL RELATIONS. 
MATVIENKO DESCRIBED HER VISIT TO REFUGEE CAMPS IN INGUSHETIA TWO DAYS BEFORE COMING TO THE CONFERENCE, OUTLINED GOR'S PRIORITIES IN THE REGION, EXPRESSED FRUSTRATION OVER THE LACK OF EFFECTIVE INTERLOCUTORS ON THE CHECHEN SIDE, AND DEMURRED ON THE PROSPECTS FOR INTERNATIONAL INVOLVEMENT AT THE PRESENT TIME, EXCEPT POSSIBLY FOR REFUGEE ASSISTANCE. MATVIENKO ALSO THANKED THE US FOR ITS CONTRIBUTION TO THE CONFERENCE, WHICH WERE ESSENTIAL TO ITS SUCCESS. END SUMMARY. 
3./wOLLOWING THEIR PRESENTATIONS TO THE OPENING SESSION OF THE CONFERENCE ON WOMEN AND DEMOCRACY IN CONJUNCTION WITH THE VITAL VOICES DEMOCRACY INITIATIVE AT THE CITY THEATER IN REYKJAVIK, ICELAND, OCTOBER 8, DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT MET PRIVATELY WITH RUSSIAN DEPUTY PRIME MINISTER VALENTINA IVANOVA MATVIENKO. ALSO PRESENT WERE RUSSIAN AMBASSADOR TO ICELAND ANATOLIY ZAITSEV, PERSONAL ASSISTANT TO DPM NELLI NAIGOVZINA, RUSSIAN MFA REPRESENTATIVE MARINA KOROUNOVA, D EXECUTIVE ASSISTANT PHILIP GOLDBERG, EUR SENIOR COORDINATOR FOR THE WOMEN'S CONFERENCE JUDYT MANDEL, AND LYCIA SIBILLA, AMERICAN EMBASSY MOSCOW. TALBOTT ASKED THE DEPUTY PM FOR HER PERSPECTIVE ON THE CONFLICT IN CHECHNYA, AND WHERE THE RUSSIAN GOVERNMENT WAS GOING. 
4.-(e1AFTER REPEATING WELL-KNOWN RUSSIAN VIEWS ON THE GOR'S OBLIGATION TO RESPOND TO THE RECENT TERRORISTS ACTS, MATVIENKO CONCENTRATED ON THE IMPACT OF THE CHECHEN INSURGENTS' GRIP ON THE REGION. SHE RECOUNTED HER VISITS TO REFUGEE CAMPS IN INGUSHETIA JUST TWO DAYS EARLIER, AND DESCRIBED THE WHOLESALE DESTRUCTION OF ECONOMIC LIFE, CLOSED SCHOOLS, AND LACK OF HOSPITALS AND FOOD SHE HAD SEEN. SHE ATTRIBUTED THE POVERTY AND DEVASTATION SHE HAD SEEN AS MUCH TO THE INSTABILITY CAUSED BY THE EXTREMISTS AS TO THE RENEWED RUSSIAN MILITARY ACTIONS IN THE REGION. SHE NOTED THAT MANY OF THE REFUGEES WERE PRIVATELY WILLING TO ADMIT THEY HAD BEEN "ORDERED" TO LEAVE CHECHNYA BY THE INSURGENTS IN ORDER TO CREATE THE APPEARANCE OF A HUMANITARIAN DISASTER.

RELOCATE TO OTHER REPUBLICS TO DO SO, BUT ITS PRIORITIES 
WERE TO ATTEND TO THE REFUGEES' BASIC NEEDS, SUCH AS FOOD ,
AND SHELTER, AND THEN TO CREATE CONDITIONS THAT WOULD 
ALLOW THEM TO RETURN TO THEIR HOMES. THE LATTER TASK 
REQUIRED RE-ESTABLISHING RESPONSIBLE GOVERNMENT. THE 
PSYCHOLOGY OF FEAR AMONG THE REFUGEES MADE IT DIFFICULT 
TO HELP THEM SOMETIMES: THEY WERE AFRAID TO REMAIN IN 
THEIR HOMES BUT MANY ALSO REFUSED TO ACCEPT SHELTER IN 
RAILWAY CARS THAT HAD BEEN SET UP TO HOUSE THEM, FEARING 
THEY MIGHT BE DEPORTED TO KOLYMA IN THE FAR EAST 
(COMMENT: THERE ARE HISTORICAL GROUNDS FOR THAT FEAR ,N 
SINCE THAT WAS INDEED THE FATE THAT BEFELL THEIR 
ANCESTORS UNDER STALIN. END COMMENT) 
6.-(CMATVIENKO WAS PESSIMISTIC ABOUT PROSPECTS FOR 
REACHING A POLITICAL SETTLEMENT WITH THE CHECHENS SOON, 
ALTHOUGH SHE ACKNOWLEDGED THAT THERE WAS NO ALTERNATIVE 
TO THIS. THE PROBLEM, SHE SAID, WAS THE LACK OF 
EFFECTIVE INTERLOCUTORS. THE SEPARATISTS WERE NOT 
INTERESTED IN A POLITICAL SETTLEMENT, SHE STATED, AND 
MASKHADOV COULD NOT DELIVER ONE. WHEN PRESSED ON WHETHER 
MASKHADOV "COULD NOT' OR "WOULD NOT," SHE SAID HE SIMPLY 
WAS NOT IN CONTROL AND "COULD NOT." MOREOVER, HE HAD 
FAILED TO CONDEMN TERRORISM AND TO PUBLICLY DISTANCE 
HIMSELF FROM THE ACTS THEY COMMITTED. SOME MEMBERS OF 
THE FORMER DEMOCRATICALLY ELECTED PARLIAMENT WERE MEETING 
IN.MOSCOW AS A GROUP OF "WISEMEN" TO PROVIDE IDEAS ON A 
SETTLEMENT, BUT THERE WERE NOT ANY CHECHEN NATIONAL 
FIGURES WHO COULD LEAD SUCH AN EFFORT. ON THE LOCAL 
LEVEL, HOWEVER, MATVIENKO SAID THERE WERE LEADERS 
EMERGING WHO WERE FED UP WITH THE FIGHTING AND COULD PLAY 
A ROLE IN ESTABLISHING ORDER. 
7r-(6-)SUMMING UP GOR PRIORITIES, MATVIENKO SAID THAT THE 
ARMED BANDS MUST BE DESTROYED OR DISARMED, THEN THERE 
COULD BE DISCUSSION OF A POLITICAL SETTLEMENT. FINALLY, 
THE GOR WAS LOOKING FOR "SENSIBLE" PEOPLE AT THE LOCAL 
LEVEL WHO COULD GOVERN AND REESTABLISH ORDER. MATVIENKO 
DID NOT SEE A ROLE FOR THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY AT 
THIS POINT, BUT DID NOT RULE OUT THE POSSIBILITY AT A 
LATER STAGE. THE GOR WOULD NOT REFUSE INTERNATIONAL 
ASSISTANCE FOR REFUGEES, BUT DID NOT SEETHE NEED FOR IT 
AT THIS POINT. 
8.-fG-)-TALBOTT ASKED MATVIENKO TO CONVEY USG CONCERNS 
ABOUT THE SITUATION IN CHECHNYA TO PRIME MINISTER PUTIN, 
WHOM HE HAD FIRST MET WHEN THE LATTER WAS CHIEF OF THE - 
RUSSIAN SECURITY COUNCIL. WHILE THE USG APPRECIATES THE 
COMPLEXITY OF THE PROBLEM IN CHECHNYA, RESPECTS THE FACT 
THAT CHECHNYA IS ON RUSSIAN TERRITORY, AND IS SENSITIVE 
TO RUSSIA'S LOSS OF CIVILIAN LIVES DUE TO THE TERRORISTS, 
NEVERTHLESS, THE USG HAD THREE AREAS OF CONCERN ABOUT THE 
SITUATION IN CHECHNYA: 
--THE CONSEQUENCES OF THE GROWING IMBALANCE BETWEEN 
POLITICAL METHODS AND FORCE. THE PENDULUM HAD SWUNG IN 
FAVOR OF THE LATTER AND THE US HOPED IT COULD MOVE THE 
OVERWHELMINGLY OTHER WAY. THE CONTINUATION OF THE 
CONFLICT REPRESENTED NOT ONLY A TRAGEDY FOR CHECHNYA BUT 
A DANGER TO THE VERY HEART OF DEVELOPING RUSSIAN 
DEMOCRACY. WHEN THOUSANDS OF PERSONS OF SOUTHERN 
NATIONALITY ARE DEPORTED FROM MOSCOW, THIS SENDS A VERY 
POOR MESSAGE TO THE REST OF THE WORLD; 
-- THE IMPACT OF THE FIGHTING IN CHECHNYA ON THE SOUTHERN 
CAUCASUS. IT WAS IMPORTANT FOR RUSSIA TO DEAL WITH THE 
SITUATION IN A WAY THAT MADE FRIENDS AND NOT ENEMIES OUT 
OF THOSE NEIGHBORING STATES, ESPECIALLY GEORGIA AND 
AZERBAIJAN. MATVIENKO REPLIED THAT THIS WAS A TWO WAY

9.4e1MATVIENKO SAID SHE WAS GRATEFUL FOR THE US VIEWS 
AND WOULD CONVEY THEM TO HER GOVERNMENT. HOWEVER, SHE 
DID NOT SEE PROSPECTS FOR A POLITICAL SETTLEMENT AT THE 
PRESENT MOMENT: TERRORIST WERE IN CONTROL OF THE CHECHEN 
POLITICAL SCENE. MASKHADOV WAS NOT A TERRORIST, SHE 
ACKNOWLEDGED, BUT HE COULD NOT CONTROL THE MILITANT 
FACTIONS. SHE SAID SHE APPRECIATED THE POINT ABOUT THE 
NEED TO UNITE FORCES IN COMBATTING TERRORISM, BUT RUSSIA 
DID NOT SEE A ROLE FOR THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY VIS A

10,-(e-)MATVIENKO THANKED TALBOTT FOR HIS COMENTS, AND 
AVOWED THAT PRIME MINISTER PUTIN HAD ALSO EXPRESSED 
CONCERNS OVER THE BALANCE BETWEEN POLITICAL METHODS AND I 
FORCE. THE CONFLICT IN CHECHNYA WAS AN UGLY PARTISAN 
WAR, SHE SAID, WHICH THE RUSSIANS DID NOT WANT BUT WERE 
COMPELLED TO WAGE NOW SO AS NOT TO GIVE THE TERRORISTS 
ANY BREATHING SPACE. 
11. (U) IN CONCLUDING, MATVIENKO SAID SHE ALSO WANTED TO 
THANK THE US FOR ITS ROLE IN THE CONFERENCE AND FOR THE 
MANY CONCRETE INITIATIVES THE US HAD BROUGHT TO THE 
TABLE. THESE WOULD BENEFIT RUSSIAN WOMEN SUBSTANTIALLY. 
SHE ADDED THAT SHE HAD NOT THANKED THE US PUBLICLY DURING 
HER REMARKS AT THE OPENING PLENARY BECAUSE SHE WANTED TO 
AVOID WOUNDING ICELANDIC SENSITIVITIES. SHE UNDERSTOOD, 
HOWEVER, HOW IMPORTANT USG SUPPORT AND PARTICIPATION WAS 
AND EXPRESSED THE HOPE THAT THE COOPERATION FOSTERED BY 
THE CONFERENCE WOULD CONTINUE.
