2. DURING DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S SEPTEMBER 30 MEET/NG 
WITH MOLDOVAN PRESIDENT SNEGUR, THE PRESIDENT REAFFIRMED 
MOLDOVA'S COMMITMENT TO ECONOMIC REFORM AND A PEACEFUL 
RESOLUTION TO THE TRANSDNIESTER DISPUTE, -AND THANKED THE 
UNITED STATES FOR ITS SUPPORT. HE NOTED THE FACT THAT THE 
TROOP WITHDRAWAL AGREEMENT INITIALLED BETWEEN MOLDOVA AND 
RUSS/A ON AUGUST 10 REMAINS UNSIGNED, BUT THAT MOSCOW HAD 
TOLD CHISINAU IT PLANS TO SIGN IT AT THE OCTOBER 21 CIS 
SUMMIT. SNEGUR EXPRESSED MOLDOVA'S INTENT TO SUBMIT SOON 
ITS PRESENTATION DOCUMENT FOR THE PARTNERSHIP FOR PEACE, 
AND THAT HE WOULD LIKE TO DEPOSIT MOLDOVA'S INSTRUMENT OF 
ACCESSION TO THE NUCLEAR NONPROLIFERATION TREATY AT SOME 
FUTURE MEETING WITH PRESIDENT CLINTON. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY PRAISED MOLDOVA FOR ITS STALWART REFORM PROGRAM 
AND THE INITIALLING OF A TROOP WITHDRAWAL AGREEMENT, AND 
BRIEFLY REVIEWED RELEVANT ASPECTS OF THE CLINTON-YELTSIN 
SUMMIT. END SUMMARY. 
3. DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT MET WITH MOLDOVAN 
PRESIDENT MIRCEA SNEGUR AND FOREIGN MINISTER MIHAI POPOV 
SEPTEMBER 30 IN NEW YORK CITY ON THE MARGINS OF THE U.N. 
GENERAL ASSEMBLY. SNEGUR AND POPOV WERE JOINED BY 
MOLDOVA'S AMBASSADOR TO THE U.S. NICOLAE TAU. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY WAS JOINED BY U.N. AMBASSADOR MADELEINE ALBRIGHT, 
SINIS SENIOR COORDINATOR JAMES COLLINS, D EXECUTIVE 
ASSISTANT VICTORIA NULAND, AND EUR/ISCA MICHAEL SNOWDEN 
(NOTAKER).

RUSSIA TO GIVE ITS NEIGHBORS FREEDOM TO DEVELOP
