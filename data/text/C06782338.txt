DEPUTY SECRETARY 
CLASSIFIED BY DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT; 
REASONS: 1.5 (B) AND (D). 
1. SUMMARY: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT MET WITH CANADIAN 
DEPUTY FOREIGN MINISTER CAMPBELL ON NOVEMBER 30. THEY 
REVIEWED RECENT DEVELOPMENTS WITH INDIA AND PAKISTAN, IRAQ, 
KOSOVO, AND RUSSIA, WITH A COMMONALITY OF VIEWS. TALBOTT 
AND CAMPBELL AGREED TO COORDINATE IN APPROACHES: (A) TO 
KEEP PRESSURE ON INDIA AND PAKISTAN; AND (B) TO ASSIST 
RUSSIA, INCLUDING WITH FOOD ASSISTANCE TO THE RUSSIAN NORTH 
POSSIBLY IN PART INVOLVING THE ARCTIC COUNCIL. END SUMMARY. 
2. -44- CANADIAN DEPUTY FOREIGN MINISTER DONALD CAMPBELL MET 
WITH DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT ON NOVEMBER 30, TO REVIEW 
GLOBAL HOT SPOTS IN THE LEAD UP TO CANADA'S ASSUMING A 
ROTATING UN SECURITY COUNCIL SEAT ON JANUARY 1. CAMPBELL 
WAS ACCOMPANIED BY AMBASSADOR CHRETIEN, ASSISTANT DEPUTY 
MINISTER FOR GLOBAL AND SECURITY AFFAIRS PAUL HEINBECKER, 
DIRECTOR GENERAL.FOR THE U.S. MICHAEL LEIR, EMBASSY MINISTER 
JOHN HIGGENBOTHAM, AND EMBASSY COUNSELOR EVELYN PUXLEY. ARA 
ACTING DAS SUSAN WOOD, EUR/CAN DIRECTOR VICTOR COMRAS, D 
SPECIAL ASSISTANT KATHLEEN DOHERTY AND EUR/CAN STEVEN CANDY 
(NOTE-TAKER) ALSO ATTENDED. 
INDIA AND PAKISTAN 
3. TALBOTT EXPRESSED CONCERN REGARDING SLIPPAGE IN THE 
POLITICAL WILL OF THE G-7 ON INDIA AND PAKISTAN. OF 
PARTICULAR CONCERN WAS THE POSITION OF SOME FINANCE 
MINISTERS, WHO WERE SUGGESTING BROADENING DEFINITIONS FOR 
IFI ASSISTANCE BEYOND BASIC HUMAN NEEDS LENDING. FOR 
EXAMPLE, THERE WERE PROPOSALS FOR WORLD BANK FINANCING OF 
POWER PLANTS. HEINBECKER ADDED THAT RAILROADS.WERE ANOTHER 
EXAMPLE. TALBOTT CAUTIONED THAT THIS TACK WAS DANGEROUS IF 
WE WERE TO GET EVEN OUR DE MINIMUS BENCHMARKS. 
4. {69- CAMPBELL AGREED, NOTING THAT THE GOC HAD ACCEPTED 
EVEN THE IMF PACKAGE FOR PAKISTAN ONLY WITH SOME 
HESITATION. CAMPBELL ASKED WHAT COULD BE DONE? TALBOTT 
SUGGESTED THAT THE USG WOULD APPROACH FOREIGN MINISTRIES TO 
ENSURE COORDINATION WITH FINANCE MINISTRIES, INCLUDING A 
REMINDER OF WHAT THE P-S AND G-8 HAD AGREED TO IN'THEIR 
DECISIONS.

6: -43- TALBOTT REPORTED ON HIS MEETINGS IN ROME WITH THE 
INDIANS. INDIA HAD DUG IN AND WAS NOT PREPARED TO MOVE AT 
ALL ON TWO OF THE FOUR BENCHMARKS:• STRATEGIC RESTRAINT AND 
A FISSILE MATERIAL MORATORIUM. THERE WAS PARALYSIS IN THE 
GOVERNMENT, PARTICULARLY NOW THAT ELECTIONS HAD GONE BADLY 
FOR THE GOVERNMENT PARTY. THE DOMESTIC POLITICAL SCENE IN 
INDIA ALWAYS MADE THINGS DIFFICULT. THE CONGRESS PARTY 
WOULD CRITICIZE THE GOVERNMENT NO MATTER WHAT WAY IT WENT. 
NONETHELESS, TALBOTT ADVISED THAT IN HIS PRIVATE MEETING 
WITH JASWANT SINGH, THE INDIANS EXPRESSED MORE WILLINGNESS 
TO LOOK FOR A SOLUTION. TALBOTT SAID WE WOULD SEE IF WE 
COULD COME UP WITH SOME SORT OF ROADMAP. 
7. ON THE CTBT, WE HAD WELCOMED THE INDIAN STATEMENT 
OF ITS INTENT TO JOIN, BUT THERE HAD BEEN BACKSLIDING SINCE 
THEN, AND WE NEEDED MOVEMENT. THERE WAS ALSO SOME POSITIVE 
WORK BY INDIA ON EXPORT CONTROLS. TALBOTT CONCLUDED THAT 
THERE WERE ŠOME IN,THE INDIAN GOVERNMENT WHO REALIZED THAT 
THEY NEEDED TO MOVE IN THE NEXT YEAR. TALBOTT WOULD HAVE 
HIS NEXT ROUND OF MEETINGS IN LATE JANUARY. 
8. 44)--HEINBECKER ASKED IF POSITIVE MOVEMENT WOULD MEAN 
THE USG WOULD RELAX MORE SANCTIONS. TALBOTT RESPONDED 
AFFIRMATIVELY, BUT NOT YET AND WITH NO FRONT-LOADING. 
FIRST THERE WOULD HAVE TO BE SOME TRUE BREAKTHROUGHS, E.G., 
INDIA'S DEMONSTRATING TRUE STRATEGIC RESTRAINT. ABSENT A 
BREAKTHROUGH, THE U.S. INTENT WAS TO HOLD THE LINE ON 
SANCTIONS. 
9. HEINBECKER ASKED ABOUT THE FRENCH AND RUSSIANS. 
TALBOTT REPLIED THAT THE BIGGER CONCERN WAS THE FRENCH, 
SINCE THEY HAD MORE TO OFFER. OF IMMEDIATE CONCERN WAS 
CHIRAC'S MENTIONING POSSIBLE COOPERATION WITH INDIA IN THE 
ENERGY SECTOR, TO INCLUDE PEACEFUL USE OF NUCLEAR ENERGY. 
TALBOTT CONCLUDED THAT IN OUR CONSULTATIONS WITH THE FRENCH 
SO FAR, THE FRENCH HAD NOT ALLAYED OUR CONCERNS (OR 
SUSPICIONS). HEINBECKER AND CAMPBELL SHARED THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY'S CONCERNS. ALL AGREED TO WORK HARD TOGETHER ON 
INDIA, IN PART TO PREVENT ANY. EROSION IN THE NPT STRUCTURE. 
10. TURNING TO PAKISTAN, TALBOTT SAID WE WOULD HAVE A 
BETTER FEEL AFTER PM SHARIF'S VISIT TO WASHINGTON DECEMBER 
2. FOR NOW, WE EXPECTED PAKISTAN LARGELY TO FOLLOW INDIA'S 
LEAD. NONETHELESS, WE HAD MORE LEVERAGE WITH PAKISTAN. 
IRAQ 
11. TALBOTT CAUTIONED THAT WE WOULD LIKELY FA6E ANOTHER, 
CRUNCH WITH IRAQ OVER THE NEXT SEVERAL WEEKS. IRAQ WAS 
STILL TRYING TO GET ITS COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW UP FRONT, 
WHICH WE STRONGLY OPPOSED. ALL AGREED THAT, WHILE THERE 
WAS A DOWNSIDE TO ANY MILITARY ACTION, THERE WAS ALSO A 
POTENTIAL DOWNSIDE TO NO ACTION. 
12. CAMPBELL NOTED THAT WHILE THERE WERE RUMORS SUGGESTING 
INSTABILITY IN SADDAM'S REGIME, THE GOC SAW NO EVIDENCE OF 
SUCH. TALBOTT CONFIRMED THAT USG1S CALCULATIONS DID NOT 
FACTOR IN THE POSSIBILITY THAT A HARD MILITARY HIT MIGHT 
TOPPLE SADDAM FOR INTERNAL REASONS.

KOSOVO 
14. -(4.-4-)- CAMPBELL RAISED KOSOVO, NOTING THE GOC WAS GEARING 
UP FOR THE KOSOVO VERIFICATION MISSION (KVM), WITH AN 
EXPECTED PERSONNEL CONTRIBUTION OF 50-100. THE GOC WAS 
CONCERNED, HOWEVER, ABOUT THE INCREASE IN UCK ACTIVITIES. 
TALBOTT NOTED THAT, UNLIKE WITH MILOSEVIC AND THE SERBS, WE 
HAD FEWER THREATS WE COULD USE AGAINST THE UCK. WE WERE 
TRYING TO DEVELOP NEW ELEMENTS OF INFLUENCE IN OUR CONTACTS 
WITH THE KOSOVARS. HOWEVER, THE BEST STEP WOULD BE TO GET 
THE NEGOTIATING PROCESS STARTED. TALBOTT NOTED THAT AMB. 
CHRIS HILL HAD REPORTED THAT THERE WAS SOME COHESION 
DEVELOPING AMONG THE ALBANIANS. BUT THERE NEEDED TO BE A 
CRITICAL MASS BEFORE IT WOULD BE POSSIBLE TO MOVE TO TALKS. 
RUSSIA 
15. -E-EY- ON RUSSIA, TALBOTT OPINED THAT THINGS WOULD LIKELY 
GET WORSE BEFORE THEY GOT BETTER, BUT WE HADN'T YET PASSED 
THE POINT OF NO RETURN. TALBOTT REPORTED THAT VICE 
PRESIDENT GORE HAD A USEFUL CONVERSATION WITH PRIMAKOV IN 
KUALA LUMPUR. THE USG WAS NOW TRYING TO PRIORITIZE THE 
ECONOMIC STEPS THAT RUSSIA COULD TAKE. WE WERE ALSO 
LOOKING HARD AT WHAT COULD BE DONE THROUGH THE IFIS, AS 
JUST ONE PART OF A SYSTEMATIC REVIEW OF THE MECHANISMS OUT 
THERE TO ASSIST RUSSIA. ANOTHER POSSIBILITY WAS 
COOPERATION IN THREAT REDUCTION, E.G., MOBILIZING 
COLLECTIVE G-7 ASSISTANCE TO HELP DEFRAY RUSSIAN COSTS OF 
ARMS CONTROL IMPLEMENTATION. THERE WERE ALSO POSSIBILITIES 
FOR THE PRIVATE SECTOR IN THE ENERGY SECTOR. WE WERE 
LOOKING AT HUMANITARIAN ASSISTANCE AS WELL. 
16. -44)- CAMPBELL NOTED THE USG PLANS FOR A FOOD AID 
PACKAGE TO RUSSIA. THE GOC'S ANALYSIS WAS THAT THE REAL 
PROBLEM MIGHT BE WITH DISTRIBUTION NOT THE ABSENCE OF FOOD. 
TALBOTT REPLIED THAT THERE STILL SEEMED TO BE REAL NEED IN 
MURMANSK AND KALININGRAD, AS WELL AS THE FAR EAST. IN ANY 
CASE, USG FOOD ASSISTANCE WOULD BE CONDITIONED ON ADEQUATE 
DISTRIBUTION METHODS AND ACCOUNTABILITY. 
17. 4.g). CAMPBELL REPORTED THAT THE GOC WAS DOING SOME 
INTERESTING WORK THROUGH THE CANADIAN INUIT COMMUNITIES, 
WHICH HAD LONG TIES AND GOOD CONTACTS WITH THE RUSSIAN 
NORTH. THE GOC WAS CHANNELING SOME 10 MILLION DOLLARS 
THROUGH THIS AVENUE. TALBOTT REPLIED THAT THIS WAS 
SOMETHING THE ARCTIC COUNCIL MIGHT GET INVOLVED IN. 
HEINBECKER CAUTIONED THAT THE PROBLEM TO DATE WAS THAT THE 
RUSSIANS HAD NOT ACCEPTED USING THE ARCTIC COUNCIL FOR THIS 
PURPOSE. _HOWEVER, THAT COULD BE CHANGING, AND WITH THE 
U.S. ASSUMING THE PRESIDENCY OF THE ARCTIC COUNCIL THERE 
MIGHT BE A NEW OPPORTUNITY. TALBOTT CONFIRMED THAT THE USG 
WOULD FOLLOW THROUGH ON THIS IDEA. HEINBECKER ADDED THAT 
THE NORWEGIANS SEEMED TO HAVE MONEY TO SPEND.
