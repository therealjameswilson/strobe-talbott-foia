Thanks for your very kind note. Your visit reminded me 
how much I miss some of my old colleagues from Time -- not 
only you but old friends like John, who are in the same building 
but whom I don't see nearly as often as I used to. 
The evening went by all too quickly, but considering the 
pressures you were under, Maggie and I appreciated that you 
took the time you did. 
I did have one item of business to raise, which I just plain 
forgot amid all the conviviality. Despite my promising lunch 
with George Stephanopoulos last summer, I've made no headway 
in the effort to get the President to have dinner with the Time 
Inc. managing editors. Goodgame calls George S. periodically, 
but it's clear the idea has fallen behind the back burner. 
I wouldn't persist if I didn't think such a meeting had merit 
for the President as well as for us. The more unhappy he is with 
the Washington press, it would seem, the more useful it would be 
for him to know some of the people who run a group of 
magazines that reaches 75 million Americans. I know how the 
Clintons feel about Time, but I think they've had positive 
experiences with People, Sports Illustrated, Life and, most 
recently, In Style, which was given privileged access to the 
Mandela dinner. 
And, heck, if the President has a serious grievance against 
Time, what better way to address it than to meet Gaines and the 
editor-in-chief under the umbrella of a Time Inc.-wide gathering? 
I'm taking the liberty of pestering you about this because I 
continue to believe the President would like to do it, but that the 
proposal is lost somewhere among all the requests he gets. If

To remind you, my notion (which George S. seemed to 
endorse) is that dinner is better than lunch, off the record is 
better than on the record, Washington is better than New York 
and a restaurant is better than the White House. The guest list 
would include the managing editors of Time, Life, Fortune, Sports 
Illustrated, People, Money, Entertainment Weekly, SI for Kids, 
Time International, Health and In Style, plus the editor-in-chief 
and myself. Obviously, we're amenable to any variations the 
White House might propose (short of excluding Time).
