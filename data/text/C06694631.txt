2. SUMMARY: IN A JAN. 25 MEETING WITH DEPUTY 
SECRETARY TALBOTT, FM KONO EXPRESSED BROAD 
SATISFACTION WITH THE STATE OF BILATERAL RELATIONS 
AFTER PM MURAYAMA'S SUCCESSFUL VISIT TO WASHINGTON. 
TALBOTT AGREED BUT EMPHASIZED THE UNFINISHED TRADE 
BUSINESS AND URGED THE GOVERNMENT OF JAPAN (GOJ) TO 
WORK WITH US TO MAKE PROGRESS IN THE AUTO SECTOR. HE 
ADVISED THAT AMBASSADOR MONDALE WOULD BE PERSONALLY 
ENGAGED ON OKINAWA BASE ISSUES. KONO SAID PM MURAYAMA 
LIKEWISE SOUGHT TO FOLLOW UP QUICKLY ON THIS PROBLEM. 
3. •ON APEC, KONO SUGGESTED THAT THE PACE OF TRADE 
LIBERALIZATION GENERALLY PREFERRED BY ASIAk COUNTRIES 
MUST BE HARMONIZED WITH THAT PREFERRED BY THE U.S. AND 
AUSTRALIA, BUT ASSURED TALBOTT THAT JAPAN HOPES TO 
MAKE PROGRESS IN BOTH DEVELOPMENT AND TRADE 
LIBERALIZATION WHILE IT IS IN THE CHAIR. TALBOTT 
SUGGESTED WE WORK TOGETHER TO FORMULATE AN APEC 
"BLUEPRINT," NOTING SANDY KRISTOFF WILL HAVE SOME 
CONCRETE IDEAS WHEN SHE VISITS TOKYO NEXT MONTH. ON 
EAEC, KONO SAID HE AND THE FOREIGN MINISTRY OPPOSE THE 
CONCEPT, BUT THE MINISTRY IS UNDER INCREASING PRESSURE 
FROM BUSINESS AND POLITICAL CIRCLES TO CONSIDER IT. 
TALBOTT EMPHASIZED THAT EAEC MUST NOT DETRACT FROM 
APEC OR DIVIDE JAPAN AND THE US. BOTH SIDES AGREED 
THAT THE ASEAN ARF HAS BEEN SOMEWHAT ECLIPSED IN THE 
MEDIA BY'APEC AND DESERVES MORE ATTENTION THAN IT HAS 
RECEIVED. 
4. KONO EXPRESSED CONCERN ABOUT CHECHNYA AND AGREED 
WITH DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S PROPOSITION THAT 
YELTSIN DESERVES SUPPORT ONI,Y AS LONG AS HE REMAINS ON 
THE PATH OF REFORM. BOTH SIDES AGREED THAT, CHECHNYA 
ASIDE, IT IS MUCH TOO EARLY TO TURN THE G-7 PLUS ONE 
INTO A FULL G-8. KONO FAVORED A G-7 FOR ECONOMIC 
TALKS AND G-7 PLUS ONE FOR POLITICAL ISSUES. KONO 
FURTHER SUGGESTED THAT, SHOULD THE CHECHNYA SITUATION 
CONTINUE TO FESTER, JAPAN WOULD QUESTION WHETHER THE 
G-7 SHOULD WELCOME YELTSIN AT HALIFAX AT ALL. END 
SUMMARY.

THAT, OF THE OVER 50 COUNTRIES WHICH OFFERED

BILATERAL ISSUES: OKINAWA BASES, TRADE RELATIONS 
6. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT APPLAUDED THE 
CLINTON-MURAYAMA SUMMIT FOR ITS SUCCESS IN 
UNDERSCORING THE HEALTH OF THE U.S.-JAPAN 
RELATIONSHIP. KONO OBSERVED THAT THE NAPLES SUMMIT 
HAD PROVIDED AV OPPORTUNITY FOR THE TWO LEADERS TO 
DEVELOP A PERSONAL RELATIONSHIP; THIS TIME, HE SAID, 
THEY WERE ABLE TO BUILD ON THAT RELATIONSHIP AND 
ENSURE A SUCCESSFUL SUMMIT IN WASHINGTON. 
7. AS FOLLOW-UP TO THE SUMMIT, DEPUTY SECRETARY 
TALBOTT INFORMED KONO THAT AMBASSADOR MONDALE-AT-THE 
PRESIDENT'S INSTRUCTION WILL WORK ON OKINAWA BASE 
ISSUES. KONO REPLIED THAT BASE ISSUES GENERATE MUCH 
CONCERN IN JAPAN; WHEN PM MURAYAMA RETURNED-FROM HIS - 
U.S. VISIT, HE PROMPTLY INSTRUCTED KONO AND DEFENSE- - 
MINISTER TAMAZAWA TO MAKE PROGRESS. PM MURAYAMA ALSO 
ASKED HIS FELLOW SOCIALIST, OKINAWA GOVERNOR OTA, FOR 
SUPPORT ON THIS ISSUE. 
8. TURNING TO TRADE, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT NOTED 
THAT OFFICIALS_ON BOTH SIDES WOULD BE FOLLOWING-UP 
THE PROGRESS ALREADY MADE UNDER THE FRAMEWORK 
AGREEMENT, AND EXPRESSED THE HOPE THAT THEY-WOULD 
SUCCEED IN RESOLVING THE AUTO SECTOR PROBLEM: HE 
REMINDED KONO THAT PRESIDENT CLINTON HAD STRESSED-W - . 
PRIME MINISTER MURAYAMA HIS INTENSE PERSONAL CONCERN- " 
ON THIS QUESTION. RESOLUTION OF TRADE ISSUES WOULD-'-" 
PUT OUR ECONOMIC RELATIONS ON AS SOUND A FOOTING1AS 
THE OVERALL RELATIONSHIP. 
9. KONO REPLIED THAT HE FULLY REALIZES THE'IMPORTANCE-= ' 
OF THE ECONOMIC RELATIONSHIP, PARTICULARLY-THE ISSUES - 
INVOLVED IN THE AUTO SECTOR. MITI MINISTER-RASHIMOTO 
-- "A TOUGH GUY" -- IS IN CHARGE OF THIS ISSUE FOR THE--
GOJ AND WILL BE WORKING HARD TO RESOLVE THE-AUTO 
PROBLEM. 
10. TALBOTT NOTED OUR BROAD COOPERATION ACROSS A WIDE 
SPECTRUM OF ISSUES, BILATERALLY, REGIONALLY AND - 
GLOBALLY. ON THE BILATERAL FRONT HE NOTED THE TWO 
SIDES PLAN TO HOLD INTENSIVE DISCUSSIONS THIS YEAR TO 
REAFFIRM THE U.S.-JAPAN SECURITY RELATIONSHIP. KONO 
RESPONDED THAT JAPAN'S APPROACH IS BASED ON THE 
FUNDAMENTAL RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE U.S. AND JAPAN 
AND, MORE SPECIFICALLY, ON THE U.S.-JAPAN SECURITY 
TREATY, WHICH KONO DESCRIBED AS THE "STARTING POINT."

11. ASKED BY TALBOTT ABOUT JAPAN'S PLANS AS APEC 
CHAIR, KONO SAID THE "TEMPO" OF THE ASIAN COUNTRIES 
AND THAT PREFERRED BY THE U.S. AND AUSTRALIA SHOULD BE 
HARMONIZED. HE ALSO EMPHASIZED THAT, TOGETHER WITH 
PROGRESS ON TRADE LIBERALIZATION, JAPAN WOULD LIKE TO 
PURSUE A "PARTNERSHIP FOR PROGRESS" ON DEVELOPMENT. 
12. TALBOTT AGREED WITH THE CONCEPT OF PURSUING HOTH 
TRADE LIBERALIZATION AND DEVELOPMENT IN TANDEM, BUT 
STRESSED THE NEED TO FLESH OUT THE BOGOR DECLARATION 
VIS-A-VIS LIBERALIZATION. THE U.S. WANTS TO WORK WITH 
JAPAN TO DEVELOP A COMPREHENSIVE APEC BLUEPRINT; SANDY 
KRISTOFF WILL HAVE SOME CONCRETE IDEAS WHEN SHE VISITS 
JAPAN NEXT MONTH.

"UNBELIEVABLE AMOUNT" OF INTEREST IN EAEC WITHIN -.._ _

BUT SAID THE PRESSURE HE IS UNDER ON IT REFLECTS 
INCREASED JAPANESE INTEREST IN BEING MORE-ATTUNED TO- -
ASIA. TALBOTT EXPRESSED CONCERN THAT ANY MOVE TOWARD-
EAEC SHOULD NOT BECOME A DIVERSION FROM APEC;--AND---- - 
EMPHASIZED THAT NOTHING, EAEC INCLUDED, SHOULD BE - 
ALLOWED TO DIVIDE THE U.S. FROM JAPAN, UNDERMINE THE 
TREND OF DISMANTLING EXISTING TRADE BARRIERS, OR 
CREATE A NEW DIVIDE IN THE PACIFIC. KONO REPLIED THAT 
THERE IS "ABSOLUTELY NO DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE VIEWS 
OF OUR TWO GOVERNMENTS ON APEC, ARF, AND EAEC."_ KONO_ 
ADDED THAT, IRRESPECTIVE OF THE CLEAR IMPORTANCE OF
