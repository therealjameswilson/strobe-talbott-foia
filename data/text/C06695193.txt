Acting Secretary Talbott called Costa Rican President Figueres to ask-__for- - - 
assistance in providing safehaven for Cuban migrants intercepted in zthe   _ _ 
waters off Florida. President Figueres said that Costa Rica would like to help 
and that he would "see how far we can go." 
The Acting Secretary began by stressing the seriousness with which the USG 
views the problem of uncontrolled Cuban immigration. He noted that Chargé 
Becelia had approached the GOCR on the safehaven question and added that 
he wanted to weigh-in personally. President Clinton and Secretary 
Christopher attach great importance to finding safehavens for the Cubans, he 
said. Figueres replied that he and other GOCR officials had discussed the 
issue following Chargé Becelia's meeting with Costa Rican FM Naranjo. He 
then asked the Acting Secretary for his assessment of the situation. 
The Acting Secretary said there were two unpredictable factors: the weather 
in the Florida Straits and Castro. He said the number of would-be migrants 
was horrendous, but that severe weather had led to a drop in the number of 
interceptions today. The Acting Secretary then briefed Figueres on Secretary 
of Defense Perry's recent trip to Guantanamo. Most Cubans held at 
Guantanamo made the decision to flee before the recent change in U.S. 
immigration policy, he said. The USG hopes that once the seriousness of the 
change in policy sinks in, the number of Cubans attempting to reach the 
United States will fall. Acting Secretary Talbott said that in order to 
"demagnetize" the Florida Coast, the U.S. needed a system of safehavens in 
the region.
