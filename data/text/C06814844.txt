SUMMARY 
1. -1Ei- During their May lOth meeting in Maputo, 
Deputy Secretary Talbott congratulated President 
Chissano on his re-election and for Mozambique's 
handling of the recent flood disaster, and underscored 
the continuing commitment of the United States to 
assist in the country's reconstruction. Chissano 
commented that Mozambique had learned many lessons 
from the experience, and that he was pleased that 
disaster management was a part of the U.S.-SADC 
Forum's programming. With respect to Mozambique's 
political atmospherics, Chissano said that opposition 
party, RENAMO, had been overconfident of victory in 
the December 1999,general elections, and was now 
seeking both a role in the political process and 
economic sustenance. He expressed concerns that 
RENAMO would become a "conductor of civil 
disobedience," and claimed that party president 
Dhlakama was "obsessed" with becoming head of state. 
2. —MI- Regarding Zimbabwe, Chissano referred to a 
parallel situation in Mozambique, where the government 
saw a need to provide economic opportunities for 
veterans. The international community could best 
assist by helping President Mugabe find a way out 
without pressing him into overstaying his term. 
Again, the answer Chissano prescribed was one of 
negotiation; elections should take place in Zimbabwe 
by Auaust. Throughout the hour-long discussion, 
Chissano presented himself as a pragmatic statesman 
who favored negotiation over precipitous action. End 
summary.

ROME SUPPORT SHOWS "WORLD KNOWS WHAT IS IMPORTANT" 
10. -tel- CHISSANO OPENLY COMPLAINED ABOUT A LETTER 
RENAMO HAD GIVEN TO THE DONOR COMMUNITY IN ADVANCE OF 
THE RECONSTRUCTION CONFERENCE HELD IN ROME ON MAY 3-4, 
STATING THAT ASKING THAT DONORS NOT GIVE AID TO 
MOZAMBIQUE FOR ITS RECOVERY EFFORTS WITHOUT VARIOUS

THEY ARE." MOZAMBIQUE HAD LOST MANY HOSPITALS, ROADS, 
AND COMMUNICATIONS FACILITIES DURING THE FLOODS, HE 
SAID, AND TO TELL OTHERS NOT TO HELP WAS UNBELIEVABLE. 
"TIME MAY BE A GOOD DOCTOR," HE ADDED, BY LETTING 
TENSIONS "COOL DOWN." FRELIMO CONTINUES TO SEND 
"FEELERS" TO THE GRASSROOTS OF RENAMO SUPPORTERS, IN 
ORDER TO GAUGE'THEIR MOOD. FROM THIS INPUT, FRELIMO 
BELIEVED THAT RENAMO WOULD SOON "START TALKING SENSE," 
PAVING THE WAY FOR OPEN DIALOGUE. MOREOVER, THE 
SUCCESS OF THE ROME RECONSTRUCTION CONFERENCE HAD 
DEMONSTRATED THAT THE "WORLD KNOWS WHAT IS IMPORTANT."

"SIGN OF TOLERANCE."

ZIMBABWE: LAND DECISION IS MUGABE'S TO MAKE 
13. --(1”.. Deputy Secretary Talbott then asked Chissano 
for his opinion on the Zimbabwe land reform issue. 
Chissano responded that the unjust distribution of 
land in Zimbabwe had now reached its twentieth 
anniversary without resolution. The underlying issue, 
he said, is that of providing economic opportunities 
for veterans. In Mozambique, he related, the 
government had taken care to provide services to 
veterans, especially after noting that they had not 
achieved progress in Mozambique's growing market 
economy. The government of Zimbabwe wants to provide 
communal land for veterans, who regard themselves as 
having been repeatedly cheated after the 199B land 
reform conference, when the UK had made a direct 
commitment to assist in the land reform process. 
Chissano said that he did not know how the land 
occupations had started, but commented that the use of 
force in dealing with them was "out." -Parties to the 
conflict must negotiate, although the final decision 
on how to resolve the matter was President Mugabe's to 
make. 
14. M.134._ Chissano also said that the Zimbabwean 
government was identifying farms where there was 
excess land. Citing the late Tiny Roland's estate as 
an example, he openly questioned how anyone could put 
600,000 hectares to good use. Land would be obtained 
for redistribution from surplus tracts, non-utilized 
areas, and farms engaged in raising non-essential 
crops, particularly in regions adjoining communal 
land. From this, he stated, 80 percent would be made 
available for the poor, and 20 percent for veterans. 
This mechanism would forge a link to Mugabe's 
political aims. 
MUGABE: SEEKS ACHIEVEMENT, BUT WON'T BE KICKED OUT 
15. --t64). Land use is "an issue for any party that 
wins elections," observed Chissano, and in Zimbabwe 
one must remember that the veterans fought for land. 
"We may be pushing him to want to stay in power," he 
added, as Mugabe realizes that the "time for him to go 
out of office is near." Mugabe may seek resolution of 
the land issue as an achievement before leaving 
office, although certainly he "won't be kicked out by 
the issue either." My advice, Chissano asserted, is 
to try to "give a bit more confidence to Mugabe," 
emphasize the positive side of the issue, and "help 
him find a way out." To do so requires understanding 
the re.al problem: land, rather than political 
motivations. Despite calls for implementing rule of 
law, as in recent articles in "The Economist" and the 
"Financial Times," Chissano noted, "he makes his own 
judgment." While he is trying to "appease veterans," 
he "wants the UK to show support to solve the 
problem."

ELECTIONS BY AUGUST; THINGS QUIETER THAN PRESS SHOWS 
17. —(47÷ Deputy Secretary Talbott asked Chissano'to 
relate his insights on the upcoming'Zimbabwean 
elections. Chissano revealed that he had spoken with 
Mugabe regarding elections, and had learned that 
people were "preparing for elections," which would 
likely be held by August. Mugabe had wished, he 
added, to hold elections in May, but a delay had 
occurred with 'respect to the demarcation of electoral 
districts. 
18. Finally, Chissano asserted that "things were 
quieter than the press shows" in Zimbabwe. He related 
the situation to that of Mozambique's recent floods, 
where the same footage was shown on three consecutive 
days. The media was now giving the false impression 
that Zimbabwe was experiencing continuous violence, 
when this was not the case.
