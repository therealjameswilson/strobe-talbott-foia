CLASSIFIED BY EAP/ANP DEPUTY DIRECTOR STEPHEN J. BLAKE,

l. ~ SUMMARY: IN A TOUR D'HORIZON MEETING MARCH 1 WITH
NEW ZEALAND AMBASSADOR BOLGER, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT
SAID THE ADMINISTRATION HAS STEPPED UP ITS EFFORTS ON
PERMANENT NORMAL TRADING RELATIONS FOR CHINA DESPITE THE
DIFFICULT PROBLEMS CAUSED BY BEIJING'S WHITE PAPER ON
TAIWAN. TALBOTT SUMMARIZED U.S. VIEWS ON THE EUROPEAN
SECURITY AND DEFENSE INITIATIVE (ESDI). ON RUSSIA,
TALBOTT SAID ACTING PRESIDENT PUTIN WAS A "WORK IN
PROGRESS" THAT THE U.S. HOPED TO INFLUENCE. CHECHNYA
REMAINED A VERY SERIOUS CONCERN, HOWEVER, AMBASSADOR
BOLGER BRIEFED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ON THE F-16 SALE,
SUGGESTING THAT. THE NEW NEW ZEALAND GOVERNMENT'PROBABLY
WOULD CANCEL THE DEAL. TALBOTT SAID HE HOPED THE DEAL
WOULD GO THROUGH. HOWEVER, BOTH EXPRESSED THE HOPE THAT A
NEGATIVE DECISION WOULD NOT RETURN U.S.-NEW ZEALAND
RELATIONS TO THE LOW POINT OF THE LATE 1980'S. END
SUMMARY.

2. ~ DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT OPENED THE MARCH 1
MEETING, SCHEDULED AT BOLGER'S REQUEST, BY EXPRESSING
PLEASURE THAT FORMER NEW ZEALAND FOREIGN MINISTER DON
MCKINNON WAS NOW HEAD OF THE COMMONWEALTH. AS WE SAW WITH
THE PAKISTAN COUP, THE COMMONWEALTH CAN MAKE A DIFFERENCE.
MCKINNON IS ONE OF THE BEST AND IT WAS GOOD TO HAVE HIM IN
THAT POSITION, TALBOTT SAID. AMBASSADOR BOLGER AGREED,
SAYING THAT MCKINNON HAD DONE A FINE JOB AS FOREIGN
MINISTER WHEN BOLGER WAS PRIME MINISTER. BOLGER NOTED
THAT THE COMMONWEALTH HAD ALSO PLAYED A USEFUL ROLE ON
NIGERIA.
3. ~ BOLGER ASKED TALBOTT FOR HIS VIEWS ON CHINA
FOLLOWING HIS RECENT VISIT. TALBOTT REPLIED THAT
BEIJING'S RECENTLY ISSUED WHITE PAPER UNQUESTIONABLY HAD
INCREASED THE CHANCES THAT CONGRESS WOULD PASS THE TAIWAN
SECURITY ENHANCEMENT ACT BY A VETO-PROOF MARGIN AND
REDUCED THE CHANCES THAT CONGRESS WOULD APPROVE PERMANENT
NORMAL TRADING RELATIONS. TALBOTT SAID HE WAS GIVEN NO
WARNING DURING HIS VISIT TO BEIJING. THE PRC MAY HAVE HAD
ITS OWN DOMESTIC REASONS FOR ISSUING THE PAPER WHEN IT
DID, BUT IT WAS POSSIBLE THAT BEIJING DID NOT REALIZE THE
FOREIGN POLICY IMPLICATIONS OF ITS ACTION. THE QUESTION
NOW WAS WHETHER THE RELEASE HAS CAUSED IRREPARABLE DAMAGE.
4. ~ BOLGER SAID THE TIMING OF THE WHITE PAPER WAS
DISASTROUS. HE NOTED, HOWEVER, THAT SOME EXPERTS WERE
LESS PESSIMISTIC ABOUT THE WHITE PAPER, SAYING THE
DOCUMENT INCLUDED SOME GOOD POINTS. TALBOTT AGREED THE
DOCUMEN'AD SOME GOOD POINTS, BUT EMPHASIZED THAT THE
NEWS HERE WAS THE WHITE PAPER'S THREAT TO USE FORCE
AGAINST TAIWAN IF NEGOTIATIONS FAILED. TALBOTT ADDED
THAT, IPRESPECTIVE, THE ADMINISTRATION HAS NOT GIVEN UP ON
ITS LEGISLATIVE PRIORITIES. INDEED, WE HAVE STEPPED UP
OUR EFFORTS.

6. ~( BOLGER ASKED FOR THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S VIEWS ON
THE EUROPEAN SECURITY AND DEFENSE INITIATIVE (ESDI).
TALBOTT REPLIED THAT THE U.S. WANTS ESDI TO SUCCEED, BUT
WITHIN NATO, NOT OUTSIDE NATO. DEFENSE ASSETS ARE
LIMITED. WE DID NOT WANT TO HAVE A COMPETITION BETWEEN
NATO AND THE EU. THE PROBLEM IS THAT EUROPE NEEDS TO
MATCH ITS BUDGET WITH ITS ASPIRATIONS, I.E.PUT ITS MONEY
WHERE ITS MOUTH IS. THE EU ALSO NEEDS TO FIGURE OUT A WAY
TO AVOID EXACERBATING TENSIONS BETWEEN THE EU AND NON-EU
NATO ALLIES, SUCH AS POLAND, NORWAY, HUNGARY, TURKEY AND
THE CZECH REPUBLIC. EUROPE TELLS US IT WILL GET IT RIGHT.
WE HOPE SO, BUT WONDER IF THAT WILL BE THE CASE. FINLAND
DID A FINE JOB ON THIS ISSUE AS EU PRESIDENT LAST YEAR,
WE ARE NOT SURE HOW MUCH PROGRESS CAN BE MADE THIS YEAR
WITH PORTUGAL AND THEN FRANCE IN THE CHAIR.
7. ~ BOLGER ASKED TALBOTT FOR HIS VIEWS ON RUSSIA'S
ACTINC PRESIDENT PUTIN. TALBOTT REPLIED THAT PUTIN IS A
"WORK IN PROGRESS," SOMEONE WHO KNOWS THAT RUSSIA CANNOT
MAKE IT ON ITS OWN. WE BELIEVE WE CAN HAVE SOME
INFLUENCE. WE KNOW RUSSIA HAS ITS OWN AMBITIONS, BUT IT
MUST ACT IN A MANNER ACCEPTABLE TO THE INTERNATIONAL
COMMUNITY. THIS IS CLEARLY NOT THE CASE WITH CHECHNYA,
THE MOST SERIOUS CRISIS IN THE PAST SEVEN YEARS. WE WILL
CONTINUE TO TRY TO IDENTIFY POINTS OF LEVERAGE AND WAYS TO
COORDINATE WITH OTHER COUNTRIES ON THIS SERIOUS PROBLEM.
SECRETARY ALBRIGHT PLANS TO RAISE CHECHNYA WITH FOREIGN
MINISTER IVANOV WHEN SHE SEES HIM IN EUROPE.
8. ((( BOLGER SAID HE WANTED TO BRING THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY UP TO DATE ON NEW 'ZEALAND'S ACQUISITION OF THE
28 U.S. F-16S. BOLGER NOTED THAT PRIME MINISTER CLARK HAD
EXPRESSED SKEPTICISM OF THE DEAL DURING HER CAMPAIGN LAST
YEAR. SINCE TAKING OFFICE, SHE HAS ASKED FORMER MP
QUIGLEY TO REVIEW THE DEAL. A TREASURY TEAM ALSO HAD COME
TO WASHINGTON. BOLGER PRAISED 'THE U.S. AIR FORCE FOR ITS
COOPERATION IN PROVIDING "THE NUMBERS" TO THE TEAM.
QUIGLEY'S REPORT WAS DUE MARCH 6. BOLGER NOTED THERE HAD
BEEN MUCH BACK-AND-FORTH ON THE ISSUE IN NEW ZEALAND, AND
THE PM HERSELF HAD NOTED THAT OPTIONS INCLUDED ACQUIRING
ALL, SOME OR NONE OF THE PLANES. STILL, BOLGER THOUGHT
CLARK WOULD DECIDE TO CANCEL THE DEAL. SHOULD THAT
HAPPEN, BOLGER NOTED THAT THE PRIME MINISTER, FOREIGN
MINISTER AND DEFENSE MINISTERS ALL HAVE SAID NEW ZEALAND
WOULD NOT REDUCE ITS DEFENSE SPENDING. THE MONEY WOULD GO
INSTEAD TO OTHER PRIORITIES, SUCH AS EAST TIMOR.
