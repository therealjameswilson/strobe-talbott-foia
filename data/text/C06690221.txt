MADRID 
January 3, 1994 
THE AMEASSADOR 
Mr. Strobe Talbott 
Deputy Secretary of State-designate 
Department of State 
Washington, D.C. 20520 
Dear Strobe: 
Warmest congratulations on your appointment as 
Deputy Secretary of State! It is good news indeed 
for the State Department and for all those who want 
to see imaginative U.S. leadership in what you have 
called "the great adventure" of building a post-
Cold War security order. 
You will perhaps recall that I was so bold as 
to forecast this happening in your office on 
December 6 when I was.back with Prime Minister 
Gonzalez. It seemed only sensible to put you to 
work "at the highest levels," and I'm gratified 
that the President and the Secretary of State reached 
the same conclusion. 
I hope you will allow me to write you from time 
to time with some ideas based on how things look 
from our vantage point in Europe. I am troubled by 
the growing gap between the requirements of a post-
Cold War foreign policy and the financial resources 
we have available to support it. I'm also concerned 
that the spirit of "practical multilateralism" that 
animated our Aspen Institute discussions is in danger 
of being lost in the wake of our disappointments in 
Somalia and Bosnia. 
I recently proposed to some of my fellow 
Ambassadors in Europe that we get together every six 
months or so to look at our common concerns in trans-
Atlantic relations with a view to sharing our,
conclusions with the State Department. I received a
