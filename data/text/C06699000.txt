ATTACHED FOR YOUR CLEARANCE IS OUR REPORT OF THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY'S DECEMBER 4-5 VISIT TO OSLO. 
SUMMARY 
2. (40 DURING SEPARATE DECEMBER 5 MEETINGS WITH FOREIGN 
MINISTER GODAL AND PRIME MINISTER JAGLAND, DEPUTY 
SECRETARY TALBOTT PRAISED NORWEGIAREFFORTS IN THE 
NORDIC-BALTIC REGION; SUGGESTED THAT RUSSIAN OFFICIALS 
WERE SHOWING RECENT FLEXIBILITY IN THEIR RELATIONS WITH 
THE UNITED STATES; URGED OSLO TO COUNTER RUSSIAN 
MISREPRESENTATIONS OF THE "NORWEGIAN MODEL"; AND 
EXPRESSED INTEREST IN REPRESENTING THE UNITED STATES AT 
THE 1997 BARENTS MINISTERIAL. HIS NORWEGIAN 
INTERLOCUTORS, SEEKING EXPANDED U.S. INVOLVEMENT IN 
NORTHWEST RUSSIA, REACTED ENTHUSIASTICALLY TO THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY'S INTEREST IN THE BARENTS. THEY ALSO 
DESCRIBED A "WAIT-AND-SEE" APPROACH TOWARDS RUSSIA, 
WHILE EMPHASIZING THEIR OWN EFFORTS IN THE BALTIC 
STATES. NORWAY WOULD LIKE TO SEE A LIAISON RELATIONSHIP 
BETWEEN SACLANT AND THE NORTHERN FLEET, AN IDEA TO WHICH 
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY RESPONDED ENTHUSIASTICALLY. END 
SUMMARY. 
FOREIGN MINISTER GODAL: RUSSIA 
3. (0 GODAL BEGAN HIS MEETING BY STRESSING CONTINUITY 
IN THE TRANSITION BETWEEN THE BRUNDTLAND AND JAGLAND 
GOVERNMENTS. EXPRESSING INTEREST IN THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY'S VIEW OF INTERNAL RUSSIAN MATTERS, GODAL 
DESCRIBED THE NORWEGIANS' "WAIT AND SEE" APPROACH. 
YELTSIN'S RAPID RECOVERY FROM HEART WAS A GOOD SIGN, BUT 
MAJOR DECISIONS WERE LIKELY TO AWAIT HIS FULL RESUMPTION 
OF DUTIES. THE PRINCIPAL ISSUE WAS HOW MUCH PROGRESS 
NATO AND RUSSIA COULD MAKE IN NEGOTIATING THEIR CHARTER. 
GODAL NOTED THAT HE-HAD A'GOOD RELATIONSHIP WITH 
PRIMAKOV.

4. 1p4. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY PRAISED NORWAY'S ROLE IN THE 
NORDIC/BALTIC REGION. NORWAY, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY

WASHINGTON FOR ITS "ABSOLUTELY INSTRUMENTAL" ROLE IN THE 
BARENTS COUNCIL, THE ATLANTIC ALLIANCE, AND IN RELATIONS 
AMONG THE NORDIC AND BALTIC COUNTRIES AND BETWEEN NATO 
MEMBERS AND NON-MEMBERS. NORWAY'S NORDIC-BALTIC-US 
SUMMIT PROPOSAL WAS CONSISTENT WITH OUR MOST IMPORTANT 
GOAL -- ENSURING THAT NATO ENLARGEMENT SERVED EUROPEAN 
INTEGRATION, RATHER THAN DIVIDING EUROPE. 
5. *at- TURNING TO RUSSIA, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID THAT 
U.S. OFFICIALS COULD "FEEL THAT YELTSIN IS COMING BACK." 
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY FOUND PRIMAKOV A FAR DIFFERENT MAN 
IN THEIR DECEMBER 4 MEETING THAN HE HAD BEEN IN HIS 
ENCOUNTER WITH SECRETARY CHRISTOPHER ON THE MARGINS OF 
THE UNGA. NOW PRIMAKOV SEEMED MORE INTERESTED IN 
ADDRESSING THE RESULTS OF ENLARGEMENT THAN IN FIGHTING 
THE ALLIED DECISION. MOREOVER, RUSSIAN PRIME MINISTER 
CHERNOMYRDAN HAD TACKLED DIFFICULT FOREIGN POLICY AND 
SECURITY ISSUES WHEN MEETING WITH THE VICE PRESIDENT IN 
LISBON, SUGGESTING THAT CHERNOMYRDAN WOULD BE AN 
ADDITIONAL AND POTENTIALLY USEFUL CHANNEL FOR ADDRESSING 
THESE MATTERS. 
6. 9(, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY OUTLINED A CROWDED CALENDAR

REACTIVATE DISCUSSIONS OVER THE NATO-RUSSIAN CHARTER, 
BUT WOULD BE CAREFUL LEST THE TIMING OF THE DOCUMENT 
SUGGEST THAT THAT THEY WERE ENDORSING NATO ENLARGEMENT. 
GODAL NOTED THAT THE RUSSIANS WERE SEEKING TO PLAY ON 
PERCEIVED SPLITS AMONG THE ALLIES, AND HAD SOUGHT TO 
INFLUENCE NORWEGIAN POLICY IN THIS MANNER. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY NOTED THAT RUSSIANS HAD MISREPRESENTED THE 
"NORWEGIAN MODEL," AND URGED NORWAY TO COUNTER WITH 
DISCUSSIONS OF THE "NORWEGIAN REALITY." THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY SUGGESTED THAT THE RUSSIANS APPEARED TO HAVE 
CROSSED A THRESHOLD, AND WERE NOW RECONCILED TO 
ENLARGEMENT. HE BELIEVED THAT RUSSIAN MILITARY.
OFFICIALS -- CONSUMED WITH THEIR OWN PROBLEMS -- WERE 
NOT AS CONCERNED joirITH ENLARGEMENT AS THEIR POLITICAL 
COUNTERPARTS.
