8. ...(Acommenting on the gou's oft-stated desire for a 
formal, nato-ukraine agreement on their developing 
relationship, the deputy secretary reiterated the u.s. 
view that the most important factor in such a relationship 
should be the actual substance of relations, rather than 
documents. the latter, the deputy secretary said, should 
be used to cap off progress, and not be an end in 
themselves. the deputy secretary continued by positing 
that the gou's interest in a nato-ukraine charter derives 
heavily from the belief that there will be a future 
nato-russian charter. in fact, he told udovenko, it is 
clear that the russians are deeply ambivalent about the 
idea of a charter: there are many on the russian side who 
are concerned that a charter would be implicit 
acceptance/recognition by russia of nato enlargement, 
which russia still strongly opposes. the deputy secretary 
said that he is not convinced there will ever in fact be a 
nato-russia charter, and that ukraine should focus on the 
substance of its relations with nato rather than on formal 
documents.
