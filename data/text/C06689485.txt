RELEASE IN PART 
B6 
Thank you for your letter of April 20 expressing the 
support of the American Jewish Congress for lifting 
Jackson-Vanik restrictions on aid to Russia. 
President Clinton has directed the Executive Branch to 
review all legislation directed at countering policies of the 
former Soviet Union and which now may be obsolete given the end 
of the Cold War. The Jackson-Vanik amendment is an example of 
such so-called "Cold War legislation". In light of the 
President's directive, we are reviewing whether the 
Jackson-Vanik amendment should continue to apply to Russia. At 
this time, we believe that before Russia can be graduated from 
Jackson-Vanik provisions, the Russian Government should make 
further progress in guaranteeing free emigration. In 
particular, we would like to see progress in resolving between 
100 and 200 "refusenik" cases and in implementing Russia's 
emigration law. In the meantime, we will recommend that Russia 
again be granted a Jackson-Vanik waiver this year. 
Thank you again, for informing me of your views on this 
issue. I value the opinions of the American Jewish Congress 
very highly, and regret that this reply is not more timely. 
Sincerely,
Strobe Talbott 
Ambassador At Large and 
Special Adviser to The Secretary 
on the New Independent States 
Mr. Mark J. Pelavin, 
American Jewish Congress, 
Washington, DC.
