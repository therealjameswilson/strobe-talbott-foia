1. -(-G4- Summary. The bulk of the Deputy Secretary's 
discussion with German FM Fischer was taken up with 
discussion of the Kosovo crisis and the need for a 
long-term, comprehensive strategy for the Balkans. 
Fischer also made a pitch for a strong U.S. ground 
presence, at least at the start of a Kosovo peace 
implementation effort. Fischer described Russia as 
more open, e.g., on Kosovo, but noted that the current 
government was in a precarious position and seemed to 
believe that the U.S. was not supporting the Primakov 
government, but was considering other options; Moscow 
also thought the U.S. was trying to weaken the Russian 
government and was the cause of its IMF problems. 
Fischer agreed with Talbott that it was 
counterproductive to provide further financial aid to 
Russia in the absence of substantial Russian reforms. 
Fischer briefly described the factors influencing 
Europe's renewed interest in a European Security and 
Defense Identity (ESDI): London's positive move at 
Poertschach, the UK-French St. Malo declaration, 
Europe's experience in Bosnia and Kosovo and the 
current discussion in Europe of multilateralism versus 
unilateralism.

accompanied by Ambassador Kornblum, NSC Senior Director 
Blinken, EUR DAS Asmus, D Executive Assistant Goldberg, 
D Special Assistant Bass and Pol-Mil Chief Hernandez 
(notetaker). On the German side State Secretary 
Wolfgang Ischinger, POLDIR Gunter Pleuger, 
DAS-equivalent Klaus Scharioth, Balkan Task Force 
Director Christian Pauls and USA Desk Deputy Director ' 
Michael Schmunkralso participated. End Summary.

important to have a strong U.S. commitment at the

higher and said the U.S. could reduce its contingent

Long-Term Balkans Strategy Needed 
5. (C)  Fischer then stressed the need to come up with 
a comprehensive long-term strategy for the Balkans that 
would focus on issues such as independence, ethnic 
minorities and borders. Diplomatic solutions or 
structures needed to be found to build confidence in 
the Balkans so they could agree to resolve disputes 
peacefully and build their economies. Fischer was 
concerned that without such a strategy, Bosnia could be 
partitioned and a Gaza-like Muslim state could be 
created in Europe. State Secretary Ischinger also 
proposed that such a strategy address the future of

6. iE4 Fischer raised the possibility of terrorism in

7. tet-Talbott then described a new U.S. proposal for a 
NATO South-East European Stability Initiative (SESI): 
NATO, perhaps in the context of its Open Door policy, 
would intensify collaborative efforts vis-a-vis the 
entire region, supporting the aspirations of some to 
become NATO members and presenting them with concrete 
ways to help tie the region, including Bosnia and 
Kosovo, to the West. EUR DAS Asmus briefly described 
the Initiative and said that over time it could help 
move the Balkans into the Western mainstream and both 
help•the Balkans and contribute to NATO's Open Door 
policy. Fischer and Ischinger responded 
enthusiastically to the concept. Ischinger said that 
FYROM, which had accepted XFOR after some initial 
difficulties, deserved the West's help; President 
Gligorov wanted as his legacy to have a new framework 
in place for FYROM's future relationship with Western 
institutions.

9. -{-G}-FM Fischer described Russia as more open than in 
the past, pointing to the Secretary's and Russian FM 
Ivanov's January 26 joint statement on Kosovo and 
Moscow's general openness on Kosovo. Russians, 
nonetheless, were conscious of their domestic weakness 
and the government seemed to be in a precarious

Ivanov had "insinuated" that the U.S. was not 
supporting the Primakov government, but was looking at 
other options. The Russian government also believed 
the U.S. was behind Russia's IMF problems and was 
trying to weaken the Russian government. Talbott 
replied that the U.S. would like to respond positively 
to Russia's request for macroeconomic help, but knew 
that, without the necessary domestic reforms, financial 
aid would simply disappear. Fischer agreed and said 
that Chancellor Schroeder had told his Russian 
interlocutors in Moscow last November that they would 
have to undertake substantial reforms if they wanted to 
attract money to Russia. Talbott suggested the 
Chancellor be urged to discuss Russia with the 
President during his upcoming trip to Washington. 
10. 444 The Deputy Secretary raised the President's 
proposal to expand cooperative threat reduction 
assistance for Russia, under which the G7 would 
provide additional resources to help Russia comply with 
its arms control, non-proliferation and disarmament 
commitments. Fischer agreed that this was an important 
subject and said Germany would have proposals on how to 
help solve the problem. Fischer emphasized the 
importance of Russia ratifying the START II Treaty and 
Ischinger added that that would enable Germany to start 
discussing unaccounted for sub-strategic nuclear 
weapons. 
Role of the UN 
11. 44'4 Fischer stressed the importance for Germany of 
the UN Security Council as a diplomatic platform and 
urged the U.S. to use it in the Kosovo context. 
Talbott agreed to look for ways to give the UN an 
appropriate role in our joint Kosovo policy.

ESDI 
12. -i-E9--The German Foreign Minister described ESDI as 
"the beginning of a wonderful (Transatlantic) 
relationship," and suggested more detailed discussion 
of the subject in future meetings. He saw a dynamic in 
Europe in favor of ESDI that had been propelled forward 
by UK PM Blair's Poertschach statement, followed by the 
positive French response. Bosnia and Kosovo had also 
played an important role. The reaction in Germany to 
Kosovo was very different from the reaction to Bosnia, 
Fischer said, because of Germany's (positive) 
experience in Bosnia. Noting that the EU had 
successfully launched the EURO, he said it was now time 
to discuss Europe's security and defense identity 
(ESDI). The current discussion in Europe of 
multilateralism vs. unilateralism was also behind 
Europe's current renewed interest in ESDI. 
13.-444 Comment: In their February 4 meetings with 
the Deputy Secretary, FM Fischer and other German 
officials expressed interest in a strong U.S. 
commitment to implement a Kosovo peace plan -- while at 
the same time extolling ESDI. Fischer's plea for a UN 
role in Kosovo, while stated briefly, reflects a major 
philosophical tenet of the Greens. He is eager to 
think strategically about the Balkans and wants to 
engage in constructive brainstorming with U.S. 
policymakers in the weeks and months ahead. On 
Russia-handling; Fischer, like Kinkel before him, wants 
to play a constructive go-between role for Moscow and 
Washington.
