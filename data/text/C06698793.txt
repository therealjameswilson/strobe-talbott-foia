1. SUAMARY: DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT AND COTE 
D'IVOIRE PM DANIEL KABLAN DUNCAN REVIEWED ECONOMIC GROWTH, 
HUMAN RIGHTS, AND LIBERIA. DUNCAN SAID THE IVORIANS ARE 
COUNTING ON NEW FOREIGN INVESTMENT TO HELP REACH THEIR 
TARGET OF 10 PER CENT GROWTH BY 1998. HE DEFENDED HIS 
GOVERNMENT'S CONDUCT IN THE JAILING OF OPPOSITION 
JOUNALISTS, AND URGED THE US TO GREATER ENGAGEMENT IN 
LIBERIA, PARTICULARLY IN FINANCING DEMOBILIZATION AND 
RE-INTEGRATION OF THE FIGHTERS. TALBOTT UHGE15 THE IVORIAN 
GOVERNMENT TO MAINTAIN DIALOGUE WITH THE OPPOSITION AND 
ENCOURAGE A FREE PRESS. HE ASSURED DUNCAN OF OUR 
COMMITMENT TO THE LIBERIAN PEACE PROCESS. END SUMMARY./ 
2. IN THEIR OCTOBER 2 MEETING, TALBOTT CONGRATULATED THE 
PM ON HIS SUCCESSFUL INVESTMENT PROMOTION VISIT TO HOUSTON 
EARLIER IN THE WEEK. DUNCAN NOTED HE HOPES TO IMPROVE ON 
THIS YEAR'S 7.5 PER CENT GROWTH RATE: THE NEXT TWO YEAR 
TARGET IS 10 PER CENT. INTERNATIONAL INVESTMENT AND 
PRIVATIZATION ARE THE KEY. SIXTY SEVEN PER CENT OF 
BUSINESS IN COTE D' IVOIRE IS NOW IN PRIVATE HANDS. THE 
GOAL IS 80 PER CENT. 
TO MAINTAIN THIS MOMENTUM COTE D'IVOIRE NEEDS TO EXPAND 
ITS MARKETS ALL OVER THE WORLD. IN THIS CONTEXT DUNCAN 
ALSO COMPLAINED OF THE EX-IM BANK'S REFUSAL TO FINANCE NEW 
US INVESTMENTS IN THE COTE D'IVOIRE, IN AN APPARENT 
ALLUSION TO THE EX-IM BANK'S DECISION TO CUT OFF FINANCING 
CONNECTED WITH THE COMSAT CONTRACT. 
3. APPLAUDING COTE D' IVOIRE FOR ITS IMPRESSIVE ECONOMIC 
PERFORMANCE, TALBOTT COMPLIMENTED DUNCAN ON HIS 
GOVERNMENT'S RECENT DECISION TO RE-SCHEDULE BY-ELECTIONS 
ABORTED EARLIER THIS YEAR, BUT ALSO SIGNALLED TO DUNCAN 
OUR CONCERN OVER REMAINING HUMAN RIGHTS PROBLEMS IN COTE 
D'IVOIRE. HE URGED THE PRIME MINISTER TO MAINTAIN A 
DIALOGUE WITH THE OPPOSITION, AND, REFERRING TO THE 
JAILING OF OPPOSITION JOURNALISTS, TALBOTT SPOKE OF THE 
IMPORTANCE OF AN ACTIVE AND CRITICAL PRESS IN A CIVIL 
SOCIETY.

5. TURNING TO LIBERIA, TALBOTT THANKED THE IVORIANS FOR 
THEIR EFFORTS TO STEM THE FLOW OF ARMS AND FIGHTERS OVER 
THEIR BORDERS. HE SPOKE OF OUR CONCERN THAT THE FIGHTERS 
MAY BE TRYING TO REPLENISH ARMS STOCKS IN CASE THE PEACE 
NEGOTIATIONS BREAK DOWN, AND ENCOURAGED THE IVORIANS TO 
TAKE ALL POSSIBLE MEASURES TO ASSURE WEAPONS DON'T GO 
ACROSS THEIR BORDERS. 
6.•DUNCAN SAID THE LIBERIAN CONFLICT HAS COST COTE D' 
IVOIRE HEAVILY -- BOTH IN MONEY AND IN LIVES. AS MUCH AS 
ANY COUNTRY IN THE REGION, COTE D'IVOIRE WANTS IT 
RESOLVED. HE URGED THE U.S. TO INCREASE OUR ENGAGEMENT 
WITH LIBERIA. HE SAID FINANCING DEMOBILIZATION AND 
RE-INTEGRATION OF THE FIGHTERS IS CRUCIAL. IF THE 
FIGHTERS HAVE NOTHING TO COME BACK TO THEY WILL SOON PICK 
UP THEIR ARMS AGAIN. 
7. TALBOTT REASSURED DUNCAN THAT THE U.S. INTENDS TO 
REMAIN ENGAGED, BUT NOTED THAT FUNDING IS AN UPHILL 
BATTLE, AND THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY AS A WHOLE WILL 
HAVE TO HELP. THE UNITED STATES HAS A CONTRIBUTION OF 30 
MILLION IN THE PIPELINE FOR ECOMOG, AND WE ARE MEETING 
WITH AN INTERNATIONAL CONTROL GROUP LATER THIS MONTH TO 
DISCUSS ISSUES SUCH AS DEMOBILIZATION.
