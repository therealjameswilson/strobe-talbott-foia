BASED ON THE SECRETARY'S LAST MEETING WITH THE 
RUSSIAN PRESIDENT AND SUBSEQUENT INFORMATION, THE ACTING 
SECRETARY EXPRESSED CONCERN THAT YELTSIN MAY HAVE 
EXCEEDINGLY HIGH EXPECTATIONS FOR THE LYONS SUMMIT. 
WHILE WE SHARED YELTSIN'S HOPE THAT THE SUMMIT BE A 
POSITIVE DIPLOMATIC AND ECONOMIC EXPERIENCE FOR RUSSIA 
AND UNDERSCORE THE WEST'S SUPPORT FOR HIS ECONOMIC 
POLICIES, WE ARE CONCERNED OVER YELTSIN'S EXPECTATION 
THAT RUSSIA WILL BE GRANTED FULL MEMBERSHIP IN THE 
GROUP, THUS TURNING THE G-7 INTO THE G-8. THE ACTING 
SECRETARY ADDED THAT YELTSIN FREQUENTLY CITES 
GERMAN CHANCELLOR KOHL AND FRENCH PRESIDENT CHIRAC AS 
ENCOURAGING THIS DEVELOPMENT. 
'.(8.4- THE ACTING SECRETARY VIEWED RUSSIAN FULL MEMBERSHIP 
AS IMPOSSIBLE UNDER PRESENT CIRCUMSTANCES. FIRST, THE 
RUSSIAN ECONOMY IS NOT READY FOR FULL INCLUSION, THOUGH 
IT IS MAKING GREAT STRIDES. SECOND, THE TIMING OF THE 
SUMMIT FALLS BETWEEN THE FIRST AND SECOND ROUNDS OF 
RUSSIAN PRESIDENTIAL ELECTIONS. 
—E.s.) THE ACTING SECRETARY CONCLUDED THAT PRESIDENT 
YELTSIN'S EXPECTATIONS NEED TO BE MODERATED TO BRING 
THEM IN LINE WITH THE WEST'S. OTHERWISE, HE ANTICIPATED 
TWO LIKELY NEGATIVE REACTIONS: FIRST, THE PROSPECT OF 
CONFRONTING A DISGRUNTLED RUSSIAN PRESIDENT AT THE 
NUCLEAR SUMMIT MEETING IN MOSCOW; AND SECOND, HEADLINES 
IN THE PRESS CLAIMING THE RUSSIANS HAD BEEN SNUBBED. 
46,34 EFFORTS SHOULD THEREFORE BE MADE TO PERSUADE 
PRESIDENT YELTSIN TO EXPECT NOTHING GREATER THAN 
PARTICIPATION ON SATURDAY WITH THE PRESIDENTS OF THE 
WORLD BANK AND IMF, AS WELL AS THE SYG OF THE UNITED 
NATIONS. 
—(-8-) MR. BITTERLICH SAID CHANCELLOR KOHL CLEARLY FAVORS 
MOVING TOWARD A G-8 SCENARIO. WHILE THE RUSSIAN ECONOMY 
STILL LAGGED BEHIND THE WEST, THE CHANCELLOR'S POSITION 
IS BASED ON POLITICAL CONSIDERATIONS. THE CHANCELLOR 
WARNED AS EARLY AS LAST YEAR THAT THE TIMING OF THE 
LYONS SUMMIT WOULD PROVE PROBLEMATIC, AS IT WOULD STILL 
BE UNCLEAR HOW PRESIDENT YELTSIN NOULD FARE IN THE FIRST 
ROUND OF ELECTIONS. ALSO, WHAT TO DO SHOULD THE 
PRESIDENT FALTER IN THE FIRST ROUND? CONSIDERING THESE 
UNCERTAINTIES, IT IS BEST TO POSTPONE ANY DECISION ON 
RUSSIAN INCLUSION UNTIL AFTER THE LYONS SUMMIT. 
4.64 MR. BITTERLICH THEN RAISED THE POSSIBILITY OF A 
STRONG SHOWING BY COMMUNIST PRESIDENTIAL CANDIDATE 
ZYUGANOV. SHOULD THIS OCCUR, THE WEST WOULD NEED TO 
REFLECT ON ITS RELATION WITH RUSSIA, EXPANSION OF NATO, 
AND SO ON, AND CONSIDER HOW ZYUGANOV IS LIKELY TO 
REACT. MORE TIME IS THEREFORE NEEDED TO REFLECT ON 
RUSSIA. THERE IS NO HURRY TO MOVE TOWARD A G-8 THIS 
YEAR. 
—e81— THE ACTING SECRETARY NOTED THIS VIEW HAS IN LINE 
WITH THAT OF THE U.S., WHICH FAVORED MOVING RUSSIA 
CLOSER TOWARD THE G-7 WHEN ITS ECONOMY HAD DEVELOPED 
ENOUGH, BUT THAT IS NOT NOW THE CASE. HE POINTED OUT 
THAT DURING THE HALIFAX SUMMITS THERE EXISTED A DISTINCT 
LINE BETWEEN THE POLITICAL AND ECONOMIC ASPECTS OF 
RUSSIAN PARTICIPATION. AT THE LYONS SUMMIT, YELTSIN 
WILL PARTICIPATE WITH THE PRESIDENTS OF THE WORLD BANK 
AND IMF IN ECONOMIC DISCUSSIONS WITH THE G-7. BUT THE 
G-7 MUST HOLD THE LINE ON MAINTAINING SOME WORKING 
MEETINGS AMONG THEMSELVES. PRESIDENT'S YELTSIN'S 
PARTICIPATION IN SUCH MEETINGS WOULD BE A MISTAKE.

MEETINGS. HOWEVER, PRESIDENT YELTSIN NEEDED TO BE
