2. SUMMARY: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT MET BELARUSIAN 
FOREIGN MINISTER SENKO FOR 20 MINUTES AT THE U.S. 
MISSION TO NATO ON JANUARY 11. SENKO WAS IN BRUSSELS 
TO SIGN THE PFP FRAMEWORK AGREEMENT, AN EVENT THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY TERMED "HISTORIC." SENKO DESCRIBED 
THE POLITICAL AND ECONOMIC SITUATION IN BELARUS, AND 
MINSK'S EFFORTS TO BUILD RELATIONS ACROSS THE BOARD --
WITH THE EU AND WITH NATO THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
STRESSED THE IMPORTANCE OF AVOIDING PRESS CENSORSHIP, 
AND SENKO SAID THE GOVERNMENT WOULD NOT BAN 
PUBLICATION OF NEWSPAPERS. SENKO STRESSED THE 
IMPORTANCE OF THE IMPENDING IMF AGREEMENT, AND S/NIS 
SENIOR COORDINATOR COLLINS EMPHASIZED THE NEED TO 
IMPLEMENT THE AGREEMENT FULLY ONCE REACHED. SENKO 
ALSO RAISED THE LEGAL ASSISTANCE TREATY, AND A DELAY 
IN ESTABLISHING A BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL. HE 
SOUGHT•REASSURANCE THAT THE NUNN-LUGAR PROGRAMS WOULD 
CONTINUE AT THEIR CURRENT PACE AND SCALE. END SUMMARY 
3. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT (ACCOMPANIED BY 
AMBASSADOR HUNTER, S/NIS SENIOR COORDINATOR COLLINS, D 
EXECUTIVE ASSISTANT NULAND AND USNATO ORDWAY) MET 
BELARUSIAN FOREIGN MINISTER SENKO (ACCOMPANIED BY 
AMBASSADOR LABUNOV AND AN AIDE) AT THE U.S. MISSION TO 
NATO SHORTLY.BEFORE THE MINISTER SIGNED THE PFP 
FRAMEWORK DOCUMENT. 
4. SENKO DESCRIBED BELARUSIAN EFFORTS TO DEVELOP 
RELATIONS WITH THE EU, AIMED AT SIGNING A PARTNERSH/P 
AGREEMENT IN FEBRUARY OR MARCH. HE PUT THESE 
RELATIONS IN THE FRAMEWORK OF ENGAGING EUROPE BROADLY 
IN ALL AREAS, ECONOMIC, POLITICAL AND MILITARY. HE 
EXPRESSED PLEASURE AT JOINING PFP, AND SAID BELARUS 
WOULD START WORK TOWARDS AN IPP. 
5. AT THE REQUEST OF THE DEPUTY SECRETARY, SENKO 
OUTLINED THE STATUS OF ECONOMIC REFORM, AND THE 
IMPENDING IMF AGREEMENT WHICH HE EXPECTED WOULD BE 
CONCLUDED SOON. HE CHARACTERIZED THE IMF AGREEMENT AS 
A "GREEN LIGHT" TO OTHERS, AND SAID THAT THE IMF WAS. 
RIGHTFULLY BEING "vERY STRICT." HE NOTED THAT BELARUS 
HAD A LONG WAY TO GO WITH PRIVATIZATION, BUT THAT THE 
LEGAL BASIS FOR THIS HAD BEEN ESTABLISHED. HE 
DESCRIBED THE DIFFICULTIES PRICE LIBERALIZATION HAD 
CREATED. S/NIS SENIOR COORDINATOR COLLINS STRESSED 
THE IMPORTANCE OF BELARUS IMPLEMENTING THIS AGREEMENT 
FULLY, SAYING ITS CREDIBILITY-WAS AT STAKE. SENKO 
SAID THE GOVERNMENT "HAD NO CHOICE" AND WAS COMMITTED 
TO IMPLEMENTATION, BUT DID MENTION THE NEED FOR THE

6. SENKO RAISED THE "WHITE SPOTS" INCIDENT, AND 
ASSURED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY THAT THERE WAS AND WOULD 
BE NO PROHIBITION OF ANY NEWSPAPERS IN BELARUS, 
ALTHOUGH THIS DID NOT MEAN THAT THE GOVERNMENT WOULD 
FINANCE NEWSPAPERS OPPOSED TO IT. HE DESCRIBED THE 
INCIDENT AS BEING IN THE PAST, BUT ONE WHICH HAD 
TAUGHT BELARUS A LESSON IN DEMOCRACY. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY EMPHASIZED THAT THE U.S. CONSIDERED FREEDOM 
OF THE PRESS ONE OF THE MOST IMPORTANT ASPECTS OF 
DEMOCRACY. 
7. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID PRESIDENT CLINTON HAD 
APPRECIATED THE OPPORTUNITY TO MEET PRESIDENT 
LUKASHENKO AT THE BUDAPEST SUMMIT, AND URGED THE 
FOREIGN MINISTER TO STAY IN CLOSE TOUCH WITH 
AMBASSADOR YALOWITZ. 
8. SENKO ASKED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY TO-LOOK INTO THE 
DELAY IN ESTABLISHING THE U.S.-BELARUSIAN BUSINESS 
DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL. (COLLINS SAID HE WOULD-RAISE 
THIS WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF COMMERCE). SENKO ALSO 
ASKED FOR A REACTION TO THE PROPOSED MUTUAL LEGAL 
ASSISTANCE AGREEMENT, AND EXPRESSED KEEN INTEREST IN 
MAINTAINING MOMENTUM ON NUNN-LUGAR PROGRAMS. (COLLINS 
NOTED THAT A DOD DELEGATION WOULD BE IN MINSK SHORTLY 
TO DISCUSS NUNN-LUGAR ISSUES.) FINALLY, SENKO ASKED 
FOR U.S. SUPPORT WITH THE IMF AGREEMENT. 
9. THIS CABLE HAS BEEN CLEARED BY THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY.
