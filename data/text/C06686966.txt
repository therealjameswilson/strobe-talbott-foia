RELEASE IN PART 
B6 
April 27, 1993 
I had the privilege today of listening to your 
luncheon address to the members of the U.S.-Russia 
Business Council in Washington. It has prompted me to 
share with you my recent speech to the Carnegie Council 
of Ethics and International Affairs. You may, disagree 
with my analysis and my suggested solutions, but I hope 
you won't dismiss my views out of hand. Dimitri Simes 
made some of the same points this morning. 
Why not learn a lesson from some of the 
structural reforms we inspired in Japan after World War 
II? Why not send the equivalent of a Dodge mission or a 
Shoup mission to Russia? Why not replace Jeffrey Sachs 
and his (failed) economic shock therapy with Peter 
Drucker? Admittedly, we don't have the same leverage in 
Russia today as we had in Japan from 1945-1952, but, 
(surely, our aid package gives us some leverage. 
I think it was you who said that the Russians 
must learn to walk before they can learn to run. Sachs 
is trying to make them run the 100-yard dash before they 
can even crawl! 
Sincerely yours, 
Isaac Shapi
