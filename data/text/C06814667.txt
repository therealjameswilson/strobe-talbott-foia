SUMARY: IN A FOLLOW-UP MEETING TO THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY'S SEPTEMBER 20 MEETING WITH FM MARTONYI 
(REFTEL), MARTONYI TOLD VISITING EUR DAS SWIGERT THAT 
THERE WOULD DEFINITELY BE A VISEGRAD STATEMENT ON 
ESDI. MARTONYI WELCOMED THE UPCOMING VISIT OF 
SANCTIONS COORDINATOR VIC COMRAS, NOTING THE EMERGING 
CONSENSUS AMONGST THE EUROPEANS THAT THEY WILL NOT 
ACCEPT MILOSEVIC'S ATTEMPT TO BLACKMAIL THEM ON THE 
ISSUE OF CLEARING THE DANUBE. MARTONYI EXPRESSED HIS 
HOPE THAT THE EUROPEAN MINISTERS COULD FIND A WAY TO 
INITIATE A PILOT PROJECT TO DELIVER HEATING OIL TO 
SOME SERBIAN CITIES, WHILE NOTING VOJVODINAN HUNGARIAN 
MAYOR KASZA'S CONCERN THAT THE VOJVODINA WAS BEING 
LEFT OUT. END SUMMARY.

CHARGE MET WITH HUNGARIAN FOREIGN MINSTER JANOS

VISA WAIVER PROGRAM-HUNGARY MAKING PROGRESS 
3.--(efDAS SWIGERT BEGAN THE MEETING BY NOTING 
MARTONYI'S CONCERN, RAISED IN THE TALBOTT MEETING, 
THAT HUNGARY WAS NOT PART OF THE US VISA WAIVER 
PROGRAM. MARTONYI MENTION THAT, INDEED., THIS APPEARED 
TO BE A GAP BETWEEN "WORDS AND REALITIES" HERE, NOTING 
THAT THERE WAS LOTS OF TALK ABOUT HUNGARY'S BEING A 
MEMBER OF NATO, AND YET HUNGARIANS STILL HAD TO STAND 
IN LINES AT THE US EMBASSY TO GET VISAS. HE URGED 
"FLEXIBILITY" ON THE US IN ITS APPROACH. SWIGERT SAID 
THE USG WANTED TO WORK WITH THE HUNGARIANS ON THE 
ISSUE, BUT GIVEN THE FACT THAT THIS WAS A PROGRAM 
APPROVED BY CONGRESS WITH SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS AND 
REGULATIONS, THE USG DID NOT HAVE MUCH FLEXIBILITY IN 
THE MATTER. HE NOTED THAT THERE WERE OTHER NATO 
ALLIES THAT DID NOT QUALIFY FOR THE PROGRAM. CHARGE 
MENTIONED THE VISIT TO WASHINGTON OF MS. ZANATHY OF 
THE MFA, AND SAID THE DETAILS OF THE PROGRAM HAD BEEN 
DISCUSSED WITH HER. HE NOTED THAT REAL PROGRESS HAD 
IN FACT BEEN MADE, AS THE GOH HAD ASSISTED THE EMBASSY 
IN IDENTIFYING FRAUDULENT VISA RINGS IN THE PAST 
YEARS, LEADING TO A REDUCTION IN THE NUMBER OF 
REJECTIONS RECENTLY. HE COMMENTED THAT THE TREND WAS 
POSITIVE WITH REJECTIONS NOW UNDER FIVE PERCENT, AND 
THAT FURTHER ECONOMIC IMPROVEMENT IN THE HUNGARY WOULD 
LIKELY MAKE IT POSSIBLE FOR THAT TREND TO CONTINUE. 
(AFTER THE MEETING, CHARGE PASSED TO KEREK-BARCZY THE 
SHORT SUMMARY ON THE VISA WAIVER PROGRAM PROVIDED TO 
MS. ZANATHY IN WASHINGTON.)

THAT THE US WAS LOOKING FOR WAYS TO USE THE DANUBE

NEWS, NOTING THAT THE EU MEMBERS HAD MADE ASSURANCES 
THAT THEY WERE PREPARED TO SUPPORT CLEARING THE RIVER. 
AT THE SAME TIME, MARTONYI SAID THERE WAS AN EMERGING 
CONSENSUS THAT WE CANNOT ACCEPT MILOSEVIC'S 
BLACKMAILING US ON THE ISSUE. ONE LOOK AT THE MAP, HE 
ASSERTED, SHOWED THAT "MILOSEVIC NEEDS US MUCH MORE 
THAN WE NEED HIM." MARTONYI NOTED THAT THE DANUBE 
COMMISSION HAD BROUGHT FORTH HELPFUL PROPOSALS. HE 
COMMENTED FURTHER THAT THE YUGOSLAV DELEGATION TO THE 
COMMISSION WAS NOT AS NEGATIVE IN THIS APPROACH ON 
THESE ISSUES AS THE GOFRY. 
STILL, ONE COULD NOT RELY 
SOLELY ON THE COMMISSION TO RESOLVE THE ISSUE. 
MARTONYI AGREED WITH SWIGERT THAT ANY FLOODING DUE TO 
ICE FLOWS WOULD OCCUR PRINCIPALLY IN YUGOSLAVIA. 
WHILE THE EXTENT OF DAMAGE WOULD DEPEND ON THE KIND OF 
WEATHER WE WOULD HAVE THIS WINTER, MARTONYI DID NOTE 
THAT THE VOJVODINA WOULD BE MOST SUSCEPTIBLE TO ANY 
DAMAGE.
