2. TALBOTT OPENED THE MEETING BY STATING THAT HE HAD BEEN FRANK IN THE LARGER MEETING AND WOULD LIKE TO SPEAK EVEN MORE BLUNTLY ABOUT THE POLITICAL DYNAMICS IN WASHINGTON. IF REPRESENTATIVES OF THE INDIAN GOVERNMENT AND SKEPTICAL MEMBERS OF CONGRESS HAD HEARD GENERAL WAHEED'S,RESPONSE TO THE USG'S PROPOSAL THEY WOULD BE QUITE WELIEVED BECAUSE THEY WOULD FEEL THEY WERE OFF THE HOOK. THESE MEMBERS OF CONGRESS DON'T BELIEVE PAKISTAN WANTS TO CAP ITS NUCLEAR PRODUCTION. THEY HAVE LONG MEMORIES. THEY FEEL THEY WERE DECEIVED IN THEIR TALKS IN ISLAMABAD. THEY CAN CITE THE DATE WHEN STATEMENTS WERE MADE, THE PROMISES THAT PAKISTAN HAD NO NUCLEAR PROGRAM. 
3. TALBOTT SAID THAT THE VIRTUE OF GENERAL WAHEED'S POSITION WAS ITS HONESTY. HE STRESSED THAT THE PRESIDENT AND SECRETARY OF' STATE WERE READY TO TRY TO RESOLVE THE PROBLEMS CREATED BY PAKISTAN'S NUCLEAR PROGRAM BUT THEY NEEDED HELP. IT WAS ESSENTIAL FOR PAKISTAN TO FORMALIZE THE POSITION IT HAS ALREADY ADOPTED (I.E. A CAP ON ALL FISSILE MATERIAL PRODUCTION). TALBOTT EXPLAINED THAT THE USG HAVE ADOPTED A TWO-TRACK APPROACH. THE /NDIANS WOULD HAVE BEEN DELIGHTED BECAUSE IT WOULD HAVE HANDED THEM A VETO OVER U.S.-PAKISTAN RELATIONS. SINCE THEY DON'T FACE A PRESSLER AMENDMENT, THEY COULD SIT BACK, DO NOTHING AND STOP THE U.S. FROM IMPROVING RELATIONS WITH PAKISTAN.
