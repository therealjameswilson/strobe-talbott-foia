4. (-64 THE ACTING SECRETARY ASKED AMBASSADOR USHAKOV TO 
PASS URGENT MESSAGES TO FOREIGN MINISTER IVANOV AND THE 
RUSSIAN FOREIGN MINISTRY, SINCE BOTH SECRETARY ALBRIGHT 
AND FM IVANOV WERE TRAVELLING AND COULD NOT SPEAK TO EACH 
OTHER EASILY. 
5. 4-) THE ACTING SECRETARY EMPHASIZED THAT FEBRUARY 20 
NOON REMAINED A FIRM DEADLINE FOR BELGRADE TO SIGN THE 
RAMBOUILLET AGREEMENT. HE TOLD USHAKOV AMBASSADOR HILL 
HAD NOT MET FEBRUARY 19 WITH MILOSEVIC IN BELGRADE, BUT 
DID DELIVER A CLEAR MESSAGE THAT MILOSEVIC MUST ACCEPT THE 
FULL CONTACT GROUP PROPOSAL. THERE WAS LITTLE TIME TO 
AVOID WHAT ALL PARTIES WANTED TO AVOID -- GOING TO ACTORD 
IMPLEMENTATION AND NATO AIRSTRIKES. THE ACTING SECRETARY 
SAID HE KNEW THE RUSSIAN GOVERNMENT'S POSITION ON THE USE 
OF FORCE, AND DESPITE THAT THE CG HAD BEEN ABLE TO SEND A 
UNITED MESSAGE TO MILOSEVIC. THE ACTING SECRETARY AND 
USHAKOV SAID THEY HAD BOTH BEEN BRIEFED ON THE ONGOING AND 
PRODUCTIVE CONVERSATIONS BETWEEN THE SECRETARY AND FM 
IVANOV, AS WELL AS BETWEEN AMBASSADOR HILL AND MAJORSKY. 
6. 44- THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID HE WAS CONCERNED ABOUT 
PRESIDENT YELTSIN'S RECENT PUBLIC STATEMENTS ON KOSOVO AND 
THE LETTER HE HAD SENT TO PRESIDENT CLINTON. THESE TWO 
MESSAGES MAY HAVE LED MILOSEVIC TO MISCALCULATE WHAT WOULD 
HAPPEN AFTER EXPIRATION OF THE DEADLINE. THEY MIGHT LEAD 
HIM TO MAKE THE PROFOUND MISTAKE OF BELIEVING HE COULD 
ESCAPE THE CONSEQUENCES OF REFUSING THE AGREEMENT. THE 
ACTING SECRETARY ASKED THAT THE RUSSIAN GOVERNMENT PASS AN 
AUTHORITATIVE MESSAGE TO MILOSEVIC, SAYING WHILE RUSSIA 
OPPOSED THE USE OF FORCE IT COULD NOT DO ANYTHING TO STOP 
IT. THE ACTING SECRETARY ADDED THE RUSSIANS WOULD DECIDE 
HOW TO MAKE THE STATEMENT CONSISTENT WITH GOR POLICY AND 
PREDICTIONS. 
7. 44 THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THE LIKELIHOOD OF NATO 
AIRSTRIKES WOULD INCREASE GREATLY IF MILOSEVIC DID NOT 
SIGN. HE HOPED RUSSIA WOULD SPEAK TO MILOSEVIC ON THE 
BASIS OF MAINTAINING U.S.—RUSSIAN SOLIDARITY. HE ALSO 
HOPED SUCH A MESSAGE WOULD SUCCEED IN PERSUADING MILOSEVIC 
HE HAD NO CHOICE BUT TO AGREE TO AVOID NATO ATTACKS IN THE 
DAYS TO COME.

8. (-Si THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID HE HOPED A KOSOVO 
IMPLEMENTATION FORCE ON THE GROUND WOULD INCLUDE RUSSIAN 
TROOPS, FOLLOWING THE PRECEDENT OF BOSNIA. MILOSEVIC'S 
CHOICE WAS THE RAMBOUILLET AGREEMENT OR AIRSTRIKES IN 
BELGRADE. HE ASKED FOR AN URGENT MESSAGE TO GO TO 
MILOSEVIC IN THE EARLY MORNING FEBRUARY 20. RUSSIA MIGHT 
DISAGREE WITH NATO ABOUT THE NEED FOR AIRSTRIKES, BUT 
FORCE WOULD BE INEVITABLE IF MILOSEVIC REFUSED TO ACCEPT 
THE AGREEMENT. 
9. AMBASSADOR USHAKOV ASKED WHAT REACTION THE ACTING 
SECRETARY SOUGHT FROM IVANOV. THE ACTING SECRETARY ASKED 
IVANOV TO CONVEY THE BASIC MESSAGE "DO IT, SLOBODAN, 
ACCEPT THE DEAL." ALTHOUGH RUSSIA WAS OPPOSED TO ANNEX 
1A, THE AGREEMENT WAS A PACKAGE DEAL. MILOSEVIC COULD NOT 
ACCEPT ONLY THE POLITICAL PART. IF HE REJECTED THE , 
SECURITY PART, HE WOULD BRING AIRSTRIKES UPON HIMSELF. 
10. THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID HE WOULD BE AVAILABLE 
AT ANY HOUR TO RECEIVE THE RUSSIAN RESPONSE. HE TOLD 
USHAKOV THE U.S. WAS PREPARING FOR THE WORST. USHAKOV 
SAID HE UNDERSTOOD THE SERIOUSNESS OF THE REQUEST AND 
WOULD DO HIS BEST TO RELAY THE MESSAGE EFFECTIVELY. THE 
ACTING SECRETARY CLOSED BY SAYING HE WOULD LIKE TO GET 
PAST KOSOVO AND DISCUSS LONG-TERM ISSUES DURING HIS 
FEBRUARY 21 MEETINGS IN MOSCOW. 
11. (U) THE CALL ENDED AT 2114 EST. 
12. +Si- AMBASSADOR USHAKOV INITIATED A FOLLOW-UP CALL AT 
2335 EST, IN WHICH HE TOLD THE ACTING SECRETARY HE HAD 
PASSED THE MESSAGE TO ONE OF IVANOV'S PRINCIPAL AIDES. HE 
SAID IVANOV WAS IN SAKHALIN, WHERE HE WOULD SPEND A FEW 
HOURS BEFORE CONTINUING TO TOKYO. THE ACTING SECRETARY 
THANKED USHAKOV AND TOLD HIM THE SECRETARY WOULD ARRIVE IN 
PARIS SHORTLY AND IT WOULD THEN BE POSSIBLE FOR HER TO 
SPEAK TO IVANOV. 
13. (U) THE SECOND CALL ENDED AT 2337 EST.
