2. SUMMARY. IN A MEETING WITH THE DEPUTY SECRETARY, 
APRIL 25, NEW ZEALAND'S DEPUTY PRIME MINISTER/FOREIGN' 
MINISTER DON MCKINNON ADMITTED THAT THE POLITICAL CLIMATE 
IN NEW ZEALAND WOULD 
MCKINNON 
THANKED THE U.S. FOR SIGNING THE SOUTH PACIFIC NUCLEAR 
FREE ZONE TREATY (SPNFZ) PROTOCOLS, MADE A PITCH FOR THE 
U.S. TO REMAIN ENGAGED IN THE SOUTH PACIFIC, AND STRONGLY 
ENCOURAGED PRESIDENT CLINTON1 S PARTICIPATION IN THE 
NOVEMBER APEC MEETINGS. HE INDICATED THAT NEW ZEALAND MAY 
SEND A FRIGATE TO THE MULTINATIONAL INTERCEPTION FORCE 
(MIF) IN THE PERSIAN GULF THIS DECEMBER. MCKINNON ALSO 
SOUGHT THE ACTING SECRETARY'S ANALYSES OF THE POLITICAL 
SITUATIONS IN RUSSIA, THE MIDDLE EAST, AND THE RACIFIC. 
END SUMMARY. 
3. DEPUTY PRIME MINISTER/MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS AND 
TRADE DON MCKINNON, ACCOMPANIED BY NEW ZEALAND AMB. JOHN 
WOOD AND OTHER EMBASSY OFFICIALS, CALLED ON ACTING 
SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT APRIL 25. MCKINNON OBSERVED THAT 
THE BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP AND IMPROVED CONTACTS WERE 
PROCEEDING AT AN APPROPRIATE PACE.

5. ON OTHER BILATERAL ISSUES, MCKINNON PRAISED THE U.S. 
DECISION TO SIGN THE SPNFZ TREATY PROTOCOLS AND TO 
ENCOURAGE THE BRITISH AND FRENCH TO DO THE SAME.' HE 
PREDICTED THAT NEW ZEALAND WOULD GRADUALLY "GET ON WITH 
IMPROVED BILATERAL TIES-WITH FRANCE" FOLLOWING THE CLOSURE 
OF THE FRENCH NUCLEAR TEST SITE IN THE SOUTH PACIFIC. 
MCKINNON NOTED THAT THE DEPLOYMENT OF ONE OF NEW ZEALAND'S 
THREE FRIGATES TO ENFORCE ANTI-IRAQ SANCTIONS IN THE 
PERSIAN GULF WITH THE MIF HAD BEEN A RENEWED TEST OF THE 
PUBLIC'S TOLERANCE FOR N.Z. DEFENSE PARTICIPATION IN 
MULTILATERAL EFFORTS. THAT TEST HAD GONE SMOOTHLY, AND A 
POST-ELECTION GOVERNMENT COULD WELL CONSIDER ANOTHER MIF 
CONTRIBUTION TOWARD THE END OF 1996.. 
6. MCKINNON MADE A PITCH FOR THE U.S. TO STAY FULLY 
ENGAGED IN THE SOUTH PACIFIC IN SPITE OF RESOURCE AND 
BUDGET CONSTRAINTS. HE MENTIONED U.S. CONTRIBUTIONS TO 
THE SOUTH PACIFIC COMMISSION AND A CONTINUED U.S. EMBASSY 
PRESENCE IN SUVA, FIJI, AND APIA, WESTERN SAMOA, AS 
EXAMPLES OF VALUABLE U.S. ENGAGEMENT. 
7. THE ACTING SECRETARY THANKED MCKINNON FOR NEW 
ZEALAND'S EARLY AND MULTI-YEAR CONTRIBUTION TO THE KOREAN 
PENINSULA ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT ORGANIZATION (KEDO). 
MCKINNON QUIPPED THAT HE WAS DELIGHTED TO FORCE THE 
AUSTRALIANS TO PAY MORE INTO KEDO THAN THEY HAD ORIGINALLY 
INTENDED, AND SAID HE WAS A BIT DISCOURAGED THAT OTHER 
(PARTICULARLY ASIAN) COUNTRIES HAD NOT BEEN OVERLY 
ENTHUSIASTIC IN FOLLOWING THE KIWI LEAD. 
8. IN A DISCUSSION OF PRESIDENT CLINTON'S TRIP TO NORTH 
ASIA, MCKINNON WAS QUICK TO AGREE TO THE ACTING 
SECRETARY1 S POINT THAT THE TRIP EMPHASIZED ONGOING U.S. 
INVOLVEMENT IN REGIONAL SECURITY. MCKINNON EXPRESSED 
APPRECIATION FOR THE U.S. SECURITY UMBRELLA, WHICH ALLOWS 
NEW ZEALAND AND OTHER COUNTRIES TO TRADE THROUGHOUT THE 
REGION. MCKINNON SAID HE KNEW THAT PRESIDENT CLINTON 
REGRETTED BEING UNABLE TO ATTEND LAST YEAR'S APEC MEETING 
IN OSAKA, AND POINTED OUT THE IMPORTANCE OF THE 
PRESIDENT'S ATTENDANCE AT THE NEXT APEC SUMMIT IN THE 
PHILIPPINES. 
9. IN MORE GENERAL DISCUSSIONS MCKINNON APPRECIATED THE 
ACTING SECRETARY'S ANALYSIS OF THE UPCOMING RUSSIAN 
PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION AND THE CURRENT POLITICAL REALITIES
