SUMMARY 
1. t23. ON OCTOBER 1, D/S TALBOTT INVITED AMBASSADORS FROM 
THE P-S AND EXPANDED GROUP OF G-8 COUNTRIES (MEMBERS OF THE 
"SOUTH ASIA TASK FORCE"), TO BRIEFINGS AT THE DEPARTMENT ON 
U.S. NON-PROLIFERATION EFFORTS IN SOUTH ASIA. ' 
D/S TALBOTT PRESENTED A BRIEF OVERVIEW OF THE CURRENT STATE 
OF PLAY AND PROSPECTS FOR THE FUTURE, NOTING THAT WE ARE 
CURRENTLY CONCENTRATING ON THE FIVE "BENCHMARKS" THAT 
ENCOMPASS THE P-S'S IMMEDIATE GOALS. HE SAID PROGRESS IS 
BEING MADE, BUT MORE IS REQUIRED, AND EMPHASIZED THAT THE 
U.S. REMAINS DETERMINED TO WORK CLOSELY WITH THE P-S AND G-
8 COUNTRIES TO PURSUE OUR COMMON AGENDA. EXPRESSING 
CONCERN ABOUT RUSSIAN AND FRENCH EFFORTS WITH INDIA ON 
PEACEFUL NUCLEAR ENERGY, D/S TALBOTT STRESSED THAT WE MUST 
STICK TOGETHER AND NOT BREAK RANKS IN ORDER FOR OUR 
STRATEGY TO SUCCEED. FOLLOWING HIS REMARKS D/S TALBOTT AND 
A/S INDERFURTH ANSWERED QUESTIONS AND EXCHANGED VIEWS WITH 
SEVERAL AMBASSADORS. END SUMMARY. 
p/s TALBOTT'S PRESENTATION 
2. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID THAT HE HAD CALLED THE 
AMBASSADORS TO THE STATE DEPARTMENT TO UPDATE THEM ON U.S. 
EFFORTS TO PERSUADE INDIA AND PAKISTAN TO JOIN THE 
INTERNATIONAL NONPROLIFERATION REGIME. D/S TALBOTT NOTED 
THAT SINCE HE LAST BRIEFED THE AMBASSADORS ON JULY 31, 
THERE HAS BEEN SOME PROGRESS. HE WELCOMED PM VAJPAYEE'S' 
AND PM SHARIF'S POSITIVE STATEMENTS TO THE UN GENERAL 
ASSEMBLY ON COMPREHENSIVE TEST BAN TREAT (CTBT) ADHERENCE. 
(CTBT) BY SEPTEMBER 1999. D/S TALBOTT SAID HE WAS ALSO 
ENCOURAGED BY THE VAJPAYEE/SHARIF MEETING IN NEW YORK AND 
THEIR AGREEMENT TO RESUME BILATERAL TALKS AT THE FOREIGN 
SECRETARY LEVEL ON ALL OUTSTANDING ISSUES, INCLUDING 
KASHMIR. INDIA AND PAKISTAN HAVE ALSO WITHDRAWN THEIR 
OPPOSITION TO NEGOTIATIONS ON A FISSILE MATERIAL CUTOFF 
TREATY (FMCT) IN GENEVA AND HAVE PROMISED TO STRENGTHEN 
THEIR CONTROL SYSTEMS ON THE TRANSFER OF SENSITIVE 
TECHNOLOGIES AND MATERIALS. 
3. 4e} D/S TALBOTT NOTED THAT HE AND A/S INDERFURTH HAVE 
HELD INTENSE AND DETAILED TALKS WITH SENIOR REPRESENTATIVES 
OF INDIA AND PAKISTAN, AND STRESSED THAT THROUGHOUT THESE 
TALKS THE USG HAS KEPT THE P-5 OBJECTIVES, LATER ENDORSED 
BY THE G-S AND UNSCR 1172, AS ITS PRINCIPAL OBJECTIVE. OF 
THE 13 "BENCHMARKS" SPELLED-OUT IN THE P-5 COMMUNIQUE, THE

SIGNING AND RATIFYING CTBT, CONSTRUCTIVE PARTICIPATION IN

FOR NUCLEAR CAPABLE MISSILES AND AIRCRAFT, AND 
STRENGTHENING OF EXPORT CONTROL SYSTEMS. IN ADDITION., WE 
HAVE URGED THE TWO NEIGHBORS TO RESUME THEIR BILATERAL 
DIALOGUE IN A POSITIVE SPIRIT. WHILE THERE HAS BEEN REAL 
PROGRESS, SAID D/S TALBOTT, MUCH REMAINS TO BE DONE, AND 
COLLECTIVE ACTION BY P-5 AND G-8 COUNTRIES REMAINS 
ESSENTIAL. 
4. —E4.34- D/S TALBOTT INFORMED THE AMBASSADORS THAT THE 
PRESIDENT HAS DECIDED TO POSTPONE HIS VISIT TO THE REGION 
IN NOVEMBER, AS THE OUTSTANDING NONPROLIFERATION ISSUES 
REQUIRE MORE TIME TO RESOLVE. HE ALSO STRESSED THAT UNTIL 
THERE IS MORE PROGRESS, THE U.S. CANNOT LIFT ITS SANCTIONS 
AGAINST INDIA AND PAKISTAN. THE PRESIDENT IS STILL EAGER, 
HOWEVER, TO UNDERTAKE THE TRIP WHEN HE CAN BE SURE IT WILL 
BE POSITIVE'AND FORWARD-LOOKING. 
5. TALBOTT SAID THE U.S. WOULD CONTINUE BILATERAL 
TALKS BOTH AT THE SENIOR AND AT THE EXPERTS' LEVEL, WITH 
INDIA AND PAKISTAN. THERE WILL BE MEETINGS BETWEEN INDIAN 
AND AMERICAN EXPERTS IN EUROPE ON OCTOBER 27 AND 28, AND WE 
HOPE TO FbLLOW THESE WITH A SECOND ROUND OF EXPERT MEETINGS 
WITH PAKISTAN. IN NOVEMBER, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY PLANS TO 
MEET WITH JASWANT SINGH AND POSSIBLY SHAMSHAD AHMAD. 
6. *et THERE WILL ALSO BE FURTHER DEVELOPMENTS IN THE 
SANCTIONS AREA, ALL OF WHICH ARE REQUIRED BY THE GLENN 
AMENDMENT IMPOSED ON INDIA AND PAKISTAN FOLLOWING THEIR 
NUCLEAR TESTS IN MAY. A TREASURY EXECUTIVE ORDER WILL 
SPELL OUT RESTRICTIONS ON THE ACTIVITIES OF U.S. BANKS IN 
INDIA .AND PAKISTAN. SEPARATELY THE USG WILL PUBLISH A 
DETAILED LIST OF ENTITIES IN INDIA AND PAKISTAN IN THE 
FEDERAL REGISTER. ACTIVITIES OF AMERICAN COMPANIES 
CONDUCTING BUSINESS WITH THESE ENTITIES WILL BE RESTRICTED. 
THE BROWNBACK AMENDMENT, IF PASSED, MAY ALLOW THE USG TO 
RESPOND TO POSITIVE STEPS TAKEN BY INDIA AND PAKISTAN. 
HOWEVER, BROWNBACK AUTHORITY WILL NOT AUTOMATICALLY BE 
INVOKED WITHOUT PROGRESS, AND THE AMENDMENT IS NOT YET LAW. 
.COLLECTIVE RESPONSE 
7. —4444- D/S TALBOTT STRESSED THE IMPORTANCE OF 
INTERNATIONAL SOLIDARITY IN ENFORCING THE "NO BUSINESS AS 
USUAL" REGIMEN AND MAINTAINING THE SPOTLIGHT ON THE SOUTH 
ASIAN PROLIFERATION PROBLEM. HE NOTED THAT P-5 AND G-8 
MEMBERS IN PARTICULAR SHOULD CARRY THE SAME MESSAGE TO 
INDIA AND PAKISTAN. A/S INDERFURTH URGED THE P-S AND G-8 
AMBASSADORS TO IMPRESS UPON THEIR GOVERNMENTS THE 
IMPORTANCE OF THE RESUMPTION OF THE UPCOMING INDIA/PAKISTAN 
BILATERAL TALKS. WE SHOULD ALL PRESS INDIA AND PAKISTAN TO 
BRING SUBSTANCE TO THE TALKS AND STRESS THAT THEY SHOULD 
MAKE A SERIOUS EFFORT TO ADDRESS THE KASHMIR PROBLEM AND 
CONFIDENCE-BUILDING MEASURES THAT WOULD REDUCE TENSION 
ALONG THE LINE OF CONTROL.

9. -(-S4- D/S TALBOTT STRESSED THAT INDIA AND PAKISTAN ARE 
NOT SIGNATORIES TO THE NON PROLIFERATION TREATY, NEITHER 
ARE THEY RECOGNIZED NUCLEAR WEAPONS STATES. UNDER THE 
TERMS OF THE INTERNATIONAL NON-PROLIFERATION REGIME, THEY 
MUST FACE THE CONSEQUENCES FOR THEIR NUCLEAR TESTS AND 
MOVES TOWARD ACQUIRING NUCLEAR WEAPONS. JUST BECAUSE SOME 
TIME HAS ELAPSED SINCE THEIR TESTS, OR OTHER ISSUES HAVE 
COME TO THE FORE, THERE IS NO REASON TO RELENT AND BEGIN 
COOPERATING WITH THEM IN PRESCRIBED AREAS ONCE AGAIN. THIS 
WOULD GIVE BOTH COUNTRIES WHAT THEY WANT: NORMAL RELATIONS 
WITH OTHER COUNTRIES AND DEFACTO RECOGNITION AS NUCLEAR - 
WEAPONS STATES.

10. +ei- SEVERAL AMBASSADORS ASKED ABOUT THE PRESIDENT'S 
TRAVEL PLANS. D/S TALBOTT EMPHASIZED THAT THE TRIP HAS NOT' 
BEEN CANCELED. THE PRESIDENT STILL HOPES TO GO TO THE 
REGION NEXT YEAR. HOWEVER, THE USG CANNOT COMMIT TO A 
DEFINITE DATE FOR THE TRIP AT THIS TIME. WE WILL CONTINUE 
TO MONITOR CLOSELY OUR NONPROLIFERATION AGENDA. THE UK 
AMBASSADOR ASKED ABOUT THE RESUMPTION OF FINANCIAL AID TO 
PAKISTAN FROM INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS (IFI'S). 
D/S TALBOTT POINTED OUT THAT THE USG WOULD LIKE TO SEE A 
RESOLUTION OF PAKISTAN'S ECONOMIC PROBLEMS, BUT FIRST AND 
FOREMOST, BEFORE THE U.S. CAN TAKE ACTION, PAKISTAN MUST 
CONCLUDE ITS NEGOTIATIONS WITH THE INTERNATIONAL MONETARY 
FUND. ONCE THIS IS RESOLVED, THE USG CAN ASSESS WHAT 
ACTIONS MIGHT BE POSSIBLE. 
11. (C) THE AUSTRALIAN AMBASSADOR ASKED WHAT IS REQUIRED 
FOR INDIA AND PAKISTAN TO OVERCOME DOMESTIC HURDLES AND 
SIGN THE CTBT. TALBOTT REPLIED THAT BOTH COUNTRIES WILL 
LIKELY CONTINUE TO INSIST THAT THEY WILL NOT ACT UNDER 
DURESS. WE WOULD HAVE TO WORK CLOSELY WITH THEM, HE SAID. 
CTBT, HOWEVER, WOULD NOT BE THE MOST INTRACTABLE PROBLEM. 
THE FMCT AND RESTRAINT REGIMES COULD PROVE MUCH MORE 
DIFFICULT. ALTHOUGH THESE ARE NOT HOT DOMESTIC ISSUES IN 
EITHER COUNTRY THEY ARE IMPORTANT TO THE DOMESTIC SECURITY 
ESTABLISHMENTS. INDIA, IN PARTICULAR, SEEMS CURRENTLY TO 
WANT TO MAINTAIN AN OPEN-ENDED LIST OF STRATEGIC OPTIONS. 
ALTHOUGH INDIA AND PAKISTAN HAVE REPEATEDLY STATED THAT 
THEY WANT A "MINIMAL NUCLEAR DETERRENT" WE NEED TO SEE 
SIGNS THAT THEY ARE SINCERE.
