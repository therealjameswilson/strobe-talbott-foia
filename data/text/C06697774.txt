Presidential Election 
5. -(-E6)- The Deputy Secretary said that President Clinton 
had just spoken with Yeltsin and had commended him for his 
statement repudiating Korzhakov's suggestion that the 
presidential vote might have to be postponed. It is 
essenf.ial at this point that the election proceed as 
scheduled. Watanabe lent his strong endorsement. 
6. -(C--) Watanabe said that the Japanese have also been 
carefully monitoring the manypublic opinion polls on the 
presidential election and believe that a runoff between 
Yeltsin and zyuganov appears very likely. Expressing a 
personal opinion, Watanabe said he thought Yeltsin might 
edge out Zyuganov in the runoff. Three factors would be 
important: Yeltsin's health, Chechnya, and whether 
Yeltsin would be able to join forces with the reformists 
who presumably would be knocked out in the first round. 
Watanabe related the recent visit to Moscow by former 
Foreign Minister Taro Nakayama; Aleksandr Yakovlev told 
Nakayama he thought Yeltsin could win, but it would be a 
mistake to underestimate Zyuganov's strength in the 
agricultural and military-industrial sectors. 
Russia-PRC Summit 
7. The Deputy Secretary asked Ambassador Watanabe for 
his assessment of Yelstin's visit to China. Watanabe said 
that it was in the interests of both sides to highlight 
what was clearly a carefully worded communique, but it 
seemed that the Chinese side wanted to play it up even 
more than the Russians. With terms like "hegemony," the 
PRC-Russian communique was probably designed in part as a 
counterweight to the U.S.-Japan joint declaration on 
security issued during-President Clinton's state visit. 
Japanese Defense Minister'i Visit to Russia 
8. -(-G-)-Watanabe commented that the visit to Russia by 
Japanese Defence Agency head Usui in early May was the 
first ever. Arrangements for Usui's visit were finalized 
by Yeltsin and Hashimoto in Moscow. Watanabe said that 
Usui, against Watanabe's advice, had been photographed in 
the cockpitof a Russian Su-27 aircraft at his hosts' 
urging. This had prompted unwarranted speculation in the 
press that the GOJ was considering a purchase of Russian 
military hardware.
