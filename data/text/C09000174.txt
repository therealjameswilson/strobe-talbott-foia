Dear Bill: 
RELEASE IN PART 
B6 
I've heard from several sources -- more reliable, I might add, than 
Elaine Sciolino's -- that you're thoroughly pissed off at me; that you believe 
that I had something to do with the Steve Holmes article on you last week in 
The New York Times; that you also suspect me of being behind the few 
references in the presš earlier this year to the ridiculous notion that you were 
in some sense slacking in your approach to the Haiti assignment. 
This all pains me very much. Let's start with the facts. Your 
suspicions about me are totally without foundation. I had zero contact with 
Holmes for his piece (which annoyed me mightily, though I'm sure not as 
much as it did you). In earlier on-the-record and background sessions I've 
had with reporters, I've always made a point of stressing your immense 
contribution and extraordinary dedication -- and, I might say, your 
substantive wisdom about the course of our policy. 
When I heard that John Goshko was writing a piece on you, I did 
something I'd never before done: I initiated a call to him specifically and 
explicitly to counter the bullshit about your not being 100% engaged and 
available. 
Now, I realize that beyond your anger at me, you feel you have a 
grievance against the State Department. All I can say there is two points: 
First, sure, the building leaks, and sometimes it leaks smelly if not 
poisonous stuff; I've gotten'a little bit of it on me from time to time. So has 
virtually everyone else who's received any publicity at all. It comes with the - 
territory -- and not just the territory of Foggy Bottom. It accounts for about 
10 degrees of the heat in the kitchen.

But I honestly believe -- and here I think I am, only naturally, more 
objective than you -- that you've fared pretty well, for a damned good 
reason: you've done extraordinarily well in an extraordinarily tough job. 
And that, in the final analysis, determines public judgment -- eventually, if 
not immediately. The "tick-tock" reconstructions of how our Haiti policy 
evolved have been inaccurate and unfair (and not just to you). Historians will 
get right what journalists got wrong. 
Second, .I know for a fact that you're very highly regarded among all 
Ihe people who matter in the Department. That doesn't just mean by the 
Secretary and the other Seventh Floor principals: it means by the "working-
level" types. They see you as having helped instrumentally in bringing us to 
the brink of a major foreign-policy victory. And more important, you're 
seen as having made sure that we did the right thing. 
Personally, I'll never forget the way, you rose to the challenge of the 
refugee crisis, working the Caribbean leaders to make sure we had safe 
havens; or taming the tigers of the Hill; or -- most crucially of all -- helping 
us to deal with President Aristide (and that meant, among other things, 
helping us to understand him and give him his considerable due). 
All in all, Bill, there are at least two nations onthis earth that owe you 
a huge debt of gratitude. 
We owe one to John as well. I can't tell you how impressed I've been 
by him. I hope the President and the Secretary prevail on you both to stay 
involved. While my first visit to Haiti last Friday was immensely gratifying 
and encouraging, it also left me in no ddubt that we're a long way from 
finished with this task. 
It pleases me to think that I'm writing this letter on the day Father 
Aristide is preaching to your own flock. I assume he's preaching 
reconciliation. Let there be a little of that between you and me.
