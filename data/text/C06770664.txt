4. -fti- FISCHER EXPRESSED CONCERN ABOUT THE IMPENDING 
EXECUTION OF WALTER LAGRAND IN ARIZONA. THE GERMANS HAD 
MADE A LEGAL INTERVENTION IN THE INTERNATIONAL COURT OF 
JUSTICE (ICJ), WHICH WOULD MAKE A RULING IN THE NEXT HALF 
HOUR. FISCHER HAD TELEPHONED THE GOVERNOR OF ARIZONA THE 
PREVIOUS EVENING; SHE WAS VERY TOUGH. A MERCY COUNCIL 
THERE HAD RECOMMENDED THE EXECUTION BE POSTPONED 60 DAYS, 
BUT THE GOVERNOR HAD ORDERED THE EXECUTION TO PROCEED 
TODAY. MORE TIME WAS NEEDED, FISCHER ARGUED. IF THE ICJ 
RULING WERE POSITIVE, THE FRG WOULD CONSIDER AN APPEAL TO 
THE U.S. SUPREME COURT. 
5.4764 THE ACTING SECRETARY REPLIED HE WAS FAMILIAR WITH 
THE ISSUE AND UNDERSTOOD ITS GRAVITY IN BOTH POLITICAL AND 
HUMAN TERMS. THIS WAS LITERALLY A LIFE-AND-DEATH ISSUE. 
HE TOOK FISCHER'S CALL WITH THE UTMOST SERIOUSNESS. 
FISCHER PROBABLY KNEW THAT THE SECRETARY WAS IN THAILAND 
AND UNAVAILABLE AT THE MOMENT, BUT HE WOULD CONVEY THE 
ESSENCE OF FISCHER'S APPEAL TO HER. THE ISSUE LAY AT THE 
HEART OF THE AMERICAN CONSTITUTIONAL SYSTEM, AND THE FINAL 
DECISION IN SUCH MATTERS RESTED WITH THE STATES. THERE 
WERE REAL AND UNALTERABLE LIMITS ON WHAT THE FEDERAL 
GOVERNMENT AND THE DEPARTMENT OF STATE COULD DO. 
6.--(44- FISCHER NOTED THE FRG FACED TWO PROBLEMS: THERE 
WAS VERY STRONG PUBLIC OPINION ON THE ISSUE IN GERMANY, 
AND THE MASS MEDIA HAD TAKEN IT UP. RELATIONS BETWEEN THE 
GOVERNMENT AND PUBLIC OPINION WERE PROBLEMATIC. THE 
ACTING SECRETARY SAID HE UNDERSTOOD THE PREDICAMENT; THE 
USG ALSO TOOK PUBLIC OPINION SERIOUSLY. THE AMERICAN 
SYSTEM PROTECTED THE RIGHTS OF THE INDIVIDUAL. HE 
UNDERSTOOD THE URGENCY. HE WOULD INVESTIGATE AND SEE IF 
ANYTHING MORE COULD BE SAID.

7. (-64- FISCHER UNDERSTOOD THE DECISION ULD BE MADE BY 
THE GOVERNOR, BUT IT WOULD BE USEFUL TO INFLUENCE HER. HE 
DID NOT KNOW THE NAME OF THE COUNCIL THAT HAD RECOMMENDED 
CLEMENCY. THE STATE ATTORNEY HAD INFORMED THE PROSECUTION 
THE LAGRAND BROTHERS WERE NOT AMERICAN CITIZENS, SO THE 
PROSECUTION KNEW THEY WERE GERMANS, BUT NO ONE HAD 
INFORMED THE CONSULATE OR GERMAN AUTHORITIES. THEREFORE, 
IT WAS ONLY FAIR TO ALLOW THE ICJ TO ISSUE ITS RULING. 
THEN THE GOVERNOR COULD DECIDE, AND SHE WOULD PROBABLY 
DECIDE NEGATIVELY, BUT AT LEAST THERE WOULD HAVE BEEN TIME 
GIVEN TO THE PROCESS.
