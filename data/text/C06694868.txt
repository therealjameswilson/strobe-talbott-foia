2. SUMMARY. ON JULY 21, ACTING SECRETARY OF STATE 
STROBE TALBOTT MET WITH RUSSIAN FEDERATION COUNCIL 
CHAIRMAN VLADIMIR SHUMEYKO. SHUMEYKO MADE A DISTINCTION 
BETWEEN THE DUMA, WHICH HE SAID WAS MOTIVATED BY PARTY 
POLITICS AND PERSONAL INTERESTS, AND THE FEDERATION 
COUNCIL, WHICH HE SAID BETTER REPRESENTED THE GROWING 
IMPORTANCE OF LOCAL AND REGIONAL INTERESTS. HE 
HIGHLIGHTED THE TRIPARTITE AGREEMENT SIGNED AMONG THE 
DUMA, THE FEDERATION COUNCIL, AND EIGHT TERRITORIAL 
ECONOMIC ASSOCIATIONS WHICH WILL ALLOW FOR THE INTERESTS 
OF THE REGIONS TO BE TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT IN THE 
DEVELOPMENT OF LEGISLATION. ON THE FOREIGN POLICY FRONT 
SHUMEYKO EXPRESSED DISMAY AT THE RECENT SENATE AMENDMENT 
THAT WOULD LINK CONTINUED PROVISION OF U.S. AID WITH 
TROOP WITHDRAWALS FROM THE BALTICS. THE ACTING SECRETARY 
IMPRESSED UPON SHUMEYKO THE NEED O TAKE BACK TO MOSCOW A 
CLEAR UNDERSTANDING OF HOW IMPORTANT IT WAS TO THE 
PRESIDENT PERSONALLY THAT THE-PARTIES FIND A MUTUALLY 
SATISFACTORY RESOLUTION .TO THE CRISIS BEFORE AUGUST 31. 
THE ACTING SECRETARY ENDED THE DISCUSSION BY HIGHLIGHTING 
THE UPCOMING VISIT OF SENATOR LEAHY TO RUSSIA AND 
EXPRESSING THE HOPE THAT BOTH SHUMEYKO AND PRESIDENT 
YELTSIN WOULD RECEIVE THE SENATOR. END SUMMARY. 
3. ON THE MORNING OF JULY 21, ACTING SECRETARY OF STATE 
STROBE TALBOTT MET AT THE DEPARTMENT WITH RUSSIAN 
FEDERATION COUNCIL CHAIRMAN VLADIMIR SHUMEYKO. THE 
ACTING SECRETARY BEGAN THE DISCUSSIONS BY NOTING THE 
SYMBOLIC IMPORTANCE OF SHUMEYKO'S VISIT TO WASHINGTON. 
THE FUNDAMENTAL TENET OF THE ADMINISTRATION'S 
RELATIONSHIP WITH RUSSIA, THE ACTING SECRETARY CONTINUED, 
IS U.S. SUPPORT FOR THE DEVELOPMENT OF THE DEMOCRATIC 
PROCESS IN RUSSIA; NO MORE IMPORTANT ASFECT OF OUR 
COOPERATION EXISTS THAN THE DEVELOPMENT OF PARLIAMENTARY 
POLITICS. 
4. SHUMEYKO, ASKED HOW THINGS WERE GOING IN THE 
PARLIAMENT, LAUNCHED INTO AN OVERVIEW OF EVENTS SINCE 
1990. HE CALLED THE PERIOD FROM JUNE 1990 TO DECEMBER 
1993 A "ROMANTIC" STAGE IN WHICH THERE WAS A FIGHT TO 
CREATE AN INDEPENDENT STATE THAT ENDED IN A "SHARP CLASH" 
BETWEEN THE EXECUTIVE AND LEGISLATIVE BRANCHES. ON

DECEMBER 12, 1993, RUSSIA ENTERED A SECOND STAGE BASED 
UPON THE CONSTITUTION. SHUMEYKO NOTED THE GROWING POWER 
OF LOCAL AUTHORITIES IN RUSSIA AND THEIR PROPENSITY TO 
ACT INDEPENDENTLY FROM MOSCOW. AMONG THE ELECTED MEMBERS 
OF THE FEDERATION COUNCIL, SOME 70 PERCENT SERVED 
CONCURRENTLY AS LOCAL AUTHORITIES. THE FEDERATION 
COUNCIL THUS STOOD IN SHARP CONTRAST TO THE DUMA. WHILE 
THERE WERE NO REAL PARTIES IN RUSSIA AND PARTIES THUS HAD 
LITTLE INFLUENCE IN THE COUNTRY, THERE WERE PARTIES AND 
PARTY POLITICS IN THE DUMA. SHUMEYKO CONTINUED THAT 
WHILE HE WORKED CLOSELY WITH RYBKIN AND WAS "TRULY 
TRYING" TO DO EVERYTHING TO AVOID CONFLICT, THE DUMA BY 
ITS NATURE WAS MOTIVATED BY PERSONAL AND PARTY 
INTERESTS. THE FEDERATION COUNCIL, ON THE OTHER HAND, 
WAS CONCERNED WITH "THE WELFARE OF THE STATE." 
5. SHUMEYKO EMPHASIZED THAT THE REGIONS SHOULD HAVE 
THEIR OWN CHARTERS AND LEGISLATION BUT THAT REGIONA 
LEGISLATION MUST BE COORDINATED NATIONALLY SO THAT THE 
INTERESTS OF ONE REGION DO NOT HARM THE INTERESTS OF 
OTHERS. THE ROLE OF THE FEDERATION COUNCIL-WAS-TO-HELP- - 
COORDINATE THE INTERESTS OF ALL REGIONS. SHUMEYKO NOTED 
THAT JUST BEFORE HE CAME TO THE UNITED STATES, A 
TRIPARTITE AGREEMENT AMONG THE DUMA, THE FEDERATION 
COUNCIL, AND EIGHT TERRITORIAL ECONOMIC ASSOCIATIONS HAD 
BEEN SIGNED. THE AGREEMENT ALLOWS FOR THE INTERESTS OF 
THE REGIONS TO BE TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT IN THE-DEVELOPMENT 
OF NATIONAL LEGISLATION DEALING WITH BOTH DOMESTIC AND - 
FOREIGN POLICY ISSUES.

SMALL BUSINESSES WHICH INVEST IN RUSSIA.
