Following the tremendous political developments in Europe, and especially in Germany, the 
American-German relationship is facing a change. Altered political and economic relationships 
present tasks and challenges to a partnership that for decades has been a guarantee of success for a 
peaceful living together of the western European states and North America. 
But while opinion polls again and again show a high degree of liking for each other, those who 
know American-German relations well note a change in the content and intensity of the dialogue 
between the two countries. Not only the intimacy of the elite of politics and society, but also the 
interest of the people with the situation in each other's country is orthe wane. The orientation of 
the people is growing apart. 
This attitude involves the risk of misunderstandings or wrong interpretations of social conditions 
that, long-term, could lead to a strain on relations between the United States and Germany. 
For us, as an internationally active media company that is just as much at home in the United States 
as in Germany, it seems to be appropriate to make a contribution to better understanding and 
convey to journalists, independent of their daily work, more insight into the background of the 
course of political events and way of thinking in each country. 
For this purpose, at the invitation of the Bertelsmann Foundation, foreign correspondents have 
come together with decision-makers in politics, business and society in two-day workshops four 
times since 1988. These meetings did not have the goal of immediate publication, but rather were 
intended as a platform for open discussion. 
As in the last two years, we have secured the services of Professor Dr. Werner Weidenfeld, co-
ordinator for German-American co-operation in the Foreign Ministry and foreign policy adviser to 
the German Chancellor, to act as moderator for the fifth workshop.
