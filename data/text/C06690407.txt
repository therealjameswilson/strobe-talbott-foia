THE DEPUTY SECRETARY OF STATE 
WASHINGTON 
June 28, 1995 
RELEASE IN FULL 
Dear Louis: 
I'appreciated your call on Monday. You and the Bureau are 
doing a tremendous job in support of the President's policy in 
Haiti. For that reason, it is important that you have a clear 
idea of what we have done to meet the very legitimate concerns 
-of-your—team—i-n—Poit--au Prince -assisting in the—Bertin murder 
investigation. Some of them, I hope, have now been resolved; 
the others should be resolved in the future. 
Our Embassy remains in constant contact with your field 
agents in Haiti. There appears now to be excellent 
communication between your people on the ground and the Embassy 
in Port au Prince. If you believe there are any specific 
issues on which we are not communicating, please let me know. 
Ambassador Swing and Paul Mallett met with Justice Minister 
Exume last week and resolved some of the FBI's concerns, 
including access to interview government officials. As you 
know, the Ambassador and FBI reps Bill Perry and Paul Mallett 
met again on Tuesday with President Aristide to deliver the 
message and talking points that we worked out with your staff. 
Our preliminary reading is that this meeting was less than 
conclusive and there are still some unresolved issues. 
I know that the Bertin investigation has been a drain on 
the Bureau's resources and understand the Bureau's desire to 
conclude the case as quickly as possible. At the same time, 
the White House asked the Bureau to undertake this 
investigation precisely because of its importance to pur 
overall Haiti policy. 
The Honorable 
Louis J. Freeh, 
Director, 
Federal Bureau of Investigation.

Dealing,with the Government of Haiti on the investigation 
has been difficult. We will continue,to work on Aristide to 
resolve the FBI's outstanding concerns. Any steps that we take 
in seeking a solution to these issues will be done in full 
coordination with you and your staff. I will be in contact 
with you soon to let you know of the progress of our efforts. 
We appreciate all the work that the FBI has done on this 
case. It is important enough that we have several times gone 
to President Aristide to keep it on track, and we will continue 
to do so whenever necessary. It is too important to withdraw. 
An FBI pullback would be counter-productive to both the FBI's 
and State's objectives. Suspending the Bertin investigation 
would simply play into the hands of those who are most 
interested in assuring that the FBI does not identify the 
perpetrators of this murder. 
Louis, if the message has somehow gotten to your people 
that the State Department is not fully supportive of their work 
in Haiti, I hope you will correct that impression immediately. 
They are doing a terrific job, and we will do everything we can 
to support them. 
Sincerely, 
_Strobe Talbott
