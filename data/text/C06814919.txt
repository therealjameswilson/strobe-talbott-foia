SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY'S MEETING WITH ESTONIAN 
PARLIAMENTARY SPEAKER 
Classified by EUR DAS Ronald Asmus for reasons para. 1.5 
(b) and (d) . 
1. Summary: Deputy Secretary Talbott met with 
Parliamentary Speaker Toomas Savi Eebruary 19. Savi 
explained Estonia's desire to join NATO, its commitment to 
increasing defense spending, and asked about the NATO 
summit. The Deputy Secretary stressed USG interest in a 
summit outcome on the Open Door that went beyond Madrid, 
and said we were committed to including Estonia among any 
aspirants named in the communique. He also provided an 
overview of U.S. ideas for a Membership Action Plan that 
would help aspirant countries prepare for possible 
membership. Savi welcomed such a program. The Deputy 
Secretary said he would travel to Moscow the next day and 
would press Russia to take a more helpful approach to the 
Balts. Also present at the meeting were Estonian 
parliamentarians Eino Tamm and Mart Laar, Amb. Kalev 
Stoicescu, DCM Lauri Lepik, and a parliamentary staffer. 
The U.S. side also included D Staff John Bass and EUP/NB 
Damian Leader (note taker) . End summary. 
2. 1.S The DePuty Secretary began by underscoring the 
Administration's commitment to the Baltics; the Secretary 
had asked him to work to make our Baltic policy successful. 
Speaker Savi thanked the Deputy Secretary for his support 
and reviewed the political situation in Estonia. He said 
that regardless of which parties win the March V elections, 
all the major parties support Estonia 's accession to NATO 
and are committed to increasing defense expenditures from 
the current,1.2 per cent of CDP to 2 per cent of GDP within 
the next two to three years.

3. -+ei- In response to Savi's question about the 
Washington Summit, the Deputy Secretary drew Savi's 
attention to the Membership Action Plan (MAP) as a key to 
helping aspirants prepare for possible membership. He 
described the MAP as concrete initiatives that will help 
flesh out our Open Door commitment. The MAP will include 
actual projects which will create a testing ground for 
interoperability and membership preparations, he said. The 
U.S. is proposing specific ideas that will build on our 
Kievenaar plan suggestions and other bilateral help with 
the explicit goal of accelerating the preparation process 
for NATO aspirants. The MAP is still a work in progress, 
but a key goal is to improve the feedback process so 
aspirants will know specifically how their preparations are 
faring. The Deputy Secretary said we are thinking of other 
initiatives as well and that he would be discussing them in 
Brussels in the coming week. He promised that the USG

4. N, Speaker Savi welcomed the Deputy Secretaryls 
commitment to a Summit outcome that went far beyond Madrid, 
and asked about whether and how aspirants would be named in 
the communique. The Deputy Secretary indicated he could 
not give a precise answer because other governments' 
preferences have yet to be reconciled. Drafting is 
difficult because some Allies, he said, are less 
enthusiastic about the Open Door. It would be a disaster, 
however, if the result gave any sense that the Door is not 
open or that there are any red lines blocking enlargement. 
At the same time we want to avoid any nomination or 
predesignation, since that is a political decision we have 
to leave for our leaders, the Deputy Secretary stressed. 
Naming names poses dilemmas, including who must be omitted 
and how to avoid discouraging any of the aspirants. 
Ambassador Stoicescu said Estonia wants to avoid naming 
groups of states. The Deputy Secretary agreed with that 
point and added that he also wants to avoid geographical 
ticket-splitting. 
5. MI Speaker Savi concluded that he strongly supported 
the MAP idea, and he thanked the Deputy secretary for all 
his help. He said that the Baltic Defense College would 
open the next week in Tartu, which will advance Baltic NATO 
aspirations because training and standardization will be' 
key. Steps taken in preparation for EU membership are also 
helpful for NATO membership he said, including 
strengthening the rule of law and market economy. The 
Deputy Secretary closed the meeting by stating that he was 
travelling to Russia the next day and would discuss the 
Baltics there, emphasizing the Open Door and making the 
case that Russia should take a constructive stance toward 
the Balts and welcome their integration into Western 
institutions.
