RUSSIAN DEBT TO INDSPEC CHEMICAL CORP. 
July 19, 1993 
RELEASE IN FULL 
1. The current outstanding principal debt to INDSPEC Chemical Corp. by the 
Russian government is 2,412,650 Dutch guilders ($1,248,137.00 at today's 
exchange rates), plus interest of 1,133,652 Dutch guilders ($586,473.00), for a 
total outstanding debt of 3,546,302 Dutch guilders or $1,834,610.00. 
2. In early 1990, Soyuzchimexport, the chemical importing agency of the Soviet 
trade office, negotiated two purchases of resorcinol, a product manufactured in 
the U.S. by INDSPEC Chemical Corp., an employee-owned company in 
Western Pennsylvania. Resorcinol is a necessary ingredient in tire 
manufacturing. 
Previous purchases from INDSPEC had been made by Soyuzchimexport for 
which payment was made promptly after delivery of the product. 
4. Given the uncertainties in early 1990, INDSPEC asked for reassurance with 
respect to payment and received a telex dated February 16, 1990, from the 
government agency that "resorcinol has priority for payment so you have no 
reason to be worried." Despite that assurance, INDSPEC rcsuestedArther

5. Despite the Russian assertions of "priority" and "agreement," no payment has 
ever been received for this American-produced product. 
6. On October 22, 1990, Frank M. Spinola, President & CEO of INDSPEC, 
informs the U.S. Department of Commerce of the Russians' failure to make 
payment through a letter to John F. Brougher, at the International Trade 
Administration. 
7. On April 29, 1991, Mr. Spinola writes to President Bush, receiving a "form 
letter" response from Alexander Vershbow, Director of Soviet Affairs at the 
U.S. Department of State, referring INDSPEC, once again, to the Commerce 
Department.

8. On July 11, 1991, Mr. Spinola meets with Alexandre Savtchouk, Deputy Trade 
Representative of the USSR in the USA, at the Soviet Embassy in Washington. 
9. On September 5, 1991, V. A. Rudniev, the General Director of 
Soyuzchimexport, affirmed the debt obligation in a letter to V. E. Lisniak, head 
of Account Balance Department, requesting that Mr. Lisniak "take urgent steps 
for providing urgent payment for INDSPEC by the account of means which are 
at the special account in VEB, Zurich, or at the expense of other hard currency 
means." 
10. On September 23, 1991, Mr. Spinola testifies before the Trade Subcommittee of 
the Ways & Means Committee of the U.S. House of Representatives, chaired by 
U.S. Rep. Sam Gibbons, on the failure of the Russian Government to pay its 
commercial debts to U.S. companies. 
11. On September 30, 1991, N. M. Zhurkin, Deputy General Director of 
Soyuzchimexport, in a letter to the same V. E. Lisniak reaffirmed the debt, 
noting that "the goods were delivered to the USSR in full value within the 
period stipulated by the contract" and urging prompt payment of the debt. 
12. On November 7, 1991, Mr. Spinola testifies before the Foreign Commerce 
Subcommittee of the Commerce Committee of the U.S. Senate, chaired by U.S. 
Sen. Jay Rockefeller, on the failure of the Russian Government to pay ,,its 
commercial debts to U.S. companies. 
13. From late 1991 through mid-1992, dozens of congressthen and senators, 
letters to the Russian Ambassador in Washington as well as to appropriate
Adininistration officials urging help for INDSPEC.

17. Throughout the '92 summer, letters from senators and congressmen are 
addressed to Peter Aven, Russian Minister of Foreign Economic Relations, 
urging resolution of the INDSPEC debt issue. 
18. On October 3, 1992, Congress approves the Freedom Support Act with report 
language and provisions calling continued attention to the commercial debt 
issue. 
19. On January 20, 1993, President Clinton takes office, foilowed by the 
appointment of Ron Brown as Secretary of Commerce. 
20. The Department of Commerce, through Lynn Fabrizio, requests another update 
on Russia's outstanding debt to INDSPEC Chemical Corp. and indicates that 
Secretary Brown has raised this issue twice with Deputy Prime Minister 
Shokhin. 
21. As of July 19, 1993, the Russian government has apparently ignored these 
entreaties from the Clinton Administration.
