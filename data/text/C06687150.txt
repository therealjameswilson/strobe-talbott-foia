RUSSIAN REGIONAL PRIVATIZATION FUND 
INTRODUCTION 
RELEASE IN FULL 
1. The Russian Government's successful introduction of mass privatization at 
each level of state owned enterprise (small, medium and large) offers the opportunity for 
Russia to move irreversibly to a market economy. As privatization takes effect it is 
important to focus immediately on the recently privatized enterprises to assist their 
growth. 
2. The two areas of support that newly privatized enterprises require are 
investment funding and technical assistance. The speed of the privatization process and 
the total absence in Russia of a banking environment necessitate an external assistance 
program that can fund investments and technical assistance. Addressing this issue, the 
United States has tabled the concept of a Special Privatization and Restructuring Fund 
for Russia. The Russian Ministry of Privatization (GKI) has also prepared a concept 
paper (entitled Program to Support Russian Privatization) addressing the same issues. 
3. IFC's experience in Russia has been developed through involvement in 
Russia's "bottom-up" approach to privatization. IFC work in the regions has helped in 
privatizing both medium and large companies and small businesses. From this 
experience, IFC believes that technical assistance and,financipgAntArgets medium,as 
well as small, enterprises is both an urgent need andan area yhere_positive short term 
results are feasible. Many medium enterprises produce. needed goods and :_ _—
fundamentally sound business areas. Improvements here can lead to productive growth 
and job creation, and a positive demonstration effect.

BACKGROUND 
Growth-led by Small and Medium Sector. 
6. While the economies of Central and Eastern Europe adopted quite 
different approaches to privatization with varying degrees of success, their common 
experience is that the turnaround of these economies, since market reforms were 
introduced in 1990, has been led by the growth of small and medium size private 
businesses. The change in these economies has come from the fast-growing small and 
medium size privatized companies and new business start ups. This perceived change 
has reinforced popular support for privatization and market reform. In Poland 50% of 
the employment and 40% of exports are now generated by this sector. The turnaround 
of large industrial enterprises has been an on-going painful process and is slow, requiring 
downsizing, unemployment, or close down and is impeded by the continuing political 
ability of large employee groups to continue receiving state. credits for their enterprises. 
Central and Eastern Europe shows clearly that the spee,0;1,of turning _ 
privatized companies is directly linked to the size of the_enterprise. . „-

of Central and Eastern Europe, i.e. that positive post-privatization change occurs fastest 
at the small and medium size level. IFC has established regional privatization programs - 
in several Russian cities and regions. IFC teams are resident in multiple sites and have 
been in place for 15 months. (IFC's work is detailed in Annex 1.). Examples from .the.

1,000 workers. Of the 206 companies announced for sale by IFC, 151 (73%) have less 
than 1,000 employees. Of the 134 medium and large companies sold, 74% have less 
than 1,000 workers. These percentages are also consistent with the total 900 medium

small enterprise group being crucial to achieve a fast growth-led change, they are also 
the dominant group in the first-wave privatizations. Timely provisioning of technical

Investment Climate. 
9. A dominant fact facing newly privatized enterprises is the absence of long 
term capital and the financial institutions for delivering it. Annexes 2 and 3 describe the 
current situation on domestic credit availability for the medium and small size 
enterprises in the regions where IFC is active. Foreign exchange is not available through 
the credit system. 
10. Financing the capital needs of the privatized companies will not be met at 
this time by either the newly emerging Russian private banks or by foreign commercial 
lenders. The conditions for prudent, commercial lending are absent. 
11. The risks faced by lenders because of political, economic, legal and 
business instability equally apply to equity investors. Given the speed at which Russian 
privatization is proceeding, corporate governance and adequate transparency standards 
are yet to be developed. The rights of a shareholder, his ability to enforce those rights, 
his ability to obtain a legitimate share of enterprise income, or the mecha.nisms for - - 
selling stock are not yet in place. The absence of corporate governance on transparency 
severely limits the ability to attract new investment capital; either domestic or foreign.

PROPOSAL 
19. Given the importance of supporting the first wave of Russian privatizations 
but recognizing the non-commercial risk nature of any such investments, it is proposed 
that IFC as a first step set up a model technical assistance and investment fund that 
would incorporate,the principles outlined above, i.e.: 
a. focus initially on one progressive region to be followed by others in due 
course; 
b. provide finance for fundamentally sound medium enterprises (as an equity 
and business partner) and provide working capital credits to small 
enterprises; 
c. fund its investments and the cost of administration in ways that recognize 
in advance the potential for total loss; 
d. provide technical assistance to enterprises in devising business plans and 
appropriate standards of accounting and disclosure; and 
e. act as a model or pilot scheme with the intention that IFC would 
subsequently set up (provided donor funds are available), or would assist

28. Examples of decisions in which the Fund would seek a right of veto are: 
Control of investment expenditure - asset expenditure in excess of specified 
amounts; 
b. Control of new debt - new credits whether short or medium term over a 
specified amount; 
c. Control of new equity - capital increases or insider trading of equity; 
d. Approval of budgets - operating budgets, accounting methods, location of 
bank accounts and auditors; and 
e. Conflict of interest - transactions involving conflict of interest or non-arms 
length dealing. 
The listed measures are in the domain of special shareholder rights and 
would be exercised as necessary and not as a matter of course. They do though set 
broad control parameters (on spending, borrowing, and internal budgeting), within which 
management would operate.

TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE 
39. The Fund would focus on three areas of technical service.activity: 
a. direct assistance to medium size enterprises where the Fund has equity 
investments and which need the Fund to participate as a business partner; 
b. direct assistance to privatized enterprises (medium or large) where there is 
no investment and that have demonstrated a willingness to restructure; and 
c. the production of generic technical assistance products for enterprises and 
funds in other regions. 
40. The Fund would, when the need arose, act as a business partner for those 
companies in which it had an equity investment. The Fund would draw on consultancy 
resources to undertake this role. The intention of this technical assistance activity would 
be to allow the fund to switch from being a passive to an active investor. Since 
investment selection criteria will target fundamentally sound medium size businesses, not 
all companies will need assistance. Given though the difficulties all Russian companies 
face in restructuring, the Fund needs the capability of fulfilling a business partner role. 
The type of technical activity undertaken will range from short term (i.e. diagnostic work 
or strategic planning) to intensive (i.e. setting up financial or management information 
systems).

44. The Fund would have a two-tier management structure comprising a Board 
made up of IFC senior management and possible donor representation. Operational 
management would be headed by individuals seconded from IFC. A compensation 
structure providing incentives for successful Fund performance would be established to 
cover staff directly hired by the Fund. 
45. The Fund would be physically located in an existing financial institution 
located in Nizhny Novgorod but would be operationally independent. The Nizhny 
Novgorod authorities would assist in identifying the specific institution and facilitate 
discussions concerning space, amenities, utilities, etc.

chief executive; the operational manager for the medium enterprise equity investment

manager for the small enterprise credit department; and a financial controller. The team 
would be fully resident in Nizhny Novgorod. It will be necessary to recruit Russian-
speaking foreigners for manager posts but priority would be given to supplementing these 
with Russian nationals which could become qualified managers. The five person would 
prepare equity investment proposals for approval by the Fund Board, manage the 
technical assistance activities and also serve as the credit committee for small loan

IFC and Privatization in the Russian Federation 
Introduction. 
1. IFC technical assistance to Russia began in late 1991 during the initial 
stages of privatization reform. Working closely with the Russian Ministry of Privatization 
(GKI), IFC designed and implemented specific privatization pilot programs to help 
identify the various impediments to privatization and to set up models that could be 
copied elsewhere in Russia. 
Approach.
2. Based upon its transaction experience, IFC formulated a grass roots 
approach which focused on designing and implementing concrete transactions. In setting 
about its privatization work, the Corporation took the following approach: 
a. transaction driven: the objective was to provide practical working 
examples of privatization, and to illustrate its positive effects. Work 
focussed at the grass roots level: small, medium and large enterprises; 
farms; and local city and oblast GKI's and property funds. 
b. pilot or model systems: IFC adopted a pilot approach in the design and 
implementation of model transactions. The objective was to establish a 
successful process which would then be transferred to other local city and 
oblast authorities and in addition provide legal, regulatory and 
administrative feedback at the national level. The model approach 
involved designing mechanisms that were easily replicable, and could be 
disseminated throughout Russia; 
c. regional focus: the focus of day to day activity was with the regional and 
local officials who were the agents responsible for privatization; 
d. resident teams: experienced staff were used to form resident teams willing 
to live long term in the region where privatization was being undertaken. 
The objective was to devise on the ground solutions and actively create the 
local consensus needed to implement privatization. IFC now employs 66 
staff (approximately 50% foreign and 50% Russian nationals) in 7 working 
offices throughout Russia; 
e. critical areas of the economy: in selecting model or pilot program, IFC 
chose areas critical for the reform process: retail and consumer 
distribution; transport; land; and the voucher mass privatization scheme.

Current Situation and Financing Constraints. 
3. The level of investment in medium and large enterprises in the four oblasts 
where IFC has resident teams has fallen by more than 50% since 1990, threatening the 
ability of the 900 companies being privatized to begin restructuring. There are several 
reasons for the fall in investment levels: 
a. Inflation and highly volatile prices make it exceedingly difficult and risky 
for enterprises to make investment decisions based on future profitability. 
The prices of raw materials and intermediate goods continue to escalate as 
manufacturers begin to factor indirect costs into their pricing mechanisms. 
b. The financial position of most enterprises has worsened, reducing the 
companies' abilities to fund investments through retained earnings. In 
many of the enterprises in Nizhny Novgorod, Volgograd, Tomsk and 
Novosibirsk, supply linkages have been disrupted, resulting in poor capacity 
utilization and a piling up of finished goods inventories. 
c. State investment funding is becoming more limited due to fiscal restraints. 
New shareholders in Nizhny Novgorod and the other three oblasts are not 
in a position to subscribe to additional equity offerings. At the same time, 
a new financial system ha.s not evolved to the point where commercial 
banks are capable of assessing and investing in projects based on expected 
profitability. In fact, the majority of the newly coinmercialized banks are 
concentrated in Moscow and Saint Petersburg with little access to oblasts 
such as Nizhny Novgorod, Volgograd, Tomsk and Novosibirsk.

installments. In many cases, workers collectives or limited liability partnerships have 
lease agreements with the option to buy the enterprise. These leases are honored if they 
were concluded prior to the Russian Law on Privatization.
