1. —tSt DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT BRIEFED NATO PERMREPS 
APRIL 29 ON KOSOVO-RELATED TALKS HELD EARLIER IN THE 
WEEK IN MOSCOW WITH RUSSIAN FM IVANOV, SPECIAL ENVOY'
CHERNOMYRDIN AND PRESIDENTIAL ADMINISTRATION HEAD 
VOLOSHIN, AS WELL AS A BRIEF TELEPHONE CONVERSATION 
WITH PM PRIMAKOV. TALBOTT CONCLUDED THAT THE RUSSIAN 
GOVERNMENT HAD BEGUN TO ADJUST TO THE CLIMATE OF 
INTERNATIONAL UNITY AND RESOLVE DEMONSTRATED SO CLEARLY 
AT THE NATO SUMMIT, AND IN PARTICULAR BY THE KOSOVO 
COMMUNIQUE. RUSSIAN OFFICIALS HAD STUDIED THE SUMMIT 
COMMUNIQUE ON KOSOVO VERY CLOSELY AND WERE CONSIDERING, 
ON A POINT-BY-POINT BASIS, HOW RUSSIA'S VIEWS SHAPED UP 
AGAINST THOSE OF THE ALLIANCE. NO RUSSIAN OFFICIALS 
HAD FULLY ENDORSED NATO'S FIVE CONDITIONS FOR 
MILOSEVIC, BUT ALL SEEMED INTERESTED IN PROBING ALLIED 
INTENTIONS AND IN CONSIDERING WHERE POINTS OF 
COMMONALITY MIGHT BE ESTABLISHED. ALL APPEARED TO TAKE 
SERIOUSLY THE ALLIANCE'S DETERMINATION TO PERSIST ON A 
MILITARY TRACK AND TO PREVAIL ON ITS OWN TERMS. 
2. -(-&)- THAT SAID, TALBOTT'S RUSSIAN INTERLOCUTORS 
CONTINUED TO OBJECT VOCIFEROUSLY TO THE BOMBING 
CAMPAIGN AND TO PREDICT, INVOKING AFGHANISTAN AND 
CHECHNYA, THAT NATO'S CAMPAIGN WOULD LEAD ONLY TO A 
BLOODY QUAGMIRE. MOST IMPORTANTLY, NOTHING IN MOSCOW'S 
WILLINGNESS TO HEAR OUT THE ALLIANCE'S VIEWS INDICATED 
ANY GREATER LIKELIHOOD THAT BELGRADE WOULD ACCEPT 
NATO'S CONDITIONS ON KOSOVO. END SUMMARY. 
DEPUTY SECRETARY'S BRIEFING 
3. (E) DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT THANKED PERMREPS FOR 
THEIR CONTRIBUTIONS TO A HIGHLY SUCCESSFUL SUMMIT, 
WHOSE MESSAGE OF INTERNATIONAL SOLIDARITY AND 
DETERMINATION HAD HAD A SOBERING EFFECT ON RUSSIAN 
OFFICIALS. THE SUMMIT HAD SET THE STAGE FOR TALBOTT'S 
OWN SERIES OF MEETINGS IN MOSCOW; THESE CONSISTED OF A 
TWO-HOUR SESSION WITH FM IVANOV, A TWO-HOUR MEETING 
WITH SPECIAL ENVOY CHERNOMYRDIN (INCLUDING A ONE-ON-ONE 
SEGMENT) AND A 45-MINUTE TALK WITH PRESIDENTIAL 
ADMINISTRATION HEAD VOLOSHIN, AS WELL AS A SHORT 
TELEPHONE CONVERSATION WITH PM PRIMAKOV.

CAUTIONARY REMARKS 
4. —tSt TALBOTT CAUTIONED THAT RUSSIAN LEADERS 
CONTINUED TO VOICE STRONG OPPOSITION TO NATO AIR 
STRIKES. THEY WARNED THAT THE ALLIED CAMPAIGN WOULD 
LEAD ONLY TO A MORASS ON THE ORDER OF MOSCOW'S 
INTERVENTIONS IN AFGHANISTAN AND CHECHNYA, AND THEY 
SKETCHED OUT A SERIES OF POSSIBLE CONSEQUENCES. THEY 
WERE CONCERNED OVER THE POTENTIAL BOOST THE BOMBING HAD 
GIVEN TO THE POLITICAL CAMPAIGNS OF NATIONALISTS AND 
COMMUNISTS RUNNING FOR DUMA SEATS IN THE DECEMBER 
ELECTIONS. THEY REFERRED TO THE POSSIBLE RUIN OF THE 
NATO-RUSSIA RELATIONSHIP AND TO EXTREME STRAINS ON 
THEIR BILATERAL RELATIONS WITH INDIVIDUAL ALLIES. THEY 
ASSERTED THAT PRESSURE WAS INCREASING ON RUSSIA TO COME 
TO SERBIA'S AID. SOME EVOKED THE POSSIBILITY OF COLD 
WAR II OR EVEN WORLD WAR III. 
5. —tS.-Y NOTHING IN THE REMARKS OF ANY OF THE RUSSIAN 
LEADERS, ADDED TALBOTT, SUGGESTED BELGRADE MIGHT BE 
CONSIDERING A SHIFT IN POLICY. ALTHOUGH SMOKE SIGNALS . 
COULD BE DETECTED REGARDING RUSSIA'S ROLE IN THE 
INTERNATIONAL EFFORT TO RESOLVE THE CONFLICT IN KOSOVO, 
THIS IN NO WAY PRESAGED A WHITE FLAG RISING OVER 
BELGRADE'S PRESIDENTIAL PALACE. 
KEY DISCUSSION THEMES IN MOSCOW 
6. —{-89- TALBOTT REPORTED THAT ALL THE RUSSIAN LEADERS, 
AND IN PARTICULAR FM IVANOV, HAD ANALYZED AND ANNOTATED 
THE SUMMIT COMMUNIQUE ON KOSOVO AND WERE THINKING ABOUT 
HOW NATO'S CONDITIONS MIGHT BE REFLECTED IN MOSCOW'S 
DIPLOMATIC EFFORTS WITH BELGRADE. NONE EXPLICITLY 
ACCEPTED THE ALLIANCE DEMANDS ON MILOSEVIC, BUT ALL 
WANTED TO PROBE OUR THINKING IN ORDER TO GAIN A BETTER 
UNDERSTANDING OF WHAT WE HAD IN MIND. THE RUSSIANS 
SOUGHT CLARIFICATION ON FOUR ISSUES: 1) THE 
INTERNATIONAL SECURITY PRESENCE; 2) WITHDRAWAL OF SERB 
FORCES FROM KOSOVO; 3) INTERIM POST-CONFLICT 
ADMINISTRATION OF KOSOVO; AND 4) CONDITIONS FOR A 
SUSPENSION OF NATO BOMBING. 
INTERNATIONAL SECURITY PRESENCE

MEETING WITH FM IVANOV IN OSLO, THE LATTER HAD REFUSED

IMPLACABLY OPPOSED.TO NATO AT THE CORE OF AN ISP, SAID 
TALBOTT, NOR DID RUSSIAN LEADERS SEEM INSISTENT THAT 
ANY ALLY BE EXCLUDED FROM THE FORCE.
