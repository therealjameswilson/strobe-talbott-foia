SUBJECT: DEPSEC MEETING WITH GERMAN NSA STEINER ON 2/4 
CLASSIFIED BY ACTING DCM JOSEPH SALOOM, FOR REASONS 1.9 
(B) AND (D). 
1. German National Security Advisor Michael 
Steiner told the Deputy Secretary February 4 in Bonn 
that the planned February 11 meeting in Washington 
between the Chancellor and President should focus on 
"getting the chemistry right." On Kosovo, Steiner said 
Germany was seeking to share the burden and accepted 
that an American ground role in a Kosovo peace plan 
would not be as large as that in Bosnia. He predicted 
a successful, if ambiguous, conclusion to Rambouillet. 
He called for accelerated implementation planning and 
for close military-civilian coordination. In response 
to the Deputy Secretary's points on the NATO Summit, 
Steiner said Germany had reservations about the 
terrorism initiative, but was less concerned about WMD. 
On naming names, he stated Germany's "strong preference 
for no names." Steiner also said: the introduction of 
the Euro was prope'lling closer EU cooperation on 
security and foreign policy; an invitation to Ankara to 
participate in EU meetings of Defense Ministers could 
help overcome Turkish-EU alienation; and Ivanov 
"complained bitterly" in Bonn about what he called the 
emerging pattern of American unilateralism. End 
Summary. 
2. (U) American participants at the February 4 
meeting in Bonn comprised the Deputy Secretary, 
Ambassador, NSC Senior Director Blinken, EUR DAS Asmus, 
D Executive Assistant Goldberg, D Special Assistant 
Bass, and Embassy notetaker. The German side comprised 
Steiner and Chancellery officials Nikel (Middle and 
Southeast Europe), Finster (military aspects of 
security polity), Krarmen (Asia and Third World), and 
Blomeyer-Bartenstein (North America). 
CLINTON-SCHROEDER MEETING 
3. (44} Steiner told the Deputy Secretary that the 
planned February 11 meeting in Washington between the 
Chancellor and President should be as informal as 
possible and focus on "getting the chemistry right." 
The Deputy Secretary noted that the President would 
most likely wish to discuss financial and security 
issues. Russia, too, would likely come up, given the 
President's profound interest in the subject, which he 
shared with then-Chancellor Kohl.

KOSOVO 
4. On Kosovo, Steiner said the Chancellor had 
been steady from the start in "behaving as a good Ally" 
and assuming part of the burden. The Chancellor 
understood the importance of threatening air strikes, 
while at the sate time believing that ground troops 
would be needed at some point. Securing Bundestag 
approval of a German deployment would be "tricky," 
according to Steiner, but manageable. Timing would be 
key. The Foreign Minister was prepared to do his part. 
In neither dimension, air threat or ground deployment, 
was ESDI a consideration. Steiner emphasized that, in 
the run-up to the NATO Summit, there was no place for 
"divided roles" between the U.S. and Europe. 
5. (-14)- As for the U.S. contribution to ground forces 
for peace implementation in Kosovo, Steiner said 
Germany understood and accepted that the American 
presence would not be as large as in Bosnia. He said 
he was satisfied with ongoing discussions about command 
arrangements. 
6. +e4 Steiner continued that he was less pleased 
with the state of planning for a peace mission in 
Kosovo, including in its personnel aspects. Drawing on 
his experience as the Deputy High Representative in 
Sarajevo right after Dayton, Steiner contended that 
strong civilian-military coordination was essential. 
He called for an acceleration of peace implementation 
planning. 
7. (-C-4— Turning to Rambouillet, Steiner predicted that 
both the Serbs and the UCK would "play games." 
Milosevic would try to sow dissension among the CG 
members. At the end of the day, both sides would agree 
to a plan, but with a "yes, but" attitude. In 
Steiner's view, steady nerves were important at 
Rambouillet. 
8. (-e-) In response, the Deputy Secretary expressed 
appreciation for the constructive role of the new 
Chancellor and Foreign Minister on Kosovo, including 
for their contribption last October before they had 
actually assumed office. One U.S. concern dealt with 
who would decide whether NATO must launch air strikes, 
a role that Solana properly held. While Solana was 
prepared to consult before pulling the trigger, he

10. 4g4 The Deputy Secretary agreed with Steiner that 
Kosovo was not the right vehicle for field-testing 
ESDI. What was important was to get the job done. If 
handled well, Kosovo could have positive implications 
for other issues and could serve as a bridging 
experience to a world where ESDI had meaning. 
NATO SUMMIT 
11. Steiner offered his impression that the 
American and European discussions on defense in the 
next century were wholly separate. In many areas --
e.g., funding, capability and technology -- the two 
discussions were simply not meshing. In response, the 
Deputy Secretary previewed his speech, "The New Europe 
and the New NATO," which he later delivered February 4 
in Bonn. He also briefed Steiner on his meeting, held 
earlier February 4, with MoD State Secretary Stuetzle 
(septel). 
12. --1H34- On the proposed terrorism initiative, Steiner 
said Germany had some reservations -- shared, he 
claimed, by Solana as well. On WMD, Germany had fewer 
concerns. If the WMD language could include terrorism, 
Germany would be supportive. There was, however, no 
final German position on WMD; it would have to await a 
decision by the Chancellor.

ESDI 
14. *e÷ Citing a German saying ("who says A must also 
say B"), Steiner argued that the introduction of the 
Euro held implications for other fields. EU leaders, 
driven by the Euro, felt an impetus to coordinate on 
security and defense matters -- which made the British 
uncomfortable, Steiner added. He summed up the German 
position on ESDI as: 
-- trying to strengthen the European pillar; 
-- seeking to help with burdensharing and assume new 
tasks; 
not alienating NATO; and

15. R.44--- The Deputy Secretary replied that the U.S. 
supported ESDI to the extent it addressed the European 
inadequacy in the area of "preemptive defense." The 
U.S. also supported the St. Malo initiative in that it 
moved ESDI forward and oriented the UK in a positive 
direction. At the same time, he continued, the U.S. 
had both procedural and substantive concerns over St..
Malo. He urged Germany, since it would be in the chair 
for bOth the EU and WEU summits this spring, to play a 
leadership role on ESDI.

16. 1-8+— Steiner laid out his thinking briefly on 
Turkey, saying the EU needed to look for opportunities 
to,address Turkish feelings of alienation. One way was 
to include Ankara in EU meetings of Defense Ministers. 
Solana, according to Steiner, thought the idea could be 
helpful. 
RUSSIA 
17. (C) In his readout on Russian ForMin Ivanov's 
recent visit to Bonn, Steiner said Ivanov "complained 
bitterly" about U.S. behavior. The Russian reportedly 
argued that a pattern of American unilateralism was 
emerging and cited Kosovo, Iraq and the ABM. Ivanov, 
whom Steiner called "friendly" in the Russian political 
spectrum, reportedly said Russia was apprehensive and 
"felt cornered."
