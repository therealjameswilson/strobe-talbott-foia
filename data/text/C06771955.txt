Tcl SUMMARY: IN THE CONTEXT OF DISCUSSION OF THE APRIL 
1999 NATO SUMMIT WITH DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT, DANISH 
DEFENSE MINISTER HANS HAEKKERUP AIRED SOME IDEAS HE HAD ON 
BRINGING NATO ASPIRANTS CLOSER TO THE ALLIANCE. HAEKKERUP 
ALSO ARGUED THAT A UNSC MANDATE WAS NEEDED TO ALLOW DENMARK 
TO PARTICIPATE IN ANY NATO ACTION IN KOSOVO. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY EXPLAINED THE U.S. POSITION THAT UN 
COVER/BLESSING FOR NATO INTERVENTION WAS DESIRABLE BUT NOT 
REQUIRED. END SUMMARY. 
2. (U) DANISH DEFENSE MINISTER HANS HAEKKERUP MET WITH 
DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT ON JUNE 30 IN THE DEPARTMENT. THE 
MINISTER WAS ACCOMPANIED BY, AMONG OTHERS, AMBASSADOR 
TYGESEN AND DEPUTY UNDER SECRETARY OF DEFENSE NIELSEN. EUR 
DAS ASMUS AND D SPECASST BASS SAT IN ON THE U.S. SIDE. 
3. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT OPENED BY EXPRESSING HIS 
GRATITUDE FOR FONMIN HELVEG-PETERSEN'S SUPPORT FOR U.S. 
PARTICIPATION AT THE JUNE CBSS MINISTERIAL. THE MINISTER 
HAD ALSO BEEN VERY UNDERSTANDING WHEN THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
HAD BEEN FORCED TO CANCEL HIS ATTENDANCE AT THE 
MINISTERIAL. HE HAD HEARD THE MEETING HAD GONE VERY WELL 
AND HE WAS IN THE MINISTER'S DEBT. 
APRIL 1999 NATO SUMMIT/FUTURE OF ENLARGEMENT 
4. tet THE DEPUTY SECRETARY THEN SOLICITED DANISH VIEWS 
ON THE APRIL 1999 NATO SUMMIT. HAEKKERUP RESPONDED THAT 
DENMARK WAS IN FAVOR OF OPENING NATO UP (A LONG-STANDING 
POSITION) AND SUPPORTED AN EARLY SECOND ROUND OF 
ENLARGEMENT. DENMARK CONTINUED TO PRESS THE CASE FOR THE 
BALTIC STATES. ALTHOUGH IT FAVORED A 1999 DECISION TO 
ENLARGE AGAIN, HAEKKERUP NOTED THAT SIGNALS FROM OTHER 
ALLIANCE MEMBERS (INCLUDING THE U.S.) INDICATED THEY WERE 
NOT PREPARED TO MAKE THIS DECISION. 
5. +-ei- REMARKING THAT HIS MIND WAS OPEN TO ALL POINTS OF 
VIEW, TALBOTT ASKED TO HEAR ARGUMENTS SUPPORTING AN 
ACCELERATED SECOND ROUND. HAEKKERUP OBSERVED THAT THE 
BALTS WANTED TO GET INTO NATO VERY MUCH AND WERE WORKING 
HARD TO IMPROVE THEIR DEFENSIVE CAPABILITIES. THE BALTS 
WERE MAKING THE NECESSARY DEFENSE PROVISIONS AND WERE ON 
THE RIGHT TRACK. ' HE ALSO CITED THE POSITIVE POLISH 
EXAMPLE. SIMILARLY KEEN ON NATO MEMBERSHIP, POLAND HAD 
PUSHED FORWARD AND HAD MANAGED TO MAKE GREAT STRIDES.

7. --Trt- WITH RESPECT TO THE BALTS' ENTRY, TALBOTT REMINDED 
THE DANES THAT, ALTHOUGH THERE WAS NO QUESTION OF LETTING 
HISTORY OR GEOGRAPHY DICTATE ELIGIBILITY, THERE WAS THE 
RUSSIA FACTOR TO CONSIDER. THIS WAS SIMPLY A FACT THAT 
NEEDED TO BE KEPT IN MIND BY ALL. FORTUNATELY, THE 
ALLIANCE HAD DONE A VERY GOOD JOB IN GETTING THE 
ENLARGEMENT PROCESS STARTED WHILE MANAGING RUSSIAN FEARS 
OVER THE PROCESS.  THE PJC WAS AN EXAMPLE OF AN EFFORT TO 
ENGAGE THE RUSSIANS CONSTRUCTIVELY. AS DELIBERATIONS 
CONTINUED, GETTING RUSSIA OVER THE THRESHOLD OF A SECOND 
ROUND SHOULD REMAIN A CONSIDERATION. THE DEPSEC ALSO NOTED 
THAT 1999 WILL BE A DIFFICULT YEAR FOR WORKING WITH RUSSIA 
ON CFE. THIS FACT WAS PLAYING IN U.S. DELIBERATIONS. 
DANISH IDEAS ON NATO 
8. 4-ei- HAEKKERUP SUGGESTED THAT IF 'THERE WERE NO SECOND 
ROUND DECISION IN 1999, THE ALLIANCE WOULD NEED TO CONSIDER 
WHAT ELSE IT COULD OFFER TO BRING ASPIRANTS CLOSER TO NATO. 
ASKED FOR DANISH-IDEAS, HAEKKERUP RECALLED THE 
(UNSUCCESSFUL) DANISH INITIATIVE TO ESTABLISH PSE'S AT THE 
SUBREGIONAL COMMAND LEVEL. THIS COULD HAVE BEEN VERY 
USEFUL AND WOULD HAVE ENGAGED PARTNERS IN PRACTICAL - 
REGIONAL PLANNING. ANOTHER POSSIBILITY WAS TO SET UP 
MULTILATERAL UNITS WITH PARTNER PARTICIPATION. ONE EXAMPLE 
OF THIS WAS THE NORDIC-BALTIC BRIGADE IN BOSNIA. FURTHER 
DEVELOPMENT OF THIS MODEL COULD BRING ABOUT THE NEEDED 
INCREASE IN PARTNER INTEROPERABILITY WITH NATO. HAEKKERUP 
DESCRIBED THESE AS PRACTICAL STEPS THAT COULD MAKE PARTNERS 
FEEL MORE INCLUDED IN NATO OPERATIONS. THIS OUTREACH COULD 
EVEN INCLUDE RUSSIA IN PREPARATIONS FOR NON-ARTICLE FIVE 
OPERATIONS.

PUT FLESH ON THE BONES OF PRONOUNCEMENTS bN THE "SPIRIT OF 
A NEW NATO." ASMUS ADDED THAT IN THE EARLY DAYS OF PFP, 
NATO HAD REJECTED THE IDEA OF FORMING MULTINATIONAL CORPS 
WITH PARTNERS. BUT DENMARK HAD SIMPLY GONE AHEAD AND

KOSOVO 
11. 'Mt- TURNING TO KOSOVO, HAEKKERUP INFORMED THE DEPSEC 
THAT A UNSC RESOLUTION APPROVING NATO ACTION WAS A 
PRECONDITION FOR INVOLVEMENT FOR MANY EUROPEAN COUNTRIES. 
HE HAD DISCUSSED THIS MATTER WITH SECDEF COHEN IN 
COPENHAGEN. IF NATO INTERVENED IN KOSOVO WITHOUT A UNSC 
MANDATE, DENMARK FEARED OTHER COUNTRIES COULD INTERVENE IN 
OTHER CASES, USING SIMILAR JUSTIFICATION. HAEKKERUP ADDED 
THAT THE UN WAS PERCEIVED DIFFERENTLY IN DENMARK AND THE 
RESTOF EUROPE; THERE IT WAS SEEN AS AN INTERNATIONAL BODY 
WITHITHE NEEDED AUTHORITY TO PROVIDE MANDATES. THE CASE, 
HE KNEW, WAS DIFFERENT IN THE U.S. 
12. ..4-e't IN THE RECENT CASE OF IRAQ, DENMARK HAD BEEN ABLE 
TO OFFER A MODEST RESPONSE TO THE U.S. REQUEST BECAUSE IT 
HAD INTERPRETED OLD UN RESOLUTIONS ON IRAQ AS PROVIDING THE 
NECESSARY AUTHORITY. TO MOVE IN KOSOVO WITHOUT A UN 
MANDATE WOULD 1rCREATE HAVOC'l IN DENMARK. DEPSEC REMARKED 
THAT THERE SHOULD BE A WAY TO FRAME THE ISSUE THAT COULD 
AVOID A GRUELING AND DIVISIVE DEBATE. KOSOVO COULD EVEN BE 
A TEST CASE FOR THE ALLIANCE. 
13. tet THE DEPSEC, NEVERTHELESS, EXPLAINED THAT THE U.S. 
BELIEVED A UN COVER WAS USEFUL AND DESIRABLE BUT IT WAS NOT 
A SINE QUO NON FOR NATO ACTION. HE FURTHER NOTED THAT IF 
KOSOVO SITUATION WERE TO DISINTEGRATE AND THE CONFLICT TO 
SPREADI, NATO WOULD DESERVE BLAME FOR HAVING HELPED BETRAY 
THE PRESERVATION OF PEACE IN EUROPE. AT THE SAME TIME, 
THERE COULD BE A WAY TO BRING THE RUSSIANS ALONG. ONE WAY 
COULD BE THROUGH INVOLVING THEM IN THE MULTILATERAL 
OBSERVER MISSION IN KOSOVO. TALBOTT REVIEWED RECENT U.S. 
DIPLOMACY AND ADDED THAT MILOSEVIC APPEARED INCREASINGLY 
AFRAID OF LOSING CONTROL OF THE SITUATION. THE U.S. WAS 
OPENING UP CONTACTS WITH THE KOSOVO LIBERATION ARMY IN PART 
TO FIND OUT WHO THESE REPS WERE.
