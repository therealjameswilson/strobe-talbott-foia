HERBERT S. OKUN .s? 
Following a diplomatic career in the U.S. Foreign Service from 1955-91, Herbert Okun is 
Executive Director of the Financial Services Volunteer Corps. FSVC, a not-for-profit 
organintion, provides voluntary assistance to help establish free-market financial systems 
in the former communist countries of Eastern. Europe, the former Soviet Union, and 
Asia. He is also the U.S. member of the United Nations International Narcotics Control 
Board and a Visiting Lecturer on International Law and Negotiation at Yale Law 
School. 
From October 1991 - May 1993, Okun was Special Advisor and Deputy to former 
Secretary of State Cyrus Vance, Personal Envoy of the Secretary-General of the United 
Nations and Co-Chairman of the International Conference on the Former Yugoslavia. 
He was also Special Advisor on Nagorno-Karabakh. 
A recognized authority on Russia, Germany, and Eastern Europe, Okun served in 
diplomatic posts in Moscow, Berlin, Munich, Lisbon, Geneva, Naples, Brasilia, and the 
UN. 
From 1985-1989, Okun was Deputy Permanent Representative and Ambassador to the 
United Nations and represented the U.S. on the Security_Council,_ the -Disarmament 
Commission, the Human Rights Commission, and other UN bodies. 
From 1980-83, he was U.S. Ambassador to the former German Democratic Republic. 
Between 1978-80 he was Vice Chairman of the SALT II Delegation and of the U.S. 
Delegation to talks with the Soviet Union and Great Britain on a Comprehensive Test 
Ban. In 1971-72 he was Deputy Chairman of the U.S. Delegation to the US-Soviet talks 
on the Prevention of Incidents at Sea. In 1973-74, he served as Political Advisor to the 
NATO Commander-in-Chief for Southern Europe. 
In the State Department he served as Special Assistant to Secretary of State William P. 
Rogers and as Director of the Office of Soviet Union Affairs. 
Okun was bom in New York City. He was awarded a B.A. with great distinction from ------- - 
Stanford and was elected to Phi Beta Kappa. He served on active duty in the U.S. 
Army. He has a Master's Degree in Public Administration from Harvard. 
The State Department granted Okun its Meritorious Honor Award in 1972 and its 
Superior Honor Award in 1980. He was awarded a Presidential Meritorious Service 
Award in 1983. He speaks French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, and Spanish. 
He is married and has three daughters.
