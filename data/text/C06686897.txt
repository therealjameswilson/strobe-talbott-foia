Thank you for your thoughtful letter of July 31 concerning 
future strategic directions for the European Bank for 
Reconstruction and Development (EBRD). 
I agree with your assessment that the challenges of reform 
in the new independent states (N'IS) of the former Soviet Union 
call for increased emphasis on supporting local institutions 
and grassroots entrepreneurs. I also agree that one of the 
highest priorities in this effort is to help these 
entrepreneurs find access to much-needed capital to build new 
businesses, create jobs, and increase local incomes, thereby 
solidifying the gains made thus far toward multiparty democracy 
and market reform. It is for this reason that the 
Administration's bilateral technical assistance package, 
designed to be felt at the grassroots level, will be targeted 
toward key Russian priorities and focused on areas and 
constituencies in Russia that can have the greatest concrete 
impact on reform's long-term success. The U.S. Government also 
is taking this same approach in the multilateral programs 
within which we participate, including those of the EBRD. 
At the July Summit of the Group of Seven (G-7) countries in 
Tokyo, the EBRD was called upon to establish a Small and Medium 
Enterprise Fund for Russia modeled loosely on the U.S. 
enterprise funds in Eastern Europe. This Fund would address 
the lack of commercial financing in Russia for small and medium 
private enterprises through developing, implementing, and 
operating appropriate mechanisms and instruments for delivery 
of finance to Russian small business. At present, the EBRD is 
initiating a pilot eff-ort in three regions of Russia (Tomsk, 
Tula, and Nizhny Novgorod) focusing on the establishment of 
regional investment companies for small equity investments, 
start-up of small finance companies for micro-credit 
operations, creation of "windows" within local banks for 
lending and guarantee activities, and provision of business 
advisory services and training. The EBRD hopes this pilot 
activity will help to define the demand for small business 
finance at the grassroots level in these three regions, and by 
extrapolation, to other regions of the country, and assess the 
distribution of that demand between alternative forms of 
finance. The U.S. Government plans to support the EBRD Small 
and Medium Enterprise Fund with $30 million in fiscal year 1994.

In addition, at the Tokyo Summit, G-7 countries agreed to 
create a multilateral Special Privatization and Restructuring 
Program (SPRP) to provide post-privatization support to large 
Russian enterprises throughout the Russian Federation. The 
Program will provide three types of support: 1) capital, 
through loans and equity, to help firms modernize obsolescent 
capital stock and restructure operations; 2) financing for 
regions where these enterprises are located, which will have 
the responsibility, at least temporarily, for many of the 
social services currently provided to enterprises (e.g., 
housing, education); and, 3) technical assistance to help 
enterprises develop business plans and financial statements and 
to assist local governments in restructuring their tax systems 
to absorb social services costs. The EBRD again will play a 
critical role in this initiative, particularly with regard to 
establishing equity funds for several regions of Russia. 
Thank you again for bringing your ideas to my attention. 
Please do not hesitate to contact me if you have additional 
questions.
