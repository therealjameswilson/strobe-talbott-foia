1. (U) Classified by Acting EUR Assistant Secretary E. 
Anthony Wayne. Reasons: 1.5 (b) and (d). 
2. fig.1„..Summary: The Secretary informed Stability Pact 
Coordinator Bodo Hombach of USG preferences for a lean Pact 
staff and for a substantive exchange of views at the 
Sarajevo Conference. Hombach raised regional concerns 
about dealing with Serbia and advocated including potential 
projects in Serbia in Stability Pact planning as an added 
enticement to foment change in Belgrade. The Secretary 
urged him to use the Pact to promote cooperation among Serb 
opposition groups and cautioned against steps that might 
decrease our leverage. The Deputy Secretary expressed 
concern that EU reluctance to finance travel costs for 
Stability Pact staff would be contrasted with the 
disproportionate U.S. role in the air campaign and provide 
additional grist for burdensharing debates. Hombach 
replied that he had fought for more EU funding, but had 
been overruled by the EU's General Affairs Council (GAC).

3. (U) The Deputy Secretary met with Stability Coordinator 
Bodo Hombach, his staff (Senior Deputy Don Kursch, Chief of 
Staff Markus Ederer, Advisor Stefano Pontecorvo), and 
German Embassy Charge Volker Schlegel at the Department on 
July 20. The Secretary and Executive Assistant Alex Wolff 
participated in part of the meeting. Ambassador James 
Dobbins, Ambassador Richard Schifter, EUR PDAS E. Anthony 
Wayne, Phil Goldberg (D Staff), and EUR/AGS note taker also 
attended. Hombach later met separately with National 
Security Adviser Sandy Berger and Treasury Deputy Secretary 
Stuart Eizenstat. 
Sarajevo Conference 
4. -M4 The Secretary congratulated Hombach on his new job. 
She advocated a "lean and mean" approach to staffing the 
Stability Pact and stressed the importance of a substantive 
aspect to the Sarajevo Conference. Reiterating points made 
earlier to German FM Fischer, she said that regional states 
should receive an opportunity to put forth their own ideas. 
The Conference should not consist merely of a string of set 
speeches. Hombach replied that he hoped that states would 
table concrete cross-border (infrastructure and energy) 
projects in Sarajevo. Hombach also noted the importance of 
focussing on economic objectives achievable without state 
funding. The Secretary asked Hombach to ensure that 
regional states receive some of these contracts. 
Dealing with Serbia 
5. ?6,11Hombach, who had just completed a tour of the 
region, said that regional leaders "very warmly" welcomed 
the Stability Pact initiative. At each stop, the main 
question was "how do we deal with Serbia?" The Secretary 
replied that we must keep our overall objectives in mind, 
i.e., a degree of stability in the Balkans that will allow 
regional states to become members of European institutions. 
This goal will not be possible until Serbia undergoes a 
democratic transition.

6.-1.6+ The Secretary emphasized the importance of supplying 
humanitarian - but not economic - assistance to Serbia at 
this time. Hombach suggested that planning.for future 
projects in Serbia be fully included in Stability Pact 
wbrk, but with the caveat that the plans will only be 
implemented after Milosevic is gone. Serbia's inclusion in 
these plans would be a clear sign for the opposition. Such 
thinking led to the GAC's proposal to permit limited 
economic aid to specific opposition mayors in Serbia. The 
Secretary said that the USG would examine the proposal. 
However, she noted that the inclusion of Serbia was a big

should not give away our leverage. We should use it now to 
encourage the opposition to work together. 
Stability Pact and Islamic States 
7. C64,,Noting that he will travel to Ankara and the UAE 
later in the month, Hombach said he hoped that the 
Stability Pact would keep contributions by Islamic states 
from reaching "the wrong causes." The Secretary agreed 
that the Pact could be used to reach out to moderates in 
the Islamic world. 
Costs of Stability Pact Staff 
8. -(-C-)- After the Secretary's departure, the Deputy 
Secretary and Hombach discussed practical details of the 
Pact. Hombach mentioned a GAC decision that all states 
must cover the costs (including travel) of their personnel 
assigned to the Pact. He had fought the decision and noted 
its detrimental effects on the regional states. The Deputy 
Secretary stated that the USG had provided Hombach with 
staff on the understanding that the EU would cover travel 
costs. The U.S. had borne substantial, disproportionate 
up-front costs during the bombing campaign. It was only 
equitable that the economic burden of the Stability Pact 
should be on Europe's shoulders; to do otherwise would 
perpetuate ongoing burdensharing debates. Ambassador 
Dobbins acknowledged that other governments were also being 
asked to finance the travel costs of their personnel, so 
the issue was not specifically targeted at the USG. PDAS 
Wayne raised the practical problem of having travel 
dependent on member-state budgets.

attitude. 
Full Membership for Ukraine and Moldova 
10>("6,), The Deputy Secretary supported the request by 
Ukraine and Moldova for full membership in the Stability 
Pact. Upgrading them,from observer status would send a 
clear signal that both states are part of Europe. Hombach 
replied that states in the region were \concerned that 
Ukraine and Moldova would be recipients instead of donors. 
He contrasted Ukraine and Moldova with Switzerland, whose 
bid for full membership had unanimous support. He had 
promised the Swiss that they would. be nominated for full 
membership at the first meeting of the regional table. The 
Deputy Secretary cautioned that admitting Switzerland 
without Ukraine and Moldova would "make the problem even 
worse." Hombach took the USG's request on board, said he 
will ask the Ukrainian Foreign Minister for a meeting to 
discuss the issue, and agreed to consider opening talks 
with Ukraine and Moldova (perhaps integrating them first 
through the working tables) . Hombach cautioned that there 
will be no EU unanimity on admitting Ukraine and Moldova 
now.
