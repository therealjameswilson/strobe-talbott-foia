SHARIF VISIT: CREATING A CONDUCIVE ATMOSPHERE 
2. —f-199- AHMAD OPENED BY STATING HE THOUGHT THE POTUS-SHARIF 
TALKS HAD OCCURRED IN A GOOD ATMOSPHERE. CONCRETE RESULTS 
WOULD COME GRADUALLY AND INCREMENTALLY. ENSURING A 
POSITIVE ATMOSPHERE ALLOWS POSITIVE OUTCOMES TO FOLLOW. 
AHMAD DESCRIBED THE PRIME MINISTER AS HAPPY ABOUT THE 
TALKS. THERE MIGHT BE A GAP IN OUR POSITIONS, HE ADDED, 
BUT TALKS WILL CONTINUE TO FILL THIS GAP. AHMAD ASSERTED 
THESE GAPS WERE A PRODUCT OF USG PERCEPTIONS. BUT MUCH 
PROGRESS HAD BEEN MADE AND HE LOOKED FORWARD TO CONTINUING 
BILATERAL DISCUSSIONS WITH TALBOTT IN FEBRUARY. 
3. (C) TALBOTT CONCURRED THAT THE TWO LEADERS SHARED A 
PERSONAL RAPPORT. THIS UNDERLINES THE IMPORTANCE AND 
PROMISE IN OUR BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP. BUT OUR 
DIFFICULTIES, TALBOTT POINTED OUT, WENT BEYOND 
PERSONALITIES AND INVOLVED CONCRETE DIFFERENCES, NOT JUST 
PERCEPTUAL.GAPS. THE USG HAD ACKNOWLEDGED FORWARD MOVEMENT 
IN PAKISTAN'S POSITION, AND HAD SO RESPONDED. WE HAD SHOWN 
OUR DESIRE TO HELP PAKISTAN IN ITS DIFFICULT FINANCIAL 
SITUATION BY' OUR POSITION ON THE IMF PACKAGE. WHILE WE 
WOULD LIKE TO DO MORE--BOTH WITH INTERI4ATIONAL FINANCIAL 
INSTITUTION LENDING AND IN ADDRESSING PAKISTAN'S DEFENSE 
NEEDS--WE ARE HAMSTRUNG BY LACK OF FURTHER PROGRESS. WE 
ARE ALREADY LEANING "FAR FORWARD."
