TO: Those interested in reform of the

FROM: Richard Hudson 
RE: Change of game plan 
The original draft of the "U.N. Charter Review 
Resolution" of the Center for War/Peace Studies 
provided that, in its single operative paragraph, the 
General Assembly: 
CALLS UPON the Secretary-General to appoint an 
independent commission to study the question of whether 
various amendments to the U.N. Charter would improve 
the functioning of the Organization, and to present its 
recommendations to the 49th session of the General 
Assembly. 
This version had the support of Ambassador Richard 
Butler of Australia, Ambassador Nabil Elaraby of Egypt, 
Ambassador Francis Hayes of Ireland, Ambassador Manuel 
Tello of Mexico, and Ambassador Narcisa Escaler of the 
Philippines. We were operating on the assumption that 
while the Security Council wculd very likely be expand-
ed in numbers, reform of the U.N. Charter would be 
limited to this one matter. That was clearly what the 
five permanent members wanted. 
The issue of whether to give a broad or a narrow 
mandate to the Working Group being e'stablished to 
consider expansion of the Security Council came up in 
an intense series of informal meetings held over a 
period of two months in the Mission of Singapore to the 
U.N. The upshot of these meetings .was a resolution 
adopted by the General Assembly on December 3, 1993, 
establishing "an open-ended Working Group to consider 
all aspects of the question of equitable representation 
on and increase in the membership of the Security 
Council, and other matters related to the Council." I 
attach a press release that sumMarizes the action.
