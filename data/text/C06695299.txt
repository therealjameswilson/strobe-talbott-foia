1. -(64- SUMMARY. ON APRIL 9, DEPUTY SECRETARY'TALBOTT MET 
WITH NEWLY-APPOINTED PAKISTANI FOREIGN SECRETARY NAJMUDDIN-
SHAIKH AND A TEAM OF SENIOR FOREIGN MINISTRY OFFICIALS. THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY EXPLAINED THE BACKGROUND OF THE U.S. 
NONPROLIFERATION INITIATIVE NOTING THAT DIFFERENCES BETWEEN THE 
TWO COUNTRIES WERE NOT MAJOR. HE URGED THE GOP TO CONSIDER THE 
U.S. PROPOSAL SERIOUSLY AND TO AGREE TO QUIET DIPLOMATIC 
DISCUSSIONS. THE FOREIGN SECRETARY NOTED WITH SATISFCATION 
THAT THE U.S. WAS NOT CALLING FOR A "ROLLBACK," BUT MADE CLEAR 
PAKISTAN STILL CONSIDERED THE PRESSLER AMENDMENT DISCRIMINATORY 
AND URGED THE U.S. TO CONSIDER ELIMINATING IT. HE ALSO ARGUED 
THAT PAKISTAN HAD EXERCISED SIGNIFICANT RESTRAINT ON TWO OF THE 
THREE NUCLEAR "REDLINES." NAJMUDDIN EXPLAINED•THE POLITICAL 
DIFFICULTIES FOR ANY PAKISTANI GOVERNMENT TO ACCEPT PUBLICLY 
THE U.S. PROPOSAL. HE WAS CAREFUL, HOWEVER,• NOT TO REJECT THE 
OFFER BUT URGED THE U.S. INSTEAD TO CONSIDER CANCELLING 
PAKISTAN'S USD 2.8 BILLION DEBT AND TO ATTRIBUTE THE CURRENT 
ANNUAL INTEREST PAYMENTS OF USD 155 MILLION TO THE PURCHASE OF 
ALL REMAINING F-16S. 
2. -tel. THE FOREIGN SECRETARY URGED THAT KASHMIR BE ADDED AS 
AN AGENDA ITEM TO THE U.S.-PROPOSED MULTILATERAL REGIONAL 
SECURITY TALKS,AND THAT THE U.S. MEDIATE THE KASHMIR DISPUTE. 
CITING INDIA'S REACTION TO THE PROPOSED MULTILATERAL SECURITY 
TALKS, TALBOTT SUGGESTED IT WOULD BE EASIER TO OBTAIN AGREEMENT 
ON THE REGIONAL SECURITY TALKS WITHOUT HIGHLIGHTING KASHMIR. 
HE NOTED THE•U.S. POSITION THAT KASHMIR WAS A BILATERAL DISPUTE 
BETWEEN PAKISTAN AND INDIA, BUT ADDED THE U.S. WAS WILLING TO 
BE OF ASSISTANCE IF BOTH PARTIES DESIRED. FINALLY, BOTH SIDES 
SUGGESTED DRAFTS OF A JOINT STATEMENT AS A WAY OF MAKING PUBLIC 
THE PROGRESS THE VISIT HAD MADE. LATE THAT EVENING, A JOINT 
STATEMENT WAS AGREED AND SUBSEQUENTLY ISSUED AT THE CONCLUSION 
OF THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S VISIT (REFTEL). END SUMMARY.
