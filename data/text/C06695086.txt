2. SUMMARY: IN A JULY 24 MEETING WITH DEPUTY SECRETARY 
TALBOTT, INDONESIAN FOREIGN MINISTER ALATAS SOUGHT TO 
ALLAY USG CONCERNS ABOUT THE EAEC, SAYING IT WOULD NOT BE 
A "MENACING" FORUM. TALBOTT SAID APEC WAS A BETTER 
VEHICLE FOR PROMOTING OPEN REGIONALISM AND SAID THE 
PRESIDENT LOOKED FORWARD TO THE NOVEMBER APEC MEETING IN 
INDONESIA, WHICH WE HOPED WOULD NOT BE MARRED BY HUMAN 
RIGHTS PROBLEMS. ALATAS DEFENSIVELY CLAIMED THAT 
PROVOCkTIONS WERE TO BLAME FOR A RECENT INCIDENT OF 
ALLEGED CATHOLIC HARASSMENT, BUT SA/D THREE MAGAZINES 
RECENTLY CLOSED BY THE INDONESIAN GOVERNMENT WOULD BE 
ALLOWED TO RESUME PUBLICATION. END SUMMARY. 
3. APEC 
DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT NOTED THAT THE PRESIDENT SAW 
APEC AS A CORNERSTONE OF HIS FOREIGN POLICY AND THAT HIS 
PARTICIPATION IN THE UPCOMING NOVEMBER MEETING WAS A 
FOREIGN POLICY HIGHLIGHT FOR THIS YEAR. ALATAS RESPONDED 
THAT PREPARATIONS FOR APEC WERE GOING QUITE WELL, THAT 
FORMAL INVITATIONS WERE IN THE PROCESS OF BEING SENT AND 
THAT THE BLAKE ISLAND FORMULA FOR INVITING TAIWAN AND 
HONG KONG TO THE LEADERS MEETING WOULD BE FOLLOWED, WITH 
A SPECIAL ENVOY DELIVERING THIS MESSAGE. 
4. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT NOTED THAT THE U.S. BELIEVES 
APEC, NOT EAEC, PROVIDES THE BEST FORUM FOR PROMOTING 
OPEN REGIONALISM. ALATAS RESPONDED THAT ASEAN NOW FULLY 
EMBRACED EAEC AS A LOOSE CAUCUS THAT WOULD NOT DETRACT 
FROM APEC. HE HOPED THE USG COULD ACCEPT THE GROUPING. 
EAEC WOULD TAKE THE FORM OF AN OCCASIONAL MEETING TO TALK 
ABOUT ECONOMIC ISSUES. EAEC WOULD /NVOLVE ECONOMIC 
MINISTERS, NOT FOREIGN MINISTERS OR POLITICAL ISSUES. 
THE PRESENCE OF JAPAN AND KOREA, AS WELL AS CHINA, WOULD 
ENSURE THAT EAEC WOULD FIND IT HARD TO ACHIEVE CONSENSUS 
ON MANY ISSUES. IN SUM, EAEC WOULD NOT BE A MENACING 
FORUM AND'PERHAPS IT WOULD MAKE MALAYSIA LESS WARY OF 
PARTICIPATING IN APEC. TALBOTT NOTED THAT, STARTING WITH 
A/S LORD'S CONFIRMATION STATEMENT, THE CLINTON 
ADMINISTRATION INDICATED THAT IT WAS OPEN-MINDED ON EAEC.

5. HUMAN RIGHTS 
DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT NOTED THAT AS INDONESIA PREPARED 
FOR NOVEMBER APEC MEETINGS THE ATMOSPHERE COULD BE 
ADVERSELY AFFECTED BY CONTINUED HUMAN RIGHTS PROBLEMS 
SUCH AS EAST TIMOR AND THE BANNING OF THREE INDONESIAN 
MAGAZINES. ALATAS EXPRESSING "EXASPERATION" ASSERTED 
THAT LOCAL HUMAN RIGHTS GROUPS MADE A PRACTICE OF 
EXAGGERATING SMALL INCIDENTS TIMED TO EMBARRASS THE 
INDONESIAN GOVERNMENT. HE EXPECTED THIS TO INTENSIFY IN 
THE RUN-UP TO NOVEMBER BUT MUSED THAT INDONESIA SHOULD 
NOT RESPOND TO PROVOCATION. ALATAS MAINTAINED THAT 
INDONESIA HAD A STRONG RECORD OF RELIGIOUS TOLERANCE AND 
THAT STORIES OF RECENT INCIDENTS INVOLVING ALLEGED' 
HARASSMENT OF ROMAN CATHOLICS WERE EITHER OVERBLOWN OR 
HAD BEEN RETRACTED BY PARTICIPANTS.
