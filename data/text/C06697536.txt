--(7511- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY WAS AWARE THAT GENERAL JEAN - 
DAIGLE, THE CANADIAN COMMANDER IN HAITI, HAD TOLD THE

SHALIKASHVILI HAD HEARD FROM HIS CANADIAN COUNTERPART

-44 DESPITE THE APPARENT DIFFERENCE IN CANADIAN VIEWS, 
THE DELAY IN THE RUSSIAN CHOPPERS MEANT THE UNMIH WOULD 
HAVE TO GO APPROXIMATELY 10 DAYS WITHOUT HEAVY-LIFT 
HELICOPTER SUPPORT. OF PARTICULAR CONCERN TO THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY WAS THE POSSIBLE PERCEPTION ON THE CANADIAN 
SIDE THAT THE GAP IN HELICOPTER SUPPORT MIGHT IMPLY THE 
U.S. HAD ABANDONED THE CANADIANS IN HAITI. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY WISHED TO DISPEL THIS VIEW. 
—FS* DEPUTY FM SMITH HAD HEARD OF NO ANXIETY WITHIN THE 
MINISTRY OF DEFENSE, AND NO ONE HAS SUGGESTED THE U.S. 
FAILED TO-MEET ITS OBLIGATIONS. MR. SMITH WOULD DISCUSS 
THE MATTER WITH CHIEF OF DEFENSE STAFF JEAN BOYLE, AND 
WOULD ADVISE THE DEPUTY SECRETARY IMMEDIATELY OF ANY 
CONTRARY INFORMATION. 
-444_ TURNING TO THE SUBJECT OF BURUNDI, MR. SMITH 
ADVISED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY HE HAD SEEN LITTLE 
WILLINGNESS ON THE PART OF THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY, 
PARTICULARLY THE E.U., TO BECOME INVOLVED IN BURUNDI'S 
IMMINENT CRISIS. HE ALSO SUSPECTED THE COUNTRY WOULD 
SOON SLIDE INTO TURMOIL IN THE MANNER OF HER NEIGHBOR 
RWANDA. HE REFERRED TO THE VIEW OF CANADIAN FM AXWORTHY 
THAT, IN THE CASE OF RWANDA, THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY 
DID NOT REACT UNTIL TELEVISION CAMERAS DEPICTED THE 
HORRORS OF GENOCIDE. MR. SMITH THEN QUESTIONED THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY OVER THE POSSIBILITY OF U.S. MILITARY 
INVOLVEMENT IN BURUNDI, ADDING THAT THE SITUATION IN 
THAT COUNTRY COULD WELL TURN INTO THE NEXT MAJOR' 
INTERNATIONAL DISASTER. 
—ES* THE'DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID THE U.S. ASSESSMENT OF 
BURUNDI WAS ALSO BLEAK. THE U.S. HAD DISPATCHED 
OFFICIALS TO THE REGION AS WELL AS TO THE UNITED 
NATIONS, AND NATIONAL SECURITY ADVISER ANTHONY LAKE HAD 
TAKEN A SPECIAL INTEREST IN DEVELOPMENTS THERE. THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY SUGGESTED THE U.S AND CANADA MAINTAIN 
AN OPEN CHANNEL ON BURUNDI BETWEEN THEIR RESPECTIVE 
FOREIGN MINISTRIES. HE VIEWED THE POSSIBILITY OF U.S. 
MILITARY INVOLVEMENT IN BURUNDI AS INCONCEIVABLE, SINCE 
U.S. INTERESTS IN THE REGION COULD NOT JUSTIFY SUCH 
ACTION. 
TURNING TO RUSSIA, MR. SMITH SAID HE HAD RECENTLY 
ENTERTAINED A RUSSIAN DELEGATION WHICH HE TERMED AS 
PUSHY, AND EXPECTED WASHINGTON WOULD SOON RECEIVE THE 
SAME. IN HIS VIEW RUSSIA WOULD NOT BE SATISFIED WITH 
ENHANCED P-8 PARTICIPATION. HE ADDED THAT RUSSIA HAD 
COOPERATED WITH CANADA OVER MATTERS OF UN REFORM, CRIME 
AND TERRORISM, BUT THAT THESE WORKS SHOULD NOT.BE USED 
AS A BACK DOOR TO PARTICIPATE IN G-7. ON THIS MATTER HE 
WOULD GO ON THE RECORD AS BEING IN FULL ACCORD WITH THE 
U.S. 'POSITION.
