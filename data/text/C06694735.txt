2. SUMMARY. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT AND DEPUTY 
NATIONAL SECURITY ADVISOR SANDY BERGER MET WITH A RANGE 
OF PARLIAMENTARIANS AND POLITICAL LEADERS DECEMBER 6 TO 
DISCUSS LEGISLATIVE ELECTIONS IN HAITI. AMBASSADOR 
SWING, AMBASSADOR DOBBINS, MR. BEERS AND MR. PLEBANI ALSO 
ATTENDED. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY STRESSED, AND MR. BERGER 
CONCURRED, THAT ELECTIONS MUST BE HELD AS SOON AS 
POSSIBLE, AND THAT STEPS NECESSARY TO THAT END, INCLUDING 
FORMATION OF AN ELECTORAL COUNCIL, AND SETTING A DATE, 
MUST OCCUR IN THE IMMEDIATE FUTURE. THE PARLIAMENTARIANS 
WERE RECEPTIVE, INFORMING THE VISITORS THAT THEY HAD MET 
THE DAY BEFORE WITH PRESIDENT ARISTIDE ON THE ISSUE AND 
EXPECTED TO SEE HIM AGAIN THAT AFTERNOON. SEVERAL, 
HOWEVER, WARNED OF GRAVE CONCERNS ABOUT SECURITY FOR THE 
ELECTIONS, SUGGESTING THAT LAVALAS CONTENDERS ARE 
INTIMIDATING AND THREATENING THE OPPOSITION. END SUMMARY. 
THE MESSAGE: "ELECTIONS, ELECTIONS, ELECTIONS!" 
3. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY OPENED THE MEETING BY SAYING HE 
HAD COME TO DELIVER ONE MESSAGE: "ELECTIONS, ELECTIONS, 
ELECTIONS" -- PREFERABLY IN JANUARY 1995. STRESSING THAT 
DEMOCRACY IS INSTITUTIONS AND PROCESS, NOT ONE MAN, HE 
EXPRESSED PRIDE AND OPTIMISM FOR HAITI'S FUTURE. HE 
ADDED, HOWEVER, THAT HE COULD BE MORE OPTIMISTIC WHEN 
THERE WAS AN ELECTORAL COUNCIL IN PLACE AND A DATE FOR 
ELECTIONS SET, LAYING EMBHASIS_ONTHE DOMESTIC POLITICAL 
NECESSITY FOR PROGRESS, WITH A NEW CONGRESS TAKING ITS 
SEAT JANUARY 4, AND ON THE ECONOMIC REQUIREMENT FOR 
DEMOCRATIC STABILITY IN ORDER TO ATTRACT DESPERATELY 
NEEDED INVESTMENT TO HAITI. 
4. DEPUTY NAT/ONAL SECURITY ADVISOR BERGER AGREED WITH 
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S MESSAGE, ADDING COMPLIMENTS FOR 
PARLIAMENT ON ITS EFFORTS THUS FAR TO KEEP THE PROCESS OF 
RESTORING DEMOCRACY IN HAITI MOVING. HE URGED THAT THERE 
BE NO INTERRUPTION IN PARLIAMENT'S OPERATIONS DUE TO 
DELAYED LEGISLATIVE ELECTIONS. 
POLITICIANS: "WE HEAR YOU" 
5. SENATE PRESIDENT FIRMIN JEAN-LOUIS RESPONDED WITH A 
"POINT WELL-TAKEN," THANKING THE VISITORS FOR THEIR 
REMARKS. HE INFORMED THE GROUP THAT PRESIDENT ARISTIDE 
HAD INVITED THE SENATE AND CHAMBER OF DEPUTIES LEADERSHIP 
TO A MEETING DECEMBER 5 AT WHICH THESE ISSUES WERE

MESSAGE FOR PRESIDENT CLINTON -- SECURITY 
6. ASKED BY THE DEPUTY SECRETARY FOR MESSAGES FOR 
PRESIDENT CLINTON, CHAMBER ACTING PRESIDENT MARC FERL 
MORQUETTE, A MEMBER OF THE OPPOSITION, URGED THAT 
CONSIDERATION BE GIVEN TO THE SECURITY SITUATION IN THE 
PROVINCES. ASSERTING THAT THERE IS INTIMIDATION OF 
OPPOSITION CANDIDATES, HE WARNED AGAINST CONDITIONS 
CREATING A PARLIAMENT WITH ONLY ONE PARTY REPRESENTED. 
HIS SUMMARY, IN RESPONSE TO THE MESSAGE SET FORTH AT 'THE 
BEGINNING OF THE MEETING WAS "ELECTIONS YES, SECURITY 
YES!" MR. BERGER AGREED FIRMLY THAT CONDITIONS FOR A 
FREE AND FAIR ELECTION WERE CRITICAL, AND TOLD THE GROU 
P

7. BOTH MORQUETTE AND CONACOM CHIEF VICTOR BENOIT 
UNDERLINED THE IMPORTANCE OF EARLY ELECTIONS TO AVOID A 
GOVERNANCE GAP, WHEN PARLIAMENT WOULD ESSENTIALLY NOT 
EXIST. BENOIT ALSO URGED THAT INTERNATIONAL OBSERVERS BE 
PRESENT THROUGHOUT THE COUNTRY. 
8. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY CLOSED BY-STATING THAT HE HAD •

COMMENT 
9. THE PARLIAMENTARIANS AND POLITICIANS PRESENT 
CERTAINLY ABSORBED THE MESSAGE BROUGHT BY THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY AND MR. BERGER. IT WAS ENCOURAGING THAT THEY 
AND PRESIDENT ARISTIDE HAD RESTARTED THE PROCESS, WHICH 
HAD SEEMED PARALYZED ONLY A WEEK AGO. THE STRONG WORDS 
ADDED BY THE VISITORS-WILL GIVE THEM MORE IMPETUS, 
RESULTING, WE HOPE, IN AN ANNOUNCEMENT ON MODALITIES AND 
A PROVISIONAL ELECTORAL COUNCIL WITHIN DAYS.
