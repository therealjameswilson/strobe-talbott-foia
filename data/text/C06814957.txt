SUMMARY 
1. 4-64NODIS) THE ACTING SECRETARY HELD A 40 MINUTE MEETING 
WITH VISITING CHINESE VFM YANG JIECHI OCTOBER 23 TO DISCUSS 
OUTSTANDING BILATERAL ISSUES AND PREPARE FOR THE 
PRESIDENT'S MEETING WITH)JIANG ZEMIN IN BRUNEI. THE ACTING 
SECRETARY URGED CONCLUSION OF CHINA'S WTO ACCESSION AND 
PRESSED FOR PROGRESS ON REACHING AN AGREEMENT ON NON-
PROLIFERATION, REJECTING YANG'S STATEMENT THAT CHINA HAD 
AGREED TO FURTHER EXPERTS' CONSULTATIONS AS A FAVOR TO THE 
U.S. BUT WOULD MAKE NO FURTHER CONCESSIONS. THE VFM SAID 
THAT CHINA HOPED TO MAKE PROGRESS IN THE THIRD ROUND OF 
TALKS WITH THE WTO WORKING GROUP, AND URGED U.S. 
SUPPORT.THE ACTING SECRETARY URGED MORE PROACTIVE CHINESE 
EFFORTS ON SOUTH ASIA NON-PROLIFERATION. YANG SAID THAT 
CHINA SHARED U.S. CONCERNS IN THE SUBCONTINENT, AND WAS 
ALSO IMPROVING TIES WITH INDIA. THE ACTING SECRETARY 
REITERATED U.S. POLICY ON TAIWAN, WHILE NOTING THAT CHINA'S 
RECENT DEFENSE WHITE PAPER HAD BEEN UNNECESSARILY 
PROVOCATIVE. YANG COMPLAINED ABOUT RECENT U.S. ARMS SALES 
TO TAIWAN. END SUMMARY. 
YANG POSITIVE ABOUT OVERALL RELATIONSHIP 
2. -(-8-/NODIS) YANG OPENED BY SAYING THAT IMPORTANT PROGRESS 
HAD OCCURRED DURING THE CLINTON ADMINSTRATION IN THE U.S.-
CHINA RELATIONSHIP DESPITE UPS AND DOWNS. THE VICE FOREIGN 
MINISTER HOPED THAT HIS VISIT WOULD HELP ENSURE THAT THE 
MEETING BETWEEN THE PRESIDENT AND JIANG ZEMIN IN BRUNEI 
WOULD BUILD ON PAST SUCCESSES, WHICH, IN TURN, WOULD 
PROMOTE THE IMPROVEMENT OF U.S.-CHINESE RELATIONS LEADING 
INTO THE NEXT ADMINISTRATION. PRAISING PNTR PASSAGE, YANG 
LAUDED THE EFFORTS OF THE PRESIDENT AND THE ADMINISTRATION. 
HE STRESSED THAT DESPITE "DIFFERENCES IN SOCIAL SYSTEMS," 
CHINA AND THE U.S. HAD IMPORTANT COMMON INTERESTS AND THAT 
IMPRC•VING TIES WOULD BE GOOD FOR BOTH COUNTRIES, THE 
REGICN, AND THE WORLD. THE KOREAN PENINSULA, THE ASIAN 
FINANCIAL CRISIS, AND SOUTH ASIAN NUCLEAR TESTS WERE 
EXAMPLES OF COOPERATION BETWEEN THE TWO COUNTRIES, SAID 
YANG. THE RELATIONSHIP WILL CONTINUE TO GO FORWARD SO LONG 
AS THE U.S. ABIDES BY THE THREE JOINT COMMUNIQUES AND THE, 
TAIWAN ISSUE IS "WELL-HANDLED," STATED THE VFM, WHO PRAISED 
THE CONSULTATIONS BETWEEN THE PRC MFA AND THE STATE 
DEPARTMENT AND HOPED FOR THEIR EXPANSION.

QUIPPED YANG, AND "IF WE WERE PLAYING TENNIS, THE BALL

5.-+GNODIS) CHINA WILL FULFILL ITS BILATERAL AGREEMENTS, 
INCLUDING THOSE WITH THE U.S., INCLUDED IN THE WTO 
AGREEMENT, SAID YANG, WHO STRESSED "WE ARE COMMITTED TO 
THESE." THE PRC OBJECTS, SAID THE VFM, TO HAVING ELEMENTS 
THAT HAD BEEN REQUESTED BUT THEN DROPPED DURING BILATERAL 
NEGOTIATIONS REAPPEAR IN THE MULTILATERAL DISCUSSIONS. 
AGREEING THAT IT IS A MULTILATERAL PROCESS, YANG STATED 
THAT THE U.S. PLAYS A KEY ROLE AND HOPED THAT WE WOULD BE A 
"POSITIVE FACTOR;" HE HOPED THE MULTILATERAL TALKS WOULD 
CONCLUDE SOON, AS BOTH SIDES WOULD BENEFIT FROM EARLY 
CHINESE ACCESS. YANG BELIEVED THAT THERE HAD BEEN SOME 
POSITIVE RESULTS AS A RESULT OF AMBASSADOR BARSHEFSKY'S 
VISIT AND LOOKED FOR MORE IN THE THIRD ROUND OF THE WORKING 
GROUP MEETING. 
TAIWAN

AGREED THAT THE ISSUE NEEDED TO BE WELL-HANDLED, BUT 
COMMENTED THAT IT WAS IMPORTANT FOR BOTH SIDES TO AVOID 
PROVOCATIVE AND INFLAMMATORY STATEMENTS. IN THAT REGARD, 
HE URGED YANG TO COMPARE AND CONTRAST THE DIFFERENCE 
BETWEEN CHINA'S UNHELPFUL DEFENSE WHITE PAPER WITH APNSA 
BERGER'S COMMENTS ON THAT PAPER IN HIS RECENT GEORGETOWN 
SPEECH. BERGER HAD DOWNPLAYED THE ISSUE DESPITE QUESTIC•NS 
CLEARLY DESIGNED TO ELICIT A STRONG REACTION. 
7. -eS7/NODIS) YANG REPLIED THAT BOTH THE U.S. AND CHINA 
HAD A ONE CHINA POLICY, BUT THAT "CHEN SHUI-BIAN AND THE 
TAIWAN AUTHORITIES" HAD MADE "PROVOCATIVE" STATEMENTS AND 
HAD NOT ENDORSED THE ONE-CHINA PRINCIPLE. THE VFM ARGUED 
THAT "LOGICALLY" THE U.S. SHOULD COME OUT AGAINST CHEN 
SHUI-BIAN'S "AMBIGUOUS" POSITION ON THE ONE-CHINA PRINCIPLE 
AND ANSWER HIS "PROVOCATIVE" REMARKS. THE WHITE PAPER, FOR 
ITS PART, EXPLAINS NATIONAL DEFENSE POLICY, ACCORDING TO 
YANG. AS SUCH IT SEEKS TO DEFEND CHINA'S DIGNITY, 
SOVEREIGNTY, AND TERRITORIAL INTEGRITY, INCLUDING THE ONE 
CHINA PRINCIPLE. YANG HOPED THAT THE U.S. WILL ADOPT AN 
APPROP.RIATE ATTITUDE TOWARD THESE POSITIONS IN KEEPING WITH 
INTERNATIONAL LAW AND AMERICAN COMMITMENTS. HE NOTED THAT 
THE U.S. HAS ISSUED MANY REPORTS DEALING WITH SECURITY, AND 
THAT CHINA DID NOT WANT TO SPEND TOO MUCH TIME COMMENTING 
ON THOSE REPORTS AS "WE KNOW U.S. POSITIONS ON THESE ISSUES 
WELL." THE IMPORTANT POINT IN THE BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP 
WAS THAT BOTH COUNTRIES HAD TO RESPECT EACH OTHER AND 
REFRAIN FROM INTERFERING IN EACH OTHER'S INTERNAL AFFAIRS. 
CHINA KNEW THE U.S. POSITION ON THESE ISSUES WELL, HE, 
REPEATED. ALSO, HE CRITICIZED THE RECENT SALE OF "LARGE

SOUTH ASIA 
9. -(-81-NODIS) NOTING YANG'S COMMENT ABOUT THE SUBCONTINENT, 
THE ACTING SECRETARY NOTED THAT THERE HAD BEEN SOME 
DIALOGUE BETWEEN THE U.S. AND CHINA, BUT THAT THE U.S. FELT 
A CERTAIN ISOLATION IN WHAT SHOULD BE A COMMON EFFORT. HE 
HOPED THAT OTHER STATES, INCLUDING THE PRC, WOULD MATCH 
ACTIONS TO THEIR RHETORIC ON THIS ISSUE, AS UNTIL NOW THEY 
HAD LEFT THE U.S. TO DO THE HEAVY LIFTING WITH THE PARTIES. 
THE ACTING SECRETARY COMMENTED THAT HE HAD BEEN GIVEN A 
"FRANK AND BRAWNY" REMONSTRATION THAT THE U.S. WAS TOO SOFT 
ON INDIA BY AMBASSADOR SHA DURING HIS FEBRUARY 2000 
MEETINGS IN BEIJING. HOWEVER, THE U.S. HAS IMPOSED AND 
TRIED TO SUSTAIN SANCTIONS ON BOTH SIDES, WHICH CHINA HAS 
NOT, THE ACTING SECRETARY EMPHASIZED. HE EXPRESSED THE 
HOPE THAT THE NEXT ADMINISTRATION WOULD FIND A MORE 
ACTIVIST CHINESE.APPROACH TO A COMMON PROBLEM. 
10. -444NODIS) YANG RESPONDED BY SAYING THAT CHINA'S 
POSITION ON INDIAN AND PAKISTANI NUCLEAR TESTING WAS CLEAR 
AND EXPRESSED IN THE RELEVANT UN RESOLUTIONS. THESE SHOULD 
BE IMPLEMENTED, AND PRESSURE SHOULD BE KEPT ON BOTH 
COUNTRIES TO RESOLVE THE NUCLEAR ISSUE. CHINP,I ALSO HAD A 
COMPREHENSIVE UNDERSTANDING OF U.S.-INDIAN RELATIONS, AND 
HAD NOTED THAT INDIA'S PRIME MINISTER HAD RECEIVED A 
STANDING OVATION FROM CONGRESS DURING HIS LAST VISIT. YANG 
SAID THE PRC SUPPORTS IMPROVED RELATIONS BETWEEN INDIA AND 
THE U.S. "OF COURSE," COMMENTED THE VFM, "LIKE YOU," CHINA 
HOLDS A DIFFERENT POSITION FROM INDIA ON THE NUCLEAR ISSUE,
