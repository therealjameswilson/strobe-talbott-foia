Dear Jim: 
I,can't tell you how much I admire the way you conducted your 
end of our discussions over the past week or so. I imposed upon 
you a week ago Saturday because of my esteem for you and my 
conviction that you could contribute immensely to the nation if we 
could entice you into government work. If anything, both 
sentiments have grown as a result of our interaction since then. 
At the same time, I totally accept your reasons for not wanting to 
ipursue the mmediate matter further. Your analysis is dead-
accurate: you would bring the most energy, experience and 
enthusiasm to bear in an area where, thanks to the good work of 
the Vice President, Larry Summers and others, we're in pretty good 
shape; where we really need help is in the bilateral area at the 
center of the original proposal. I can easily understand why that 
didn't grab you. 
I've alerted Larry, Jeff Garten, Lloyd. Cutler, and' Roger 
Altman to the outcome, as well, of course, as Nick Burns, Tom 
Simons, Sandy Berger, and Bill Taylor. Your ears would burn if 
you heard what they'd said on getting the news. cTo a man, my 
colleagues who had known you only by reputation but who met you in 
the course of these deliberations now wish they knew you as the 
Czar in the office down the hall. 
That goes for Secretary Christopher too. 
Also, even though we failed to attract you into our 
bureaucratic midst, we got the benefit of a concentrated dose of 
your insights and advice. Potent and much needed medicine that 
is. However we end up solving the Czar problem, you will have 
substantially helped us in our efforts.
