SUMMARY: ON SEPTEMBER 30, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT MET WITH HUNGARIAN FOREIGN MINISTER LASZLO KOVACS, WHO USED THE MEETING TO STRESS HUNGARY'S COMMITMENT TO WESTERN INTEGRATION, NATO MEMBERSHIP, AND IMPROVED RELATIONS WITH ITS NEIGHBORS. HE ALSO STATED HE EXPECTS NO CHANGE IN THE GOH POLICY THAT PERMITS NATO AWACS USE OF HUNGARIAN AIR SPACE TO MONITOR SERBIA. MR. TALBOTT WELCOMED EFFORTS TO 
IMPROVE REGIONAL RELATIONS BUT EXPRESSED CONCERN A 
NATIONALIST GOVERNMENT IN SLOVAKIA COULD BE SPAWNED BY ETHNIC-HUNGARIAN PARTIES' DEMANDS FOR AUTONOMY. HE ASKED HUNGARY, AS APPROPRIATE, TO PLAY A CONSTRUCTIVE ROLE. ON NATO EXPANSION, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY STATED THAT WHILE HUNGARY COULD WELL BE AMONG THE FIRST ADMITTED WHEN EXPANSION OCCURS, HE NOTED THE PROCESS WILL BE GRADUAL. AVOID POTENTIAL DAMAGE TO POLITICAL AND COMMERCIAL 
RELATIONS." FINALLY, MR. TALBOTT BRIEFED KOVACS ON

4. KOVACS OPENED BY DRAWING ATTENTION TO THE FACT THAT 
THE MEETING WAS HIS FIRST WITH A HIGH U.S. OFFICIAL SINCE 
ASSUMING HIS POST IN JULY. HE USED THE CALL TO REVIEW GOH 
FOREIGN POLICY PRIORITIES; WESTERN INTEGRATION AND 
IMPROVED RELATIONS WITH ITS NEIGHBORS. ON THE FORMER, 
KOVACS ADDRESSED EFFORTS TO PREPARE HUNGARY FOR EU 
MEMBERSHIP. HE NOTED HUNGARY WANTS TO ENGAGE IN OFFICIAL 
TALKS WITH THE UNION SOON AFTER THE 1996 REVIEW CONFERENCE 
AND EXPRESSED HOPE IT WILL BE POSSIBLE TO DEVELOP A 
MEMBERSHIP TIME SCHEDULE TO DEMONSTRATE HUNGARY'S 
ACCEPTANCE AS A RESPONSIBLE COUNTRY AND TO SEND A MESSAGE 
TO THE INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS COMMUNITY. 
5. ON THE SECURITY SIDE, KOVACS WELCOMED "PARTNERSHIP FOR 
PEACE," NOTING HIS PARTY HAD BEEN MORE ENTHUSIASTIC ABOUT 
THE PROPOSAL THAN THE PREVIOUS RULING COALITION. HE 
STRESSED THE GOH COMMITMENT TO JOINING NATO BUT EXPRESSED 
APPRECIATION IT WILL TAKE TIME TO CREATE THE PROPER 
ENVIRONMENT. IN A FINAL REFERENCE TO SECURITY ISSUES, 
KOVACS VOLUNTEERED THAT HE DOES NOT EXPECT AN ON-GOING 
GOVERNMENT REVIEW TO EFFECT THE POLICY OF ALLOWING NATO 
AWACS ASSISTING SERBIAN SANCTIONS ENFORCEMENT ACCESS TO 
HUNGARIAN AIR SPACE. 
6. ON NATO ENLARGEMENT, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT QUOTED 
THE PRESIDENT; "IT IS A QUESTION OF WHEN, NOT IF." HE 
FURTHER STATED,THAT WHEN NEW MEMBERS ARE ADMITTED, HUNGARY 
AND OTHER VISEGRAD COUNTRIES WILL LIKELY BE AMONG THE 
FIRST. THAT SAID, MR. TALBOTT CONTINUED THAT THE 
ADMINISTRATION FACES THE CHALLENGE OF EXPANDING NATO IN AN 
INTEGRATED EUROPE. ALTHOUGH QUICK TO ADD NO COUNTRY WILL 
HAVE A VETO, HE SAID RUSSIA WILL HAVE TO BE BROUGHT ALONG 
TO AVOID ITS VIEWING EXPANSION AS ANTI-RUSSIAN. IN THIS 
CONNECTION HE DISCUSSED PRESIDENT YELTSIN'S VISIT TO 
WASHINGTON AND REVEALED THAT YELTSIN'S THINK/NG HAS 
EVOLVED; ASKING THAT THE PROCESS BE "GRADUAL" AND THAT NO 
COUNTRY BE EXCLUDED.

BREAKTHROUGHS, BUT THOUGHT NORMALIZATION WAS POSSIBLE IN

ROMANIA. HE EXPRESSED A BELIEF THAT, MEETINGS IN 
BRATISLAVA WITH THE SLOVAKS AND IN BUDAPEST WITH THE 
ROMANIANS HAD REG/STEREO PROGRESS, !THE DEPUTY SECRETARY

WOULD NOT BE IN THE INTEREST OF HUNGARIANS IN SLOVAKIA OR 
HUNGARY. AMBASSADOR ALBRIGHT NOTED THAT PRESIDENTS KOVACS 
AND GONCZ HAVE WORKED HARD TO RESOLVE ETHNIC ISSUES AND 
CALLED ON THE HUNGARIAN GOVERNMENT TO USE WHATEVER 
INFLUENCE IT MAY HAVE TO ENCOURAGE THESE PARTIES TO LAY 
ASIDE THEIR DEMANDS AND FIND A WAY TO WORK WITH SLOVAK 
POLITICIANS IN ORDER TO AVOID A NATIONALIST GOVERNMENT. 
9. FOREIGN MINISTER KOVAC RESPONDED THAT THE GOH ACCEPTS 
THE CONCEPT OF AUTONOMY WITHIN ACCEPTED EUROPEAN STANDARDS 
AND WITH RESPECT FOR THE TERRITORIAL INTEGRITY OF STATES. 
HE NOTED THAT THE LEADERS OF THE HUNGARIAN PARTIES IN 
SLOVAKIA, INCLUDING MIKLOS DURAY, HAD EARLIER IN THE WEEK 
RENOUNCED ANY COMMITMENT TO TERRITORIAL AUTONOMY. THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY AND THOSE IN HIS PARTY WELCOMED THE NEWS 
AS A STRONG AND POSITIVE SIGNAL THAT FOLLOWING THE SLOVAK 
ELECTION, ETHNIC COOPERATION MAY STILL BE POSSIBLE. 
BILATERAL ISSUES 
10. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY TERMED U.S.-HUNGARIAN RELATIONS 
"EXCELLENT". NEVERTHELESS, HE STRESSED THE ISSUE OF 
HUNGARIAN SALES/TRANSFERS OF WEAPONS/MILITARY TECHNOLOGY 
TO IRAN "MUST BE RESOLVED TO AVOID POTENTIAL DAMAGE TO 
POLITICAL AND COMMERCIAL RELATIONS." THE FOREIGN MINISTER 
RESPONDED BY ACKNOWLEDGING THE SERIOUSNESS OF THE ISSUE, 
NOTING THE GOVERNMENT IS SEIZED WITH IT AND SHOULD PREPARE 
A RESPONSE SOON, AND BY PROMISING TO WORK DIRECTLY WITH 
THE EMBASSY TO RESOLVE OUTSTANDING QUESTIONS/ISSUES. ON A 
SEPARATE ISSUE, ASSISTANT SECRETARY SHATTUCK URGED HUNGARY 
TO ADOPT A LIBERAL MEDIA LAW.
