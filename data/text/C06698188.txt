SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S MEETING WITH

1.   ENTIRE TEXT. 
2. SUMMARY. DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT AND 
HUNGARIAN FOREIGN MINISTER LASZLO KOVACS AGREED TO TRY 
TO WORK TO BUILD A CONSENSUS FOR AMBASSADOR GYARMATI AS 
OSCE SECRETARY-GENERAL AND FOR AMBASSADOR FROWICK AS 
HEAD OF THE OSCE MISS/ON /N BOSNIA BY THE END OF THE 
OSCE MINISTERIAL IN BUDAPEST. KOVACS SAID THAT THE GOH 
IS VERY SATISFIED WITH THE NATO EXPANSION PROCESS. 
DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT EXPRESSED HIS BELIEF THAT NATO 
WOULD WORK WITH EACH COUNTRY INDIVIDUALLY IN LATE 1996 
AND THAT MANY RUSSIAN OFFICIALS HAVE ACCEPTED THE
INEVITABILITY OF CENTRAL EUROPEAN ACCESSION TO NATO. 
KOVACS DEFENDED HUNGARY'S RIGHT TO RAISE ITS OBJECTIONS 
TO THE SLOVAK LANGUAGE LAW IN INTERNATIONAL FORA SUCH 
AS THE OSCE. END SUMMARY. 
OSCE SECRETARY-GENERAL RACE 
3. FOREIGN MIN/STER KOVACS WELCOMED DEPUTY SECRETARY 
TALBOTT'S EXPRESSION OF U.S. SUPPORT FOR AMBASSADOR 
ISTVAN GYARMATI'S NOMINATION AS OSCE SYG, NOTING THAT 
THE HUNGARIAN CANDIDATE ENJOYED WIDESPREAD SUPPORT, 
INCLUDING FROM MOSCOW. HE CLAIMED THAT ROMANIA WOULD 
NOT VETO GYARMATI, LEAVING ONLY SLOVAKIA FIRMLY • 
OPPOSED. TALBOTT CAUTIONED THE HUNGARIANS THAT THE 
U.S. WAS OFFERING QUIET SUPPORT, TO AVOID THE 
APPEARANCE THAT THE U.S. BLESSING WOULD BE THE DECISIVE 
FACTOR. HE ADDED THAT THE U.S. WAS NOT JUST JOINING A 
CONSENSUS BUT HAS FULL CONFIDENCE IN GYARMATI'S 
ABILITIES AND WOULD LIKE THE ISSUE RESOLVED BEFORE THE 
MINISTERIAL ENDS. 
,4. ON BOSNIA, KOVACS AND TALBOTT AGREED THAT A 
RESOLUTION OF AMBASSADOR FROWICK'S NOMINATION AS HEAD 
OF THE OSCE BOSNIA MISSION WAS LIKELY IN THE NEXT DAY 
OR TWO. KOVACS THOUGHT THE PROPOSAL TO CREATE DEPUTY 
POSITIONS TO BE FILLED BY EU AND/OR RUSSIAN PERSONNEL 
COULD BREAK THE DEADLOCK: THE HUNGARIANS AND-THE U.S. 
AGREED ON THE NEED TO GET THE ARMS CONTROL AND 
CONFIDENCE BUILDING MEASURES EFFORT STARTED QUICKLY BY 
RELEASE IN FULL

NATO EXPANSION 
5. KOVACS SAID THAT THE GOH IS SATISFIED WITH THE 
PACE OF NATO EXPANSION AND THE DECISION TO INTENSIFY 
THE DIALOGUE ON AN INDIVIDUAL BASIS IN 1996. HE ALSO 
RECOGNIZED THE INDIRECT LINK BETWEEN IFOR AND NATO 
EXPANSION, IN THAT HUNGARY'S IFOR PERFORMANCE WILL HELP 
PROVE ITS CAPABILITY AND PREPAREDNESS FOR NATO 
MEMBERSHIP. TALBOTT TOLD KOVACS THAT 1996 WOULD LOOK 
AND FEEL DIFFERENT FROM 1995 BECAUSE NATO WILL RELATE 
TO EACH COUNTRY INDIVIDUALLY. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
BELIEVES THAT A SIGNIFICANT NUMBER OF RUSSIANS IN HIGH 
POS/TIONS HAVE TACITLY ACCEPTED NATO EXPANSION TO 
CENTRAL EUROPE. HE ADDED, HOWEVER, THAT BETTER ANSWERS 
ARE NEEDED ON HOW TO PREVENT POSSIBLE, FUTURE CENTRAL 
EUROPEAN MEMBERSHIP FROM DISADVANTAGING OTHER FORMER 
SOVIET STATES (THE BALTICS AND UKRAINE) WHICH ARE MORE 
SUSPECT TO RUSSIAN PRESSURE. 
SLOVAK LANGUAGE LAW 
6. FINALLY, KOVACS DEFENDED HUNGARY'S RIGHT TO BRING 
ITS OBJECTIONS TO THE RECENTLY PASSED SLOVAK LANGUAGE 
LAW TO THE ATTENTION OF MULTILATERAL FORA, LIKE THE 
OSCE AND COUNCIL OF EUROPE. DESCRIBING THE LAW AS VERY 
RESTRICTIVE IN TERMS OF MINORITY RIGHTS, KOVACS - - 
NONETHELESS SAID THAT THE GOH WOULD NOT DOWNGRADE • 
BILATERAL RELATIONS WITH BRATISLAVA AND IS7HOPING THAT, 
SLOVAKIA WOULD QUICKLY ENACT ADDITIONAL LEGISLATION 
GUARANTEEING MINORITY LANGUAGE RIGHTS. HE REPORTED 
THAT BUDAPEST'S PROPOSAL TO SLOVAKIA TO JOINTLY TURN TO 
THE COE--A POSITION SUPPORTED BY THE COE'S SYG--HAD 
BEEN REJECTED. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT AGREED THAT 
THE ISSUE WAS APPROPRIATE FOR MULTILATERAL FORA.
