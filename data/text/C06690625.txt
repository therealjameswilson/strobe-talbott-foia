Dear Larry: 
Joan Spero has kept me abreast of the discussion you and she have been 
having about Treasury's idea for Russian debt rescheduling. I'm writing you from 
the road because I gather there may be some action at the G-7 Finance Ministers!• 
meeting over the weekend. It's not clear to me how far that will go in committing 
the Administration to a particular approach. If the intention is merely to conduct 
further soundings along the lines of the paper you sent to Joan, Toby and me this 
past Monday, then perhaps what I have to say in this letter could have waited until 
.we have a chance to talk next week. However, this whole business is sufficiently 
complicated and important for me to feel that it's just as well for 
this opportunity to put on the record between us where I stand. 
There are two issues here, one of substance and the other of 
me to take 
process. On 
the first, you and your colleagues are engaged, in some extremely imaginative, 
constructive brainstorming that could break an impasse on a crucial subject and 
set the stage for a number of other important steps in the vital area of macroeconomic 
engagement between the G-7 and the N.I.S. You've wasted no time in demonstrating 
the kind of bold thinking that I hope will characterize this Administration's policy 
toward the FSU. 
On the question of process, you've made a real contribution to the FSU Policy 
Steering Group in its start-up phase; you've been hospitable and collegial to me 
personally on several occasions; you've distinguished yourself from your predecessor 
in your willingness to share your thinking and, in one important instance, a Treasury 
statement in advance of taking a public position; the memo you gave Joan, Toby and me

on Monday was among the most lucid, stimulating and persuasive that I, at least,

Still, we have to do more to establish the right procedures. If we're not 
careful, the standstill idea could unfold in a way that will make it harder to nail 
down an interagency consensus rather than easier. 
Here's the relevant history to date of the matter as I see it. Yes, you 
floated the idea past Toby and me, but you did so at the end of a long meeting, late 
in the evening, in a cursory, casual and I'd even say cryptic fashion, after Bob Fauver 
had left the room. Yes, you did call me last weekend; but while I have a distinct 
recollection of your telling me that you had cautiously tested the waters at the 
Deputies meeting, I do not recall your telling me that some sort of proposal was 
going before the Ministers. (I'm still not clear on exactly in what manner and with 
what degree of commitment that's going to happen, which takes me back to the disclaimer 
in the first paragraph of this letter.) Yes, you sent a memo on the subject to Joan, 
Toby and me. But that memo, both from its dating and its content, seemed to have been 
written in order to bring us up to speed on the question before the Deputies meeting; 
yet it arrived in our hands after the meeting. 
- --- 
The problem with the way this has developed so far is that other key people 
and offices have been left to wonder, and in some instances worry, about what's going 
on that's not conducive to interagency understanding or support. In a favorite 
Washington word, - it's counterprodnctive - from everyone's standpoint, including, 
I submit, your own. 
While the immediate issue is Russian debt, which is Treasury's province, standstill 
has implications for our overall policy toward the FSU, and it would have an impact on 
other Departments' areas of responsibility. As we've discussed -- and I know you've 
sensitive to this -- Toby and I are especially concerned about the ramifications for 
Russian-Ukrainian relations, which in turn have a bearing on our security/de-nuclearization 
policy (a special concern of Lynn Davis and our Pentagon colleagues).

It may well be that standstill will contribute to solutions in those and 
other areas. But it will have a much better chance of success if it's part of an 
overall coordinated policy -- which is what the Steering Group is all about. 
My suggestion is that as soon as possible, we get together and figure out how 
to put this matter back on track. My goal, as I trust I've made clear by now, is not 
to question Treasury's responsibility for Russian debt issues; rather, it's to make 
good on what I see as my own responsibility. I want to establish a process and 
a set of relationships among key offices and people that. will put the full force 
of the Executive Branch behind good ideas that will increase the chance that those 
ideas will be translated into effective policy. In other words, Larry, I want to 
help you advance ventures of this kind. 
Best regards, 
(signed) 
Strobe Talbott
