SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S OCTOBER 2 MEETING 
WITH ITALIAN DEFENSE MINISTER ANDREATTA 
1. CONFIDENTIAL ENTIRE TEXT. CLASSIFIED BY EUR/WE 
DIRECTOR SEBARNES. REASON 1.5 (B) AND (D). 
SUMMARY 
2. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT MET OCTOBER 2 WITH ITALIAN 
DEFENSE MINISTER BENIAMINO ANDREATTA. DISCUSSION CENTERED 
ON NATO ENLARGEMENT. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REVIEWED U.S. 
VIEWS, AND NOTED THAT RUSSIAN CONCERNS ABOUT EXTENDING 
NATO WERE MANAGEABLE IF THE ALLIANCE REMAINED UNITED. 
ANDREATTA VOICED GO1 SUPPORT FOR SLOVENIA'S EARLY 
ACCESSION TO NATO, WHILE TALBOTT UNDERSCORED THE NEED FOR 
LJUBLJANA TO PLAY A CONSTRUCTIVE ROLE IN THE BALKANS. 
ANDREATTA WAS ACCOMPANIED BY AMBASSADOR SALLEO, CJCS 
ADMIRAL VENTURONI, CHIEF OF STAFF VICE ADMIRAL BATTELLI, 
AND MOD DIPLOMATIC ADVISOR SESSA. THE U.S. SIDE INCLUDED 
AMBASSADOR BARTHOLOMEW, D SPECIAL ASSISTANT GOLDBERG, 
EuR/wE DIRECTOR BARNES, EUR/WE DEPUTY MCGINNIS, AND ITALY 
DESK OFFICER CHACON'(NOTETAKER). END SUMMARY. 
3. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY WELCOMED ANDREATTA ON BEHALF OF 
THE SECRETARY, WHO WAS INTENSIVELY INVOLVED IN ONGOING 
MIDDLE EAST DISCUSSIONS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY UNDERSCORED 
THE SECRETARY'S DESIRE FOR CLOSE COOPERATION AND 
CONSULTATION WITH THE GO1, NOTING THAT THE SECRETARY HAD 
SPOKEN WITH FM DINI SEPTEMBER 30 ON MIDDLE EAST SUMMIT 
DEVELOPMENTS. ANDREATTA SAID HE APPRECIATED THE 
OPPORTUNITY TO MEET WITH U.S. OFFICIALS, RECALLING HIS 
MANY PREVIOUS MEETINGS AND VISITS, INCLUDING AS FOREIGN 
MINISTER UNDER THE CIAMPI GOVERNMENT. HE COMMENDED 
SECRETARY PERRY'S COOPERATION AND INVITATION TO COME TO 
WASHINGTON TO DISCUSS DEFENSE REFORM AND ACQUISITION 
ISSUES. 
4, ANDREATTA ASKED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S VIEWS ON 
RUSSIA'S REACTION TO ALLIED PLANS FOR ENLARGING NATO, 
NOTING CONCERNS ABOUT ANTAGONIZING MOSCOW BY EXTENDING 
NATO MEMBERSHIP, PARTICULARLY TO THE BALTIC STATES AND 
UKRAINE. ACCORDING TO ANDREATTA, THE PERCEPTION AMONG 
EVEN PRO-WESTERN ELEMENTS IN RUSSIA WAS THAT THE ALLIANCE 
MUST PROVIDE RUSSIA SUFFICIENT GUARANTEES. 
5. TALBOTT REVIEWED THE U.S. POSITION ON ENLARGEMENT, 
STRESSING THE OVERRIDING OBJECTIVE OF PROMOTING FREEDOM,

THAT RUSSIA WAS ABSOLUTELY CENTRAL TO THE ISSUE. ALTHOUGH 
RUSSIA'S FUTURE WAS UNCERTAIN, THE U.S. WAS ENCOURAGED BY 
ITS REFORMIST TRACK. WE SHOULD TRY TO AVOID ENLARGEMENT 
BECOMING A RUSSIAN DOMESTIC PRETEXT FOR MOVEMENT IN THE 
WRONG DIRECTION. AT THE SAME TIME, TALBOTT DISPUTED THE 
NOTION THAT NATO ENLARGEMENT WAS FOREMOST IN RUSSIAN 
MINDS; THE POPULATION'S IMMEDIATE CONCERNS WERE FOOD, 
PENSIONS, QUALITY OF LIFE, AND CRIME. TALBOTT NOTED THAT 
SECRETARY PERRY AND RUSSIAN MOD RODIONOV HAD PRODUCTIVE 
MEETINGS AT BERGEN, WHERE THEY EMPHASIZED OPPORTUNITIES 
FOR COOPERATION. 
6. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY CONTINUED THAT FOR RUSSIA THE 
ENLARGEMENT ISSUE WAS MORE IMPORTANT IN TERMS OF POLITICAL 
PRESTIGE AND SYMBOLISM, AS THE DEBATE ON IT HAD CREATED 
THE IMPRESSION THAT RUSSIA HAD NO SAY OR INFLUENCE ON THE 
PROCESS. THE ALLIANCE NEEDED TO OFFER MOSCOW AN 
OPPORTUNITY OR FRAMEWORK TO MITIGATE RUSSIAN CONCERNS. IN 
THIS REGARD, SECRETARY CHRISTOPHER HAD PROPOSED IN HIS 
RECENT STUTTGART SPEECH THE GOAL OF A RUSSIA-NATO 
CHARTER. IN THE U.S. VIEW, AS LONG AS RUSSIA BELIEVED THE 
ALLIANCE WAS SPLIT OR INTIMIDATED ON THIS ISSUE, PROGRESS 
WOULD BE SLOW. THE U.S. WOULD NONETHELESS CONTINUE TO 
MOVE AHEAD ON ENLARGEMENT, PREPARING FOR THE NAC 
MINISTERIAL AND NATO SUMMIT NEXT SPRING. 
7. ON THE BALTICS AND UKRAINE, TALBOTT SAID THAT WE 
MUST LEAVE THE DOOR OPEN FOR ALL POTENTIAL NEW 
MEMBERS, FULLY CONSIDERING HOW TO DEAL WITH THOSE WHO DO 
NOT ENTER NATO IN THE FIRST TRANCHE AS WELL AS THOSE WHO 
DO. 
8. ANDREATTA SAID THERE WERE IMPORTANT PROBLEMS TO 
ADDRESS IN RELATION TO A RUSSIA-NATO CHARTER, INCLUDING 
CREATING CONFIDENCE BUILDING MEASURES REGARDING THE LEVEL 
OF ARMAMENTS. HE AGREED THAT, WHILE IT WAS NOT LOGICAL 
FOR NATO TO REDUCE ITS DEGREE OF MILITARY PREPAREDNESS, WE 
MUST USE ALL AVAILABLE DIPLOMATIC AVENUES FOR DEALING WITH 
RUSSIA. ANDREATTA ALSO RAISED THE POSSIBILITY OF A 
BELARUS-RUSSIA FEDERATION, WHICH MIGHT HELP TO COOL DOWN 
MATTERS ON RUSSIA'S BORDERS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AGREED 
THAT THE OSCE AND CFE WERE USEFUL VEHICLES FOR DEALING 
WITH RUSSIA, BUT NOTED THAT THESE COULD NOT DEAL WITH MANY 
OF THE BROADER ISSUES OF CONCERN TO RUSSIA, I.E., TROOP 
DEPLOYMENT. 
9. IN RESPONSE TO THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S QUESTION, 
ANDREATTA SAID ITALY SUPPORTED SLOVENIA'S EARLY ACCESSION

MEMBERSHIP COULD DO SO. TALBOTT ARGUED THAT NATO SHOULD 
NOT SERVE TO RATIFY OR ABSOLVE SLOVENIA OF RESPONSIBILITY 
FOR ITS ROLE IN THE BLOODY BREAK-UP OF THE FORMER 
YUGOSLAVIA. AS A NATO ASPIRANT, SLOVENIA MUST TAKE A 
CONSTRUCTIVE ROLE AND BE PART OF THE SOLUTION IN THE 
BALKANS. AMBASSADOR BARTHOLOMEW AGREED, ADDING THAT 
SLOVENIA'S MEMBERSHIP WOULD NOT REMOVE IT FROM THE BALKANS 
BUT INVOLVE IT, AS SLOVENIA WOULD BE RESPONSIBLE FOR 
HELPING TO INSURE THE PEACE AND BALKAN RECOVERY. 
10. TALBOTT SAID THAT THE TARGET ENTRY DATE FOR THE FIRST 
TRANCHE OF MEMBERS COULD BE AS EARLY AS 1999, THE 
ALLIANCE'S 50TH ANNIVERSARY. ON FRANCE'S SUPPORT FOR 
INCLUDING ROMANIA IN THE FIRST TRANCHE, ANDREATTA 
EXPRESSED UNDERSTANDING OF THE GEOSTRATEGIC IMPORTANCE OF 
INCLUDING ROMANIA, BUT SAID THAT COUNTRY'S 
POLITICAL/ECONOMIC SITUATION ARGUED AGAINST EARLY 
ACCESSION. SLOVAKIA AND BULGARIA WERE OTHER WORTHY 
CANDIDATES FOR NATO ACCESSION, BEYOND POLAND, THE CZECH 
REPUBLIC AND HUNGARY, IN ANDREATTA'S VIEW. TALBOTT 
UNDERSCORED THAT NEW MEMBERSHIP WOULD HAVE TO BE APPROVED 
BY THE RESPECTIVE LEGISLATIVE BODIES OF EACH NATO COUNTRY, 
WHICH WOULD LOOK CLOSELY AT SUCH KEY FACTORS AS COST AND 
THE PROPOSED ENTRANT'S MILITARY PREPAREDNESS. IT IS ALSO 
IMPORTANT NOT TO GIVE THE IMPRESSION THAT THE DOOR TO NATO 
WOULD ONLY OPEN ONCE, HE SAID; WHILE ENLARGEMENT WILL TAKE 
PLACE IN A DELIBERATE FASHION, WE MUST MAKE CLEAR THAT THE 
DOOR WILL REMAIN OPEN.
