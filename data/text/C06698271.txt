SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S MEETING WITH 1DB 
PRESIDENT IGLESTAS 
1. Classified by Strobe Talbott - Reason 1.5(d). 
2. Summary. Deputy Secretary Talbott, accompanied by ARA 
Acting Asst. Secretary Davidow met with 1DB President 
Iglesias on June 21 in anticipation of Iglesias' June 30 
visit to Haiti. The Deputy Secretary told Iglesias that 
the U.S. would make available dollars 19.6m in balance of 
payments assistance this fiscal year once the various 
conditionalities were satisfied. He urged that the 1DB 
disburse dollars 24m to help finance the program. The two 
agreed that although there is sufficient international 
assistance for Haiti, the problem is poor policy 
execution, which is in turn the symptom of a weak state. 
Iglesias said that he would try to persuade 
parliamentarians that IFI programs will help their 
constituents. The Deputy Secretary urged him to give 
encouragement to Preval and Denize for their efforts to 
build an effective police force. The discussion closed 
with an exchange on Venezuela. End Summary. 
3. The Deputy Secretary opened the meeting by 
characterizing the situation in Haiti as leading more or 
less in the right direction. He stressed that the economy 
was central. A11 other issues -- democracy, security --
lead back to the country's economic prospects. He told 
Iglesias that despite an adverse budget environment and a 
difficult political climate in Washington, we will make 
all resources that we can available. That includes 
dollars 19.6m in balance of payments assistance this year, 
assuming conditionalities are met. We will also, he said, 
support President Preval's modernization (privatization) 
initiative. In this respect, when Talbott visited Haiti 
last month, he urged Aristide to be supportive. Aristide 
had pledged his backing for the "modernization program." 
The DepSec said that we need to work with the IFIs, and 
urged Iglesias to make dollars 24rn available to support 
the economic reform program this year. 
4. Iglesias referred to Haiti as "the challenge that , 
tests us as an institution." He stated that the IDB is 
the largest contributor to Haiti, with commitments in the 
pipeline in the past two years totalling dollars 330m, of 
which dollars 80m has been disbursed. The bank has 
dollars 100m in projects pending this year, he said.

5. The problem, said Iglesias, is not a lack of external 
funds or the macroeconomic situation, which is 
manageable. Political restoration has also beeli a success 
thus far. The challenge is how to make the economy viable 
in the medium-term. This means starting from scratch in 
constructing institutions of the state, assuring public 
security, and improving policy execution in delivering 
services. Iglesias thought that Aristide's support would 
help improve the political climate for reform, and said 
that they would meet during his trip. He reported that 
the IMF, World Bank, and 1DB were putting together a task 
force to help Haiti with basic governance. 
6. The Deputy Secretary told Iglesias that Preval has 
shown leadership and independence. He is not a 
placeholder for Aristide, as some had feared. However, he 
is a loner, with a small staff and a weak economic team.. 
The Deputy Secretary said, and Iglesias agreed, that the 
economic elite is still holding back from assuming civic 
responsibility. 'Iglesias said that Preval needs 20-30 
capable technical advisors but that the IFIs preliminary 
plans to supply them touch sensitive issues, not the least 
of which is that they will earn several times civil 
Servants' salaries. 
7. The Deputy Secretary described parliament as the 
number one challenge. Parliamentarians have to be 
convinced that policy reform and foreign assistance will 
benefit their constituents. Given Haiti's history, their 
skepticism is understandable. Iglesias said that he would 
meet with parliamentarians and try to move them. 
8. Turning to long term issues, the DepSec said that U/S 
Wirth had visited Haiti recently, and'as a result of that 
visit, we will incorporate population and deforestation 
objectives in our economic policy. Amb. Davidow said that 
UN Rep TerHorst had developed a 15 year plan, and 
suggested this time horizon would be appropriate for the 
1DB. 
9. Iglesias expressed concern about the ability of the 
HNP to maintain internal security without international 
support. The DepSec predicted the UN force will be 
extended for 6 months, and that we may have to find a way 
to extend some reduced international presence beyond 
then. He portrayed violence, in part, as the dark side of 
good development. As bad actors have been weeded out of 
the police, some are seeking their revenge. He asked 
Iglesias, on his upcoming trip, to give Preval and Denize 
encouragement, describing Denize's work as courageous.

10. The discussion of Haiti closed with the DepSec 
suggesting that Iglesias meet Lava las leader Pierre

saw and heard on the economic policy front during his 
recent visit there. He said that he and Davidow delivered

promote the Agenda Venezuela. Iglesias agreed with the 
DepSec's observation that the economic policy team is a 
well integrated group. He was impressed that Petkoff, a 
statist, has embraced reform and appears eager to sell the 
package to venezuela's people. 
11. Iglesias said that venezuela's balance of payments is 
in good shape. The problem is the fiscal side, and this 
has blocked about dollars 1.8b in 1DB lending because of 
lack of Venezuelan counterpart funds. He continued that 
government has still to convince the populace that it will 
address social problems. The DepSec expressed admiration 
and thanks to Iglesias.
