SUMMARY 
2. DURING AN OCTOBER 1, 30-MINUTE MEETING WITH DEPUTY 
SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT, SLOVAK FOREIGN MINISTER HAMZIK 
REAFFIRMED SLOVAKIA'S COMMITMENT TO JOIN NATO AND THE EU, 
AND SAID SLOVAKIA "BELONGED" TO AND HAD MORE IN COMMON 
WITH THE PUTATIVE "FIRST GROUP" OF PROSPECTIVE NATO 
ENTRANTS THAN WITH THE OTHERS. HAMZIK ACKNOWLEDGED 
SLOVAKIA HAD INTERNAL POLITICAL PROBLEMS AND SAID THE GOS 
WAS WORKING TO SOLVE THEM. TALBOTT WELCOMED THE FOREIGN 
MINISTER'S REMARKS BUT RAISED CONCERNS ABOUT DRAFT 
LEGISLATION WHICH WAS ERODING THE DEVELOPMENT OF DEMOCRACY 
IN SLOVAKIA. HE URGED HAMZIK TO STAY IN CLOSE TOUCH WITH 
THE U.S. ON ALL ISSUES IN THE COMING MONTHS. END SUMMARY. 
3. EMPHASIZING SLOVAKIA'S DESIRE TO JOIN NATO AND THE EU 
AND TO BE A PART OF A STABLE EUROPE, FOREIGN MINISTER 
HAMZIK OPENED THE MEETING BY APPEALING FOR U.S. 
UNDERSTANDING OF SLOVAKIA'S TRANSITION PROCESS. ALTHOUGH 
BILATERAL RELATIONS WERE GOOD, HAMZIK SAID THERE WAS STILL 
ROOM FOR A MORE CONCRETE DIALOGUE ON POLITICAL ISSUES. ON 
THE ECONOMIC FRONT, HAMZIK STATED HIS DESIRE FOR EXPANDED 
ECONOMIC COOPERATION AND FOREIGN INVESTMENT, SPECIFYING 
THAT THE END OF PRIVATIZATION IN THE COMING MONTHS WOULD 
CREATE ROOM FOR FOREIGN PARTNERS AND CAPITAL TO ENTER 
SLOVAKIA. HAMZIK ALSO SAID THE SLOVAK MFA WAS INTERESTED 
IN HOLDING POLICY PLANNING TALKS WITH U.S. COUNTERPARTS. 
4. WITH REGARD TO NATO EXPANSION, HAMZIK DETAILED 
SLOVAKIA'S PROGRESS IN RESTRUCTURING THE SLOVAK MILITARY, 
HIGHLIGHTING ITS CIVILIAN CONTROL. THE RESULTS WERE 
SIMILAR TO THOSE ACHIEVED BY OTHER CENTRAL EUROPEAN 
COUNTRIES. MAMZIK STATED THAT SLOVAKIA WANTED TO BE PART 
OF THE SAME GROUP AS ITS NEIGHBORS WHERE NATO MEMBERSHIP 
WAS CONCERNED, AND HAD OBTAINED THEIR SUPPORT IN THIS 
EFFORT. 
5. TALBOTT ASKED A/S KORNBLUM FIRST TO ADDRESS THE ISSUE 
OF EUROPEAN SECURITY, AND KORNBLUM OUTLINED U.S. PROPOSALS 
FOR A COMMON EUROPEAN SECURITY POLICY, INCLUDING THE OSCE 
LISBON SUMMIT, THE U.S. DIALOGUE WITH RUSSIA ABOUT NATO, 
AND ENHANCEMENT OF PFP. KORNBLUM NOTED NATO WAS ONLY ONE 
PART OF THIS EFFORT, AND EMPHASIZED SLOVAKIA'S CENTRAL

6. HAMZIK SAID HE HAD RECENTLY DISCUSSED THESE SUBJECTS 
WITH NATO SYG SOLANA IN BRUSSELS. HE NOTED THAT SOME WERE 
QUESTIONING IF SLOVAKIA WAS READY FOR NATO MEMBERSHIP. 
HAMZIK SAID SLOVAKIA BELONGED TO THE FIRST GROUP OF NATO 
ENTRANTS, AND THAT THE GOS HAD IDENTIFIED THE PROBLEMS AND 
WOULD SOLVE THEM. THE POLITICAL SITUATION WAS NOT EASY, 
AND SLOVAKIA HAD LOST CONTINUITY AFTER THE CZECHO-SLOVAK 
SPLIT. BUT SLOVAKIA WAS STABLE ECONOMICALLY. WHILE 
ACHIEVING FULL DEMOCRACY WOULD TAKE TIME, THE SITUATION IN 
SLOVAKIA WAS NOT WORSE THAN IN NEIGHBORING COUNTRIES. 
7. TALBOTT EMPHASIZED THAT THE U.S. WANTED SLOVAKIA TO BE 
A FULL PARTNER IN NATO, THE EU, AND THE OECD. HE SAW THAT 
THERE WERE LOTS OF GROUNDS FOR STRATEGIC OPTIMISM IN 
SLOVAKIA, GIVEN ITS ACTIVE PARTICIPATION IN PFP, AND WORK 
IN BOSNIA AND EASTERN SLAVONIA. HOWEVER, HE WOULD BE 
REMISS NOT TO STATE CANDIDLY THAT THE U.S. WAS CONCERNED 
ABOUT DEMOCRATIZATION IN SLOVAKIA. FOR EXAMPLE, THERE 
WERE INDICATIONS THAT LEGISLATION REGARDING THE PROTECTION 
OF THE REPUBLIC, THE MEDIA, AND THE UNIVERSITIES WAS 
SLOWING DEMOCRACY DOWN. DEMOCRACY HAD BEEN A FUNDAMENTAL 
CRITERION FOR PROSPECTIVE NATO ENTRANTS SINCE 1994. 
ALTHOUGH NO DECISIONS HAD YET BEEN MADE, THE PROSPECT OF 
ENTRY WAS AN INDUCEMENT FOR COUNTRIES TO MOVE FORWARD AND 
OVERCOME OBSTACLES. 
8. HAMZIK MAINTAINED THAT THE LEGISLATION RAISED BY THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY WAS NOT A PROBLEM, AND SAID THAT THE 
PROTECTION OF THE REPUBLIC LEGISLATION AND MEDIA LAWS WERE 
STILL ONLY PROPOSALS. IN FINAL FORM, HE PROMISED, THEY 
WOULD BE ACCEPTABLE TO SLOVAKIA'S PARTNERS. THE 
UNIVERSITIES LAW HAD ALSO BEEN PASSED BY THE PARLIAMENT 
WITH FIFTY CHANGES. RAMZIK ADDED THAT SLOVAKIA KNEW IT 
HAD HOMEWORK TO DO TO ADAPT TO THE STANDARDS OF WESTERN 
DEMOCRACIES, AND THAT IT WOULD TAKE SOME TIME. HE 
ACKNOWLEDGED THERE WAS A LACK OF POLITICAL DIALOGUE 
BETWEEN THE GOVERNMENT AND OPPOSITION, BUT SAID IT WAS THE 
RESPONSIBILITY OF BOTH SIDES. THE OPPOSITION, HE CHARGED, 
WAS MAKING ITS CASE ABROAD, AND MANY WESTERN EUROPEAN 
COUNTRIES HAD ACCEPTED THEIR OVER-SIMPLIFIED ARUGUMENTS. 
9. TALBOTT RESPONDED THAT THE U.S. WAS NOT TRYING TO 
LECTURE OR PATRONIZE, BUT THAT THE DECISION TO EXPAND NATO 
WAS AT ITS CORE ABOUT THE ISSUE OF COLLECTIVE DEFENSE. IT 
WOULD BE A DIFFICULT DECISION. THEREFORE, THE U.S. TOOK 
SERIOUSLY THE QUESTION OF SLOVAKIAlS POLITICAL SITUATION. 
TALBOTT ASKED HAMZIK TO STAY IN CLOSE TOUCH ON ALL ISSUES 
SO BOTH SIDES COULD BETTER UNDERSTAND EACH OTHER'S 
POLICIES AND PERSPECTIVES.

10. TURNING TO MINORITY ISSUES, HAMZIK SAID SLOVAKIA'S 
BILATERAL TREATY WITH HUNGARY WAS A GOOD BASIS FOR PUTTING 
THE QUESTION OF NATIONAL MINORITIES BEHIND IT. IN 
MEETINGS WITH HUNGARIAN FM KOVACS IN BUDAPEST, THE TWO 
MINISTERS DISCUSSED A WHOLE RANGE OF BILATERAL ISSUES. 
SLOVAKIA WANTS STABLE RELATIONS; THEY ARE IN THE INTEREST 
OF BOTH COUNTRIES. THERE ARE ALWAYS DETAILS TO IMPROVE 
UPON, BUT SLOVAKIA CONSIDERS THE OVERALL SITUATION OF THE 
HUNGARIAN MINORITY IN SLOVAKIA TO BE GOOD. HAMZIK SAID HE 
HAD ALSO DISCUSSED THE NEED TO PRESERVE THE LANGUAGE AND 
CULTURAL IDENTITY OF THE SLOVAK MINORITY IN HUNGARY. 
BALANCE IS WHAT IS NEEDED. TALBOTT SAID HAMZIK'S 
ASSESSMENT OF THE MINORITY SITUATION GENERALLY PARALLELED 
WHAT KOVACS HAD TOLD HIM RECENTLY. 
11. IN CONCLUSION, HAMZIK SAID NATO AND T-HE EU WERE THE 
ONLY REASONABLE ALTERNATIVE FOR SLOVAKIA, AND THAT ALL 
NATIONS NEEDED TO WORK ON• WAYS TO DEVELOP STABLE RELATIONS 
WITH RUSSIA. TALBOTT AGREED, AND SAID THE U.S. WOULD WORK 
TO BE OPEN AND TRANSPARENT AND CONSULT WITH HAMZIK. 
12 PRESENT AT THE MEETING WERE ACTING SECRETARY TALBOTT, 
EUR A/S JOHN KORNBLUM, EUR/NCE DIRECTOR JONATHAN RICKERT, 
AND EUR/NCE NOTETAKER. THE SLOVAKS WERE REPRESENTED BY 
FOREIGN MINISTER HAMZIK, MFA DIRECTOR-GENERAL EMIL KUCHAR, 
CHIEF OF STAFF IVAN SURKOS, EU/NATO DIRECTOR MIROSLAV 
YENCA, SLOVAK AMBASSADOR BRANISLAV LICCHARDUS, AND SLOVAK 
EMBASSY NOTETAKER.
