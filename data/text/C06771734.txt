1. +G4 Summary. Acting Secretary Talbott met with Polish 
Defense Minister Onyszkiewicz January 26. The Acting 
Secretary observed that everything seems to be on track for 
NATO accession in March, though we still do not have an 
exact date. He described the formulation we are looking 
for on open door for the April NATO Summit. He appealed 
for Polish trust and flexibility in the CFE adaptation 
negotiations. Onyszkiewicz explained Polish domestic ' 
political sensitivities on this issue. The Acting 
Secretary also urged support on European Security and 
Defense Identity (ESDI); Onyszkiewicz responded that 
Poland's fundamental policy on such issues is, "Don't hurt 
NATO." The Acting Secretary reviewed the state of play on 
Kosovo and, at Onyszkiewicz's request, outlined U.S. 
thinking on Belarus and Ukraine. Onyszkiewicz put in a 
brief pitch in favor of certification to Congress that 
Ukraine has made positive progress on reform and business 
disputes. End Summary.

3..4-ei—After opening pleasantries, the Acting Secretary 
observed that everything looks to be on track for NATO 
accession sometime in March, though we are still waiting 
for SACEUR General Clark to specify a date by which Minimum 
Military Requirements will be met. We want this to happen 
for many reasons, including so that the new members can 
participate fully in preparations for the April Summit.

5. it9--The Acting Secretary reassured Onyszkiewicz that the 
U.S. would not support any outcome in CFE adaptation that 
singled out or discriminated against any NATO ally - 
especially the three invitees. Our own diplomacy on CFE 
adaptation would continue to be based on the NATO proposals 
tabled in Vienna; all NATO negotiating positions will 
conform with the strategic and military needs of the 
Allies, as they define them. Within the framework of 
NATO's proposals, we believe allies need to look for 
creative ways to make a virtue -- progress in the CEE 
negotiations -- out of a necessity - allies' need, for 
their own military and economic reasons, to implement 
military restructuring that could include force reductions. 
We hoped Poland would approach the problem along these 
lines. The Acting Secretary noted that later that day, 
U.S. officials traveling with the Secretary in Moscow would 
be briefing Polish officials, there with FM Geremek on the 
results of the Secretary's meetings, especially on CFE 
issues. 
6. 4r..4. Onyszkiewicz responded that the issue of national 
ceilings on force levels is politically very sensitive in 
Poland. The Polish press has picked up on the fact the 
Belarus, a smaller country than Poland, has higher ceilings 
and is even asking to raise them. Ukraine too has 
relatively high ceilings. The Acting Secretary assured him 
that the principle of reciprocity is a key element of the 
U.S. negotiating position on CFE. 
7. tei- The Acting Secretary also appealed for support from 
Poland, as well as the Czech Republic and Hungary, 
regarding the European Security and Defense Identity 
(ESDI) . It is no secret that we have had differences with 
some NATO Allies regarding the further development of ESDI; 
we were disappointed, for example, with the conclusions 
drawn by the French and British at the St. Malo summit in 
December. Our bottom line is that it is not only 
acceptable but desirable that the Europeans be willing and 
able to undertake certain missions on their own, without 
the United States. However, we all agreed in Berlin to 
"rules of the road" for developing ESDI, especially that 
there would be no decoupling from or duplication of NATO.

8. +ei Onyszkiewicz responded that Poland's basic policy on 
this and any other European security issue is similar to 
the Hippocratic oath, "Don't harm NATO." However, he 
admitted that Poland is under pressure from some European 
countries "to be more European than we are." 
9. (-614- The Acting Secretary reviewed the state of play on 
Kosovo. We need to find ways to strengthen the muscularity 
of international efforts to maintain a ceasefire and 
implement a political settlement. What that means in 
military terms is being worked out within NATO. We are 
acutely aware of the European view that if there is to be a 
ground presence the United States must take part. However, 
we hope Europeans also understand the heavy load the United 
States is already carrying for our collective security, in 
the Balkans and elsewhere, and the political and logistical 
constraints on doing more. 
10. fe)-At Onyszkiewicz's request, the Acting Secretary 
outlined U.S. thinking on Belarus and Ukraine. He 
characterized Belarus as a country that has lost its way, 
perhaps in part because of its weak sense of national 
identity. Lukashenko's actions are not only putting his 
country at odds with the rest of Europe, but they are also 
a complicating factor in relations with Russia. Even those 
Russians who favor unification can not contemplate 
burdening Russia with Belarus in the condition it is in. 
Badly off as it is, we should not write Belarus off as 
hopeless. We need to stay engaged, and in particular the 
United States hopes that historic ties might give 
neighboring countries such as Poland the ability to 
exercise special influence over the situation there. 
11. On Ukraine, the Acting Secretary said we see a 
combination of economic stagnation, due to lack of progress 
on reform, and political ambivalence. We are concerned by 
recent indications that President Kuchma's commitment to 
democracy may be wavering as he faces the uncertainty of 
upcoming elections. (He encouraged Ambassador Kozminski to 
follow up with 5/NIS Special Advisor Ambassador Sestanovich 
on this point.) Onyszkiewicz argued that precisely at this 
critical moment, as elections approach, it is especially 
important to send positive signals that strengthen the 
position of pro-reform forces. In particular, he urged 
that the Secretary certify to Congress that Ukraine has 
made significant progress on economic reform and continued 
progress on resolving business disputes with U.S. 
companies. This was important not only to allow U.S. 
assistance for Ukraine to continue, but also to provide.
