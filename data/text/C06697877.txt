2. SUMMARY: IN A 20-MINUTE MEETING APRIL 26, ACTING 
SECRETARY TALBOTT TOLD AUSTRALIAN AMBASSADOR MCCARTHY THAT 
THE U.S. WANTED TO COORDINATE CLOSELY WITH CANBERRA 
REGARDING DEFENSE RELATIONS WITH NEW ZEALAND. MCCARTHY 
RESPONDED THAT THE HOWARD ADMINISTRATION STILL MUST 
DETERMINE ITS POSITION ON THE U.S.-NEW ZEALAND 
RELATIONSHIP. MCCARTHY SAID HE WOULD CONTINUE TO URGE 
CANBERRA TO FOCUS ON THIS ISSUE. MCCARTHY ALSO ASSURED 
THE ACTING SECRETARY THAT THERE WOULD BE LITTLE CHANGE IN 
THE U.S.-AUSTRALIAN BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP UNDER PRIME 
MINISTER HOWARD. END SUMMARY 
3. ACTING SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT MET WITH AUSTRALIAN 
AMBASSADOR JOHN MCCARTHY ON APRIL 26. MCCARTHY BEGAN BY 
REASSURING THE ACTING SECRETARY THAT THERE WOULD BE LITTLE 
CHANGE IN AUSTRALIA'S BILATERAL RELATIONS WITH THE U.S. 
UNDER THE NEW HOWARD GOVERNMENT. WHILE HOWARD AND FOREIGN 
MINISTER DOWNER HAD SAID DURING THE ELECTION CAMPAIGN THAT 
THEY WANTED TO IMPROVE THE U.S. BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP, 
THIS WOULD NOT BE EASY TO DO CONSIDERING HOW SOLID THE 
RELATIONSHIP HAD ALREADY BECOME. THE ONLY REAL INITIATIVE 
THE NEW HOWARD ADMINISTRATION HAD IN THIS REGARD WAS TO 
PRE-POSITION U.S. DEFENSE EQUIPMENT IN AUSTRALIA. 
4. WITH HIS APRIL 25 MEETING WITH NEW ZEALAND FOREIGN 
MINISTER DON MCKINNON IN MIND, TALBOTT SAID THE U.S. 
NEEDED TO REMAIN IN CLOSE POLICY COORDINATION WITH THE GOA 
AS WE TRIED TO "NOURISH" AN IMPROVEMENT IN OUR BILATERAL 
SECURITY RELATIONSHIP WITH WELLINGTON. NO SIGNIFICANT 
CHANGE WAS LIKELY IN THE SHORT TERM, BUT MCKINNON WAS 
SOLUTION-ORIENTED AND HE WOULD LIKE TO SEE SOME 
IMPROVEMENT OVER TIME. 
5. MCCARTHY RESPONDED THAT THE HOWARD GOVERNMENT HAS NOT 
YET TAKEN A POSITION ON THE U.S.-NEW ZEALAND 
RELATIONSHIP. MCCARTHY HAD SENT A CABLE TWO WEEKS AGO 
URGING JUST SUCH A REVIEW. COMMENTING THAT THIS IS A 
SENSITIVE ISSUE, MCCARTHY STATED "PERSONALLY AND OFF THE 
RECORD" THAT THERE ARE TWO LINES OF THINKING WITHIN THE 
GOA:
