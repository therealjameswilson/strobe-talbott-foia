2. Summary: In an hour-long meeting with Deputy Secretary 
Talbott on April 20, Acting Russian Foreign Minister Ivanov 
asserted that Russia does not want to compete with the U.S. 
in the Middle East, Bosnia or anywhere else. Foreign 
Minister Primakov had not wanted to go to the Middle East; 
Yeltsin had ordered him to go. Talbott warned that 
multiple missions and messages would further complicate an 
already dangerous situation; he urged that Primakov stay in 
close touch with'Secretary Christopher and that he use his 
meeting with the Iranian Foreign Minister to push for 
Hezbullah restraint and a recommitment to the peace 
process. Ivanov and Talbott disagreed over the wisdom of 
a Madrid IT. Talbott urged Russia not to "snatch defeat 
from the jaws of victory by insisting on the abolition of 
the G-7; instead, Russia should work with us to enhance the 
P-B portion of the Lyon Summit. Ivanov and Talbott also 
discussed Chechnya, security issues, China, Korea, Yamantau 
mountain and the Nikitin case. end summary. 
3. Acting Foreign Minister Ivanov opened by noting that 
President Yeltsin had been very satisfied with the P-8 
Summit. It was too bad Foreign Minster Primakov was not 
there to Participate; but Yeltsin had decided it was more 
important for him to go to the Middle East to try to get a 
ceasefire. We don't want to compete in the Middle East, he 
said. It's not a question of who gets the credit, but of 
getting the ceasefire. We don't need this for our 
elections. Domestically it matters less than two tenths of 
a Percent. 
4. Chechnya, on the other hand, is very sensitive 
domestically. It could have acute election consequences. 
That's why, he continued, we're so concerned about outside 
support for the Chechens. We now have "absolute proof" 
that Chechnya is getting helP from Turkey. We turned this 
proof over to the Turks today.

8. Talbott asked if Primakov planned to meet with Iranian 
Foreign Minister Velayati. when Ivanov confirmed he did, 
Talbott stressed that the U.S. regarded Iran as perhaps the 
single most unrelenting enemy of the peace process. At the 
same time, Iran has influence with Hezbullah, so Primakov 
should use.his meeting to push the Iranians to urge 
restraint on Hezbullah and to work for a ceasefire. 
9. Talbott welcomed Ivanov's statement that Russia does 
not intend to compete with the U.S. in the Middle East 
since it is essential to our success that we not float 
competing peace plans. Secretary Christopher had been 
quite surprised to learn that Primakov had gone to the 
Middle East, since the Russian Foreign Minister had not

10. Ivanov asserted that Primakov had not wanted to go to 
the Middle East. Yeltsin had ordered him to go and the 
decision had come after Primakov and Secretary Christopher 
spoke. Me agreed they should speak again very soon. 
11. Ivanov suggested we consider a Madrid II to register 
the accomplishments of the peace process so far and try to 
move forward. Talbott responded that now is not the time 
to be talking about a Madrid II; the task of the moment is 
to rescue the peace process, not to be planning new 
conferences. .

12. On Chechnya, Talbott suggested that if the Russians 
have solid information about Turkish involvement they 
consider sharing it with us, perhaps through service-to-
service channels. Ivanov said he would seek permission to 
do so.• He said the fatal attack on the one hundred Russian 
servicemen the previous day bore all the hallmarks of an 
Afghan mercenary operation. The Chechens themselves 
couldnit do anything that sophisticated, clearly indicating 
they had outside help. His implication was that the Turks 
are funding Mujahhadin mercenary operations in Chechnya. 
Talbott queried whether an "Afghan-style" ambush 
automatically implicated Turkey. 
13. Returning to the nuclear summit, Talbott said Yeltsin 
had made a real contribution to the safety of the planet by 
hosting it. President Clinton would say this publicly, but 
he would decline to speculate on the outcome of the Russian 
elections. Instead he would say that we have great respect , 
for the Russian people and their right and ability to 
choose their leaders. We would also drop the P from "P-
6" and talk instead about "the Eight,11 in recognition of 
the diversity of issues now covered in that format. We 
would work hard with the French to ensure Russial s role in 
Lyon was enhanced over Halifax, and that the agenda of the 
Eight was broadened to include environment and other 
issues. That said, we would not support abolishing the 0-
7. It has important work to do on issues where Russia does 
not play a role like currency flows, international debt, 
etc. So at Lyon there would be both a G-7 and an enhanced 
11Eight.11 Russia should not llsnatch defeat from the jaws of 
victoryll by trying to insist we abolish the G-7 as an 
institution. 
14. Turning to security issues, Talbott said we had made 
good progress over the last few days on both ABM-TMD and 
CFE. On ABM, we may havea formula that allows us to move 
ahead in a way that will help President Clinton defend and 
protect the ABM treaty, while helping President Yeltsin to 
get START II ratified in the Duma. On CFE, we don't expect 
a breakthrough at the Summit, but we hope the Presidential 
meeting will give an impulse to both bureaucracies to reach 
a solution by mid-May.

during his upcoming visit to Beijing. He noted that this 
would help counteract speculation that Russia may be trying 
to play a "China card" against the U.S. or that Russia was 
allowing itself to be played as a card by China against the 
U.S. Talbott also welcomed Yeltsin's willingness to push

16. On Korea, he explained that we had pushed the 2 plus 2 
formula because we felt it offered the most promising 
chance to avoid a downward spiral on the peninsula. Still, 
Russia has an important role to play. Talbott.suggested we 
continue to rely on the Lord-Panov channel to synchronize

17. Talbott then raised the Nikitin "espionage" case. 
While we are sensitive to the fact that it is an internal 
legal matter, he said, we hope everything will be done to 
ensure Nikitin is treated accordingto international legal 
standards. Specifically, he should be able to choose his 
own lawyer and get a fair trial. 
18. Talbott noted that Yamatau mountain was attracting a 
lot of attention in the U.S. Talbott asked for maximum 
transparency about the facility to ensure we can accurately 
assert to Congress that it does not pose any contradictions 
to continued Nunn-Lugar funding.
