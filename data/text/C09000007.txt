First, a belated "thank you" for having me along on the Africa 
trip. Quite apart from it's intrinsic interest, it was just plain 
good fun - a delightful bunch of people all of whom (as fas as i 
could judge) got along famously: quite a testimony to their boss. 
was particularly pleased to meet Howard French, whose work I've 
long admired. Always good to discover that someone's as nice as 
they're impressive. 
Now to the other reason for this letter. For my sins, I'm editing 
an end-of-year double issue for Newsweek, on the misguided as-
sumption that as The Economist managed to pull off something like 
this each year, I must have some secret store of knowledge. Our 
operating concept (between ourselves) is "On to the Millenium". 
I'm commissioning a series of pieces, long and short, from inside 
and outside Neweweek. The pieces, with luck, will tell readere 
why they should be either optimistic about the next few years, or 
the opposite. 
I'd love you to contribute. Ideally, I'd like about 1,000 words 
(three columns) on why the world will be a better place (or, if 
that's not what you think, why it won't be) over the medium term. 
Precise mix of people, places, events, for you to decide. Range 
narrowly or widely. My only stricture is that I'm not allowing 
any abstract nouns in the book (so no pieces on "world hunger" or 
"global democracy"). But I might waive even that rule for you. 
Delivery in about three weeks. Tempted? I hope so; call me either 
way - and thanka again for Africa. 
Bestest, 
michae lliott
