Dear Ambassador Talbott: 
RELEASE IN FULL 
I am writing to you in regard to the $800 million that has 
been made available under Department of Defense appropriations 
for the dismantlement of the former Soviet nuclear arsenal. 
It is my understanding that it may be possible for some of 
the funding available under this program -- the so-called "Nunn-
Lugar" initiative -- to be applied toward the dismantlement of 
the former Soviet nuclear submarine fleet. This raises a timely 
and relevant question: what is the possibility that some of the 
work generated by this dismantlement may be carried out in 
private yards here in the United States? 
While I do not present myself as an expert on the technical 
capabilities of the former soviet Uniori, I would imagine that the 
safe dismantlement of a modern nuclear submarine presents a stiff 
technological challenge -- especially to a former empire whose 
infrastructure has by all accounts been severely weakened by 
years of economic stagnation and decline. Likewise I know from 
news reports appearing as recently as last week that the 
environmental safety record of.the former Soviet Union when it 
comes to nuclear materials has certainly becn far from exemplary. 
At the same time, I am also well aware of the very serious 
economic challenges facing our nation's submarine yards and the 
consequent threat to our submarine industrial base. I have no 
doubt that the additianal work generated by the dismantlement of 
former Soviet submarines would be greatly welcomed by these 
submarine yards and the many thousands of men and women who 
depend on those yards for a living. 
Naturally, I assume the idea of towing nuclear submarines to 
this side of the North Atlantic for decommission is not without 
its difficulties, and I would suggest it is a proposal whose 
potential drawbacks need to be examined at least as carefully as its merits. I certainly would not advocate implementation of such an initiative, for example, if_it were to be carried out in such a manner as to free up resource3 for Russia or any other 
former Soviet republic to continue its development of a modern 
nuclear submarine program.

Nevertheless, in this time of limited resources I believe we 
owe it to our nation's submarine builders and their employees --
who have dutifully provided some of America's most critical 
military needs for many decades -- to examine every possible 
opportunity to keep them engaged in important national security 
projects. Accordingly, I ask that you give this proposal your 
fullest consideration and that you provide me with a coordinated 
executive branch analysis of the feasibility of such an 
initiative. 
I appreciate your attention to this matter and I look 
forward to the benefit of your reply.
