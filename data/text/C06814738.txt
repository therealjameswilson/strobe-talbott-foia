1. -4-e-)-- SUMMARY: The Deputy Secretary met with Macedonia 
Foreign Minister Dimitrov on April 24. Dimitrov 
reiterated his government's concern over the Open Door 
language in the communique, specifically that it recognize 
Macedonia's aspirations for membership on equal terms with 
those of other candidates. At the Deputy Secretary's 
request, EUR DAS Ron Asmus reviewed the final text 
underscoring that significant changes were' made from 
earlier drafts to address the GOM's concerns. He noted 
that the language has no operational implications for 
future rounds of enlargement and encouraged the GOM to 
make good use of the Membership Action Plan to advance its 
candidacy. The Deputy Secretary praised the GOM for its 
efforts to build a democratic, prosperous and multiethnic 
society and pledged that the U.S. and NATO would stand-by 
Macedonia in the face of Milosevic's efforts to 
destabilize the country. He also highlighted U.S. efforts 
to help Macedonia cope with the humanitarian and economic 
impact of the Kosovo conflict. He reviewed our assistance 
and predicted additional progress on refugees in the 
coming weeks. He also highlighted the President's San 
Francisco speech and underscored that the U.S. was 
prepared to make a long-term commitment to Southeast 
Europe. END SUMMARY 
2. (u) On Saturday, April 24, the Deputy Secretary met 
with Macedonian Foreign Minister Dimitrov,on the margins 
of the NATO Summit. The Macedonian Ambassador to NATO 
accompanied FM Dimitrov. EUR Deputy Assistant Secretaries 
Asmus and Swigert, D Executive Assistant Phil Goldberg and 
D Special Assistant John Bass also participated in the 
meeting.

5. —ht.-)- The Deputy Secretary noted that others in the GOM 
had conveyed these concerns directly to him in recent 
days, underscoring that the issue of the communique's 
reference to Macedonia had received the highest degree of 
attention among the U.S. and Allied governments. He 
stressed that'Allies wanted to do everything they can at 
the Summit to recognize Macedonia's courage and 
cooperation with NATO. Highlighting the difficulty of 
building consensus both within the USG and the Alliance on 
Open Door language, the Deputy Secretary reported that 
there had been substantial changes to the Macedonia 
language, which he believed addressed the GOM's concerns. 
He asked EUR DAS Ron Asmus to review the new language. 
6. 4-e+ Asmus noted that.there are two sections of the 
communique of direct concern to Macedonia: the Open Door 
language and the language on Southeast Europe (SEE). With 
regards to the former, the communique now included 
reference to all nine NATO aspirants in a single 
paragraph, which eliminated the implied tiering of earlier 
texts that had referenced groups of aspirants in distinct 
paragraphs. Asmus emphasized that the sequencing of 
countries in the communique has no operational, 
implications for future rounds of enlargement. He noted 
that the language refrains from applying adjectives to any 
country to underscore this basic political message The 
language is descriptive, not prescriptive. .The collapsing 
of the previous three paragraphs into a single paragraph 
is a major change from Madrid,''which included separate 
paragraphs on Southeast-European and Baltic candidates. 
7. The language announcing and describing NATO's new 
Membership Action Plan (MAP) is the most important section 
of the Open Door language, Asmus continued. The MAP makes 
clear that the Alliance is committed to helping aspirants 
become the best possible candidates for membership and 
provides the aspirants with a set of tools that they can 
use to advance their aspirations. The challenge for each 
is to make the best use of them, Asmus stressed. In 
addition, the communique welcomes the aspirations of all, 
nine candidate countries, without distinction, and commits 
NATO to review, no later than 2002, the enlargement 
process. Finally, the communique's SEE language 
recognizes the need for a new level of engagement between 
NATO and the countries of the region and establishes a 
number of mechanisms to enure that this happens.

10. -(-e-)--Dimitrov thanked the U.S. for everything it has 
done to meet Macedonia's urgent needs. We cannot cope 
with the refugees or the economic impact of the conflict 
without outside assistance, he stressed. Dimitrov 
underscored that the GOM needs additional assistance 
beyond that already provided and recommended that the 
U.S., EU, and IFIs hold a donors conference to discuss how 
best to "compensate" Macedonia for its efforts. 
11. -f-e-)- Talbott recommended that the GOM refrain from 
using the word "compensate" to describe its requests for 
assistance. The term implies that the GOM requires 
assistance as a consequence of some NATO action against 
it, rather than because of Milosevic's actions. Talbott 
underscored that the President is strongly committed to 
working with our transatlantic partners to provide further 
assistance. As the President noted in his San Francisco 
speech, the U.S. is prepared to make a long-term 
commitment to building a new Southeast Europe, one that is 
stable and fully integrated into the Euro-Atlantic 
mainstream. Talbott noted that a democratic Serbia could 
also be part of this larger effort. 
12. Dimitrov stressed that the GOM fully supports the 
U.S. proposals. Our interest is in more development, but 
also in a democratic Yugoslavia that respects human 
rights. Dimitrov noted that several other countries had 
also tabled proposals for reconstructing Southeast Europe 
and suggested that it might be useful to discuss all of 
these at an international conference in Skopje. The goal 
would be to agree on a common approach to the region and a 
plan of action. He also requested that the Secretary 
consider visiting Skopje during her upcoming trip the 
Southeast Europe. The Deputy Secretary agreed to bring 
this request to the Secretary's attention.
