DEPARTMENT OF STATE 
WASHINGTON 
May 16, 1994 
Dear Bob: 
I apologize that it's taken us so long to get back to you 
on the Gaydar ideas. 
I asked my staff to examine the requests for assistance you 
relayed from him through Balgovolin. Gaydar was interested in: 
0 funds to help reformers acquire a nationwide TV channel 
immediate assistance to help miners' trade unions 
A additional housing aid for retired army officers 
• agricultural equipment for western Siberia. 
We are already active in every area Balgovolin mentioned, 
though for legal and political reasons I explain below, we have 
carefully avoided even the appearance of political partisanship 
in all our programs. With our existing programs, we support 
the development of an independent media, but not state or 
party-owned news outlets. We support mine safety programs and 
union organizing procedures, but not short-run union operating 
expenses. We have a very large officer housing program that is 
being supplemented by a D0D housing effort. We are increasing 
efforts to ensure the military and Russian people are aware of 
these programs. we are also sponsoring agribusiness 
partnerships between U.S. and Russian enterprises, including a 
number in Siberia. 
Our existing free media support programs and union-to-union 
activities a.re designed to promote reform broadly without 
creating the appearance of taking political sides in the 
internal debate. As you know, there are provisions in U.S. law 
prohibiting the use, directly or indirectly, of foreign aid 
funds to influence the outcome of any election in any country. 
while these provisions do not apply to NIS funds as a matter of 
law, they do reflect congressional sensitivity about this type 
of activity. 
Mr. Robert Ellsworth, 
President, 
Robert Ellsworth & Co. Inc., 
818 Connecticut Avenue, N.W. 
Washington, D.C.

Even apart from the law, however, our sense is that FREEDOM 
Support Act funds should not be used for the purpose of 
supporting specific parties or factions. We think that use of 
our assistance funds to affect the outcomes of local political 
struggles would not be broadly welcomed in Russia or here at 
home. You will recall the minor flap when Russia's Choice was 
accused of doctoring an advertisement for privatization 
prepared by Sawyer Miller to make it into a plug for that 
political party. For us to take sides in local politics leaves 
us and the reformers open to the charges of U.S. meddling in 
Russian internal political affairs. With our own Congress, we 
have repeatedly explained the need to support free and fair 
elections, not a particular party. 
In short, I believe we are responding to the spirit of 
qaydar's request -- i.e. that we support a free media', free 
trade union activity, the needs of retired officers and the 
private agricultural sector, particularly in Siberia --
although we can not always support the specific projects he 
recommends. I appreciate the time you took to write. Our door 
is always open to your suggestions, Gaydar's, and Balgovolin's) 
on ways to improve our NIS assistance program. 
Sinc ly, 
e Talbott 
Acting Secretary
