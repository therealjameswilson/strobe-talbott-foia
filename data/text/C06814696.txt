1. -(1,1-)' SUMMARY. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT MET APRIL 24 WITH 
SLOVENE PRIME MINISTER JANEZ DRNOVSEK.AND FOREIGN MINISTER 
BORIS FRLEC ON THE MARGINS OF THE NATO SUMMIT. IN ADDITION 
TO DISCUSSING SLOVENIA'S ASPIRATIONS TO JOIN NATO, TALKS 
FOCUSED ON .THE ONGOING CONFLICT IN YUGOSLAVIA. DRNOVSEK 
SUGGESTED BELGRADE WOULD NEVER ACCEPT A SETTLEMENT THAT DID 
NOT ALLOW A FACE-SAVING SOLUTION, PRINCIPALLY INVOLVING A 
DIMINISHED POST-HOSTILITIES ROLE FOR NATO. TALBOTT NOTED 
THAT NATO WOULD NOT ACCEPT ANY SOLUTION THAT LEFT SERB 
FORCES IN KOSOVO AND WOULD INSIST ON A CORE NATO ROLE IN AN 
INTERNATIONAL FORCE. THE SLOVENES NOTED THE VULNERABILITY 
OF THE MONTENEGRIN LEADERSHIP. DRNOVSEK SAID.SLOVENIA WAS 
DISAPPOINTED IT HAD NOT MADE MORE PROGRESS TOWARD NATO 
MEMBERSHIP, BUT TALBOTT STRESSED SLOVENIA WAS AN EXTREMELY 
STRONG CANDIDATE AND URGED THE GOS TO LEAD IN PUSHING 
PUBLIC OPINION IN A POSITIVE DIRECTION. END SUMMARY. 
2.  THE DEPUTY SECRETARY MET SLOVENE PRIME MINISTER 
DRNOVSEK AND FOREIGN MINISTER FRLEC APRIL 24 IN THE CUSTOMS 
BUILDING SUMMIT SITE IN WASHINGTON. THE U.S. SIDE INCLUDED 
EUR 'DAS JIM SWIGERT, TALBOTT'S EXECUTIVE ASSISTANT PHIL 
GOLDBERG, AND SLOVENIA DESKOFF EDWARD JOHNS. DRNOVSEK AND FRLEC WERE ACCOMPANIED BY SLOVENE AMBASSADOR RUPEL. MR. 
TALBOTT BEGAN THE MEETING BY REVIEWING LATEST ALLIANCE 
VIEWS OF THE KOSOVO CONFLICT, NOTING THAT NATO HAD AVOIDED 
LETTING KOSOVO DOMINATE THE SUMMIT AND HAD FOCUSED 
ATTENTION ON REASSURING PARTNERS OF THE VIABILITY OF THE 
OPEN DOOR. HE SAID SLOVENIA WAS A PIVOTAL COUNTRY -- ON 
THE EDGE OF THE BALKANS, BUT CLEARLY LOOKING NORTH AND WEST 
FOR ITS FUTURE. 
3:  (C) THE DEPUTY SECRETARY STRESSED THAT THE ALLIANCE WAS 
ENTERING A SUSPENSEFUL NUMBER OF WEEKS, MAYBE MONTHS. HE 
SAID NO ONE SHOULD MISTAKE NATO'S RESOLVE TO PREVAIL, 
NOTING THE IMPORTANCE OF SLOVENIA'S CONTRIBUTION TO THIS 
EFFORT. HE SAID SLOVENIA'S SUPPORT DEMONSTRATED THAT THE 
RIGHT COUNTRIES WERE MOVING TO CROSS THE THRESHOLD OF THE 
OPEN DOOR, 
4.  DRNOVSEK SAID THE SLOVENE GOVERNMENT AND PUBLIC 
SUPPORTED NATO'S CAMPAIGN, NOTING THAT PUBLIC OPINION IN 
SLOVENIA WAS MORE PRO-NATO THAN IN SOME ALLIED COUNTRIES. 
HE SAID HE WAS CONVINCED THAT THE ONLY SOLUTION ACCEPTABLE 
TO BOTH BELGRADE AND MOSCOW WOULD BE A UN MILITARY FORCE 
AND ASKED IF SUCH A "FACE-SAVING" STRATEGY WOULD BE 
CONSIDERED BY NATO. IF THIS WAS NOT AN OPTION, DRNOVSEK 
SAID HE SAW NO ALTERNATIVE THAN AN ESCALATION TO GROUND

RUSSIA AND THE U.S. WOULD NOT PREJUDGE THE UN ROLE. . STILL, 
THERE WAS A FUNDAMENTAL DIFFERENCE BETWEEN NATOIS AND 
RUSSIA'S VIEW OF THE SITUATION -- THE RUSSIAN VIEW WAS 
LIMITED TO WHAT BELGRADE WOULD ACCEPT, BUT NATO'S VIEW WAS 
LIMITED. TO WHAT BELGRADE MUST ACCEPT. 
6. --4-e-Y-DRNOVSEK SAID HE AGREED WITH MOST OF WHAT TALBOTT 
SAID. HE SUGGESTED THAT THE IMPLICIT PARTITION OF KOSOVO 
(A RUSSIAN-LED FORCE COVERING THE NORTHERN, PREDOMINANTLY 
SERB AREA AND NATO PATROLLING THE MAJORITY OF TERRITORY TO 
THE SOUTH) MIGHT BE ACCEPTABLE TO MILOSEVIC. HE ADDED THAT 
IT WAS IMPOSSIBLE TO PREDICT WHAT MILOSEVIC WOULD ACCEPT 
GIVEN'HIS OFTEN IRRATIONAL DECISION-MAKING. 
7. -f-E9.-THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REPLIED THAT THE U.S. REJECTED 
THE IDEA OF PARTITI1ON. IT WOULD SEND THE WRONG SIGNAL 
ABOUT THE.NEW EUROPE. FURTHERMORE, THE TYPE OF PARTITION 
ACCEPTABLE TO BELGRADE WOULD BE ADMINISTRATIVELY IMPOSSIBLE 
AND ETHNOGRAPHICALLY UNJUST. IT WOULD CREATE A "CRAZY 
QUILT" OF SERB AND ALBANIAN REGIONS .AND WOULD FORCE NATO 
AND RUSSIA TO RECREATE THE IRON CURTAIN IN MICROCOSM. IN' 
ADDITION, THE U.S. WAS OPPOSED TO INDEPENDENCE FOR KOSOVO. 
KOSOVO SHOULD REMAIN TERRITORIALLY A PART OF THE YUGOSLAV 
STATE, BUT SHOULD BE IN EFFECT A SPECIAL REGION WHERE 
BELGRADE HAD BEEN FIRED FROM ITS JOB AS RULING POWER FOR A 
SIGNIFICANT PERIOD OF TIME. FINALLY, NATO COULD NOT 
UNDERESTIMATE MILOSEVIC'S CAPACITY FOR SPASMODIC VIOLENCE 
ON AN IMMENSE SCALE. 
8. -*E4-TALBOTT REJECTED THE POSSIBILITY OF A FACE SAVING 
SOLUTION. NATO MUST BE THE WINNER AND MILOSEVIC THE LOSER. 
STILL, THERE WAS A WAY FOR MILOSEVIC TO REMAIN PRESIDENT OF 
YUGOSLAVIA, AND THAT WAS BY ACCEPTING AND IMPLEMENTING 
NATO'S FIVE CONDITIONS. DRNOVSEK NOTED THAT NATO MUST THEN 
GIVE A CLEAR WARNING THAT IT WAS PREPARED TO GO. ALL THE WAY 
MILITARILY, INCLUDING A GROUND INVASION. HE SAID YUGOSLAV 
MILITARY.LEADERSHIP WOULD NOT WANT TO FACE A GROUND FORCE - 
- THE MORE CREDIBLE THE THREAT OF A GROUND FORCE, THE MORE‘ 
LIKELY THE TOP BRASS OF THE VJ WOULD TRY TO INFLUENCE 
MILOSEVIC INTO A COMPROMISE. DRNOVSEK THEN TURNED TO 
MONTENEGRO, WARNING THAT THE LONGER THE CONFLICT CONTINUED, 
THE MORE LIKELY.DJUKANOVIC WOULD LOSE POWER. TALBOTT URGED 
DRNOVSEK TO CONVEY ANY IDEAS HE HAD ON HELPING DJUKANOVIC 
TO THE U.S. AND NATO. 
9. --4-e-Y-FOREIGN MINISTER FRLEC SAID HE BELIEVED MILOSEVIC 
WAS PERSONALLY SHAKEN BY THE RECENT BOMBINGS IN BELGRADE. 
HE ALSO NOTED THAT MANY OF THE ANTI-NATO PROTESTS IN SERBIA 
WERE STAGED BY THE REGIME AND THE PROTESTERS WERE COMPELLED 
BY THE POLICE TO PARTICIPATE. FRLEC ADDED THAT MONTENEGRAN 
PRESIDENT DJUKANOVIC HAD ASKED THESLOVENES FOR MONEY TO 
PAY HIS POLICE AND CIVIL SERVANTS.
