SUBJECT: TFSR02: ANNAN FLOATS POST-CONFLICT FORMULA 
WITH DEPSEC; AGREES TO KEEP RUSSIA ENGAGED 
CLASSIFIED BY POLITICAL MINISTER-COUNSELOR BRIAN FLORA FOR 
REASONS 1.5 (B) and (D). 
Summary 
1. UNSYG Kofi Annan told Deputy Secretary Talbott in Berlin 
April 28 that he would use his talks with President Yeltsin 
to emphasize that Russia could play a crucial role on 
Kosovo. The DepSec briefed Annan on his April 27 talks in 
Moscow. Both agreed that while Moscow was beginning to 
accept the logic of NATO's conditions, there was no 
indication Milosevic was prepared to comply. Annan agreed 
all FRY forces and paramilitaries must be withdrawn and 
inquired whether the U.S. thought NATO's conditions and 
other settlement requirements should be reflected in a 
UNSCR. He suggested IFOR/SFOR offered a useful model for an 
international security presence (underscoring the UN's 
desire to avoid another "dual key" situation) and shared UN 
brainstorming on a Bosnia-style civilian implementation 
structure. End Summary. 
2. DepSec Talbott reviewed his April 27 talks in Moscow with 
UNSYG Annan in Berlin April 28. He noted potentially 
positive developments in Chernomyrdin's and Ivanov's 
thinking; they appeared to be considering the situation and 
possible solutions more pragmatically. The Russians are 
particularly focused on three aspects: withdrawal of FRY 
forces; an international security presence; and a post-
conflict transitional administration in Kosovo. Talbott 
suggested the newfound pragmatism was prompted in part by 
NATO's unity at the Washington Summit and leaders' 
reaffirmation of NATO's conditions. But the West needed to 
be very careful not to publicly portray Moscow as "jumping 
on NATO's bandwagon." More importantly, none of this 
nascent movement in Moscow meant Milosevic was prepared to 
comply.

incorporated. The two briefly discussed the character of an 
international security presence (ISP), with Talbott 
reiterating our firm position that NATO must be at the core 
of the ISP and preserve its chain of command. We would not 
allow another "dual-key" arrangement. Annan agreed, noting,

Status - Avoid Secession and Chaos 
4. The DepSec noted the future status of Kosovo was crucial 
to the Russians, who were intensely concerned about the 
Precedental implications for potential ethniC conflicts 
within their own territory and that of neighboring states in 
the CIS. They want the arrangements to track as closely as 
possible to those outlined at Rambouillet. The U.S. 
believed the situation on the ground had eclipsed the 
Rambouillet provisions; those arrangements would not create 
conditions allowing refugees (the "right kind of Kosovar 
Albanians") to return home or fulfill the other relevant 
NATO conditions. Instead, we would need to create what 
would be, in effect, whatever its designation, an 
"internationally protected area" within the FRY's 
boundaries; the dnternationally protected status was a 
condition-subsequent/consequent of removing Serb forces. 
Without Belgrade's having an armed presen 
ce in Kosovo, there 
were only four alternatives: 1) chaos, 2) the reign of the 
UCK, 3) secession and 4) the international community in some 
real sense taking over; only Number 4 is a desirable 
outcome. In a post-Milosevic, democratic Yugoslavia, Kosovo 
might be reintegrated into the FRY, but until then, it would 
require a high degree of autonomy with at best token symbols 
of FRY sovereignty. Annan asked if UNSC authorization would 
be sought for such an arrangement; the DepSec observed that 
there should be roles for both the UN and,OSCE in such a 
formula. 
5. Talbott recounted that this concept of an IPA generated 
lots of anxiety in Moscow. Ivanov and others suspected that 
the concept equated a permanent NATO military governorship 
in Kosovo. Annan observed that Russian generals had 
harbored the same suspicions of NATO's role in Bosnia, but 
he believed Ivanov was too astute to believe this. Talbott 
emphasized that the best rebuttal to this argument was the 
truth - a military governorship was the last thing NATO 
sought. 
Wit-hdrawal: Annan seeks specifics 
6. Anan underscored there was no doubt that all FRY forces 
would have to withdraw from Kosovo in order for the refugees 
to return. The trick would be the process of actually 
getting them out, adding "military leaders consider 
withdrawal the most dangerous maneuver an army performs." 
Combined with Milosevic's suspicious mind, this would 
greatly complicate an actual withdrawal. How would NATO 
create the conditions for a withdrawal - did it expect the 
FRY to withdraw its forces while bombing continued? Talbott 
reiterated NATO's position, as reaffirmed by Alliance 
leaders at the Washington Summit; we would continue to 
insist Milosevic meet all five conditions. Two.arguments on 
this held weight with the Russians: the absence of full 
withdrawal would discourage refugee returns while serving as 
a magnet for the KLA; in such an environment, any 
peacekeepers - including a Russian contingent - would not be

7. Annan asked how NATO would respond once the FRY agreed 
to withdraw but had not yet commenced doing so. Would NATO 
stop bombing at this stage? The DepSec reiterated NATO's 
position - "verifiable withdrawal according to a rapid and 
precise timetable" - and indicated the Alliance was 
beginning to look at how this could be implemented. Annan 
suggested using "bad weather" as one possible way to 
interrupt the bombing and allow FRY withdrawal to begin. 
Civilian Implementation: UN Outline 
8. Annan had A/SYG Desoto outline UN brainstorming on an 
implementation structure; Annan stressed it was "very 
preliminary" thinking. DeSoto detailed a Bosnia-like 
structure: the UN would authorize an operation containing 
both military and civilian components, with the latter 
consisting of four "pillars" or functions, operating under a 
UN coordinator. Different organizations would take the lead 
for implementing each of the functions; DeSoto was 
provisionally thinking about the following division of 
labor: reconstruction - EU lead; humanitarian aspects - 
UNHCR; institution-building - OSCE; and security/police - an 
IPTF-style arrangement. 
9. Annan suggested this approach might be attractive to the 
Russians; they could play a prominent role in civilian.
implementation within the UN context and under a UNSCR. 
Depending on how his talks in Moscow went, he might float 
the idea. After confirming the SYG had not already done so, 
the DepSec suggested Annan discuss this in detail with OSCE 
CIO Vollebaek. The OSCE had lots of experience and a key 
role to play, especially give its KVM experience. 
Annan's Nightmare Scenario 
10. Annan concluded by laying out his "nightmare scenario," 
in which Milosevic concludes he is trapped and does not 
yield; the conflict would then drag into the summer. If 
public support ebbed, as Annan bel 
ieved it would, would NATO 
alter its conditions? The DepSec firmly reiterated NATO's 
commitment to its five conditions and underscored the 
Alliance's belief that it could compel Milosevic's 
compliance. 
11. Annan was accompanied by political affairs A/SYG 
DeSoto, Executive Assistant Lindemeyer and two other 
staffers; Talbott, by EUR A/DAS Swigert, D staffers Goldberg 
and Bass and Embassy notetaker. 
12. The Deputy Secretary has cleared this message.
