RELEASE IN FULL 
Friday, September 13, 1996 
Chris: 
This memo is to supplement the brief that Jim and I will 
give you on our return to the Department. Attached is a 
memcon and a copy of the framework nonpaper that came out 
of our meetings. 
Mamedov came to Paris with a license from Primakov to 
explore in a much more focused way a framework/Charter 
along the lines of what we've proposed, as opposed to the 
maximalist wish-list that Afanasyevsky has been shopping 
around Europe (a NATO-Russia treaty that is a precondition 
for enlargement, that gives Russia numerous vetoes, that rules 
out Baltic and Ukrainian eligibility for membership, that makes 
Central Europe into a nuke-free-zone, that transforms NATO 
into a peacekeeping organization under the UN or OSCE). In 
Paris, the horribles on the Afanasyevsky list had all but 
disappeared. Unlike in Ottawa, when he'd had to pay 
lipservice to Afanasyevsky, this time Yuri was playing entirely 
on our field. There are still plenty of details in which reside 
plenty of devils — but as each of said to the other, no 
showstoppers. 
If Mamedov were the Russian government or even just 
the Russian foreign minister, I'd be pretty confident that we 
could get, by Lisbon, a framework very close to the one we've 
tabled and that we'd get a Charter next spring.

But it's still not clear where Primakov is. You'll see that 
Mamedov was a bit ambiguous on this. I suspect•Primakov is 
still covering his bets. And Mamedov-versus-Afanasyevsky is 
not necessarily an either/or proposition: Primakov could very 
well try to see if he can't push us toward the Afanasyevsky end 
of the spectrum on some key points. As late as this week, the 
Russians were still playing the Afanasyevsky tune with Jacques 
Blot when he was in Moscow. 
Your meeting with Primakov a week from Monday will 
be the best chance yet to smoke him out and to impress on him 
that a double game is a loser for them; that the approach we 
discussed with Mamedov is the only one that will lead 
anywhere; and that the more time he spends screwing around 
and trying to have it both ways, the less the chance that there 
will be a NATO-Russia Charter, at least in anything like the 
timeframe of the NATO Summit next year. 
Mamedov is coming to Washington next Friday. I've 
cancelled my Seattle/Anchorage speaking trip in order to 
prepare for that meeting and to help make sure that your 
Primakov meeting is as well tee'd up as possible (also to deal 
with Haiti, Iraq, Bosnia, etc., as required). 
Solana will meet with Primakov in Vienna a week from 
today. We had a terrific meeting with Solana at Orly 
yesterday. He'll do a great job of softening Primakov up for 
his session with you (if that's possible). Also, thanks to the 
time difference, I'll have a full read-out from Solana before I 
go into my Friday afternoon/evening session with Mamedov.

If and only if your session with Primakov session goes 
well, I think we should push hard for a brief meeting between 
him and POTUS. I realize how difficult that will be from a 
sked standpoint (not to mention an aesthetic one), but if it will 
help cement progress, it will be worth it. Sandy will work with 
us on this. 
Overall, we're in pretty good shape with the Allies on 
NATO-Russia. In addition to POTUS's phone conversation 
with Kohl, we've also got read-outs of Kohl's calls to Chirac 
and Solana, and we had 90 minutes with Ischinger this 
morning. Kohl's principal concern is to keep any aspect of the 
subjects of NATO enlargement and NATO-Russia out of the 
headlines and out of the hands of the Brussels bureaucracy. He 
has two concerns, only one explicit: that NATO not seem to be 
moving ahead on either front while Yeltsin is out of action, thus 
making it look as though Yeltsin has lost control. Kohl's 
unarticulated, but unmistakable, second concern is that he keep 
a degree of control over the pace and content of this process. 
He still thinks, no doubt, that POTUS, once past November 5, 
will be more amenable to Kohl's argument that we should 
perhaps slow down the whole thing. In any event, I'm satisfied 
that what we've heard indirectly from Kohl himself and what 
we got directly from Ischinger this morning constitutes a 
satisfactory degree of German acquiescence in our approach 
and timetable. 
The Brits are four-square (we met with Jeremy 
Greenstock), and the French are okay. In fact, Chirac's

after coordinating with the other three, and give the Council a
