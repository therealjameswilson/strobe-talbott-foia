2. (U) Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs State 
Secretary Anslaug Haga called on Acting Secretary Talbott 
on January 26. Ms. Haga was joined by Norwegian Ambassador 
to the United States Tom Vraalsen; the MFA Special Advisor 
for Russian Nuclear Safety, Ambassador Norendal; MFA Deputy 
Director General Knut Hauge; and Embassy political officer 
Inge Nyhamar. The Acting Secretary was joined by EUR 
Acting DAS Barbro Owens-Kirkpatrick; D Special Assistant 
John Bass; PM/NE's Charles Newstead; and EUR/NB's 
Christopher Beede. 
Kosovo 
3. (C) Secretary Haga asked whether there was any news 
from Secretary Albright's trip to Moscow, especially 
regarding developments in Kosovo. She also asked for an 
update on USG thinking about possible use of ground forces 
in Kosovo. Acting Secretary Talbott prefaced his reply by 
highlighting the Administration's great apprecia-tion and 
admiration for the role of Norwegian Foreign Minister Knut 
Vollebaek (now OSCE Chairman-in-Office) on Kosovo issues, 
particularly Vollebaek's prompt, categorical assertions 
about the KVM and the threatened expulsion of Ambassador 
Walker. Developments in.Kosovo were relevant to the 
success of the NATO summit in April, Talbott continued. 
Kosovo was a test of NATO, and of the European Union and 
the OSCE. But Kosovo could be turned into a positive 
moment if NATO, the United Nations, the OSCE, the EU, and 
the Contact Group worked supportively and synergistically 
in coordination, along with individual countries.

Russian engagement and contributions to resolve the crisis. 
Talbott sketched out a series of events in the coming days 
to compel Serb compliance, including a NAC warning to

ESDI 
7.   The Acting Secretary turned to Haga's next area of 
interest, the European Security and Defense Identity 
(ESDI). The United States had been disappointed with St. 
Malo - both in terms of substance and the inadequate UK and 
French consultations with the U.S. before and after. 
British representatives acknowledged that much better 
cross-Atlantic consultations were needed if this latest 
phase in building ESDI were to be successful. No one 
thought that the ESDI would be completed by the EU summit, 
but we would support completion of a framework for further 
work provided the "three D's" were fully respected and the 
overall effort remained embedded in the transatlantic ' 
framework. The United States had urged Britain to focus on 
defining key concepts which would be reflected in NATO 
summit documents. It remained to be seen whether the 
summit documents could be described as "Berlin-plus."

security and foreign policies; St. Malo was a sign that 
this could progress quickly. Norway, as a non-EU member, 
was concerned. If the WEU was transformed and placed 
within the EU, Norway would lose its input into planning

9. (C) Acting Secretary Talbott observed that the United 
States and Norway held several similar concerns. For the 
U.S.'s part, we were not opposed in principle to ESDI. We 
recognized, however, that there were many practical 
military questions to be considered. We assumed that 
Norway would press for maximum EU transparency across the 
Atlantic and with non-EU military allies. Haga opined that 
whatever was to come out of the Washington NATO Summit 
should be well coordinated with what would come out of the 
EU in June. Haga urged the United States to be firm with 
the EU on ESDI.
