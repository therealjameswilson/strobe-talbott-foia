Dear Mr. Shapiro: 
I appreciated your letter of April 27, in which you 
forwarded to me the text of your address to the Carnegie 
Council on Ethics and International Affairs. 
While I was interested in your use of post-war Japan's 
industrial policy as a model for Russia's economic transition, 
I continue to believe that only a true market economy will 
bring a country as large and diverse as Russia the prosperity 
it seeks. Toward that end, inflationary fiscal and monetary 
practices need to be curtailed and privatization ac'celerated. 
To help with the transition to a market-based economy, we 
have three objectives in our economic relations with Russia: 
o To create, with our allies, external economic 
conditions to support market-oriented reform. This 
includes bilateral humanitarian and technical 
assistance, as well as macroeconomic support-through 
debt rescheduling and an IMF program, to include a 
ruble stabilization fund. 
o To work to'integrate Russia into the western ecpnomic 
system by supporting its membership and participation 
in international institutions such as the IMF, the 
World Bank and the GATT. Russia has formally begun 
negotiations on GATT accession. 
To normalize our economic relations by removing Cold 
War-era restrictions on trade and negotiating a new 
network of economic agreements to support expanded 
trade and investment. 
Mr. Isaac Shapiro, Esq., 
Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom,

At the Tokyo Summit, the G-7 endorsed several 
Administration initiatives for supporting, economic reforms in 
Russia, including the creation of a $3 billion Special 
Privatization and Restructuring Program (SPRP), half of which 
would come from the G-7 countries and half from the 
international financial institutions. The SPRP would provide 
technical assistance, loans and equity investments to transform 
large inefficient state-owned enterprises into productive 
private companies. The G-7 also agreed to an Administration 
proposal to establish a Support Implementation Group in Moscow 
that would coordinate the implementation of this program and 
increase the effectiveness of G-7 assistance. 
As Secretary Christopher has emphasized, the stakes for the 
United States in the reform process now underway in Russia are 
monumental, affecting the very'foundation of our security and 
prosperity into the next century. In responding to this 
historic opportunity, the United States and the other Western 
democracies must extend a hand of partnership to President 
Yeltsin and the Russian people, a partnership based on 
democratic and free market values. We are gratified by your 
support as we take up this partnership.
