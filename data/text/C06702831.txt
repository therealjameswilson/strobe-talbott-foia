2. Summary: Canadian Deputy Foreign Minister Gordon 
Smith discussed Haiti at his FebFuary 14 meeting with 
Deputy Secretary Talbott. Smith said that the situation 
in Haiti was not much different krom earlier and was not 
certain that President Preval would want a continued 
military presence. Yet, he could only conceive of - 
continued CivPol presence if military were also present. 
He said that some sort of UN authority would be necessAry 
for Canada, although financing was a separate matter. He 
said that Foreign Minister Axworthy strongly preferred a 
UN umbrella, but was not averse to operating under OAS 
authorization. Smith noted the importance Canada 
attributed to Haiti and also recognized its importance to 
the U.S. DepSec Talbott thanked Smith and Canada for 
their important role in Haiti and noted the President-s 
determination that Haiti policy succeed. The DepSec said 
that no decision had been taken on the U.S. military role 
after UNSMIH. He said that he did not believe it possible 
to secure another UNSC renewal for UNSMIH, but that 
approval of something beyond Civpol was possible. End 
Summary. 
3. Deputy Foreign Minister Smith was accompanied by 
Ambassador Chretien, POLMINCOUNS Meyer and POLMIN 
Higginbotham. The DepSec was accompanied by Special Haiti 
Coordinator Sullivan, EUR/CAN Kunsman and ISD/A Sullivan. 
4. Asked for his assessment of Haiti, DepFonMin Smith 
said that the situation was not much better and the issue 
was what could be done next. He asked whether Preval 
would agree in wanting an international military 
presence. Smith could not conceive that Civpol could 
remain without some military presence. Canadian police 
would not wish to participate under those circumstances, 
even if helicopter transport remained available. Smith 
said that Canada needed a UN authority similar to the 
authority provided for activity in Zaire. Financing was a 
separate issue. There have been two llfinal' UNSMIH 
resolutions and he did not imagine it would be possible to 
secure another such resolution. But, Canada attached 
great importance to Haiti and also knew that it was 
important to the United States. More Canadian aid went to 
Haiti per capita than to any other country.
