Pex our phone conversation on March 30 (p.m. on March 29 
your time), I reached Mamedov within the hour. He told me 
Ivanov was already in the air. They had imposed a security 
blackout on times of departure and availability in light of 
terroxist threats. He said he wou].d, however, do his best 
to convey the message from the Secretary. 
I then went through your points, telling Mamedov the 
Secretary believed it important for the Ministex and Prime 
Minister to know clearly the allied position on any halt in 
fighting. I stated that: 
-- the Secretary wished the Prime Minister and his party 
well and hoped for positive results; and 
-- the allied position is firm on two points: that a 
cease-fire will not be adequate to bring,a halt to NATO,
action and that Milosevic must accept as well the pullback 
of forces consistent with the October agreements. I noted 
that the Secretary would have made clear to Ivanov that any 
cease-fire in place would leave the Serbs in far too strong 
a military position in Kosovo to be acceptable. 
Mamedov said he hoped for his paxt that the U.S. understood 
Primakov is not a miracle worker. He said the Russians 
have been told Milosevic's position at this point is 
equally firm: the October agreements between him and NATO 
are dead given NATO's opening of military action. It is 
against this background that Primakov will be working. I 
said I would convey his points but reiterated the substance 
of the Secretary's message. Mamedov said he would do his 
best to convey these points to the Minister.
