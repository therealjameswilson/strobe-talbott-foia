4. Ter ACTING SECRETARY TALBOTT TOLD DEPUTY FM MAMEDOV 
THAT THEY HAD HANDLED MANY CRISES TOGETHER IN THE PAST, 
BUT HE WAS PHONING TO DISCUSS WHAT HE CONSIDERED IN ONE 
SENSE THE SINGLE WORST CRISIS THEY HAD EVER FACED. 
MAMEDOV INTERJECTED THAT HE HOPED THE ACTING SECRETARY 
WAS OVERDRAMATIZING THE SITUATION, TO WHICH MR. TALBOTT 
SAID HE ONLY WISHED MAMEDOV COULD CONVINCE HIM OF THAT. 
THE ACTING SECRETARY ASKED MAMEDOV IF HE WAS AWARE OF 
WHAT RUSSIAN FM PRIMAKOV HAD DONE WITH THE DRAFT OF THE 
SECRETARY'S SPEECH TO BE DELIVERED IN STUTTGART ON 
SEPTEMBER 6. MAMEDOV SAID HE WAS UNAWARE OF THE 
SITUATION. 
5. -(151- THE ACTING SECRETARY EXPLAINED THAT HE ASKED 
AMBASSADOR PICKERING TO PREVIEW THE SPEECH WITH THE GOR 
IN THE SPIRIT OF AVOIDING PROBLEMS OF THE SORT THAT 
AROSE AFTER THE PRAGUE SPEECH. HE SAID HE ASSUMED 
MAMEDOV FAXED THE DRAFT TO PRIMAKOV IN BONN; MAMEDOV 
CONFIRMED THIS. THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THAT THE 
RUSSIAN FM HAD DISCLOSED ITS CONTENTS IN A MEETING WITH 
GERMAN FM KINKEL AND USED IT TO CRITICIZE U.S. POLICY. 
ALTHOUGH THE BREACH OF PROTOCOL MAY HAVE BOTHERED THE 
HOST GOVERNMENT, MR. TALBOTT SAID HE WAS NOT CONCERNED 
ABOUT ITS EFFECTS ON U.S.-GERMAN RELATIONS, WHICH WERE 
VERY STRONG. HE ADDED THAT HE INTENDED TO SPEAK TO 
WOLFGANG ISCHINGER, POLITICAL DIRECTOR OF THE GERMAN 
MFA, TO EXPLAIN THE SITUATION. ISCHINGER UNDERSTOOD THE 
IMPORTANCE OF CLOSE USG-GOR COOPERATION. 
6. *Si- THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THE INCIDENT 
SERIOUSLY CALLED INTO QUESTION THE ABILITY OF THE USG 
AND GOR TO WORK TOGETHER USING CURRENT CHANNELS AND 
GROUND RULES. MAMEDOV THEN NOTED THAT HE HAD HAD NO WAY 
OF KNOWING OR CONTROLLING WHAT HIS FOREIGN MINISTER 
WOULD DO WITH THE DRAFT ONCE HE RECEIVED IT. MAMEDOV 
SAID HE HAD TOLD PRIMAKOV THAT THE USG HAD SHARED THE 
DRAFT IN CONFIDENCE, WITH NO INTENTION OF NEGOTIATING 
THE TEXT.

7. MR. TALBOTT REPEATED THAT THE BREACH WAS A VERY 
SMALL ISSUE IN U.S.—GERMAN RELATIONS, BUT A BIG ISSUE IN 
U.S.—RUSSIAN RELATIONS. ALTHOUGH THE ACTING SECRETARY 
BELIEVED HE HAD DEVELOPED A GOOD RELATIONSHIP WITH 
PRIMAKOV, MR. TALBOTT SAID HE REGARDED THE BREACH AS AN 
ACT OF PERSONAL BETRAYAL BY PRIMAKOV. MR. TALBOTT ADDED 
THAT THE CRISIS CAST CLOUDS OVER HIS ABILITY TO CARRY ON 
CONSTRUCTIVE MEETINGS WITH THE GOR. FINALLY, HE NOTED 
THAT HE WOULD DO HIS BEST TO OVERCOME THESE PROBLEMS. 
8. (S.)._ MAMEDOV RESPONDED THAT MANY PEOPLE CAN MAKE 
LIFE DIFFICULT AND PLACE DIPLOMATS IN NARROW CORNERS. 
DIPLOMACY IS A RISKY BUSINESS. HE SAID HE WAS TOTALLY 
COMMITTED TO FINDING SOLUTIONS TO PROBLEMS. HE 
REASSURED THE ACTING SECRETARY OF HIS WILLINGNESS TO 
HELP RESOLVE THIS PROBLEM. 
9. MR. TALBOTT NOTED THAT GOR POLICY TOWARD 
U.S.—RUSSIAN RELATIONS IN EUROPE HAD BEEN MOVING ON TWO 
TRACKS. ON THE FIRST TRACK, THE RUSSIANS SOUGHT TO 
PREVENT OR OBSTRUCT NATO ENLARGEMENT AND TO CREATE 
DIVISIONS BETWEEN THE U.S. AND ITS ALLIES. THE SECOND 
TRACK, HOWEVER, FOCUSED ON FINDING SOLUTIONS TO COMMON 
PROBLEMS. THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THIS EPISODE COULD 
ONLY BE INTERPRETED AS INDICATING THAT TRACK ONE IS 
PREDOMINANT. MAMEDOV STRESSED AGAIN HIS COMMITMENT TO 
STAYING ON THE SECOND TRACK OF SOLVING PROBLEMS. 
10. (U) THE CALL ENDED AT 1303 EDT.
