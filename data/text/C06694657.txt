3. SUMMARY: PRIME MINISTER BOLGER AND FOREIGN MINISTER 
MCKINNON WARMLY WELCOMED DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT To NEW 
ZEALAND JANUARY 30-31 AND WERE CLEARLY DELIGHTED BY THE 
GOOD NEWS HE BROUGHT OF A WHITE HOUSE INVITATION TO 
BOLGER FOR MARCH 27. THkY APPRECIATED THIS CULMINATION 
OF THE CLINTON ADMINISTRATION'S NEW CONTACT POLICY 
VIS-A-VIS NEW ZEALAND AND WILLINGNESS TO PUT THE 
RELATIONSHIP ON A SOUNDER FOOTING, CONSISTENT WITH OUR 
MANY SHARED REGIONAL AND GLOBAL INTERESTS AND AREAS OF 
COOPERATION. BOLGER TRACED THE EVENT TO TALKS HE HAD 
WITH PRESIDENT CLINTON AT THE SEATTLE APEC SUMMIT IN 
1993. TALBOTT'S NEWS ON INTELLIGENCE SHARING WAS 
LIKEWISE WARMLY GREETED. BOLGER AGREED HIS MEETING WITH 
THE PRESIDENT SHOULD HAVE A BROAD TRADE AND 
NON-PROLIFERATION AGENDA, INCLUDING, BUT NOT FOCUSING 
SOLELY ON, THE NUCLEAR SHIP VISIT ISSUE. TALBOTT 
UNDERSCORED THE CONSISTENCY OF THE USG POSITION ON SHIP 
VISITS AND NCND AND EXPRESSED THE HOPE THAT EVENTUALLY 
NEW ZEALANDERS WILL REALIZE THE IMPORTANCE OF A RESUMED 
ALLIANCE RELATIONSHIP AND CHANGE THEIR ANTI-NUCLEAR 
LEGISLATION. 
4. MCKINNON AND DEFENSE MINISTER COOPER SAID RENEWED 
HIGH-LEVEL CONTACTS WOULD HELP CREATE AN ATMOSPHERE THAT 
IS ESSENTIAL TO EVENTUAL CHANGE IN THE LEGISLATION. IF 
THE ISSUE IS KEPT OFF THE PUBLIC AGENDA UNTIL AFTER THE 
NEXT ELECTION (EXPECTED IN 1996), MCKINNON HOPED CHANGE 
WOULD BE POSSIBLE THEREAFTER. MEANWHILE NEW ZEALAND 
WOULD CONTINUE TO TAKE ON INTERNATIONAL 
RESPONSIBILITIES, INCLUDING A FINANCIAL CONTRIBUTION TO 
KEDO, WHICH WOULD HAVE TO BE KEPT QUIET FOR A WHILE SO 
AS NOT TO BE PERCEIVED AS A PAY-OFF FOR THE BOLGER WHITE 
HOUSE VISIT. AMIDST THEIR PLEASURE AT THE IMPROVED 
BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP, THE NEW ZEALAND CABINET 
MINISTERS GAVE MUTED VOICE TO THEIR CONCERN OVER THE 
U.S. DEIP DECISION TO SUBSIDIZE DAIRY EXPORTS IN ASIA. 
END SUMMARY.
