GHANAIAN ACCEPTANCE OF LIBERiAN 
REFUGEES 
5. -{-19} THE DEPUTY SECRETARY TELEPHONED PRESIDENT 
RAWLINGS TO REQUEST GHANAIAN ASSISTANCE WITH THE 
.REFUGEE-LADEN FREIGHTER "BULK CHALLENGER," WHICH IS OFF 
THE COAST OF GHANA. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY URGED 
PRESIDENT RAWLINGS TO ACCEPT LEGITIMATE LIBERIAN' 
REFUGEES ON THE SHIP. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ESTIMATED 
THAT THERE ARE 1,000-1,500 LEGITIMATE LIBERIAN REFUGEES 
ABOARD THE FREIGHTER WHO ARE ELIGIBLE TO STAY IN GHANA. 
HE MADE CLEAR THE USG DID NOT EXPECT THE GHANAIANS TO 
ACCEPT ECOMOG DESERTERS, FACTION FIGHTERS, NOR THIRD 
COUNTRY NATIONALS WHO THE GHANAIANS ARE WITHIN THEIR 
RIGHTS TO REPATRIATE TO THEIR HOME COUNTRIES. 
6. -(-8-)- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY EXPRESSED SYMPATHY FOR THE 
DIFFICULT SITUATION OF THE LIBERIAN REFUGEES AND SAID 
THAT PRESIDENT RAWLINGS HAS OUR SUPPORT. HE ADDED THAT 
THE MATTER HAD BEEN DISCUSSED AT THE HIGHEST LEVELS, AND 
CONVEYED NATIONAL SECURITY ADVISOR LAKE'S REGARDS FROM 
BURUNDI. 
7. -(7f.H- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY NOTED THE UNHCR IS ALREADY 
ON THE GROUND IN GHANA AND IS PREPARED TO SCREEN THE 
PASSENGERS FOR LEGITIMATE ASYLUM SEEKERS. HE PLEDGED 
USG FUNDING IN SUPPORT OF THE UNHCR OPERATION. HE 
COMMENTED THAT THE USG RECOGNIZES THAT THIS WILL ADD TO 
THE NUMBER OF REFUGEES ALREADY IN GHANA. HE SAID THE 
USG WILL SUPPORT ECOMOG EFFORTS TO SECURE THE PORT IN 
MONROVIA IN ORDER TO DISCOURAGE A MASS EXODUS OF 
REFUGEES TO GHANA AND OTHER WEST AFRICAN COUNTRIES. 
8. -I4H- PRESIDENT RAWLINGS TOLD THE DEPUTY SECRETARY HE 
HAD HOPED GHANA COULD KEEP THE REFUGEES FOR THREE DAYS 
AND PROVIDE THEM WITH PROPER FOOD AND MEDICAL CARE 
BEFORE SENDING THEM BACK TO A SAFE PLACE IN MONROVIA. 
IT HAS BEEN DIFFICULT, HOWEVER, TO CONVINCE HIS PEOPLE 
TO ADOPT THAT COURSE OF ACTION. PRESIDENT RAWLINGS 
NOTED THAT SOME OF THE LIBERIAN REFUGEES ALREADY IN 
GHANA ARE CREATING SOCIAL.PROBLEMS, INCLUDING ENGAGING 
IN PROSTITUTION AND IN COCAINE USE. THERE IS A GROWING 
SENSE OF ANGER AND BITTERNESS AMONG GHANAIANS AGAINST 
THE REFUGEES. PRESIDENT RAWLINGS TOLD THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY HE WISHED HE COULD HELP, BUT STATED THAT HE 
KNOWS HOW HIS PEOPLE FEEL ON THE ISSUE. 
9. +94- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY EXPRESSED SYMPATHY AND 
CONCERN FOR THE DILEMMA IN WHICH GHANA FOUND ITSELF, AND 
LAUDED RAWLINGS AND HIS PEOPLE FOR THE FORTITUDE WITH 
WHICH THEY HAVE FACED THE LIBERIAN CRISIS. HE ARGUED, 
HOWEVER, THAT THERE IS NO SAFE PLACE IN MONROVIA TO 
WHICH TO RETURN THE REFUGEES. THE USG FEARS THE 
FREIGHTER COULD BECOME A "PLAGUE SHIP," WITH HUNDREDS 
AND POSSIBLY THOUSANDS OF REFUGEES DYING AS PORT AFTER 
PORT TURNS THE SHIP AWAY. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY . 
COMMENTED ON THE POTENTIAL TRAGEDY OF MARRING GHANA'S 
OUTSTANDING REPUTATION BY SUCH AN OCCURRENCE. THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY REPEATED HIS ADMIRATION FOR PRESIDENT 
RAWLINGS AND ALL OF HIS POSITIVE WORK. HE NOTED THAT 
THE HIGHEST LEVELS OF THE USG, INCLUDING NATIONAL 
SECURITY ADVISOR ANTHONY LAKE, APPRECIATE GHANA'S 
DIFFICULTIES, BUT URGE RAWLINGS' ASSISTANCE IN STAVING 
OFF A HUMANITARIAN DISASTER. 
10. -4,84- PRESIDENT RAWLINGS REPLIED THAT HE WOULD 
"CONSULT" WITH HIS PEOPLE AND ASSURED THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY THAT HE WOULD DO HIS BEST. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY ADVISED PRESIDENT RAWLINGS THAT HE WOULD BRIEF 
SECRETARY CHRISTOPHER ON THEIR CONVERSATION, AND AGAIN 
EXPRESSED USG SUPPORT FOR GHANA. 
(U) THE CALL ENDED AT 1714 EDT. 
11. END TEXT.
