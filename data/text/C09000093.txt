ELIZABETH 13. MOYNIHAN

Hon. Strobe Talbott

Washington, D.C. 
Dear Mr. Secretary: 
(Sounds great!) Strobe - congratulations to you, 
Warren Christopher and the President - perhaps in 
reverse order. 
You'll not be surprised that I write about India. It 
was twenty years ago that Pat negotiated the PL 480 Rupee 
settlement which led to your trip covering Henry 
Kissinger's visit to sign the agreement. 
Included in the agreement was the creation of the 
Indo-U.S. SUbcommission on Education and Culture. 
As a member, I will be going to Delhi for the meetings 
on February 14 and 15. Pat (and possibly a few other 
Senators) will also go to mark the anniversary. 
The U.S. has so little involvement with India that 
this odd quasi-government, impoverished institution 
is one of the best (almost the only) things going 
between the two Democracies. ;However, the Rupee funds 
have been used up years earlier than expected and the 
Subcommission may disappear soon. This puzzles the 
Indians - who still think of us as rich and have 
increased their conttibution to this joint endeavor - 
so we hope to keep it alive. 
As you know, India is still feeling the aftershocks 
of the collapse of the Soviet Union. It is such a critical 
period with the Prime Minister and Finance Minister 
trying to move away from state socialism, open markets 
and encourage foreign investment. Tom Pickering did a 
terrific job there and made a difference during the 
few months he was in Delhi. He recognized the positive 
results of the educational and cultural projects and 
exchanges and had a plan to continue them. I am not 
sure the Pickering Plan could be implemented now, but 
there is a way to restructure to save and continue 
this work.

If you have any interest (or - less likely - time) 
to hear about this, two or three of us could brief

Incidentally, on a few occasions Hillary R.C. has 
mentioned that she would love to go to India and we 
lhave talked of doing so. Such a visit would be a 
igreat way to heal bruised feelings created by not 
having an Ambassador in Delhi for so long. Of course, 
as I told her, I doubt she could visit India unless 
it there was an American Ambassador there. 
If you are on the verge of naming someone, it would 
add a positive note to our meetings Feb. 14 and 15 
if the nomination was known or announced. 
Great luck and I hope you enjoy your assignment and 
that we all prosper because of it. 
Sincerely,
