2. (((.SUMMARY. ON THE MARGINS OF THE G-8 MINISTERIAL IN
MIYAZAKI, JAPAN, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT AND EC
COMMISSIONER PATTEN DISCUSSED IVANOV'S HEATED REACTION TO
THE EARLIER G-8 DISCUSSION ON CHECHNYA, THE DIFFICULTY OF
DELIVERING HUMANITARIAN ASSISTANCE TO CHECHNYA AND CROSS-
BORDER CONCERNS IN GENERAL AND THE APPARENT EASING OF
TENSIONS ON THE BORDER BETWEEN GEORGIA AND CHECHNYA. THE
DEPUTY SECRETARY STRESSED THE NEED TO CONTINUE TO RAISE
CHECHNYA WITH THE RUSSIANS. HE BRIEFED PATTEN ON HIS
RECENT TRIP TO THE BALTICS AND THE COMBINATION OF REALISM
AND DETERMINATION OF THE BALT1C FOREIGN MINISTERS REGARDING
EU ENLARGEMENT AND NATO EXPANSION. THEY SHARED VIEWS ON
THE PHILOSOPHY BEHIND PUTIN'S STATE OF THE UNION ADDRESS
AND THE EU'S DECISION TO RESTART ITS TECHNICAL AID PROGRAMS
TO RUSSIA, FOCUSING ON INSTITUTION BUILDING. THEY WERE
SKEPTICAL ABOUT WHETHER THE SITUATION IN BELARUS IS
IMPROVING, AND EXCHANGED VIEWS ON THE CURRENT SITUATION IN
SERBIA AND THE BALKANS. PATTEN EXPLAINED THAT HE
PERSONALLY FAVORED A LARGER FUNDING INCREASE FOR KEDO, BUT
WAS OVERRULED BY MEMBER STATES. END SUMMARY.
3. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY MET WITH EUROPEAN COMMISSIONER
FOR EXTERNAL AFFAIRS CHRISTOPHER PATTEN ON JULY 13, 2000 IN
MIYAZAKI, JAPAN ON'HE MARGINS OF THE G-8 FOREIGN
MINISTERS'EETING. HE WAS ACCOMPANIED BY UNDER SECRETARY
FOR POLITICAL AF'FAIRS THOMAS R. PICKERING, EB ASSISTANT
SECRETARY E. ANTHONY WAYNE, S/NIS DEPUTY JOHN BEYRLE, D
SPECIAL ASSISTANT TINA KAIDANOW, P SPECIAL ASSISTANT DAN
SHIELDS, AND NOTETAKER-LAURIE TROST. COMMISSIONER PATTEN
WAS ACCOMPANIED BY POLITICAL DIRECTOR FERNANDO VAENZULA
MARZO, DEPUTY HEAD OF PATTEN'S CABINET CHRISTIAN LEFFLER,
G-8 OFFICER PHILLIPE COESSENS, AND PHILIPPE DUPONTEIL FROM
THE EC DELEGATION IN JAPAN.

CHECHNYA
T). ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND PATTEN MET AFTER THE
COMPLETION OF THE LAST WORKING SESSION OF THE G-8 FOREIGN
MINISTERS. PATTEN OFFERED HIS OPINION THAT IVANOV'S HEATED
INTERVENTION ON CHECHNYA (SEPTEL) WAS A LONG WAY OF'ELLING
G-8 MEMBERS NOT TO INTERFERE IN THIS CONFLICT. THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY AGREED, BUT NOTED THAT IVANOV COOLED OFF ALMOST
IMMEDIATELY AND BEGAN ANSWERING QUESTIONS POSED BY THE
OTHER FOREIGN MINISTERS. IT WAS IMPORTANT TO DISCUSS
CHECHNYA AND CONTINUE TO RAISE OUR CONCERNS, HE EMPHASIZED,
BECAUSE COMMENTS DO REGISTER WITH THE RUSSIANS.
5. (HFM) PATTEN REPLIED THAT THE DELIVERY OF ASSISTANCE TO
CHECHNYA WAS A CONCERN. THE EU'S HUMANITARIAN MISSION HAD
FOUND THE SITUATION FAR WORSE THANRANTICIPATED, WITH MORE
DISPLACED PERSONS, WORSE CONDITIONS, AND LESS CHANCE OF
RETURNING To GROZNY. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REFERRED TO HIS
DISCUSSION OF CROSS-BORDER CONCERNS WITH PATTEN SIX WEEKS

5000 REBELS, THERE ARE MAYBE 450 FIGHTERS AND THE REMAINDER
ARE WOMEN AND CHILDREN.
6. ~ PATTEN MENTIONED THAT THE GEORGIANS HAD REQUESTED EU
FINANCING FOR BORDER GUARDS, AND THAT THE EU HAD SENT A
FACT-FINDING MISSION AT GEORGIAN REQUEST TO THE GEORGIAN
BORDERS WITH CHECHNYA TO ANALYZE GEORGIA'S NEED FOR
ASSISTANCE. PATTEN NOTED THAT THE EU HAS NO INSTRUMENTS TO
PAY FOR THE BORDER GUARD DIRECTLY BUT COULD PROVIDE
TECHNICAL SECURITY TRAINING. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY
COMMENTED THAT THE BULK OF U.S. ASSISTANCE TO GEORGIA IS
DEVOTED TO BORDER SECURITY AND PROMISED TO PROVIDE THE EU
DETAILS OF THE U.S. PROGRAM AND SHARE ANY AVAILABLE NEEDS
ASSESSMENT INFORMATION.
7. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REMARKED THAT IT WAS DIFFICULT
TO KNOW PRECISELY WHERE THE SITUATION IN CHECHNYA WAS
HEADING. THE CHECHEN SIDE IS FAR FROM DEFEATED AND IS NOT
QUITTING: THE VICIOUS CYCLE IS NOT ENDING. PATTEN AGREED,
REAFFIRMING THE NEED FOR A POLITICAL DIALOGUE BETWEEN THE
TWO SIDES IN THE CONFLICT.
8. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY CONVEYED HIS IMPRESSION THAT,
REGRETTABLY, THE OTHER G-8 MEMBERS -- LLOYD AXWORTHY ASIDE
WERE CLOSE TO A CONSENSUS TO BACK OFF ON CHECHNYA.
PATTEN RELUCTANTLY CONCURRED, NOTING THAT EU MEMBER STATES
WERE EAGER TO GET "BACK TO BUSINESS AS USUAL." THE EARLIER
TOUGH EU LINE ON CHECHNYA MAY HAVE BEEN MORE OF AN ANOMALY
THAN THE NORM, HE SAID. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REITERATED
THE NEED TO KEEP PRESSING RUSSIA ON ITS ACCOUNTABILITYNIN
CHECHNYA AND ITS RESPONSIBILITY TO ADDRESS HUMAN RIGHTS AND
REFUGEE CONCERNS; THE RUSSIANS'OCUS ON A PURELY MILITARY
SOLUTION SIMPLY WOULD NOT WORK. TALBOTT NOTED DIFFERENCES
BETWEEN THE RUSSIAN MILITARY AND SOME POLITICAL LEADERS ON
WHAT TO DO IN CHECHNYA, NOTING THAT THE RUSSIAN MILITARY
WAS INVESTED IN A HARD LINE WHICH ARGUED FOR KEEPING
INTERNATIONAL AID AND PERSONNEL OUT.
9. ~ TALBOTT SUGGESTED THAT PATTEN READ CLOSELY PUTIN'S
STATE OF'HE UNION ADDRESS, A COPY OF WHICH HAD BEEN HANDED
OUT TO THE ASSEMBLED G-8 MINISTERS BY FM IVANOV, AS A
SOPHISTICATED ARTICULATION OF COMPLEX RUSSIAN DOMESTIC
POLICY GOALS. THOUGH "THE SPEECH WAS NOT ALL SWEETNESS
AND LIGHT" AND THE TREATMENT OF THE STRONG STATE ISSUE WAS
HEDGED, TALBOTT COMMENTED, THE SPEECH HAD THE RIGHT TILT,
AND WE ALL SHOULD F'IND WAYS TO ENCOURAGE THE POSITIVE
TENDENCIES.
THE BALTICS AND'EU ENLARGEMENT

TRIP TO THE BALTICS, INDICATING THAT HE WAS IMPRESSED WITH
THE REALISTIC BALTIC ATTITUDE TOWARDS NATO ACCESSION,
REFLECTED IN THE VILNIUS STATEMENT AND INDIVIDUAL BALTIC
STATES'FFORTS TO UNDERTAKE THE NECESSARY ECONOMIC AND
MILITARY REFORMS. THE BALTIC STATES WERE TRYING TO READ
THE SIGNALS FROM BOTH NATO AND RUSSIA, HE COMMENTED; THE
HAD NO FALSE EXPECTATIONS BUT WERE TAKING DETERMINED

AN ENTITLEMENT. PATTEN ATTRIBUTED PUTIN'S ATTITUDE TO

RUSSIA. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AGREED, NOTING THAT THE
ESTONIANS WERE QUITE HELPFUL ON BORDER SECURITY IN THE
CAUCASUS.
EU AID AND PROSPECTS FOR REFORM IN RUSSIA
12. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND PATTEN THEN TURNED TO A
DISCUSSION OF AID TO RUSSIA. PATTEN MUSED IT WOULD BE
INTERESTING TO SEE HOW THE RUSSIANS WOULD RESPOND WHEN THE
EU RESTARTS ITS TACIS AID PROGRAM, FROZEN IN THE INITIAL
REACTION TO THE CHECHEN CONFLICT. HE SAID THAT THE EU HAD
ONLY SPENT $ 30 MILLION IN TACIS FUNDS THIS YEAR, BUT WOULD
UNFREEZE THE REST OF THE SPENDING (THROUGH 2003) THIS YEAR,
AMOUNTING TO BUSINESS AS USUAL, PARTLY REFLECTING FRANCE'
REQUEST AND PARTLY THE COMMISSION'S. HE MENTIONED PLANS TO
TAKE AN INVENTORY OF ASSISTANCE TO RUSSIA AND SEE WHAT
WORKED. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY COMMENTED ON HIS EARLIER
DISCUSSION WITH FRENCH FM VEDRINE ON THE PROPER EMPHASIS
FOR AID TO RUSSIA; HE UNDERSTOOD VEDRINE'S POINT TO BE 'IHAT
WE ARE IN DANGER OF DEFINING REFORM TOO NARROWLY AS
ECONOMIC CONDITIONALITY AND WE ARE OVERLOOKING THE
IMPORTANCE OF CULTIVATING 'EFFECTIVE INSTITUTIONS AND A
STRONG RUSSIAN STATE. ~ THIS WAS A MISDIAGNOSIS; HE POINTED
OUT THAT SINCE 1994 BOTH THE U.S. AND EU HAD GIVEN PRIORITY
TO INSTITUTION BUILDING IN RUSSIA.
13. ~ PATTEN ASSESSED THE EU PROGRAMS HAD BEEN TOO
SCATTERSHOT, AND THAT THE EU NOW WANTS TO SHARPEN ITS FOCUS
ON INSTITUTION-BUILDING. HE NOTED THAT SOME THINGS RUSSIA
COULD DO WOULD MAKE A HUGE DIFFERENCE — LIKE ACCEPTING
INTERNATIONAL ACCOUNTING STANDARDS: TALBOTT SAID THAT
PUTIN AND WORLD BANK PRESIDENT JIM WOLFENSOHN HAVE
DEVELOPED A GOOD RELATIONSHIP AND THIS WOULD PROVE HELPFUL
IN PRESSING THE RUSSIANS TO MAKE THE NECESSARY ECONOMIC
REFORMS. HE SAID THAT WE HAD ALREADY'ROVIDED TECHNICAL
EXPERTISE TO THE RUSSIANS, BUT INTENSIFIED ENGAGEMENT
FOR EXAMPLE VISITS BY YOUNG RUSSIAN BUSINESSMEN AND
POLICYMAKERS TO PRICE WATERHOUSE AND OTHER U.S. ACCOUNTING
FIRMS --MIGHT PROVE PARTICULARLY USEFUL.

BELARUS
14. ~ PATTEN THEN ASKED FOR TALBOTT'S OPINION ON A
RESUMPTION OF DIALOGUE WITH BELARUS, AN IDEA WHICH HE SAID
WAS GAINING CREDIBILITY AMONG SOME EU MEMBERS. PATTEN SAID
HE HAD DETECTED PERHAPS A SLIGHT CHANGE IN THE POLITICAL
ATMOSPHERE IN MINSK, BUT THAT HE WAS STILL VERY SKEPTICAL.
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ECHOED THIS SKEPTICISM, NOTING THAT
THERE WASN'T MUCH REASON FOR ENCOURAGEMENT WITH MINSK'S
REFUSAL TO GIVE THE OPPOSITION ANY CREDIBLE ROLE,
POLITICALLY-MOTIVATED DISAPPEARANCES, AND THE SUPPRESSION
OF DEMOCRACY. HE CAUTIONED THAT THE U.S. AND EU SHOULD BE
CAREFUL ABOUT THE SIGNALS THEY SEND, ALTHOUGH WE SUPPORT
THE CONDITIONS THAT THE TROIKA WOULD CONVEY TO MINSK.
THE BALKANS
.15.~ PATTEN MENTIONED THE SITUATION IN SERBIA AND
MONTENEGROs AND SAID HE HAD JUST SEEN A VERY GLOOMY, VERY
CONCERNED DJUKANOVIC IN BRUSSELS. TALBOTT AGREED THAT THE
SITUATION LOOKED INCREASINGLY VOLATILE, PARTICULARLY AS A
CONSEQUENCE OF THE RECENT UNILATERAL MOVE BY MILOSEVIC TO
MANIPULATE THE FRY CONSTITUTION. HE UNDERSCORED THE
IMPORTANCE OF REASSURING DJUKANOVIC THAT MEANINGFUL
ECONOMIC SUPPORT WOULD KEEP FLOWING TO MONTENEGRO, AS WELL
AS THE URGENCY OF KEEPING MILOSEVIC --WHO COULD BE CALLED
AN EVIL GENIUS FOR HIS ABILITY TO STAY IN POWER AFTER
BRINGING SERBIA TO ITS KNEES -- UNCERTAIN ABOUT OUR
REACTION TO A VJ ATTACK ON MONTENEGRO. PATTEN AGREED.
ELSEWHERE IN THE BALKANS, FATTEN NOTED, THINGS WERE LOOKING
BETTER, PARTICULARLY IN MACEDONIA AND CROATIA. HE THOUGHT
THAT RECONSTRUCTION IN KOSOVO WAS MOVING FORWARD, ALTHOUGH
THE SECURITY SITUATION WAS WORRYING. HE ADDED THAT
ROMANIA'S POLITICAL AND ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT WAS ALSO CAUSE
FOR CONCERN, AND TALBOTT CONCURRED.
KEDO FUNDING
16. ~ EB ASSISTANT SECRETARY WAYNE RAISED KEDO, NOTING
THE SMALL INCREASE (APPROXIMATELY 2.5 MILLION EURO)
AUTHORIZED IN THE EU BUDGET. COMMISSIONER PATTEN MENTIONED
THAT HE WAS KEEN TO GET A BIGGER INCREASE IN KEDO FUNDING,
BUT THAT HE WAS BEATEN BACK, WHICH WAS A PITY BECAUSE THERE
WERE FUNDS IN THE BUDGET. PATTEN VOLUNTEERED THAT THE EC
WAS LOOKING AT SOME SMALL MEASURES FOR NORTH KOREA, SUCH AS
OPEN MARKET ACCESS FOR TEXTILES, WHICH PATTEN LABELED A
"SMALL BUT SENSIBLE" WAY TO SEND A MESSAGE. HE SAID THIS
DIDN'T GET MUCH SUPPORT FROM MEMBER STATES, BUT IT WAS
RELATIVELY INEXPENSIVE AND SENSIBLE AND THAT HE HOPED THERE
MIGHT BE PROGRESS ON MOVING SOME PROGRAM AHEAD IN THE FALL.
