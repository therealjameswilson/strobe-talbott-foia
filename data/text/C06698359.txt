4. 4Si ACTING SECRETARY TALBOTT TOLD DEPUTY FM MAMEDOV 
THAT THEY HAD HANDLED MANY CRISES TOGETHER IN THE PAST, 
BUT HE WA'S PHONING TO DISCUSS WHAT HE CONSIDERED THE 
SINGLE WORST CRISIS IN U.S.-RUSSIAN RELATIONS THEY HAD 
EVER FACED. MAMEDOV INTERJECTED THAT HE HOPED THE 
ACTING SECRETARY WAS OVERDRAMATIZING THE SITUATION, TO 
WHICH MR. TALBOTT SAID HE ONLY WISHED MAMEDOV COULD 
CONVINCE HIM OF THAT. THE ACTING SECRETARY ASKED 
MAMEDOV IF HE WAS AWARE OF WHAT RUSSIAN FM PRIMAKOV HAD 
DONE WITH THE DRAFT OF THE SECRETARY'S SPEECH TO BE 
DELIVERED IN STUTTGART ON SEPTEMBER 6. MAMEDOV SAID HE 
WAS UNAWARE OF THE SITUATION. 
5. -(131- THE ACTING SECRETARY EXPLAINED THAT HE ASKED 
AMBASSADOR PICKERING TO SHARE A DRAFT OF'THE SPEECH WITH 
THE GOR*IN THE SPIRIT OF OPENNESS. HE SAID HE ASSUMED 
MAMEDOV FAXED THE DRAFT TO PRIMAKOV IN BONN; MAMEDOV 
CONFIRMED THIS. THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THAT THE 
HUbS1AN FM HAD DISCLOSED ITS CONTENTS IN A MEETING WITH 
GERMAN FM KINKEL AND USED IT TO CRITICIZE U.S. POLICY. 
ALTHOUGH THE BREACH OF PROTOCOL WAS OFFENSIVE TO THE 
HOST GOVERNMENT, MR. TALBOTT SAID HE WAS NOT CONCERNED 
ABOUT ITS EFFECTS ON U.S.-GERMAN RELATIONS, WHICH WERE 
VERY STRONG. HE ADDED THAT HE INTENDED TO SPEAK TO 
WOLFGANG ISCHINGER, POLITICAL DIRECTOR OF THE GERMAN 
MFA, TO EXPLAIN THE SITUATION. ISCHINGER UNDERSTOOD THE 
IMPORTANCE OF CLOSE USG-GOR COOPERATION. 
6. -(44- THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THE INCIDENT 
SERIOUSLY CALLED INTO QUESTION THE ABILITY OF THE USG 
AND GOR TO WORK TOGETHER USING CURRENT CHANNELS AND 
GROUND RULES. MAMEDOV THEN NOTED THAT HE HAD HAD NO WAY 
OF KNOWING OR CONTROLLING WHAT HIS FOREIGN MINISTER 
WOULD DO WITH THE DRAFT ONCE HE RECEIVED IT. MAMEDOV 
SAID HE HAD TOLD PRIMAKOV THAT THE USG HAD SHARED THE 
DRAFT IN CONFIDENCE , WITH NO INTENTION OF NEGOTIATING 
THE TEXT.

7. 4g} MR. TALBOTT STRESSED THAT HE HAD FULL 
CONFIDENCE IN MAMEDOV'S RECONSTRUCTION OF EVENTS. HE 
REPEATED THAT THE BREACH WAS A VERY SMALL ISSUE IN 
U.S.-GERMAN RELATIONS, BUT A BIG ISSUE IN U.S.-RUSSIAN 
RELATIONS. ALTHOUGH THE ACTING SECRETARY BELIEVED HE 
HAD DEVELOPED A GOOD RELATIONSHIP WITH PRIMAKOV AND WAS 
GRATEFUL FOR THE GENEROSITY OF HIS TIME AND ATTENTION 
CONSIDERING PRIMAKOV'S HIGHER RANK, MR. TALBOTT SAID HE 
REGARDED THE BREACH AS AN ACT OF PERSONAL BETRAYAL BY 
PRIMAKOV. MR. TALBOTT ADDED THAT THE CRISIS CAST CLOUDS 
OVER HIS ABILITY TO CARRY ON CONSTRUCTIVE MEETINGS WITH 
THE GOR. FINALLY, HE NOTED THAT HE WOULD DO HIS BEST TO 
OVERCOME THESE PROBLEMS. 
8. -Mr MAMEDOV RESPONDED THAT MANY PEOPLE CAN MAKE 
LIFE DIFFICULT AND PLACE DIPLOMATS IN NARROW CORNERS. 
DIPLOMACY IS A RISKY BUSINESS. HE SAID HE WAS TOTALLY 
COMMITTED TO FINDING S.OLUTIONS TO PROBLEMS. HE 
REASSURED THE ACTING SECRETARY OF HIS WILLINGNESS TO 
HELP RESOLVE THIS PROBLEM. 
9. -(44- MR. TALBOTT NOTED THAT GOR POLICY TOWARD 
U.S.-RUSSIAN RELATIONS IN EUROPE HAD BEEN MOVING ON TWO 
TRACKS. ON THE FIRST TRACK, THE RUSSIANS SOUGHT TO 
PREVENT OR aeSTRUCT NATO ENLARGEMENT AND TO CREATE 
DIVISIONS BETWEEN THE U.S. AND ITS ALLIES. THE SECOND 
TRACK, HOWEVER, FOCUSED ON FINDING SOLUTIONS TO COMMON 
PROBLEMS. THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID THIS EPISODE COULD 
ONLY BE INTERPRETED AS INDICATING THAT TRACK ONE IS 
PREDOMINANT. MAMEDOV STRESSED AGAIN HIS COMMITMENT TO 
STAYING ON THE SECOND TRACK OF SOLVING PROBLEMS. MR. 
TALBOTT SAID IF THE GOR TRULY WANTED TO RESOLVE THIS 
ISSUE, PRIMAKOV WOULD APPLAUD LOUDLY AND PUBLICLY DURING 
THE SECRETARY'S SPEECH IN STUTTGART AND WOULD FIND THE 
OPPORTUNITY TO SPEAK PRIVATELY TO THE SECRETARY 
REGARDING THIS EPISODE, BUT SUCH A WISH WAS ONLY 
DREAMING. 
10. —144 MAMEDOV CONCLUDED THAT TOO MUCH IN 
U.S.-RUSSIAN RELATIONS DEPENDS ON PURSUING PROBLEM 
SOLVING TO GET BOGGED DOWN IN DIVISION AND MISTRUST. 
MR. TALBOTT CONCLUDED BY AGREEING TO FOCUS ON POSITIVE 
ASPECTS OF THE RELATIONSHIP. MAMEDOV ECHOED HIS 
SENTIMENTS AND PROPOSED THEY SPEAK AGAIN SOON. 
11. (U) THE CALL ENDED AT 1303 EDT.
