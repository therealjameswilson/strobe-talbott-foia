NATO ENLARGEMENT 
5. 4e, TALBOTT EMPHASIZED THE ADMINISTRATION HAD NOT YET 
MADE ANY DECISION ON NEW NATO MEMBERS. THE U.S. DID NOT 
SUPPORT ANY PRE-CONCEIVED CLASSES, CATEGORIES, OR BLOCS TO 
DEFINE NEW ENTRANTS. BY EXTENSION, THE U.S. COULD NOT 
ACCEPT "LINKAGES" THAT TIE THE ENTRY OF ONE NATO ASPIRANT 
TO THAT OF ANOTHER. KOVACS RESPONDED THAT SOME OBSERVERS 
ARGUED THAT HUNGARY AND ROMANIA'S ENTRY SHOULD BE NEARLY 
SIMULTANEOUS TO AVOID TRIGGERING AN ARMS RACE BETWEEN THE 
TWO. MENTIONING ROMANIA SPECIFICALLY, KOVACS SAID HUNGARY 
STRONGLY SUPPORTED THE ENTRY OF AS MANY OF ITS NEIGHBORING 
COUNTRIES AS POSSIBLE. HE ADDED HUNGARY'S ENTRY SHOULD 
NOT BE DELAYED FOR THE SAKE OF ROMANIA'S ENTRY, BUT RATHER 
HUNGARY HOPED THAT ROMAN IA WOULD SPEED UP ITS DEMOCRATIC 
AND ECONOMIC TRANSFORMATION SO THAT IT AND HUNGARY COULD 
ENTER NATO TOGETHER.

COME," AND WE MUST DO ALL WE CAN TO CONTINUE TO ENCOURAGE 
FURTHER PROGRESS AND AVOID ANY POLARIZING EFFECTS, SHOULD 
ROMANIA NOT BE IN THE FIRST NATO TRANCHE. EUR DAS 
MARSHALL ADAIR ADDED THAT ROMAN IA HAD CONCERNS ABOUT A 
POTENTIAL VETO BY HUNGARY'S PARLIAMENT OVER ITS' ENTRY, IF 
HUNGARY JOINS FIRST. KOVACS RESPONDED THAT ROMANIA'S 
ENTRY WAS IN HUNGARY'S INTEREST, AND THE OVERWHELMING 
MAJORITY OF HUNGARY'S ELECTORATE SUPPORTED THE BILATERAL 
TREATY AND ROMANIAN ENTRY INTO NATO. AS A RESULT, KOVACS 
WAS "ABSOLUTELY CERTAIN" THAT THE CURRENT PARLIAMENT WOULD 
SUPPORT NATO ENTRY FOR ROMANIA. HOWEVER, KOVACS ADMITTED 
THE GOH COULD NOT GIVE AN IRON-CLAD GUARANTEE ON FUTURE 
PARLIAMENTARY ACTION -- JUST AS THE U.S. ADMINISTRATION 
COULD NOT FOR CONGRESS.
