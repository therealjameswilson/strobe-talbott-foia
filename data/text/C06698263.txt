SUBJECT: MEMORANDUM OF CONVERSATION BETWEEN ACTING 
SECRETARY TALBOTT AND POLISH MINISTER OF DEFENSE DOBRZANSKI 
1.. classified by the deputy secretary, reasons: 1.5 (c) 
and (d). 
2. summary. defense minister dobrzanski told acting 
secretary talbott august 29 that poland is actively 
seeking to-forge closer relations with all its neighbors, 
particularly ukraine and the baltics. poland is also 
restructuring its military to ensure complete civilian 
control. talbott congratulated dobrzanski on the progress 
poland had made toward meeting the requirements of the ' 
nato enlargement study, and urged poland to focus on the 
countries that will not soon join nato. he said the 
december nac ministerial would evaluate the results of the 
current intensified dialogue. it would then decide upon 
next steps, which would (in the u.s. view) include a 
summit in the first half of 1997, at which several 
countries would be invited to begin accession 
negotiations. talbott emphasized the importance of 
viewing enlargement in the larger context of european 
security, and of carefully managing the relationship with 
russia. dobrzanski expressed pleasure at the congruence 
of u.s. and polish views. talbott also expressed concern 
about possible new sales of polish arms to iran. he 
thanked the minister for ensuring rockwell's ability to 
compete for the huzar helicopter project. end summary. 
3. acting secretary talbott met with polish minister of 
national defense dobrzanski at the state department on 
august 29, 1996. among the participants on the polish 
side were deputy minister of defense karkoszka, chairman 
of the sejm defense committee szmajdzinski, and ambassador 
kozminski. in addition to mr. talbott, s/p director 
steinberg, acting eur assistant secretary Wayne, and pm 
pdas newsom participated on the american side. 
4. dobrzanski began by outlining poland's efforts to 
forge close, cooperative relations with all of its 
neighbors. he noted that relations with belarus are 
moving forward, and poland is building a relationship with 
russia. cooperation with ukraine and the-baltics is 
excellent, particularly in the military field. dobrzanski 
pointed to the joint polish-ukrainian peacekeeping 
battalion, and a similar battalion planned with lithuania, 
as evidence.of close security cooperation with these

and is helping lithuania with its air defenses. he added
that poland is restructuring its own military to conform 
to the western model of civilian control. "I believe we 
have done almost everything to achieve the trust of nato 
states in order to become a member," dobrzanski said. he 
then asked what additional steps poland could take. 
5. the acting secretary began his reply' by pointing out 
that president clinton's policy toward europe is driven by 
a vision of integration. central to this is the 
integration of the new democracies into nato. "what more 
can poland do?" he asked rhetorically; "more of the same." 
poland itself is the easy part, talbott continued. the 
country has made huge progress'toward meeting the criteria 
for nato membership, but we need to think as well about 
the countries that will not soon join nato. the u.s, has 
given a lot of thought to making sure the president can 
say that the first country to join nato will not be the 
last. the process of nato enlargement must coptribute to 
the larger process' of integration, rather than creating 
new dividing lines, talbott emphasized. He added that the 
U.S. was very pleased with Poland's emphasis on building 
ties with its neighbors to the North, East and South. 
6. dobrzanski then asked how the process of nato 
enlargement will occur. talbott replied that the U.S., 
and he personally, are deeply engaged with the 15 nato 
allies on this question. the nac ministerial is the 
appropriate forum to review progress on the ongoing 
individual dialogues. the conclusion will likely be that 
there should be a summitmeeting in the first half of - 
1997. the assembled leaders would then suggest certain 
countries begin accession negotiations. talbott asked 
that the above be treated as a confidential diplomatic 
exchange, since the alliance operates by consensus and 
agreement must still be sought. "i am optimistic we can 
establish and maintain a timetable," talbott continued. 
he noted that the next public statement will come at the 
secretary's speech in stuttgart, which will establish the 
context and strategic rationale for enlargement. 
7., s/p director steinberg added that enlargement cannot 
be seen in isolation; it should ensure security for all of 
europe. "we need," he continued, "to ensure the place of 
countries that don't join nato, especially russia."

8. dobrzanski replied that the acting secretary's 
presentation had touched his heart and mind. "i am glad 
we are in accordance in our thinking; it shows we are on 
the right path," the minister said. he noted that there 
are still many questions about russia's internal 
stability. he then asked talbott what would bp the future 
of u.s.-russian relations, and how he thought russia would 
develop. 
9. "the first question is easier," talbott replied, "but 
it depends on the second." u.s.-russian relations are 
guided by one simple, basic principle: we will support 
russia as long as it stays the course of reform. 
politically, this means democratic government, the ability 
of the russian people to choose their own leaders. 
economically, it means a free market. and on foreign 
policy, it means respect for the sovereignty of other 
countries. talbott said the prospects for russia 
remaining on course are high enough that we can base our 
policy on them. the essence of this policy is to hope for 
and encourage the best, while being prepared for the 
worst. he urged the poles to keep in mind the importance 
of what russia hears from those seeking to join nato, and 
suggested the u.s. and poland stay in close touch on this 
subject. 
10. dobrzanski said that his russian interlocutors had 
insisted that opposition to nato en'largement would lessen 
following the russian presidential elections; however, 
"these people are no longer with yeltsin." talbott 
responded by saying that russian policy toward enlargement 
would have two tracks: opposition and accommodation. "we 
must ensure that they fail on the first and succeed on the 
second," he said.
