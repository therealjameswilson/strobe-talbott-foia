2. -(-5-) Deputy Secretary Talbott met with German MFA 
Minister of State Werner Hoyer September 16 for a 
forty-five minute exchange of views on the IGC, NATO, and 
NATO-Russia relations. Hoyer noted some progress was 
being made in the IGC, but that a "crisis" would be 
necessary to energize member-states to make the key 
political decisions for a successful conclusion. On NATO, 
he supported the Deputy Secretary's argument that Germany 
and the US must pay particular attention to the 
sensitivities of those nations not in the first tranche of 
new NATO members. 
IGC Progress Report 
3. ic.) Hoyer began the meeting by briefing the Deputy 
Secretary on recent IGC developments. The Irish had been 
doing an excellent job adding substance and pushing the 
process along and forcing member-states to view the IGC 
within a broad political framework. He hoped significant 
progress would be made before the December Dublin summit 
on CFSP, and subsidiarity. The key -- and much more 
difficult -- question to be addressed by December, though, 
was "flexibility," constitutional provisions which would 
adapt EU structures to a potential union of 27 members. 
Hoyer emphasized the IGC was already a nightmare" with 15 
members and that flexibility was absolutely essential for 
an effective, enlarged EU. 
4. 191 Hoyer noted that progress to date had been slow 
and he felt a catalytic "crisis" at (or before) the 
December Dublin summit could be necessary to energize the 
member-states to focus on the key political decisions 
which needed to be made. He was most worried about the 
UK, which had been the major impediment, and also wondered 
whether it would be in a position to ratify the treaty two 
months after the May, 1997 eleCtions. If not, this would 
put the June EU summit at risk, and delay ratification to 
1998 -- a very bad year in view of French and German 
elections, and key decisions on the EU budget.and EMU.

5. A411 The Deputy Secretary noted the lack of close 
coordination despite the close relationship between EU and 
NATO enlargement. Hoyer expressed the German belief that , 
NATO and EU enlargement were inextricably linked and that 
EU enlargement could not be viewed as a residual of the 
NATO process. The Deputy Secretary said the 
Administration was focussed on how to handle those not in 
the initial group of membership candidates, so that they 
(and Russia as well) not feel abandoned. 
6. v54,- The Deputy Secretary then asked where Germany 
stood on Baltic EU membership. Clearly, Hoyer noted, 
Baltic membership in the EU was more acceptable to the 
Russians than Baltic membership in NATO. The problem was 
that EU membership could well be more difficult for the 
Balts themselves. Only Estonia was making the necessary 
progress to face the high level• of economic competition 
EU membership entailed. The Deputy Secretary expressed 
hope that Estonia could remain under consideration for the 
first tranche of new EU members. 
7. Gin On a related matter, Hoyer asked whether the 
enlargement issue was going to play a major role in the US 
elections. The Deputy Secretary responded in the 
negative, noting our elections were primarily about 
pocketbook issues. (Even the Gulf War didn't get George 
Bush reelected.) Talbott emphasized the President had 
adopted his NATO approach for reasons of grand strategy, 
not electioneering. 
Russia 
8. V The Deputy Secretary turned to relations with 
RusSia. The Secretary had outlined in his September 6 
Stuttgart speech our wish to work out a NATO-Russia 
charter. This would not be a legally-binding document, 
but a series of political commitments regulating the 
relationship. We need to proceed with this in parallel 
with the preparations for the 1997 NATO summit in which 
accession negotiations for new members would be announced.
