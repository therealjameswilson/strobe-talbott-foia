SUBJECT: A/UNDERSECRETARY HOLUM MEETING WITH ATOMIC 
ENERGY MINISTER ADAMOV, FEBRUARY 25, 1999 
CLASSIFIED BY AMBASSADOR COLLINS FOR REASON 1.5 (F) 
1. -f-S-)---SUMMARY. FOLLOWING DEPUTY SECRETARY OF STATE 
STROBE TALBOTT'S HIGH LEVEL MEETINGS IN MOSCOW, ACTING 
UNDERSECRETARY JOHN HOLUM ENGAGED RUSSIA'S MINISTER OF 
ATOMIC ENERGY YEVGENIY O. ADAMOV AT THE MINISTRY'S 
FOREIGN AFFAIRS HEADQUARTERS. ADAMOV HAD INITIALLY 
RESISTED THE MEETING, PROTESTING THAT HOLUM'S AGENDA 
WOULD BE LARGELY POLITICAL AND SHOULD BE DIRECTED TO 
THE MFA. AFTER ACQUIESCING, ADAMOV PROVED TO BE AN 
ENERGETIC, AND OFTEN COOPERATIVE HOST. TOPICS INCLUDED 
FORMATION OF A NUCLEAR COMMITTEE IN THE GORE-PRIMAKOV 
COMMISSION, THE HIGHLY ENRICHED URANIUM-LOW ENRICHED 
URANIUM (HEU-LEU) AGREEMENT, PLUTONIUM DISPOSITION 
NEGOTIATIONS, A SUGGESTION THAT RUSSIA REPROCESS U.S. 
SPENT NUCLEAR FUEL, CORE CONVERSION, AND IRAN. 
IRAN 
2. .!;* ADAMOV HIMSELF RAISED THE ISSUE OF IRAN MID 
MEETING, COMPLAINING THAT DESPITE ASSURANCES DURING THE 
GORE-KIRIYENKO MEETING LAST JULY THAT A COMMISSION OR 
CHANNEL WOULD BE-ESTABLISHED TO EXPLORE WAYS TO BRIDGE 
U.S. AND RUSSIAN DIFFERENCES ON RUSSIA'S NUCLEAR 
COOPERATION WITH IRAN, NOTHING HAS BEEN DONE. PAST 
MEETINGSWITH A NUMBER OF SENIOR U.S. OFFICIALS HAVE 
BEEN ON A PURELY POLITICAL PLANE HE SAID AND NO 
PRACTICAL STEPS HAVE BEEN OUTLINED. ADAMOV SAID THAT 
SINCE MARCH OF LAST YEAR HE HAS BEEN RECOUNTING THE 
HISTORY OF RUSSIA'S PREPARATION OF A CONTRACT FOR A 
RESEARCH CENTER IN IRAN. RUSSIA DISCUSSED VARIOUS 
OPTIONS FOR IRAN INCLUDING HEAVY WATER REACTORS; LIGHT 
WATER REACTORS, HOT CELLS FOR ISOTOPE PRODUCTION, AND 
REPROCESSING FACILITIES. HE SAID THIS WAS THE STANDARD 
"PACKAGE" FOR A RESEARCH CENTER. SUCH A NUCLEAR DEAL 
WOULD HAVE COMPLIED WITH THE 1992 RUSSIA-IRAN PEACEFUL 
USES OF ATOMIC ENERGY AGREEMENT. ADAMOV SAID THAT 
CONTRACT CONTINUES TO "LIE IN A DRAWER SOMEWHERE" 
DISCARDED FOR POLITICAL REASONS. ADAMOV ALSO SAID THAT 
A HEAVY WATER REACTOR PLACED UNDER IAEA INSPECTION 
WOULD NOT HAVE VIOLATED INTERNATIONAL NORMS. ADAMOV 
SAID MINATOM IS VERY DISCIPLINED, AND ALL ITS 
ACTIVITIES ARE CONSISTENT WITH NUCLEAR SUPPLIER GROUP 
GUIDELINES.

3. 44- ADAMOV CLAIMEDTHAT RUSSIA'S EXPORT CONTROL 
SYSTEM WORKS, BUT THE SYSTEM NEVER LOOKED AT RUSSIAN-
IRANIAN NUCLEAR COOPERATION THROUGH THE LENS OF THE 
"NOTHING BUT BUSHEHR" PLEDGE BY FORMER PRIME MINISTER 
CHERNOMYRDIN. NONE OF THE NUCLEAR CENTERS WERE ADVISED 
HOW, OR EVEN WHETHER TO INTERPRET CHERNOMYRDIN'S PLEDGE 
AND THE PLEDGE WAS NOT FOLLOWED. TO REMEDY THIS, 
ADAMOV URGED CREATION OF AN EXPERT WORKING GROUP TO 
REPLACE "NOTHING BUT BUSHEHR" WITH SOMETHING MORE 
PRAGMATIC AND TO REALIZE THE UNDERSTANDING REACHED 
DURING THE GORE-KIRIYENKO MEETING. 
4. -(4.+- ADAMOV THEN REITERATED HIS FAMILIAR THEME THAT 
RUSSIA HAS AN INTERNATIONAL COMMITMENT TO FURTHER 
PEACEFUL ATOMIC ENERGY DEVELOPMENT IN NON-NUCLEAR 
STATES AS ENVISIONED IN THE NONPROLIFERATION TREATY. 
HE ALSO ASSERTED THAT "NOTHING BUT BUSHEHR" CONTRADICTS 
THE NPT. A/U HOLUM REMINDED ADAMOV THAT THE U.S. IS 
CONVINCED THAT IRAN IS ACTIVELY PURSUING A NUCLEAR 
WEAPON CAPABILITY AND THUS IS NOT A MEMBER IN GOOD 
STANDING OF THE NPT. IT IS THEREFORE NOT ELIGIBLE FOR 
SUCH COOPERATION FROM NUCLEAR STATES. ADAMOV CONTINUED 
THAT HE IS LOOKING FOR WAYS TO MEET HALF WAY. HE SAID 
THE SANCTIONS WILL HURT A WIDE RANGE, OF RUSSIAN 
ENTERPRIS.ES AND IMPACT NUCLEAR SAFETY. THE SAFETY 
PARAMETER DISPLAY SYSTEMS FOR KURSK AND KRASNOYARSK FOR 
EXAMPLE MAY NOT BE DELIVERED BY NIKIET. HE ASKED THE 
.U.S. TO CONSIDER ACCEPTING A SIGNED DOCUMENT THAT WOULD 
EXCLUDE NIKIET, OR ANY OTHER INSTITUTE WE SPECIFIED, 
FROM ALL WORK WITH IRAN. ADAMOV HOPED THE U.S. WOULD 
CONSIDER SUCH A DOCUMENT OVER THE NEXT MONTH AND SAID. 
IT WOULD REPRESENT A "REASONABLE COMPROMISE." HE SAID 
HE COULD EXCLUDE ANY CONTACTS BY NIKIET WITH IRAN AS 
PART OF THIS. 
5. 11-4- U/S HOLUM SAID THE U.S. WAS NOT OUT TO MAKE 
POLITICAL POINTS BUT TO SOLVE PROBLEMS. HE SAID THE 
U.S. REMAINS CONCERNED ABOUT HEAVY WATER; GRAPHITE AND 
RESEARCH REACTOR COOPERATION AND HE SUGGESTED ADAMOV 
LOOK NOT ONLY AT THE EXCHANGE OF HARDWARE, BUT TO LOOK 
CLOSELY AT THE,END USE AND END USER. HE SAID THE 
RECENT DISCLOSURE (BY MINATOM'S MIKHAILRYZHOV) OF WORK 
PERFORMED BY RUSSIAN INSTITUTES WAS A USEFUL STEP AND 
THAT THE U.S. IS EAGER TO HEAR WHAT ACTION WILL BE 
TAKEN AGAINST INDIVIDUALS INVOLVED IN WORK WITH IRAN. 
WE ALSO WANTED ASSURANCES THAT NIKIET AND MENDELEYEV 
INSTITUTES WILL DISCONTINUE ALL NUCLEAR COOPERATION 
WITH IRAN. HE SAID THE U.S. IS READY TO RESTORE 
FUNDING FOR jOINT PROJECTS TO THE INSTITUTES ONCE ALL 
CONTACTS CEASE. THE U.S. WILL CONSIDER ADAMOV'S 
SUGGESTIONS ON FOLLOWING UP ON THE GORE-KIRIYENKO 
MEETING PROPOSAL FOR AN EXPERT WORKING GROUP ON IRAN. 
6. "1-S,1 ADAMOV CONTINUED TO PUSH ON•IRAN, SAYING THE 
U.S. WAS REDEFINING NUCLEAR EXPORT LAWS AND HAD ENTERED 
A "NEW SPHERE." AT ONE POINT HE QUESTIONED WHETHER 
HOLUM KNEW WHAT HEAVY WATER WAS. HOLUM ASSURED ADAMOV 
THAT HE DID. ADAMOV REPEATED HIS PROPOSAL TO CREATE AN 
EXPERTS GROUP ON IRAN, ONE THAT MINATOM AND ADAMOV 
PERSONALLY HAVE RAISED-REPEATEDLY SINCE LAST SUMMER.

HEU 
7.- —U/S HOLUM SAID THE REMAINING CHALLENGE ON THE 
HEU-LEU AGREEMENT IS PREVENTING LEGAL CHALLENGES BY 
THIRD PARTIES. HE SAID HE URGED DEPUTY FOREIGN MINISTER 
MAMEDOV TO SUPPORT A PRESIDENTIAL DECREE ASSERTING THAT 
THE GOVERNMENT HAD NO PRIOR COMMITMENTS WITH OTHER 
FIRMS. HOLUM SUGGESTED THAT SECRETARY RICHARDSON AND 
MINISTER ADAMOV COULD MEET THE WEEK OF MARCH 6 TO SIGN 
A PACKET OF HEU AGREEMENTS, CLOSING THE ISSUE OF THE 
NATURAL URANIUM COMP.ONENT IF THE LEGAL QUESTIONS WERE 
QUICKLY RESOLVED. ADAMOV CREDITED THE DEPARTMENT OF 
—STATE—AND—DE-PA.I.TMENT—OF—ENERGY—WITH—CONTRIBUTING TO—THE 
PROGRESS TO DATE AND HE SAID PRESSURE SHOULD BE EXERTED 
ON THE "THIRD PARTY," WHICH HE NOTED IS AN AMERICAN 
FIRM. HE RAISED THE POSSIBILITY OF SIGNING A JOINT 
DOCUMENT (RATHER THAN SIMPLY A DECREE) THAT WOULD 
ANSWER U.S. CONCERNS AND PROVIDE SECURITY FOR THE 
COMMERCIAL FIRMS. HE JOKED THAT THE MEETING COULD EVEN 
TAKE PLACE IN MOSCOW IF SECRETARY RICHARDSON DID NOT 
INSIST ON GOING OFF TO THE AVANGARD FACTORY IMMEDIATELY 
UPON ARRIVAL IN RUSSIA. HE SAID A PARIS SIGNING WAS 
POSSIBLE. HOLUM RESPONDED BY SAYING THE DECREE WAS THE 
BEST MECHANISM TO RESOLVE THE PROBLEM. 
PLUTONIUM 
8. +el-) U/S HOLUM BEGAN THE PLUTONIUM DISCUSSION BY 
NOTING THE FOUNDATION OF COMMON INTERESTS, A CLEAR GOAL 
FROM NATIONAL LEADERS, AND FUNDING FROM THE U.S. 
CONGRESS. THE U.S. AND RUSSIA SHOULD MOVE AHEAD 
PROMPTLY AND TRY NOT TO LOSE THE MOMENTUM ON PLUTONIUM. 
U/S HOLUM ALSO SAID THE IVANOV-GUHIN DISCUSSIONS WERE 
ENCOURAGING AND INDICATE THAT TECHNICAL AND FINANCIAL 
PROBLEMS CAN BE OVERCOME AND THE DIFFERING APPROACHES 
TO THE NUCLEAR FUEL CYCLE SHOULD NOT BE AN IMPEDIMENT 
TO REACHING AGREEMENT BY FALL AND SAID HE HOPED MINATOM 
WOULD SHORTLY OBTAIN NEGOTIATING AUTHORITY. 
9. -rt4--ADAMOV SAID THERE WERE SOME ENCOURAGING STEPS 
TAKEN ALREADY, INCLUDING UNDER THE U.S.-RUSSIA 
TECHNICAL AGREEMENT. HE SAID WE NEED A UNIVERSAL 
SOLUTION TO ALL ENVIRONMENTAL, ECONOMIC, AND ENERGY 
EFFICIENCY DIMENSIONS OF THE PLUTONIUM EQUATION AND 
CLOSED FUEL CYCLE. HE SUGGESTED THE TWO-SIDES FIND 
ANSWERS TO THE WHOLE SUITE OF CONCERNS BEFORE SIGNING 
AN AGREEMENT. HE ALSO URGED INTENSIFICATION OF WORK 
UNDER THE TECHNICAL AGREEMENT. ADAMOV SAID THE $200 
MILLION APPROPRIATED BY THE U.S. CONGRESS "WAS NOT THE 
BEST CARROT" FOR RUSSIA AS IF'THE IDEA WAS THAT MOST OF 
THE MONEY WOULD GO TO FRENCH AND GERMANY COMPANIES 
ALREADY MAKING MOX FUEL. HE EMPHASIZED THE IMPORTANCE 
OF USING RUSSIAN TECHNOLOGY AND EXCLUDED THE 
POSSIBILITY OF MOVING THE HANAU MOX PLANT TO RUSSIA. 
(COMMENT: THIS APPARENTLY COMES ON THE HEELS OF 
ADAMOV'S CONVERSATION WITH GERMAN MINISTER TRITTIN, WHO 
REPORTEDLY DISCOURAGED THE IDEA OF TRANSFERRING HANAU 
EQUIPMENT. END COMMENT.) ADAMOV SAID THAT WHILE 
RUSSIA IS NOT PREPARED TO MAKE A COMMITMENT BEYOND THE

11.--1-64._HOLUM SAID THE U.S. AND RUSSIA SHOULD PROCEED 
IN PHASES TO PRESERVE OPTIONS AND NATIONAL OBJECTIVES. 
THE FIRST PHASE WILL TRANSFORM TWO TONS OF PLUTONIUM A 
YEAR THROUGH IRRADIATION AS MOX FUEL AND (AT LEAST FOR 
THE U.S. SIDE) IMMOBILIZATION. GOING TO FIVE TONS A, 
YEAR (PHASE TWO) WILL REQUIRE EUROPEAN OR NEW RUSSIAN 
REACTORS. HOLUM SAID IF FEASIBLE AND MUTUALLY AGREED, 
ADVANCED RUSSIAN REACTORS COULD BE USED IN THE FUTURE 
BUT THAT WE NEED TO START NOW WITH EXISTING REACTORS TO 
TRANSFORM PLUTONIUM TO A FORM THAT IS NOT WEAPONS 
USEABLE. HE STRESSED THE IMPORTANCE OF 
IRREVERSIBILITY. REGARDING TAXATION, LIABILITY AND 
INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS, HOLUM SAID THOSE ISSUES 
WERE BEING ADDRESSED IN OTHER FORA AND SOME WILL BE 
RESOLVED AT A HIGHER LEVEL. PLUTONIUM NEGOTIATIONS 
COULD TAKE ACCOUNT OF THESE EFFORTS AND RESOLUTIONS. 
12. ‘k,g ASIDE FROM URGING THE U.S. TO LOOK AT THE 
PLUTONIUM ISSUE WRIT LARGE, ADAMOV ALSO PROPOSED THAT 
THE U.S. APPROACH THE FRENCH, GERMANS AND'JAPANESE WITH 
THE SUGGESTION THAT THEY BURN RUSSIAN MOX IN THEIR 
REACTORS, AS THIS WOULD TAKE CARE OF THE FIFTY TONS 
MUCH MORE QUICKLY. MICHAEL GUHIN SAID THE U.S. AND 
RUSSIA COULD NOT SOLVE ALL THE ECONOMIC, ENVIRONMENTAL 
AND FUEL CYCLE QUESTIONS FOR A "UNIVERSAL SOLUTION", 
BUT THIS SHOULD NOT GET IN THE WAY OF CRAFTING AN 
AGREEMENT BY THIS FALL THAT MEETS BOTH COUNTRIES 
REQUIREMENTS. HE ADDED THAT RUSSIA WILL BE ABLE TO 
REPROCESS PLUTONIUM UNDER THE AGREEMENT AND THAT THE 
AGREEMENT WILL REFER TO "AGREED END STATES" RATHER THAN 
DEFINING THE SPENT FUEL STANDARD. HE CLARIFIED THAT 
WHICH DISPOSITION METHODS RUSSIA USES IS RUSSIA'S 
DECISION, NOT OURS, AND THAT THE AGREEMENT WOULD NOT 
PRECLUDE REPROCESSING FUEL CONTAINING SUBJECT PLUTONIUM 
AFTER ALL PLUTONIUM UNDER THE AGREEMENT IS TRANSFORMED 
TO AN AGREED END STATE. ADAMOV SAID HE "FULLY AGREED."
