SUBJECT: ACTING SECRETARY TALBOTT'S MEETING WITH SLOVAK 
PARLIAMENT CHAIRMAN IVAN GASPAROVIC 
1.   ENTIRE TEXT. CLASSIFIED BY DEPUTY 
SECRETARY OF STATE STROBE TALBOTT. REASONS: 1.5 (B). 
SUMMARY 
2. SLOVAK PARLIAMENT CHAIRMAN IVAN GASPAROVIC USED HIS 
SEPTEMBER 6, 30-MINUTE MEETING WITH ACTING SECRETARY 
TALBOTT TO REAFFIRM SLOVAKIA'S COMMITMENT TO JOIN 
NATO AND THEEU,BUT WARNED THAT SLOVAKIAOS EXCLUSION FROM 
NATO WOULD CREATE TENSION AND NERVOUSNESS WITHIN THE 
COUNTRY AND IN THE REGION. TALBOTT WELCOMED THE 
CHAIRMAN'S COMMITMENT TO INTEGRATION WITH THE WEST AND 
PLEDGED U.S. SUPPORT, TO MEET THAT GOAL. REMINDING 
GASPAROVIC OF THE IMPORTANCE OF SHARED DEMOCRATIC VALUES 
FOR MEMBERSHIP IN NATO, TALBOTT RAISED CONCERNS ABOUT 
DRAFT LEGISLATION WHICH WAS ERODING THE DEVELOPMENT OF 
DEMOCRACY IN SLOVAKIA. GASPAROVIC EMPHASIZED HIS 
GOVERNMENT'S DESIRE TO ADDRESS U.S. CONCERNS, YET DENIED 
LEGISLATION CITED BY TALBOTT WAS A PROBLEM, SAYING THE 
U.S. HAD RECEIVED BIASED INFORMATION. END SUMMARY. 
CONSEQUENCES OF SLOVAKIA'S OMISSION FROM NATO 
3. CHAIRMAN GASPAROVIC OPENED THE MEETING BY OUTLINING 
THE ADVERSE CONSEQUENCES OF OMITTING SLOVAKIA FROM NATO 
MEMBERSHIP. CITING RECENT CONGRESSIONAL LEGISLATION WHICH 
DID NOT NAME SLOVAKIA AS ONE OF THE COUNTRIES WITH THE 
GREATEST POTENTIAL FOR NATO MEMBERSHIP, GASPAROVIC SAID 
THIS DEVELOPMENT WAS CAUSING GREAT NERVOUSNESS IN THE 
SLOVAK REPUBLIC. . TO OMIT SLOVAKIA WOULD BE UNFORTUNATE 
AND IS THE KIND OF STEP THAT LEADS TO TENSION AND ENHANCES 
THE POSITION OF THOSE POLITICAL FORCES IN SLOVAKIA WHICH 
DO NOT SUPPORT NATO MEMBERSHIP. IF SLOVAKIA IS THE ONLY 
COUNTRY NOT TO BE ADMITTED IN THE FIRST GROUP, IT WOULD 
CREATE A FEELING OF INFERIORITY AND COMPETITION WITH 
NEIGHBORING STATES. ALL GOVERNMENT AND OPPOSITION PARTIES 
IN SLOVAKIA SUPPORT MEMBERSHIP IN NATO AND THE EU, AND IT 
IS THE MAIN PRIORITY OF THE GOVERNMENT.. SLOVAKIA HAS BEEN 
IN EXISTENCE ONLY THREE AND ONE-HALF YEARS AS AN 
INDEPENDENT AND SOVEREIGN STATE, AND THE PROGRESS MADE IS 
SURPRISING EVEN TO THE SLOVAK PEOPLE. IT IS DIFFICULT TO 
UNDERSTAND WHY SLOVAKIA IS NO LONGER BEING CONSIDERED FOR 
NATO MEMBERSHIP. 
RELEASE IN FULL

VISEGRAD NOT A NATO BLOCK 
4. THE ACTING SECRETARY CAUTIONED THAT GASPAROVIC'S 
CONCERNS WERE PREMATURE BECAUSE NATO HAD MADE NO DECISIONS 
TO INCLUDE OR EXCLUDE POTENTIAL MEMBERS. NATO WILL 
ENLARGE BY THE CONSENSUS DECISION OF ITS 16 MEMBER STATES, 
AND DECISIONS WILL BE MADE ON A CASE-BY-CASE BASIS. IN 
THIS REGARD, TALBOTT SAID HE VIEWED VISEGRAD AS A CITY IN 
HUNGARY, NOT THE NAME OF A BLOCK OF COUNTRIES TO COME INTO 
NATO TOGETHER. NATO IS DETERMINED THAT EXPANSION WILL BE 
AN ONGOING, INCLUSIVE PROCESS AND THAT ONCE THE DOOR 
OPENS, IT WILL STAY OPEN. TALBOTT RECOGNIZED THERE WERE 
DIFFERENCES IN THE ASPIRING MEMBER STATES: GEOGRAPHICAL 
LOCATION, DIFFERING PACES OF DEVELOPMENT, AND VARIOUS 
LEVELS OF POLITICAL INSTITUTIONS. 
5. THE ACTING SECRETARY THEN REVIEWED FOR GASPAROVIC THE 
TIMETABLE FOR NATO EXPANSION. THERE WILL BE A NAC 
MINISTERIAL IN EARLY DECEMBER WHICH WILL REVIEW THE 
RESULTS OF NATO'S INTENSIVE DIALOGUES WITH PFP STATES AND 
ANNOUNCE A SUMMIT MEETING OF HEADS OF NATO MEMBER STATES 
IN THE FIRST HALF OF 1997. NATO KEY ALLIES HAVE AGREED 
THAT THE SUMMIT WILL ISSUE INVITATIONS TO CERTAIN 
PROSPECTIVE MEMBERS TO BEGIN ACCESSION TALKS. BEYOND 
THIS, THERE HAVE BEEN NO OTHER DECISIONS MADE. 
CONCERNS ABOUT SLOVAKIA'S INTERNAL POLITICAL DEVELOPMENT 
6. TURNING TO THE CASE OF SLOVAKIA, TALBOTT SAID NATO WAS 
NOT JUST A COLLECTIVE DEFENSE BUT A COMMUNITY OF 
DEMOCRACIES. THE STATE OF A COUNTRY1S DEMOCRACY IS A VERY 
IMPORTANT CONSIDERATION AS NATO ALLIES DECIDE WHICH STATES 
WILL JOIN WHEN. TALBOTT SAID HE WAS WELL AWARE THAT 
SLOVAKIA IS A YOUNG STATE IN THE PROCESS OF DEVELOPING ITS 
DEMOCRATIC AND POLITICAL INSTITUTIONS. HE DID NOT WANT TO 
BE PATRONIZING OR HYPOCRITICAL, BUT HE DID WANT TO BE 
CANDID. THE U.S. HAS SEROUS CONCERNS ABOUT INTERNAL 
DEVELOPMENTS IN SLOVAKIA, SPECIFICALLY ABOUT CERTAIN 
LEGISLATIVE TRENDS AND THE IMPLEMENTATION OF OTHER 
LEGISLATION. CITING THE LAW ON THE PROTECTION OF THE 
REPUBLIC, THE PROPOSED UNIVERSITIES LAW, AND DRAFT MEDIA 
LEGISLATION, TALBOTT SAID HE FEARED THESE LAWS WOULD ERODE 
THE DEVELOPMENT OF DEMOCRACY IN A COUNTRY WHERE DEMOCRACY 
HAS NOT YET HAD THE CHANCE TO SET DOWN DEEP ROOTS. THE 
MONTHS AHEAD WOULD BE CRUCIAL. SEdRETARY CHRISTOPHER HAD 
ASKED HIM TO TELL GASPAROVIC THAT THE NATO FOREIGN 
MINISTERS AND DEFENSE MINISTERS WOULD BE PAYING PARTICULAR 
ATTENTION TO THE LEGISLATIVE ACTIVITIES OF THE SLOVAK .

7. GASPAROVIC THEN ADDRESSED THE ACTING SECRETARY1 S 
CONCERNS ABOUT THE PROPOSED LEGISLATION. ON THE 
PROTECTION OF THE REPUBLIC AMENDMENTS TO THE CRIMINAL 
CODE, GASPAROVIC STATED HE DID NOT VOTE TO SUPPORT THE 
BILL IN THE PARLIAMENT, DESPITE PRESSURE FROM HIS OWN 
PARTY. HE STATED THERE WERE POINTS IN THE BILL THAT WOULD 
HAVE TO BE AMENDED, BUT THAT THERE WERE NO ITEMS IN THE 
BILL MANDATING IMPRISONMENT FOR NONCOMPLIANCE WITH THE 
PROVISIONS OF THE LAW. THE BILL WOULD STAY IN HIS OFFICE 
UNTIL THE CHANGES WERE COMPLETED. HOWEVER, THE RECENT 
DECLARATION OF THE CONFERENCE ON "HUNGARY AND HUNGARIANS 
ABROAD," WHICH ADVOCATED AUTONOMY FOR ETHNIC HUNGARIANS, 
WAS UNACCEPTABLE TO SLOVAK CITIZENS AND MAKES CERTAIN 
SLOVAKS FEEL THIS LEGISLATION IS NECESSARY TO SECURE 
SLOVAKIA'S BORDER. NEVERTHELESS, SHOULD THE LEGISLATION 
COME TO A VOTE IN PARLIAMENT, GASPAROVIC PROMISED HE WOULD 
DO HIS BEST TO SEE THAT IT MET ALL OF THE CRITERIA OF THE 
U.S. GOVERNMENT. 
8. REGARDING THE UNIVERSITIES ACT, GASPAROVIC STATED THIS 
WAS A DISPUTE BETWEEN THE SLOVAK ACADEMY OF SCIENCES AND 
THE UNIVERSITIES, AND HAD NOTHING TO DO WITH THE SLOVAK 
GOVERNMENT. THE PROVISION GIVING THE POWER TO AWARD 
DEGREES OF PROFESSOR TO THE MINISTRY OF EDUCATION HAS BEEN 
DELETED. 
9. IN CONCLUSION, GASPAROVIC REASSURED TALBOTT THAT THE 
GOVERNMENT OF SLOVAKIA AND ITS CITIZENS -WANTED TO CONTINUE 
THE GOS' POLICY OF ORIENTATION TO THE EU AND NATO. HE 
NOTED IT WAS UNFORTUNATE TO SEE BIASED INFORMATION 
ATTRIBUTED TO THE SLOVAK PARLIAMENT OR THE GOS, BUT TO 
HEAR THAT SLOVAKIA IS NOT YET RULED OUT OF NATO GAVE HIM 
HOPE. GASPAROVIC STATED SLOVAKIA WAS AWARE OF THE U.S. 
POSITION, AND THAT THE RECENT CABINET CHANGES WERE TAKEN 
TO IMPROVE THE SLOVAK GOVERNMENT. HE HOPED THE NEW 
FOREIGN MINISTER WOULD BE ABLE TO MEET THE SECRETARY AT 
UNGA. 
10. TALBOTT SAID IT WAS A VERY POSITIVE SIGN TO HEAR 
GASPAROVIC'S STATEMENT THAT THE GOS AND THE SLOVAK PEOPLE 
WANT TO CONTINUE THEIR WESTERN ORIENTATION. THE U.S. 
WOULD DO EVERYTHING TO SUPPORT THAT GOAL DURING THE MONTHS 
AND YEARS AHEAD. THE DECISIONS NATO MADE WOULD BE BASED 
ON THE VERY BEST AND UNBIASED INFORMATION POSSIBLE. THE 
PRESIDENT AND SECRETARY WERE FORTUNATE TO HAVE AMBASSADOR 
JOHNSON SERVING IN BRATISLAVA AND COULD COUNT ON HIM.

11. PRESENT AT THE MEETING WERE ACTING SECRETARY TALBOTT, 
EUR DAS MARSHALL ADAIR, AND EUR/NCE NOTETAKER. THE 
SLOVAKS WERE REPRESENTED BY PARLIAMENT CHAIRMAN 
GASPAROVIC, SLOVAK AMBASSADOR BRANISLAV LICHARDUS, SLOVAK 
DCM PETER BURIAN, AND SLOVAK EMBASSY NOTETAKER. 
COMMENT 
12. GASPAROVIC CAME ACROSS AS MEASURED, WILLING TO ENGAGE 
IN DIALOGUE, AND EAGER TO REASSURE HIS INTERLOCUTORS THAT 
THE SLOVAK GOVERNMENT AND PEOPLE REMAIN COMMITTED TO 
ACHIEVING MEMBERSHIP IN NATO AND THE EU. HOWEVER, HIS 
OFT-STATED COMMENT THAT U.S. WAS RECEIVING BIASED 
INFORMATION -- IMPLYING OUR CONCERNS ABOUT THE INTERNAL 
POLITICAL SITUATION IN SLOVAKIA ARE UNJUSTIFIED -- IS A 
FAMILIAR DODGE. SLOVAKIA, LIKE ALL ASPIRING MEMBERS, MUST 
MEASURE UP TO-THE NATO YARDSTICK NOT ONLY IN WORD, BUT 
ALSO IN DEED.
