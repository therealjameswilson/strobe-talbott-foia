SUBJECT: THE DEPUTY SECRETARY MEETS SPD CHAIRMAN 
LAFONTA1 NE 
SUMMARY AND INTRODUCTION. 
1. IN A HALF-HOUR MEETING WITH GERMAN SPD CHAIRMAN OSKAR 
LAFONTAINE, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT ELUCIDATED U.S. 
POLICIES ON RUSSIA, BOSNIA, AND NATO ENLARGEMENT. ON 
YUGOSLAVIA, LAFONTAINE EXPRESSED CONCERN ABOUT THE 
SURVIVAL OF THE FEDERATION AND UNDERSCORED THE NEED FOR A 
TROOP PRESENCE IN BOSNIA AFTER IFOR'S DEPARTURE IN 
DECEMBER. ON THE EU, LAFONTAINE SAID THAT HE SUPPORTED 
ENLARGEMENT, BUT NOT ON THE BASIS OF THE CURRENT FINANCING 
ARRANGEMENT. 
RUSSIA • 
2. LAFONTAINE MENTIONED THAT ON HIS DECEMBER TRIP TO 
RUSSIA, HE HAD MET WITH ZYUGANOV, YAVLINSKIY, 
CHERNOMYRDIN, AND KOZYREV.,TALBOTT SAID THAT FOR THE 
PRESIDENT'S UPCOMING TRIP, THERE WOULD BE A ROUND TABLE AT 
SPASO HOUSE AT WHICH EE WOULD MEET WITH A NUMBER OF 
OPPOSITION LEADERS INCLUDING ZYUGANOV, BUT NOT 
ZHIRINOVSKIY, WHOM WE VIEW AS ANATHEMA. LAFONTAINE ASKED 
FOR THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S ASSESSMENT OF THE RUSSIAN 
ELECTIONS, INCLUDING GORBACHEV'S PROSPECTS. LAFONTAINE 
ADDED PARENTHETICALLY THAT SINCE MANY RUSSIANS BLAME 
GORBACHEV FOR THEIR COUNTRY'S CURRENT PROBLEMS, HE WOULD 
PROBABLY FARE POORLY; NEVERTHELESS, GORBACHEV REMAINS A 
POPULAR FIGURE IN GERMANY. TALBOTT AGREED THAT 
GORBACHEV'S ELECTION PROSPECTSWERE MINIMAL, BUT NOTED 
THAT HISTORY WOULD TREAT HIM KINDLY. WITH REGARD TO THE 
ELECTION OUTCOME, TALBOTT NOTED THAT SOME CONTACTS, 
INCLUDING NIZHNIY NOVGOROD MAYOR BORIS NEMTSOV, WERE 
PESSIMISTIC ABOUT YELTSIN'S CHANCES, BASED ON TRENDS IN 
THEIR REGIONS AND HIS CONCERN THAT COMMUNISTS MAY TAMPER 
WITH THE ELECTORAL PROCESS. THE GAP BETWEEN YELTSIN AND 
ZYUGANOV IS NARROWING, TALBOTT SAID, BUT WE MUST BE 
PREPARED TO DEAL WITH ANY OUTCOME, INCLUDING A ZYUGANOV 
VICTORY.

OF THE DAYTON ACCORDS WAS GOING WELL. WITH REGARDS TO 
CIVILIAN ASPECTS, WE HAVE HAD SOME SUCCESSES, BUT MUST

4. LAFONTAINE SAID THAT HE WAS CONCERNED WITH THE 
QUESTION OF A FUTURE MILITARY PRESENCE AFTER IFOR LEAVES, 
AND THAT THIS WAS CURRENTLY BEING DEBATED IN GERMANY. HE 
AGREED THAT ECONOMIC ASSISTANCE WOULD BE IMPORTANT, BUT 
WAS TROUBLED BY THE APPARENTLY DISORGANIZED EFFORT TO 
IMPLEMENT THE CIVILIAN ASPECTS OF DAYTON. TALBOTT REPLIED 
THAT ALTHOUGH THE EUROPEANS' ANXIETIES WERE 
UNDERSTANDABLE, IFOR SHOULD STICK TO THE DECEMBER DEADLINE 
FOR WITHDRAWAL. THE ISSUE OF A POST-IFOR MILITARY 
PRESENCE SHOULD BE DISCUSSED IN A LOW-KEY MANNER, THROUGH 
DIPLOMATIC CHANNELS. PUBLICLY CASTING DOUBT ON IFOR'S 
DECEMBER DEPARTURE, TALBOTT NOTED, WOULD REMOVE AN 
INDUCEMENT FOR THE PARTIES TO COMPLETE THEIR DAYTON 
IMPLEMENTATION.COMMITMENTS. HOWEVER, IF NECESSARY, WE CAN 
WORK QUIETLY TO ARRANGE FOR A REDUCED POST-IFOR 
INTERNATIONAL MILITARY FORCE IN BOSNIA, PERHAPS AS A 
POLICE OR OBSERVER PRESENCE. IN THE MEANTIME, WE MUST 
WORK TO ADDRESS THE MILITARY IMBALANCE BETWEEN THE 
PARTIES. THIS CAN BE ACCOMPLISHED IN PART BY FULFILLING 
THE DISARMAMENT PROVISIONS OF THE DAYTON ACCORDS. 
HOWEVER, TO FULLY REDRESS THE IMBALANCE BY SEPTEMBER, WE, 
MUST ALSO EQUIP AND TRAIN THE BOSNIAN ARMED FORCES, A TASK 
WHICH WOULD BE COMPLICATED BY THE WEST EUROPEANS' 
SKEPTICISM. 
5. LAFONTAINE RESPONDED THAT THE SPD HAD OBJECTED TO 
RECOGNIZING THE YUGOSLAV BREAKAWAY STATES. IN HIS VIEW, 
THE WEST'S RECOGNITION OF THESE STATES HAD ENCOURAGED 
ETHNIC CLEANSING. THE SITUATION HAD BEEN EXACERBATED BY 
THE TENDENCY OF INDIVIDUAL EU MEMBERS TO SIDE WITH VARIOUS 
SUCCESSOR STATES. HOWEVER, THIS WAS PART OF THE PAST. 
NOW, LAFONTAINE AVERRED, WE MUST THINK ABOUT A TROOP 
PRESENCE FOR BEYOND DECEMBER. THE HATRED THAT PRODUCED 
THIS WAR WOULD NOT DISSIPATE AFTER A YEAR. TALBOTT

THAT THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY MUST CONTINUE TO MAKE 
CLEAR THAT ETHNICALLY CLEANSED" STATES ARE UNACCEPTABLE. 
WE HAD HAD SOME SUCCESS IN IMPRESSING THIS ON THE PARTIES, 
TALBOTT NOTED. FOR THE TIME BEING, THE NOTIONS OF A

6. LAFONTAINE FOREIGN POLICY AIDE HORST EHMKE COMMENTED 
THAT GERMANS WERE VERY SKEPTICAL ON THIS POINT. THE 
EXPERIENCE OF EU ADMINISTRATOR HANS KOSCHNIK IN MOSTAR HAD 
LED MANY TO CONCLUDE THAT THE FEDERATION WAS NOT WORKING. 
TUDJMAN HAD BEEN UNWILLING TO WITHDRAW HIS SUPPORT FROM 
THE CROAT HARDLINERS. IF THE FEDERATION CANNOT WORK, 
EHMKE SAID, THEN ANY TALK ABOUT RESTORING A MILITARY 
BALANCE WAS MISGUIDED. TALBOTT REPLIED THAT THE US IS A 
LONG WAY FROM GIVING UP ON THE FEDERATION, AND THAT WE 
MUST USE OUR LEVERAGE TO PRESSURE THE PARTIES INTO 
SUPPORTING THE IT. THE FRG HAD LEVERAGE WITH THE c.ROATS 
IN PARTICULAR, TALBOTT NOTED. EHMKE SAID THAT THE FRG HAD 
OVERPLAYED ITS HAND WITH THE CROATS, WHO NOW VIEW THE US 
AS THEIR MAIN PARTNER. THE POINT, TALBOTT SAID, IS THAT 
WE MUST CONSOLIDATE OUR INFLUENCE AND USE IT 
COLLECTIVELY. WE SOMETIMES DIFFER ON APPROACHES, AS WITH 
THE PETERSBURG FEDERATION CONFERENCE, RECENTLY POSTPONED 
BY FM KINKEL. TALBOTT POINTED OUT THAT WE ARE CONSIDERING 
OUR CONTRIBUTION TO BOSNIAN RECONSTRUCTION, BUT PLAN TO 
PLACE THE MONEY IN ESCROW UNTIL THE PARTIES COMPLY. 
TALBOTT AGREED WITH EHMKE THAT ONE SHOULD BE CAREFUL AND 
FIRM IN DEALING WITH TUDJMAN.

OTHER DIMENSIONS OF EUROPEAN SECURITY. LAFONTAINE REPLIED 
THAT THE SPD SUPPORTED EU EXPANSION, BUT NOT ON THE BASIS 
OF ITS CURRENT FINANCIAL ARRANGEMENTS. GERMANY MUST THINK 
THROUGH THE FINANCIAL CONSEQUENCES OF EU EXPANSION, 
LAFONTAINE SAID. ALREADY SPAIN AND OTHER MEMBERS WERE 
VOICING CONCERN ABOUT A DIVERSION•OF CASH TO PROSPECTIVE 
NEW EASTERN MEMBERS. EHMKE ADDED THAT THE EU'S FINANCING 
OF STRUCTURAL FUNDS AND AGRICULTURE POLICIES POSED SERIOUS 
DIFFICULTIES. GERMANY'S ANNUAL EU CONTRIBUTION, 
LAFONTAINE SAID, WAS DM 29 BILLION. GERMANY CANNOT 
INCREASE THIS AND STILL HOPE TO REDUCE ITS DEFICIT; 
THEREFORE A NEW FINANCING SCHEME FOR THE EU MUST BE FOUND 
IF IT IS TO ADMIT NEW MEMBERS. TALBOTT STRESSED THAT •IT

NATO EXPANSION 
S. EHMKE ASKED FOR TALBOTT'S OPINION ON TWO QUESTIONS 
RELATING TO NATO ENLARGEMENT. THE FIRST WAS HOW TO 
RESPOND TO HINTS THAT RUSSIA WOULD ACCEPT NATO EXPANSION 
UNDER CERTAIN CONDITIONS, EG, NO NUCLEAR WEAPONS OR 
FOREIGN TROOPS ON THE SOIL OF NEW MEMBERS. THE SECOND 
QUESTION WAS WHETHER THE US, EUROPE, AND RUSSIA COULD 
REACH A BROAD SECURITY FRAMEWORK OUTSIDE OF THE NATO 
CONTEXT. THE TWO QUESTIONS WERE LINKED BY THE REALITY 
THAT THE RUSSIANS SHOULD KNOW NOW WHAT THE WEST WAS 
PREPARED TO OFFER THEM NOW IN EXCHANGE FOR ACCEPTANCE OF 
NATO EXPANSION. TALBOTT PREFACED HIS REPLY BY CAUTIONING 
THAT WE SHOULD NOT NEGOTIATE WITH THE RUSSIANS IN A WAY 
THAT WOULD UNDERCUT THE ALLIANCE'S COHESION. TALBOTT 
POINTED OUT THAT HALF OF THE ALLIANCE'S MEMBERS DO NOT 
HOST,FOREIGN TROOPS, THIS INDICATES THAT A FOREIGN TROOP 
PRESENCE IS NOT AN A PRIORI REQUIREMENT FOR NATO' 
MEMBERSHIP; THE NATO ENLARGEMENT STUDY MAKES THIS CLEAR. 
ON THE NUCLEAR ISSUE, EXISTING ALLIANCE NUCLEAR DOCTRINE 
SHOULD ALLAY THE RUSSIANS' CONCERNS. BOTH OF THESE 
FACTORS WERE A BASIS FOR ADDRESSING THE RUSSIANS' 
ANXIETIES ABOUT NATO ENLARGEMENT, TALBOTT SAID. HOWEVER, 
TALBOTT EMPHASIZED, WE CANNOT ACCEPT CONDITIONS THAT WOULD 
CREATE TWO TIERS OF SECURITY WITHIN NATO. 
9. TWO PROBLEMS WE WILL CONTINUE TO GRAPPLE WITH, TALBOTT 
CONTINUED, ARE NATO'S RELATIONS WITH THE BALTIC STATES AND 
WITH UKRAINE. HOWEVER, AS WE WORK TO RESOLVE THEM, 
TALBOTT ASSERTED, WE MUST REFRAIN FROM PUBLIC DECLARATIONS 
THAT RUSSIA CAN NEVER BE A NATO MEMBER. INSTEAD, WE • 
SHOULD EMPHASIZE OUR COMMITMENT TO AN ENLARGEMENT PROCESS 
THAT IS GRADUAL, TRANSPARENT, NOT EXCLUSIONARY, AND OPEN 
TO ALL PFP MEMBERS. THEORETICALLY, RUSSIA COULD BECOME AN 
ALLIANCE MEMBER, BUT THIS COULD ONLY OCCUR IN A DIFFERENT 
EUROPE, WITH A DIFFERENT RUSSIA, AND A DIFFERENT NATO. 
EHMKE ARGUED THAT RUSSIAN MEMBERSHIP WAS NOT VIABLE DUE TO 
ITS TREMENDOUS INTERNAL DIFFICULTIES, THE SITUATION IN THE 
CAUCASUS, AND THE POTENTIAL FOR CONFLICT ON ITS SOUTHERN 
FLANK AND WITH CHINA.
