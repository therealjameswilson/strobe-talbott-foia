SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S MEETING WITH ROMAN 
SHPEK, CHAIRMAN OF UKRAINE'S NATIONAL AGENCY FOR 
RECONSTRUCT ION AND DEVELOPMENT 
1. Summary: In a July 10 meeting, Roman Shpek, Chairman 
of Ukraine's National Agency for Reconstruction and 
Development, told Deputy Secretary Talbott that U.S. 
support contributed to ratification of Ukraine's new 
constitution by increasing the stature of the Kuchma 
government and adding legitimacy to its choice of a new 
government structure. He provided a detailed list of new 
Prime Minister Lazarenko's reform priorities and assured 
the Deputy Secretary that Lazarenko is in full agreement 
with President Kuchma's economic policy. Shpek delivered 
a letter from President Kuchma to President Clinton 
seeking dollars 400 million in assistance. End Summary. 
2. Shpek, joined by Deputy Minister of Economy Volodymyr 
Rohovyy, Embassy of Ukraine DCM Valeriy Kuchinskiy and 
embassy officer Yaroslav Voytko, told Talbott that U.S. 
support of the Kuchma government not only played a part in 
ratification of Ukraine's new constitution but also 
enhanced Ukraine's stature with Russia and its European 
neighbors. Other nations now perceive Ukraine as 
recovering from its post-independence weakness and ready 
to take part in European affairs as a full-fledged 
partner, he said. 
3. The Deputy Secretary, who was joined by Ambassador 
Richa'rd L. Morningstar, coordinator for U.S. assistance to 
the New Independent States, Daniel V. Speckhard, deputy 
coordinator in the office of the special adviser to the 
Secretary for the NIS, and Executiye Assistant Victoria 
Nuland, had opened the meeting by congratulating Shpek on 
Ukraine's achievement in reaching a consensus that 
resulted in a resounding approval of the new 
constitution. The Rada's overwhelming vote was further 
evidence that democracy was putting down deep roots in 
Ukraine, he said. 
4. Shpek said that Ukraine had entered a difficult phase 
in its development, requiring hard decisions on the part 
of the government's leadership. He proceeded to outline 
new Prime Minister's Lazarenko's priorities: 
-- Maximizing government revenues and creating a larger 
tax base;

5. Lazarenko and President Kuchma are in full agreement 
on Ukraine's economic course, Shpek said. The fact that 
Lazarenko was confirmed by the Rada as new prime minister 
on July 10 despite initial leftist opposition demonstrated 
his political acumen and ability to work with all 
factions. Personnel changes at the highest level would 
not affect policy, Shpek said. 
6. Lazarenko also intended to strengthen the powers of 
the finance and economy ministers and try to streamline 
the sectoral ministries (the former government structure 
included 25 ministers) . Lazarenko also stressed the need 
to improve the functioning of the government and internal 
cooperation in asking him to stay on as chairman of the 
new agency, Shpek said. Such improvements are 
particularly needed in assistance programs, he said. 
7. Ambassador Morningstar said that he frequently 
discussed Ukraine's reform program with the IMF and World 
Bank. He was aware that several hundred million dollars 
in loans were available if Ukraine was able to implement' 
the necessary reforms, and that Ukraine and the 
international financial institutions need to work together 
to see that such loans are disbursed.
