-- Haiti: I've been thinking a lot, as I'm sure 
you have, about this matter. I feel a combination of 
relief, resolve and apprehension after yesterday's 
meeting in the Oval. The meeting produced a decision 
- the right decision though a tough one -- and 
decisiveness is an important part of what is at issue 
here (and in the unfolding story of the Clinton 
presidency more generally). 
There are two reasons for 'my apprehension. The 
obvious one is that getting from here to September 20 - 
- and beyond -- is exceedingly difficult, and an awful 
lot is riding on our doing it right. 
My second reason for concern is the President's 
own ambivalence, which was apparent in the meeting and 
dramatically evident in his phone call to me later. 
Somehow we need to shore him up. Otherwise, his 
uncertainty will feed that of others. That could have 
operational consequences. At a minimum, anything less 
'than resolve on the President's part will unavoidably 
be picked up by the world as it watches his, and the 
Administration's, body English over the days and weeks 
to come. 
I think you should consider writing a confidential 
message to the President that would reach him next week 
on the Vineyard. It would be a kind of extended night 
note; maybe 5 or 6 pages. It would make, in as

-- NATO expansion: Speaking of decisions and 
dedisiveness and resolve, George Shultz once said, 
"Nothing is ever decided in Washington." He meant 
simply that policy is always open to relitigation (as, 
arguably, it should be). I've contributed to that 
phenomenon myself, as recently as yesterday in your 
office, when I was party to the sedition of reopening 
the question of the ultimate desirability of lift. 
Anyway, apother example is NATO expansion -- and 
fast-track admission for Poland and the other Visegrad 
countries. This is coming back in the form of a 
looming internal deliberation over how to follow 
through on the President's carefully crafted 
formulations in Riga and Warsaw (and his somewhat more 
forthcoming, improvised comments in Berlin).

Without having re-focused on this problem as

I wanted to run past you how I think we should focus on

-- After some back-and-forth, Tony (via Sandy) has 
agreed with our insistence that State chair the 
interagency committee on NATO expansion. Who do 
you think should be in that chair? My instinct 
would be Peter. 
-- I think we should also set up an in-house group 
to work this, including: Jim Steinberg, Dick 
Holbrooke, Jim Collins, and Lynn Davis (or a 
designee)..
-- I also want tp put the matter on the agenda of 
the NIS Policy Steering Group, since there is a 
very immediate task of preparing for the Clinton-
Yeltsin summit. If -- as I suspect is.the case 
we're going to be doing or'saying something on 
timetable and criteria later this year, we need to 
prepare the Russians for that; and we need to 
involve them in a process of some kind that makes 
clear we're still committed to integration and the 
lowering of walls, even as we make good on the 
President's commitment to expansion in a form and 
on a timetable that does not include the Russians.
