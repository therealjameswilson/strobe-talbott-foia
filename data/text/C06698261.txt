2. '11M4.„,.Summary: Ambassador Takehiro Togo, the new 
Japanese envoy to the Russian Federation, paid a courtesy 
call September 3 on 5/NIS Special Advisor Collins. Deputy 
Secretary Talbott dropped in briefly on the meeting. Togo 
requested U.S. assessments of the political and economic 
situation in Russia and of President Yeltsin's health. 
The discussion also touched on military reform, the new 
Yeltsin cabinet, political jockeying for power, the 
Northern Territories issue, Russia's relative lack of 
attention to its wider Asia policies, and the strength of 
the Russian communists. Ambassador Togo agreed that 
U.S.-Japan policy coordination on Russia is critical and 
shared some of his ideas about "EDUCATING" Russia on 
Asia-Pacific issues through greater involvement in 
regional fora, such as ARF and APEC. The Deputy Secretary 
was asked to outline U.S. thinking behind the decision to 
respond to Saddam Hussein's thrust into the Kurdish zone 
of Northern Iraq. End Summary. 
3. 1%..Takehiro Togo, the recently-named Japanese

Ambassador Collins. Deputy Secretary Talbott dropped by

1986-88. He sought Ambassador Collins' assessment of 
prospects for President Yeltsin's second administration. 
Ambassador Collins reviewed the elections and political 
process since December 1995 and said he believed a 
rudimentary, different type of system was emerging. It 
remained fragile, with weak institutions, and too 
dependent on personalities. Orderly politics probably

More Discipline in the Economic Sphere 
5. Deputy Secretary Talbott, ACCOMPANIED by S/P Deputy

expressed appreciation for Japanese assistance on 
important Russia issues, and inquired about several

Collins continued his assessment by noting that the 
economic sphere was far more disciplined as Chernomyrdin

standards. On that level, the Russians have learned some

not would be readily apparent. Ambassador Collins noted 
that the U.S. remains very concerned about continuing high 
levels of corruption in Russia. That said, the Russians 
have done a remarkable job in controling inflation and 
keeping the budget in bounds.

7. (611.k Ambassador Togo asked about Yeltsin's health. 
Ambassador Collins said he was not well but had surprised 
his doctors during the electoral campaign with his ability 
to keep a schedule. After the election, Yeltsin had

day-to-day policies. Ambassador Collins said that the 
longer Yeltsin is able to discharge the duties of his 
office the better. 
The Deputy Secretary on U.S.-Japan Cooperation on Russia 
8.-i-et-Deputy Secretary Talbott said he could not improve 
the assessment and wanted to add a few quick points. He 
said the U.S.-Japan partnership and cooperation'is 
critical globally and particularly on this issue. Russian 
thinking on the Asia-Pacific area is not as advanced as it 
is on Europe, although there are competent people working 
on it. Although Russia is geographically more Asian, it 
has been ambivalent towards the region, and has thought of 
itself more as European. European security architecture 
is,easier for Russia to understand. In the Asia-Pacific, 
there is no OSCE and Russia is still trying to figure out 
what the ASEAN Regional Forum (ARF) really is. To the 
East, Russia remains preoccupied with China and redefining 
its relationship with Beijing. The Deputy Secretary said 
he and Mr. Romberg were thinking of ways to involve Russia 
in Korean Peninsula issues with the two Koreas, China, 
Japan, and the U.S. He stressed that Japan and the U.S. 
needed to keep in touch as the Russians draw lessons from 
the process of NATO expansion. He noted that he did not 
expect the freeze on the Northern Territories issue to 
thaw anytime soon. On this last, Ambassador Togo 
expressed appreciation for U.S. support. Preparing to 
depart, the Deputy Secretary said that Foreign Minister 
Primakov is a sophisticated, pragmatic, foreign policy 
professional who thinks of himself as an expert on 
Asia-Pacific. Deputy Foreign Minister Panov, the new 
Russian ambassador to Japan, has also always been a good 
interlocutor. Japan and the U.S. will have to contribute 
as much as possible to the education of Aleksander Lebed.

Military Reform 
12. 1.C.LAmbassador Collins said that one great issue 
Ambassador Togo would encounter during his assignment was 
Russian decisions on military power. Russia does not have 
the money or even manpower to fund and supply the kind of 
military it has maintained in the past. However, for 
understandable reasons, the government is reluctant to 
take on restructuring of the military, a critical 
decision. Rodionov, the new Minister of Defense, has been 
a major spokesman for military reform, as has Lebed, but 
matters become difficult when talk really turned to 
eliminating general positions.
