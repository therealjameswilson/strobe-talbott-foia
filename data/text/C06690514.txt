RELEASE IN PART 
B6 
Belated but sincere thanks for your kind letter. Why 
you might wonder, haven't I replied more promptly? The ans-
wer is simple: my life has been totally crazed since 
nomination; still is; probably always will be, or at least 
for the duration of my impersonation of a diplomat and 
bureaucrat. The "H" people are running me ragged, shuttling 
me back and forth to the Hill in quest of good will from 
Senators who will decide my fate sometime in the next 
several years when the White House sends up our papers. 
Meanwhile, I've been trying to set up an office, put to-
gether a staff, and even think a little about substance 
(though there's not much time for that!).
While my reply may be tardy, your letter was actually 
very timely, since it contains advice on a matter I'm work-
ing on this next week or so: a good economics deputy. Rich 
Kauzlarich (know him? he's great), who's my principal 
deputy, is running the talent search, and I'll give him 
name. One possible problem is that we're feeling 
under some pressure on the diversity front. But anyway, 
we'll certainly pursue the matter. 
Brooke mentioned she saw you at Woolsey's swearing-
in. Getting to see something of you and Maggie was one of 
the best things about the incredible year gone by. I sure 
hope we can continue to be in touch. 
Cheers,
