1. 4634- IN A NOVEMBER 10 MEETING WITH JAPANESE AMBASSADOR 
SAITO, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT DESCRIBED PRESIDENT 
CLINTON'S DECISION TO SUPPORT IFI LENDING AS PART OF AN 
IMF STABILIZATION PACKAGE FOR PAKISTAN UNRELATED TO THE 
NON-PROLIFERATION AGENDA. TALBOTT SAID THE PRESIDENT MADE 
THE DECISION BASED ON THE PROSPECT OF AN IMMINENT 
PAKISTANI ECONOMIC COLLAPSE. THE PRESIDENT APPROVED OTHER 
STEPS FOR BOTH PAKISTAN AND INDIA (EXIM, OPIC, TDA, AND 
IMET) TO ENCOURAGE FURTHER PROGRESS ON NON-PROLIFERATION. 
SAITO SAID PAKISTANI FM AZIZ WOULD VISIT TOKYO ON NOVEMBER 
18 AND THE GOJ EXPECTED TO RESERVE ANY SPECIFIC 
ANNOUNCEMENT ABOUT ITS INTENTIONS TO LIFT SANCTIONS UNTIL 
AFTER AZIZ'S VISIT. TALBOTT BRIEFLY MENTIONED THE IRAQ 
CRISIS AND DESCRIBED THE SITUATION AS VERY GRAVE. SAITO 
REQUESTED ADVANCE NOTICE OF MILITARY ACTION, IF AT ALL 
POSSIBLE. END SUMMARY. 
PAKISTAN 
2. 4E4- DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT BEGAN THE MEETING BY 
DESCRIBING PRESIDENT CLINTON'S DECISION TO SUPPORT AN IMF 
PACKAGE FOR PAKISTAN AS TARGETED RELIEF UNRELATED TO THE 
NON-PROLIFERATION AGENDA. HE SAID THE U.S. SUPPORTED THE 
PROPOSED IMF SUPPORT OF PAKISTAN BUT NOTED THAT THIS WAS, 
OF COURSE, CONTINGENT UPON THE IME AND PAKISTAN ACTUALLY 
REACHING AGREEMENT ON A PACKAGE. GIVEN PAKISTAN'S 
ECONOMIC DIFFICULTIES, AGREEMENT WAS BY NO MEANS CERTAIN. 
THE PRESIDENT ALSO DECIDED TO USE THE RECENTLY APPROVED 
BROWNBACK WAIVER AUTHORITY TO RESUME EXIM, OPIC, TDA, AND 
IMET FOR BOTH COUNTRIES TO ENCOURAGE FURTHER PROGRESS ON 
OUR NON-PROLIFERATION AGENDA. OTHER SANCTIONS WILL REMAIN 
IN PLACE. TALBOTT SAID HE WOULD MEET WITH INDIAN DEPUTY 
CHAIRMAN OF THE PLANNING COMMISSION SINGH IN ROME NOVEMBER 
19 TO SEEK FURTHER PROGRESS WITH INDIA. PAKISTANI FOREIGN 
SECRETARY SHAMSHAD AHMAD HAD BEEN IN WASHINGTON LAST WEEK. 
ADDITIONALLY, PAKISTANI PRIME MINISTER SHARI F WAS 
SCHEDULED TO MEET WITH PRESIDENT CLINTON IN WASHINGTON ON 
DECEMBER 2.

IRAQ 
5. -1.5* TURNING TO IRAQ, TALBOTT DESCRIBED THE SITUATION AS 
VERY GRAVE. OVER THE PAST TWO WEEKS, THE IRAQIS HAD 
APPARENTLY HARDENED THEIR POSITION AND SHOWED NO 
INCLINATION TO RESPECT UN REQUIREMENTS. IN FACT, THERE 
WAS GROWING EVIDENCE THAT SADDAM HUSSEIN DESIRED A 
MILITARY SHOWDOWN. TALBOTT NOTED THAT THE PRESIDENT AND 
HIS NATIONAL SECURITY TEAM WERE CAREFULLY CONSIDERING 
OPTIONS, AND THAT MILITARY FORCE WAS A REAL POSSIBILITY. 
SECRETARY COHEN, U/S PICKERING AND GENERAL ZINNI HAD JUST 
RETURNED FROM AN ELEVEN-COUNTRY MIDDLE EAST TRIP AND TOLD 
THE PRESIDENT THAT THERE IS SUPPORT IN THE REGION FOR 
MEANINGFUL MILITARY ACTION. TALBOTT SAID HE WAS NOT IN A 
POSITION TO-DISCUSS ANY POSSIBLE TIMETABLE FOR THAT 
ACTION. 
6. 44 SAITO APPRECIATED THE INFORMATION AND ASKED THE USG 
TO GIVE THE GOJ ADVANCE NOTICE OF MILITARY ACTION IF AT 
ALL POSSIBLE; ADVANCE NOTICE WOULD MAKE IT EASIER FOR THE 
GOJ TO BE SUPPORTIVE OF THE U.S. ACTION. TALBOTT SAID HE 
UNDERSTOOD SAITO'S CONCERN. THE USG WOULD DO ALL IT COULD 
TO KEEP THE GOJ INFORMED. HOWEVER, HE NOTED, DECISIONS ON 
MILITARY ACTIONS TENDED TO MOVE QUICKLY, AND THE PRESIDENT 
HAD TO BALANCE TACTICAL SECURITY QUESTIONS WITH A DESIRE 
TO BE AS FORTHCOMING AS POSSIBLE WITH OUR ALLIES. 
PRESIDENT'S ASIA TRIP 
7. -(-S+ SAITO ALSO INQUIRED ABOUT A NEW YORK TIMES REPORT 
THAT THE PRESIDENT MIGHT HAVE TO CANCEL OR CURTAIL HIS 
ASIA TRIP. TALBOTT SAID THAT THE PRESIDENT ATTACHED GREAT 
IMPORTANCE TO THE APEC MEETING, PARTICULARLY AT THIS TIME 
OF E'CONOMIC CRISIS, AND AS OF TODAY HAD EVERY INTENTION OF 
GOING. THE PRESIDENT ALSO FELT STRONGLY ABOUT THE 
IMPORTANCE OF HIS VISITS TO JAPAN AND KOREA. SAITO CLOSED 
BY SAYING THAT PRIME MINISTER OBUCHI AND THE.JAPANESE 
PEOPLE VERY EAGERLY AWAITED THE PRESIDENT'S VISIT.
