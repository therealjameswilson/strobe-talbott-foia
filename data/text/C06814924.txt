1. ~ SUMMARY: FM SATTAR ON JULY 20
PESSIMISTICALLY CHARACTERIZED PAKISTAN'S
RELATIONS WITH INDIA AS BEING "AT A STALEMATE."
SATTAR HINTED THAT THE GOP WOULD ACCEPT A
SETTLEMENT ON KASHMIR THAT WAS ACCEPTABLE TO THE
ALL PARTIES HURRYAT CONFERENCE. (APHC) AND URGED
CONSIDERATION OF CONFLICT PREVENTION IN KASHMIR
BY THE UN GENERAL ASSEMBLY IN SEPTEMBER. HE SAID
THE MOST IMPORTANT ASPECT OF THE RECENT DECISION
OF THE INTERNATIONAI COURT OF JUSTICE ON THE
DOWNED ATLANTIQUE P-3 AIRCRAFT WAS A CALL ON BOTH
PARTIES TO SETTLE DISPUTES BY PEACEFUL MEANS.
SATTAR REITERATED THE GOP'S WILLINGNESS TO SIGN
CTBT --AND THE PAKISTANI PUBLIC'S UNWILLINGNESS
TO SEE CTBT SIGNED. HE ASSURED THE U.S. THAT THE
GOP WOULD IMPLEMENT MEASURES TO ENSURE CUSTODIAL
SAFETY OF ITS NUCLEAR WEAPONS. HE UNDERLINED THE
GOP'S INSISTENCE ON VERIFIABILITY AND COMPLIANCE
FOR THE FMCT. END SUMMARY.

INDO-PAK RELATIONS,
WAY FORWARD FOR KASHMIR
2. ~ GOP FM ABDUL SATTAR MET FOR ONE HOURs 45
MINUTES ON JULY 20 WITH CHARGE AND VISITING
FORMER U.S. AMBASSADOR TO INDIA (1981-1985) HARRY
G. BARNES, IN A SESSION WHOSE TONE COULD BEST BE
DESCRIBED AS PESSIMISTIC. SATTAR DESCRIBED
PAKISTAN'S RELATIONS WITH INDIA AS BEING "AT A
STALEMATE" AND DECRIED THE "WASTED TIME" OF "MANY
ABLE DIPLOMATS," PAKISTANI AND INDIAN, WHO HAVE
SPENT THEIR CAREERS ON INDO-PAK RELATIONS.
3. ~ ASKED WHAT MIGHT CONSTITUTE A RESOLUTION
OF THE KASHMIR DISPUTE, SATTAR SAID THE GOP HAS
SAID THAT IT COULD ACCEPT-ANY SETTLEMENT THAT IS
ACCEPTABLE TO THE KASHMIRI PEOPLE. PRESSED AS TO
WHO REPRESENTS THE KASHMIRIS, SATTAR RUMINATED ON
THE HISTORY OF THE KASHMIR INTIFADA AND THE
PEOPLE'S "PEACEFUL POLITICAL PROTEST." HE LAUDED
APHC LEADER GILANI AS A "SIMPLE, POOR MAN" WHOSE
INTEGRITY HAS WON THE SUPPORT OF THE KASHMIRIS.
APPROACHING THE POINT, HOWEVER; SATTAR DID NOT
CONDEMN DIRECT TALKS BETWEEN THE GOI AND THE
APHC, ALTHOUGH HE NOTED THE APHC HAS FORMALLY
PREFERRED TRILATERAL TALKS, TO INCLUDE THE GOP.
(NOTE: A PRESS STATEMENT ISSUED BY THE APHC
JULY 22 RESTATED THAT POSITION.) HE ADDED THAT
THE GOP UNDERSTOOD THERE MAY BE INTERNAL
DIFFERENCES WITHIN THE APHC ABOUT THE WAY FORWARD.
BUT, A RESOLUTION THAT WAS ACCEPTABLE TO THE APHC
WOULD PROBABLY BE ACCEPTABLE TO THE GOP, HE SAID.

4. ~ SATTAR AND BARNES SPOKE AT LENGTH OF THE
PROS AND CONS OF VARIOUS FORMULATIONS OF CONFLICT
REDUCTION MECHANISMS FOR KASHMIR. THE FOREIGN
MINISTER SUGGESTED THAT THE UN CHARTER PROVIDED
THE WORLD COMMUNITY WITH ENOUGH LATITUDE TO
OPERATE ALREADY, IF NATIONS HAD THE POLITICAL
WILL TO STEP INTO A GIVEN SITUATION, SUCH AS
BOSNIA. WHILE ACKNOWLEDGING THAT NGO-SPONSORED
CONFERENCES ALLOW INFLUENTIAL PEOPLE OF BOTH
COUNTRIES (INDIA AND PAKISTAN) TO LISTEN TO EACH
OTHERS'IEWPOINTS, SATTAR WONDERED IF THE
BENEFITS OF SUCH EVENTS JUSTIFIED THE COSTS.
WHEN BARNES BROUGHT UP THE ACTIVITIES OF FORMER
PRESIDENT JIMMY CARTER, SATTAR AGREED THAT
SOMEONE WITH STATURE AND SKILL COULD SOMETIMES,
AGAINST THE ODDS, MAKE A POSI'TIVE IMPACT ON
INTRACTABLE SITUATIONS. THE PRESTIGE OF THE
PRESIDENT OF THE UNITED STATES CAN ALSO BE USED
TO AMELIORATE THE INTENSITY OF CONFLICTS, HE
ADDED. SATTAR SAID HE HOPED THE UN GENERAL
ASSEMBLY WOULD TAKE UP CONFLICT PREVENTION IN
SOUTH ASIA AT.ITS SEPTEMBER SESSION.
ICJ ATLANTIQUE DECISION
5. SATTAR DREW U.S. ATTENTION TO THE RECENT
DECISION OF THE INTERNATIONAL COURT OF JUSTICE
(ICJ) ON THE DOWNED PAKISTANI ATLANTIQUE P-3
PATROL AIRCRAFT. THE FIRST FIFTY PARAGRAPHS OF
THE JUDGEMENT, ELABORATING ON THE COURT'5 LACK OF
JURISDICTION, ARE RELATIVELY UNIMPORTANT, SATTAR
OPINED. THE CRUX OF THE DECISION, HE EMPHASIZED,
IS CONTAINED IN PARAGRAPHS 51-55, AND IN
PARTICULAR IN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE, QUOTE: THE
COURT'S LACK OF JURISDICTION DOES NOT RELIEVE THE
PARTIES OF THE OBLIGATION TO SETTLE DISPUTES BY
PEACEFUL MEANS. END QUOTE. THIS IS THE FIRST
TIME THE COURT HAS GONE "SO FAR, " SATTAR
ASSERTED. THE COURT DID NOT DENY THAT A DISPUTE
EXISTED, BUT REMINDED THE PARTIES THAT HAVING
ACCEPTED THE UN 'CHARTER, THEY ARE REQUIRED UNDER
ARTICLE 22 TO ACT IN GOOD FAITH TO FULFILL
CHARTER OBLIGATIONS, INCLUDING THE OBLIGATION TO
SETTLE DISPUTES PEACEFULLY, ACCORDING TO THE
FOREIGN MINISTER'S ANALYSIS.
AFGHANISTAN AND CONTROL
OF TERRORISM IN PAKISTAN
6. ~ THE FOREIGN MINISTER WAS EQUALLY
PESSIMISTIC ON AFGHANISTAN, ADMITTING THAT THE
GOP "MADE A MISTAKE" REFERRING TO THE AFGHAN
GOVERNMENT'S EXERCISING "CONTROL." "WHAT
GOVERNMENT? WHAT CONTROL?" SATTAR ASKED
RHETORICALLY. "THE GOVERNMENT IN AFGHANISTAN HAS
CEASED TO EXIST." (FURTHER DETAILS OF THE
AFGHANISTAN DISCUSSION ARE REPORTED SEPTEL.)

YEARS TOWARD EXTREMISM AND MILITANCY IN PAKISTAN
"CANNOT BE REVERSED IN A DAY." ONCE, HE
COMPLAINED, EVERYONE WANTED TO EMBRACE THE

NUCLEAR WEAPONS, CTBT, AND FMCT
9 SATTAR REMEMBERED HIS DISCUSSION WITH
DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT; SAYING HE HAD EXPLAI'NED
HIS PERSONAL COMMITMENT TO PAKISTAN'S POSSESSION
OF NUCLEAR WEAPONS BY DESCRIBING HAVING "WATCHED
PAKISTAN BEING CUT INTO TWO" IN 1971 FROM THE GOP
EMBASSY COMPOUND IN NEW DELHI HE HAD ALSO
HOWEVER, RESEARCHED THE ETHICAL ASPECTS OF
NUCLEAR WEAPONS AND BELIEVED THE GOP HAD A MORAL
RESPONSIBILITY TO ENSURE SAEEGUARDS OF THESE
WEAPONS. "WE DON'T NEED AN AGREEMENT," SATTAR
AVERRED. "WE NEED TO TAKE UNILATERAL MEASURES TO
ENSURE CUSTODIAL SAFETY." HE EXPLAINED THE GOP
HAD ALLOCATED 200 MILLION DOLLARS TOWARD THIS
PROGRAM OVER THE NEXT THREE YEARS, AND SAID
PAKISTAN WOULD WELCOME SHARING THE EXPERIENCE OF
OTHER COUNTRIES ON THIS TOPIC.
10. ~ SATTAR REITERATED THE GOVERNMENT'S
WILLINGNESS TO SIGN CTBT --AND THE EXPLANATION
THAT THE GOP'S FOCUS ON "DOMESTIC PRIORITIES"
WOULD NOT PERMIT CTBT SIGNATURE AT THIS TIME..
CHIEF EXECUTIVE MUSHARRAF IS WHERE HE IS DUE TO
THE DOMESTIC SITUATION, SATTAR EXPLAINED. HIS
PRIORITIES ARE TO STABILIZE THE ECONOMY AND
IMPROVE THE SYSTEM OF GOVERNANCE'EEKING TO
ELIMINATE MIS-RULE AND CORRUPTION. IN ORDER TO
ACCOMPLISH THIS, SATTAR CONTINUED, "WE CANNOT
CAUSE PEOPLE TO COME INTO THE STREETS," AND SOME
IN PAKISTAN HAVE HARDLINE VIEWS. BASED ON TWO
FACTORS -- THE FACT THAT SIGNING OF CTBT BY
PAKISTAN WILL NOT BRING THE TREATY INTO FORCE,
AND THE GOP'S KNOWN POLICY THAT IT NEVER HAS BEEN
AND NEVER WILL BE THE FIRST TO TEST — — THE GOP
'AS
DECIDED TO COMPLY WITH CTBT WITHOUT SIGNING
THE TREATY. "WE ARE PREPARED TO SIGN WHEN WE
CAN, " HE CONCLUDED.

11. ~ BARNES ASKED'ABOUT PROSPECTS FOR
PROGRESS ON THE FMCT NEGOTIATIONS. AFTER NOTING
THAT "THE PROBLEM IS IN GENEVA," SATTAR REPEATED
THE GOP'S INSISTENCE ON COMPLIANCE AND
VERIFICATION. HE NOTED THAT WHILE IN NEW DELHI
IN 1991, HE HAD WITNESSED SIGNATURE OF AN
AGREEMENT BETWEEN PAKISTAN AND INDIA THAT NEITHER

ENGENDER, THE FOREIGN MINISTER ASKED.
COMMENT
12. ~ SATTAR'S COMMENTS, PARTICULARLY WITH
REGARD TO INDIA AND AFGHANISTAN, INDEED SEEMED TO
PORTRAY WHAT THE AMBASSADOR DESCRIBED IN REFTEL
AS A GOVERNMENT GRAPPLING FOR NEW IDEAS.
UNFORTUNATELY, SATTAR AND THE FOREIGN MINISTRY
ARE NOT THE MOST INFLUENTIAL GOP ACTORS IN THE
SEARCH FOR NEW DIRECTIONS IN EITHER KASHMIR OR
AFGHANISTAN.
