1. -effl- Summary. State Secretary Wolfgang Ischinger 
told Deputy Secretary Talbott February 4 that the FRG 
is on the same wavelength as the U.S. regarding the 
Washington NATO Summit. However, he called for 
assigning discussion of non-first-use (NFU) to a NATO 
sub-group in order to provide Foreign Minister Fischer 
political cover from the left wing of his Greens Party. 
While preferring not to name names of NATO candidate 
countries in Washington, the FRG sees naming all nine 
as probably unavoidable. Ischinger underscored German 
support of NATO and stressed the need to avoid any 
legal or constitutional barriers to German 
participation potentially caused by a redefinition of 
NATO's scope. The FRG wants to emphasize the UNSC and 
international law in defining mandates, but recognizes 
the need for a loophole for freedom of NATO action.
2. 4E4 Summary continued. Ischinger characterized.the 
proposed framework agreement for Rambouillet as likely 
a hard sell for both the French and the Russians and, 
as a package, likely to be rejected by Milosevic. He 
said that consensus is emerging on the need for ground 
forces in Kosovo should a political agreement be 
reached, and called for a review of problems in Albania 
in developing a regional strategy. Germany believes 
ESDI should concentrate on crisis management and 
peacekeeping and leave aside Article V issues. It 
hopes to use its EU and WEU presidencies to put ESDI on 
the right track and offer Paris the opportunity to 
re-examine its relationship with NATO. The Germans 
detect some flexibility from Russia on CFE. 
3. (U) Summary continued. FRG Foreign Office State 
Secretary Wolfgang Ischinger hosted DEPSEC Talbott, 
Ambassador Kornblum, NSC Senior Director Blinken, EUR 
DAS Asmus, and D staffers Bass and Goldberg for a 
working luncheon and office meeting February 4 to 
review, inter alia, Kosovo and preparations for the 
Washington NATO Summit. Ischinger was accompanied by 
Political Director Gunter Pleuger, DAS-equivalent for 
Security Affairs Klaus Scharioth, Arms Control 
Commissioner Rudiger Hartmann, Balkan Task Force 
Director Christian Pauls, and Deputy Director for U.S. 
Affairs Michael Schmunk. End Summary.

4. After exchanging pleasantries, DepSec Talbott 
noted that, while close, the U.S and the FRG had some 
work to do to harmonize positions, particularly in WMD 
and terrorism, before the Washington NATO Summit. He 
also underscored the need to agree on Open Door 
language. Ischinger emphasized that the FRG wanted the 
Summit to be successful. Scharioth characterized the 
U.S. and the FRG as "on the same wavelength" regarding 
NATO's Open Door. Ideally, no names of would-be 
candidates would be specified, but he noted that in 
practical terms, the Alliance likely will have to name 
those mentioned in Madrid. Given the need to include 
Bulgaria if Romania is mentioned, a chain reaction 
would end in naming all candidate countries. While 
not exactly pleasing, this result is "probably 
unavoidable." Ischinger noted the FRG would favor no 
names, at least as a starting proposal. 
5. -474— Ischinger and the DepSec agreed that Open Door 
language and any Membership Action Plan should not 
build in automaticity. Ischinger underscored the need 
for NATO to make a political decision on each country's 
application only when that country is actually ready 
for membership, and not before; DepSec Talbott agreed, 
emphasizing that discussion of a second round of 
enlargement should not distract from the Summit's 
centerpiece - a strong vision statement. DEPSEC 
Talbott highlighted the WMD proposal as a free-standing 
initiative. He averred that terrorism should not be 
treated in isolation but rather within the WMD context. 
This, he reasoned, has nothing to do with globalizing 
NATO but everything to do with NATO's goals - including 
those under Article V. 
6. *E” Ischinger noted that inclusion of the WMD issue 
"should not be a problem for us," but expressed concern 
that any approach on WMD be balanced. He cautioned 
against sending a signal that NATO's answer to non-
proliferation issues is strictly a military one. 
Rather, one should focus on implementing and energizing 
existing treaties; U.S. efforts in South Asia are an 
example of this. Scharioth underscored the FRG 
position that any NATO WMD response be mainly 
political. After cautioning that any summit WMD 
declaration not create a friend-foe mentality and be 
perceived in the southern Mediterranean as the "next 
crusade" against the Arabs, Ischinger said that any 
perceived differences between the U.S. and the FRG on 
this were a matter of emphasis rather than of

7. tei DepSec Talbott underscored that all NATO 
members face an increased threat from WMD and that 
various members have sophisticated intelligence 
capabilities which could be shared for the common good. 
He explained how including WMD in the NATO rubric 
would help with domestic constituency-building in the

NFU - ASSIGN IT TO COMMITTEE REVIEW 
8. -44- Scharioth highlighted the German desire for 
directing a body (possibly the Joint Committee on 
Proliferation (JCP), the Senior Political Committee 
(SPC), or a new NAC sub-group) to deal with NFU within 
the larger context of disarmament. If a primarily 
political body (not/not the HLG) could wrap NFU into a 
larger discussion, possibly also covering risk-reducing 
measures, CSBMs, transparency questions, and perhaps, a 
WMD initiative, Foreign Minister Fischer could gain 
political cover from the left wing of his own Greens 
Party for not having NFU on the summit agenda proper. 
Scharioth handed over a position paper in which he 
noted the FRG had avoided using the term "no first 
use." Likewise, Fischer would not raise this issue at 
the Summit as long as the issue would be eventually 
handled by some group within NATO. 
9. -E-G-Ischinger argued that, should the Summit not 
succeed in "hiding" the NFU issue by relegating it to a 
back-burner discussion in a body covering a myriad of 
related issues, the Bundestag inevitably will revive 
debate on NFU. Pleuger noted that Fischer is very 
pro- U.S. and pro-NATO. By giving him this concession 
and pacifying the Greens' left wing, Fischer will have 
increased freedom of action in other areas. DEPSEC 
Talbott rejected questioning a basic NATO principle in 
order to accommodate the Greens. He emphasized that 
NFU is not only a political issue, but also an 
important question of military strategy. DAS Asmus 
noted that, while the Summit is not the proper forum to 
review NFU, the Secretary's NAC intervention noted that 
the USG is not afraid of discussing the issue. That 
said, he noted the difficulty of both opening the NFU 
issue and determining terms of reference for it.

10. (C) On NATO's scope, Scharioth urged aiming 
toward a conservative geographic framework. Ischinger 
underscored that, for the FRG, NATO remains the 
cornerstone of its military decision-making. "We are 
completely integrated," he eaid, and Bonn does not want 
any light between the FRG and NATO because of possible 
scenarios presenting legal and/or constitutional 
barriers to German participation. "We want NATO ,to 
remain what it is," Ischinger said. The Germans 
emphasized the need to highlight international law, 
particularly UNSC resolutions, when defining mandates. 
While a "loophole" is essential for exceptional 
circumstances, such as the humanitarian crisis in 
Kosovo, Scharioth stressed that this should be the 
exception, not the rule. 
11. tft Talbott underscored that the U.S. would not 
support defining mandates "under the authority" of the 
UN, noting that the Brussels 1994 Summit language was 
Bosnia-specific. "Authority" connotes subordination. 
He emphasized U.S. consideration of German views on 
scope as well as U.S. support for the UN and for the 
prerogatives of all UNSC members. That said, NATO 
could not be paralyzed at a crunch time on Kosovo in 
the coming weeks if the UNSC were tied down by the 
threat of a Russian veto. The international community 
would lose everything it is trying to accomplish in 
Kosovo, and Europe would experience another war. 
12. 4G4- Scharioth offered the phrase, "NATO is 
prepared to act under a mandate of the UN or the OSCE," 
while including a reference to NATO's acting "in 
accordance to the UN Charter" elsewhere in the 
Strategic Concept, as one possible formula to tie the 
mandate issue to international institutions while 
allowing NATO freedom of action. Ambassador Kornblum 
noted that this phraseology reflects the drafting 
exercises before the 1992 NATO Oslo and the then-CSCE 
Helsinki communiques. DepSec Talbott agreed to 
consider German views. According t'o the Germans, the 
French want to go beyond the German language.but will 
consider it nevertheless. They noted that the French 
are not wedded to using the term "authority," but 
definitely want to refer to a "mandate." The DepSec 
said any language should protect NATO principles in all 
possible contingencies. He urged the FRG, particularly 
as EU Presidency, to push for a consensus. Ischinger 
said he would raise this in Paris February 15.

CFE: GERMANS SEE SOME RUSSIAN FLEXIBILITY; FRG COULD 
LOWER ITS CEILING 
15. 4-e+ Hartmann characterized the German-Russian arms 
control discussions (on the margins of Ivanov's 
February 1-2 visit - see ref B) as "constructive." 
Ivanov said progress on CFE is a condition for Russian 
participation at the Washington Summit. Fischer 
cautioned Ivanov that there almost certainly would not 
be a breakthrough on CFE before the summit. The DepSec 
and Ischinger agreed that it is up to Warsaw to decide 
how to address Polish national ceilings. Hartmann said 
he had told the Russians that they could reassure the 
Poles by demonstrating restraint in Kaliningrad and the 
Leningrad Military District. The Russians agreed to do 
so. Hartmann emphasized that the FRG is also willing 
to examine reducing its own territorial ceiling in this 
vein. Should the Poles, Czechs, and Hungarians reduce 
their national ceilings, the outcome would not be that 
far from Russian demands, and the Russians had 
indicated that this difference could be overcome. The 
DepSec emphasized that this is not only a political 
question, but also a genuine strategic concern. He 
welcomed German consideration of reducing its own 
ceiling and said the FRG could help in reassuring the 
Poles. The DepSec said he would consult with the FRG 
before the next round of strategic talks in Moscow 
later in the month.

16. (C) Pauls characterized the proposed framework 
agreement and annexes as, "parcel-by-parcel, 
reasonable," but taken as a whole a "huge demand on 
Belgrade." The requirement that Belgrade basically 
give up sovereignty over Kosovo would reinforce the 
Serbs' belief that the West is taking Kosovo away from , 
them forever. In any case, .consensus is emerging that 
an international military force will be needed in 
Kosovo after a settlement is reached. Ischinger 
stressed the need to stabilize Albania and include it 
in any regional strategy. He said that increased 
financial resources, as well as possibly civilian and 
military personnel, might be needed there to stabilize 
the situation and to help cut off arms trafficking to 
Kosovo. He also stressed that, should negotiations 
lead to a settlement in Kosovo, an implementation, 
structure should include a visible and meaningful role 
for the EU and its member-states. In this light, 
Ischinger said that Fischer was not pleased with the 
Contact Group's procedural arrangements for 
Rambouillet. The FRG will have to respond to the other 
12 (not including France and the UK) EU members' 
potential complaints that the EU does not have a vital 
role at Rambouillet. The DepSec reasoned that Belgrade 
would continue its brutalization in Kosovo, which would 
reinforce ethnic Albanian radicalization, absent a 
settlement envisioned by the framework agreement. 
Russia, neuralgic about Chechnya, must realize the 
implications of Moscow's own objectives and recognize 
that there are no alternatives which would be more 
palatable, in the long run, to Moscow. 
ESDI: FRG EU/WEU PRESIDENCIES AN OPPORTUNITY 
17. -ter Scharioth termed the current German EU and WEU 
presidencies an opportunity for the FRG to move ESDI 
forward, while leaving aside Article V questions. He 
said the range of possibilities.for European 
participation in crisis management operations 
essentially boiled down to five options: (1) NATO; (2) 
NATO and partners; (3) Europeans under control of the 
NAC; (4) Europeans on the ground under the political 
control of the WEU but using NATO assets; and (5) 
Europeans on the ground under WEU control without using 
NATO assets. Option five could be devised in a way to 
ensure that NATO is not damaged. German ESDI efforts 
concentrate exclusively on crisis management and 
peacekeeping. Ischinger underscored that they are 
consistent with U.S. interests and should not 
raise serious U.S. concerns.

18. -(-C-4- To strengthen the security pillar, the FRG 
supports havina one individual serving as both Mr./Ms. 
CFSP and WEU Secretary General. After terming St. 
Malo "a bunch of hot air," Ischinger said the FRG's 
operational objectives are relatively modest but 
achievable. The FRG, as EU President, will present a 
report on ESDI at the EU Summit in June. Germany will 
not seek an endorsement of the paper; rather it will 
outline the situation and offer various ways to 
proceed. Ischinger noted that neither the Finnish nor 
the Portuguese presidencies likely wili make much 
progress on ESDI. Therefore, "if the FRG and the U.S. 
do it right," ESDI may be on the right track by the 
French presidency, offering Paris an opportunity to 
take a fresh look at its relationship with NATO. 
19. 444.-DepSec Talbott reiterated U.S. support for 
ESDI. DAS Asmus noted concern that the Europeans may 
be focusing too much on institutional questions and not 
enough on capabilities. He stressed the need to 
provide greater clarity on the operational level and to 
frame ESDI in a manner consistent with our "three D's.." 
20. 444- Comment: In Embassy discussions with German 
officials, the latter stress that NATO would naturally 
be the organization to take on larger military 
operations and that Options 4 and 5 would probably be 
used, rarely, in smaller crisis management actions in 
cases where the U.S. could not or did not choose to 
participate. They have also pointed out that the 
easier European access is to NATO assets (ESDI within 
NATO), the less credibility those who argue in favor of 
an autonomous European capability (read: the French) 
would have. End Comment.
