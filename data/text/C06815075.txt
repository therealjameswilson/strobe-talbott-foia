4. ~ ASGRIMSSON SAID HE HAD MAJOR PROBLEMS WITH THE U.S.
POSITION ON CONTRACTING LANGUAGE IN PROPOSED ICELANDIC
LEGISLATION AFFECTING THE U.S.-ICELANDIC DEFENSE
RELATIONSHIP. ASGRIMSSON SAID HE HAD TOLD THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY, DURING MEETINGS ON MAY 2, ICELAND WOULD TRY TO
PROCEED ON THE BASIS OF THE DEPUTY SECRETARY'S FOURTH
SUGGESTION, WHICH HE HAD PERCEIVED TO BE ICELANDIC
AGREEMENT TO DELAY IMPLEMENTATION ON THE PROVISIONS FOR
CONTRACTING FOR ONE YEAR. THE GOI COULD NOT CONSIDER
DELAYING IMPLEMENTATION UNTIL THE U.S. AND ICELAND CAME TO
AN AGREEMENT ON THE ISSUE. THE GOI WAS WORKING UNDER THAT
IMPRESSION AND WHEN ASGRIMSSON LAST SPOKE WITH THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY HE UNDERSTOOD THE U.S. WANTED ICELAND TO REMOVE
THE CONTRACTING LANGUAGE, BUT ASGRIMSSON HAD SAID THE GOI
COULD DELAY FOR ONE YEAR. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND
ASGRIMSSON THEN DISCUSSED WHETHER THE DEADLINE WOULD BE
APRIL, MAY OR JUNE. THE MONTH DID NOT MATTER TO ICELAND.
ASGRIMSSON NOW UNDERSTOOD, HOWEVER, THE U.S. POSITION WAS
THE CONTRACTING LANGUAGE HAD TO BE REMOVED TO REACH A
CONCLUSION. THIS POSED A LARGE PROBLEM.
5.~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID THIS POSED A GREAT
PROBLEM FOR HIM AS WELL. HE BELIEVED HE HAD A RECIPROCAL
COMMITMENT TO WORK WITH ASGRIMSSON TO CREATE AN
ENVIRONMENT WHERE THEIR WORKING-LEVEL COLLEAGUES COULD
FASHION AN AGREEMENT. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID HE HAD
LISTENED CAREFULLY TO ASGRIMSSON' RECONSTRUCTION OF THE1R
MAY 2 MEETING AND WANTED TO SHARE HIS RECOLLECTION, THOUGH
IT DID NOT DIFFER FROM THE FUNDAMENTAL POINTS OF I
ASGRIMSSON' SUMMARY.
6. ~THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID THE LACK OF UNDERSTANDING
STEMMED FROM TWO WORDS: REMOVE AND DELAY. WHEN THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY LAID OUT OPTIONS ON MAY 2, BOTH SIDES AGREED
THEY WOULD HAVE TO STUDY THE OPTIONS IN LIGHT OF DOMESTIC
CONSIDERATIONS. WHEN THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SPOKE WITH
ASGRIMSSON ON MAY 4, HE HAD LISTENED TO ASGRIMSSON'S
VARIANT OF POINT FOUR, WHICH ASGRIMSSON HAD COMBINED WITH
OPTION TWO. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID HE HAD THEN
OUTLINED THREE POINTS: 1) WHILE THE PROBLEM WAS NOT
SOLVED, THE GOI WAS CLEARLY MAKING A GOOD FAITH EFFORT; 2)
THEY NEEDED TO CLARIFY WHAT THE UNDERSTANDING HAD BEEN;
AND, 3) THEY NEEDED TO WORK OVERNIGHT TO CLARIFY WHAT THE

7. ~ THE GOI HAD PROVIDED ASGRIMSSON'S TALKING POINTS

MADE BY THE DEPUTY SECRETARY, THE POINT DEALING WITH SEA-

8. + THE DEPUTY SECRETARY NOTED IF ASGRIMSSON HAD
INTENDED TO SEND A SIGNAL DURING THIS DISCUSSION THAT THE
FORMULATION WAS IMPOSSIBLE, HE HAD NOT HEARD IT. THE
DEPUTY SECRETARY ASSURED ASGRIMSSON HE HAD NOT RAISED THIS
TO QUESTION ASGRIMSSON' RECOLLECTION OR HIS GOOD FAITH.
HE REITERATED BOTH SIDES WERE TRYING VERY HARD AND IT WAS
A MIXED BLESSING TO HAVE SUCH A TECHNICAL ISSUE RAISED TO
THE MINISTERIAL LEVEL.
9. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID IT WOULD BE IMPORTANT TO
KEEP WORKING-LEVEL TECHNICAL EXPERTS ENGAGED AT EVERY
STEP. THE PROBLEM COULD NOT BE SOLVED OVER THE COURSE OF
THIS PARTICULAR CONVERSATION, BUT IT WOULD BE IMPORTANT TO
AGREE WHETHER OR NOT DEPUTY ICELANDIC FM GUNLAUGSSON COULD
REMAIN IN WASHINGTON THROUGH THE WEEKEND TO DISCUSS THE
ISSUE FURTHER. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY OFFERED TO HAVE
ASSISTANT SECRETARY GROSSMAN, U.S. AMBASSADOR TO ICELAND
GRIFFITH, AND EUR/NB DIRECTOR GRAZE MEET WITH GUNLAUGSSON
AGAIN TO ATTEMPT TO RESOLVE THE SITUATION.
10. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY THEN OUTLINED EFFORTS MADE BY
DIRECTOR GRAZE OVER THE LAST, TWENTY-FOUR HOURS TO
RECONCILE THE GOI'S VARIANT OF OPTION FOUR, WHICH DID NOT
INCLUDE THE USG'S PROPOSAL TO INCLUDE A REFERENCE TO AN
AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE U.S. AND ICELAND, THE U.S. VARIANT.
GUNLAUGSSON HAD INDICATED THIS WOULD NOT WORK, BUT THE
DEPUTY SECRETARY FELT THIS SHOULD NOT MEAN THE ISSUE COULD
NOT BE RESOLVED.
11. ~ ASGRIMSSON APOLOGIZED FOR THE PROBLEM BUT
INDICATED HE DID NOT SEE A MISUNDERSTANDING. HE WAS
TRYING TO FIND A SOLUTION ACCEPTABLE TO THE GOI, WHICH WAS
IN THE VEIN OF WHAT HAD BEEN AGREED TO AT THE MAY 2 AND 4
MEETINGS, BUT IF THAT WAS NOT GOOD ENOUGH FOR THE U.S.,
THEN THERE WAS A BIG PROBLEM. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY NOTED
HE WAS TRYING ALSO, AND IF THAT WAS NOT GOOD ENOUGH FOR
THE GOI, THAT TOO WAS A PROBLEM.

12. ~ ASGRIMSSON SAID HIS PROBLEMS ALSO STEMMED FROM
PARLIAMENT, WHICH WAS WAITING FOR ANSWERS AND
EXPLANATIONS. THIS WAS NOT AN EASY POSITION. HE ALSO DID
NOT UNDERSTAND THE DIFFERENCES POSED BY THE LAWYERS AND
OTHERS IN THE INTERAGENCY PROCESS. GUNLAUGSSON WOULD
REMAIN IN WASHINGTON AND HAD SPOKEN WITH GRAZE. HE
THOUGHT AMBASSADOR GRIFFITHS HAD HIS NUMBER, BUT HE HAD
NOT HEAR FROM HER. TIME WAS AN ISSUE AND HE HOPED
DISCUSSIONS COULD RESUME EARLY ON MAY 8.
13. ~ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID ASSISTANT SECRETARY
GROSSMAN, AMBASSADOR GRIFFITHS, AND DIRECTOR GRAZE WOULD
DISCUSS THE MATTER WITH GUNLAUGSSON ON MONDAY MORNING. HE
SAID GRAZE HAD SUGGESTED INCLUDING LANGUAGE IN THE LAW
THAT ACKNOWLEDGED IT WOULD BE MODIFIED FOLLOWING AGREEMENT
BETWEEN THE U.S. AND ICELAND. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID
HE DID NOT EXPECT ASGRIMSSON TO COMMENT ON SUCH A
PROPOSAL, BUT NOTED IT TOOK INTO ACCOUNT THE GOI POSITION
THAT CONTRACTING LANGUAGE HAS TO STAY, AND THE U.S.
POSITION THAT IT HAD TO GO. GRAZE'S SUGGESTION WAS
DESIGNED TO ALLOW EACH SIDE ITS DE MINIMUS POSITION. OF
COURSE IT WOULD STILL HAVE TO BE SOLD TO THE LAWYERS, THE
PENTAGON AND OTHER CONSTITUENTS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY
SAID HE RAISED THIS POINT IN ORDER TO ASSURE THE GOI WE
DID NOT INTEND TO GIVE UP. IF THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTION
REMAINED, THEY MIGHT NOT BE ABLE TO SUCCEED. THE GOI
WOULD PASS A LAW AND THE U.S. WOULD HAVE TO ACCEPT IT.
NONETHELESS, IT WAS WORTH ONE MORE TRY. THE DEPUTY
SECRETARY NOTED HE WOULD BE TRAVELING TO SOUTH AFRICA, BUT
AGREED TO MAKE HIMSELF AVAILABLE TO TALKi IF IT PROVED
NECESSARY.
14. (~ ASGRIMSSON AGREED AND REITERATED HIS FEELING THAT
EVERYONE WAS WORKING IN GOOD FAITH. HE WAS CONCERNED,
HOWEVER, THAT NOT EVERYONE IN WASHINGTON FELT ICELAND WAS
WORKING IN GOOD FAITH. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ASSURED
ASGRIMSSON HE HAD NOT HEARD ANYONE QUESTION ICELAND' GOOD
FAITH. IN FACT, IT WAS A MORE PRACTICAL QUESTION OF
WHETHER A LAW PASSED BY THE PARLIAMENT OF A SOVEREIGN
NATION, AN ALLY AND HOST TO AMERICAN FORCES, WOULD PASS A
LAW WE'COULD NOT LIVE WITH. WE WOULD NOT VIOLATE THE LAW,BUT WE COULD ALSO FIND OURSELVES IN THE POSITION OF NOT
BEING ABLE TO BE A GUEST OF THAT COUNTRY ANYMORE. WE
WOULD'AVE TO CONSIDER THE COMMERCIAL LAWS, THE MILITARYEQUITIES AND ISSUE ADJUDICATED IN OUR COURTS. NO ONE HAD
ACCUSED ICELAND OF PLAYING DIRTY, BUT ICELAND HAD A
DIFFERENT VIEW OF WHAT WAS APPROPRIATE.

16. M ASGRIMSSQN NOTED THAT MANY PEOPLE WERE ASKING

RELATIONSHIP AND WERE WORRIED ABOUT WHAT THE FUTURE HELD.
MANY GOI OFFICIALS AND ICELAND'S PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEE
QN FOREIGN RELATIONS OFFICIALS WERE WORRIED ABOUT THE
ISSUE. HE FELT IT WAS CLEAR THAT BY DELAYING THE MATTER A

WORRIED ALL THE TIME. ASGRIMSSON SAID HE HAD TROUBLE
UNDERSTANDING THE PROBLEM ANDP THEREFORE P FOUND IT

17.~THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ASSURED ASGRIMSSON HE DID NOT
WISH TO CAUSE HIM ANY PROBLEMS. HE REITERATED THAT IDEAS

SPIRIT I AND IN THE SPIRIT OF THI S CONVERSATION I PERHAPS

SECRETARY ASSURED ASGRIMSSON HE WOULD SPEAK WITH ASSISTANT

THIS MATTER WOULD HAVE THE HIGHEST POLICY LEVEL SUPPORT IN
THE STATE DEPARTMENT. FURTHER, PRINCIPAL DEPUTY UNDER
