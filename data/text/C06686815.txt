Dear Barry, 
RELEASE IN FULL 
Here are some thoughts on what you might want to present to 
the Russian General Officers. 
Armies reflect the people, the culture, and the technology 
of the nation they defend. As an institution in the society of 
the country, they must change as the needs of the people change. 
From the Dukes of Muscovy to Stalin the Russian state 
pursued an expansionist territorial policy. At different times 
so did Egypt, Persia, China, Rome, the Ottoman Empire, England, 
France, Germany, Japan, and the United States. 
Your Army has well served the needs of Russia during this 
long period of expansion. In general, territorial acquisition 
today is a losing proposition. The future of the Russian 
state, I submit, would not be served by the expansion of 
territory and you certainly do not need more client states to 
drain your treasury and involve you in their domestic problems. 
The only major country in the world today with military 
ambitions may be China. Given the appropriate political 
circumstances China might still seek military solutions to some 
of its problems such as Taiwan. 
Let me suggest to you that the primar-y role of the Russian 
Army today is nation building. The Army is the one institution_ 
in society that can move on a moment's notice, feed itself, 
shelter itself and maintain order. 
Technological cultures need order and stability to 
function. Raw materials have to be moved, sophisticated 
manufacturing operations carried out over time, and wealth 
distributed on some sort of a rational basis. The Russian Army 
is the final guarantor of that stability. Somalia is a 
classical example of what happens when the Armed Forces of a 
society become brigands. The nation dies. 
Therefore I suggest that the organization of the Army .and 
its equipment must change to meet the current needs of the 
Russian people. The Russian Army was organized to conquer.
Europe and with the help of the Navy, to drive the Americans 
from the continent. We are withdrawing from Europe, and you 
have enough problems at home so that the prospect of conquering 
and governing Europe is not an attractive option.

Your history, even more than ours tells you of the immense 
suffering of a people during a civil war. There are countrymen 
of yours alive today who saw the misery that followed the 
destruction of the monarchy and the introduction of Communism. 
In our country we are still working our way through the legacy 
of destruction that followed our Civil War that ended over a 
century ago in 1865. 
Over the centuries men have fought and died for salt, for 
gold, for women, for religion, for political philosophy, and 
most recently for Nationalism. Nationalism and Muslim 
fundamentalism appear to be the only causes for which, men are 
prepared to die today. .These two concepts are tearing many of 
todays political boundaries asunder. Certainly they are a 
problem for the Russian Army. 
The loyalty, common sense, and compassion of the Russian 
Officer Corps and the Army as an institution is bound to be 
tested in the coming years. The explosion of Nationalism and 
Muslim fundamentalism means you will have to devise new 
political systems flexible enough to permit your country to 
flourish again. 
Russia did a remarkable job of governing the 120 odd 
nationalities that made up the Soviet Union during the years of 
communist rule...but it could not last forever. 
Peace, law and order are basic to any prosperous society. 
• Only the Russian Army can make this possible in your .homeland. 
Like soldiers everywhere you will no doubt, from time to 
time, find much fault with your politicians. Don't let this 
discourage you. The political process and soldiering requite 
quite different talents and characteristics. Very few soldiers 
have made useful politicians and very few politicians have been 
very good at soldiering. The essence of politics is compromise 
-- the essence of soldiering is dedication. 
Coups d'etat by the military have frequently been quite 
successful in the early years of a takeover. Where they lead 
to disaster as in Germany under Hitler and in Italy under 
Mussolini is that they tend to be rigid.

What will be the legitimate missions of the Russian Army 
for the next generation of so? 
First, to maintain a deterrent military posture sufficient 
to discourage your neighbors from rearranging your borders 
without consulting you. 
Second, to maintain forces capable of supporting the 
political authority of the government inside your national 
boundaries. 
Third, to do some nation building at home and to maintain 
forces capable of providing relief in cases of natural disaster 
such as the recent earthquake or civil disorder such as is 
currently the problem in Georgia and Tajikistan. 
FinallY, to maintain forces which can participate with the 
United Nations in peacekeeping operations. 
It must be difficult for you to believe but the United 
States is not your enemy. We have no economic or territorial 
disputes with you. The bone of contention between us was that 
we did not want to adopt your political system. It was party 
dogma that the dictatorship of the proletariat would be 
established worldwide and by force if necessary. 
The Hoover Commission tried to feed you during the 30s when 
the Kulaks were destroyed. We supported you at great cost to 
ourselves during WW II. Today the people of the-United States 
overwhelmingly want to support the Russian people. 
We respect the genius of the Russian people, we admire the 
courage and dedication of the Russian Army. 
Both the Russian Empire and the American sphere of 
influence are contracting. We as well as you are withdrawing 
from Europe. We are withdrawing in the Pacific. We have given 
up our bases in the Philippines. 
The utility of,military power is challenged by the economic 
success of a, disarmed Japan. By 1995 we will have an Army of 
approximately 500,000 men -- smaller than any force we have had 
since 1939. • 
My father told me that when he was a cadet at West Point in 
the 1930s they studied Napoleon's campaigns and derived a 
military maxim.

The maxim was "Never go' marching to Moscow." 
I expect My grandson, Captain Sean McCaffrey, will some day 
command an American Battalion alongside a Russian Battalion. I 
suggest in the not too distant future our Armies will be 
working together to bring peace and prosperity, if we can work 
together for a peaceful and prosperous world are fantastic. 
Perhaps among the group listening to me today are several 
of the great heroes of Russian history. Upon your shoulders 
will descend complex problems as severe,as any encountered by 
men in any age. If you and the Russian Army can rise to meet 
these challenges successfully you will have earned the 
gratitude of generations of Russians yet unborn. 
I hope you will remember in the difficult days sure to come 
that you have well wishers all over the world who will rejoice 
in your success and suffer if you lost. 
Good Luck.
