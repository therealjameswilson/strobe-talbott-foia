U.S. CONCERNS•OVER WHITE PAPER 
3. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT BEGAN THE MEETING BY 
LAYING OUT FOR AMBASSADOR LI U.S. CONCERNS OVER CHINA'S 
JUST-RELEASED WHITE PAPER ON TAIWAN. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
NOTED THAT HE, DAPNSA STEINBERG, DOD U/S SLOCOMBE AND DJCS 
GENERAL RALSTON HAD WHAT THEY BELIEVED WAS A USEFUL, 
PRODUCTIVE AND POSITIVE VISIT TO BEIJING THE PRIOR WEEK. 
UNFORTUNATELY, THE PRC'S ISSUANCE OF ITS WHITE PAPER 
IMMEDIATELY AFTER THE DELEGATION'S DEPARTURE HAD CAST A 
CLOUD OVER THAT JUDGEMENT. 
4. -(.C.)- THE DEPUTY SECRETARY TOLD LI THAT CHINA'S WHITE 
PAPER WAS VERY TROUBLESOME IN ITS TONE, TIMING, SUBSTANCE 
AND CONTEXT. MOST TROUBLESOME WAS THE ADDITION OF A NEW 
CRITERION FOR USE OF FORCE - THE WHITE PAPER'S ASSERTION 
THAT CHINA WOULD .USE FORCE AGAINST TAIWAN IF TAIWAN 
REFUSED "INDEFINITELY" TO NEGOTIATE WITH BEIJING. THIS 
ADDED A NEW, UNHELPFUL ELEMENT TO THE CROSS-STRAIT 
EQUATION AND WOULD HAVE A NEGATIVE IMPACT ON SECURITY IN 
THE REGION. IT COULD ALSO HAVE A NEGATIVE IMPACT ON OUR 
SHARED GOAL OF A PEACEFUL RESOLUTION OF THE CROSS-STRAIT 
ISSUE.

WITH A VARIETY OF KEY CHINESE LEADERS ON JUST THIS 
SUBJECT, AND HAD RECEIVED.NO HINT EITHER OF THE IMPENDING 
RELEASE OF THE WHITE PAPER OR THAT CHINA WAS PREPARED TO

6. -teT-THE DISCUSSION IN BEIJING, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
EXPLAINED TO AMBASSADOR LI, HAD APPEARED TO BE OPEN AND 
FRANK. BOTH SIDES HAD AGREED TO DISCUSS ALL ISSUES 
OPENLY, INCLUDING WHERE THEY DISAGREED. VICE FOREIGN 
MINISTER YANG AND THE DEPUTY SECRETARY HAD SHARED THE 
OPINION THAT IT WAS IMPORTANT TO THE RELATIONSHIP THAT 
THERE BE NO "DISAGREEABLE SURPRISES;" THAT IT WAS, IN 
YANG'S WORDS, IMPORTANT TO "LEVEL WITH EACH OTHER." THE 
RELEASE OF THE WHITE PAPER FLEW IN THE FACE OF BOTH THOSE' 
AGREEMENTS AND THE OVERALL TENOR OF THE DISCUSSIONS. 
7. 4G+ THE FOREIGN MINISTRY PRESS SPOKESMAN HAD EVEN 
HIGHLIGHTED THE NEW LANGUAGE ON USE OF FORCE (REFTEL). 
THIS STRESS ON USE OF FORCE WAS NOT CONSISTENT WITH WHAT 
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND HIS GROUP HAD HEARD IN BEIJING. 
THE MESSAGE WE HEARD LAST WEEK HAD BEEN ONE OF PATIENCE 
AND CAUTION AND WILLINGNESS TO MOVE FORWARD WITH DIALOGUE. 
8. -4,4)- THE U.S. AND CHINA HAD MANY SHARED INTERESTS 
BEYOND THE PEACEFUL RESOLUTION OF THE TAIWAN ISSUE, THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY REMARKED. WE WERE WORKING FOR THE 
PASSAGE OF PNTR LEGISLATION THROUGH CONGRESS AND THE 
DEFEAT OF THE TAIWAN SECURITY ENHANCEMENT ACT. THE 
CHINESE WHITE PAPER MADE POSITIVE OUTCOMES MORE DIFFICULT 
TO ACHIEVE. WHILE STRESSING TO LI THAT THERE WAS NO 
CHANGE IN THE U.S. COMMITMENT TO OPPOSE THE TSEA AND TO 
GET PNTR PASSED, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY EMPHASIZED THAT THE 
WHITE PAPER WOULD COMPLICATE OUR EFFORTS. CHINESE ACTIONS 
WERE DIRECTLY COUNTER PRODUCTIVE TO OUR SHARED INTERESTS. 
CHINESE RESPONSE 
9. AMBASSADOR LI, NOTING THAT HE HAD NOT FINISHED 
READING THE REPORT, ASSERTED THAT IT CONTAINED "NOTHING 
NEW" IN IT. LI REPEATED THE PRC'S STANDARD (FORMERLY TWO) 
CRITERIA FOR USE OF FORCE AGAINST TAIWAN - DECLARATION OF 
INDEPENDENCE OR OCCUPATION OF TAIWAN BY FOREIGN FORCES - 
AND SA=D THE WHITE PAPER DID NOT CHANGE THAT. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY AND DAPNSA STEINBERG MADE CLEAR OUR RECOGNITION 
THAT THE WHITE PAPER WENT BEYOND PAST LANGUAGE AND HAD 
ALREADY CREATED UNCERTAINTY AND DOUBT IN TAIWAN AND IN THE 
REGION. 
10. 4E} AMBASSADOR LI DEPLOYED FAMILIAR CHINESE ARGUMENTS 
THAT THE U.S. WAS INSINCERE IN RESPECTING THE "ONE CHINA" 
PRINCIPLE. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND DAPNSA STEINBERG 
REPLIED THAT THE U.S. WAS FULLY IMPLEMENTING ITS 
COMMITMENTS, AND STRESSED THAT THE PROBLEM HERE WAS THAT 
CHINA HAD CREATED A NEW CONDITION FOR USE OF FORCE. 
11. --(-G-THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND DAPNSA STEINBERG 
EMPHASIZED THAT THE CHINESE HAD NOT WARNED THEM OF THE 
WHITE PAPER OR ITS CONTENT. THIS DETRACTED FROM THE 
STRATEGIC DIALOGUE THAT THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND VICE 
FOREIGN MINISTER YANG HAD WORKED SO HARD TO MAKE A SUCCESS 
IN BEIJING. "PEOPLE ASK US IF THE CHINESE TOLD US THIS 
WAS COMING, AND WE HAVE TO SAY NO," THE DEPUTY SECRETARY 
SAID.

15. (C) ACTION REQUEST: CHARGE IS REQUESTED TO DEMARCHE 
VFM YANG JIECHI, OR THE MOST SENIOR OFFICIAL AVAILABLE IN 
YANG'S ABSENCE, USING THE FOLLOWING POINTS WHICH THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY USED IN HIS 2/22 DEMARCHE TO AMBASSADOR 
LI. CHARGE MAY ALSO DRAW FROM THE ABOVE TO SUPPLEMENT HIS 
APPROACH. 
TALKING POINTS: 
-- Visit went well; productive talks on wide range of 
issues, including Taiwan. 
-- Messages we took away from Beijing on Taiwan generally 
positive. 
-- Beijing exercising restraint in run—up to election. 
Use of force rhetoric highly restricted; aimed at ensuring 
force does NOT have to be used. China ready to work with 
new president. 
-- Therefore surprised to see White Paper. Concerned 
about tone, substance and timing of White Paper. 
- Particulirly surprised to see what appears to be new 
criterion for PRC threat of/use of force, i.e. if Taiwan 
refuses indefinitely to negotiate. Expands context for 
use of force. Moves it in wrong direction. 
-- Our common commitment to peaceful resolution is the 
bedrock of our policy. 
-- Understand your concerns about the Taiwan Security 
Enhancement Act. Expressed forcefully in Beijing. 
However, the White Paper - its timing, tone and new threat 
of use of force - is just the kind of ammunition that 
makes passage of the TSEA more likely, that increases 
pressure on us to sell arms, makes PNTR more difficult.
