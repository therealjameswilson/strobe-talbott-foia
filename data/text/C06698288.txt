SUBJECT: AMB. SHUGARYAN MEETING WITH DEPUTY SECRETARY: 
U.S. REJECTS ARMENIAN CHARGE OF PRO-AZERI "TILT" 
REF: YEREVAN 2462 (NOTAL) 
1. (u) classified by deputy secretary talbott. reasons 
1.5(b), (d). 
summary 
2. during a july 9 meeting with deputy secretary talbott, 
armenian ambassador shugaryan expressed his government's 
concern over a perceived u.s. "tilt" toward azerbaijan and 
proposed that an unofficial american emissary come to 
yerevan to discuss the bilateral relationship. in 
response, the deputy secretary vigorously rejected the 
notion of a tilt; Our policy remains focused on promoting 
peace in the region through evenhanded treatment of both 
Armenia and Azerbaijan. he stressed that, while armenia 
was free to invite whomever it wanted to visit and that we 
would consider meeting with him following the visit, such 
a trip would have absolutely no formal or official 
diplomatic status. end summary. 
3. armenian ambassador shugaryan met for thirty minutes 
july 9 with deputy secretary talbott at the former's 
request. s/nis ambassador collins, eur/casa director 
young and d executive assistant nuland also attended. 
4. shugaryan began by reviewing the july 3-4 bonn meeting

between armenian nk negotiator libaridian and azeri 
president aliyev and his negotiator quluzade, which 
generally paralleled reftel readout. while positively 
assessing the session, shugaryan claimed it was now clear 
to his government that Aliyev expects Armenia to make "all 
the proposals," which the azeri side could then shop 
through looking for the best deal. shugaryan called this 
an "oriental game" which armenia would prefer to avoid, 
since it was not an "oriental state." 
5. shugaryan then complained that reference to the 
territorial integrity of azerbaijan in the june 12 U.S. 
administration statement on the porter amendment was only 
contributing to azeri inflexibility in nagorno-karabakh 
peace negotiations. by not referring to "self rule" in 
connection with support for "territorial integrity," the 
american statement lacked balance, shugaryan argued. 
expressing his personal opinion that a peace settlement 
was possible this year, shugaryan claimed that armenia 
remained ready to work toward peace, even through their 
presidential elections this september. it was prepared to 
table more proposals in the runup to the lisbon summit in 
december, though azerbaijan must be prepared to do 
likewise. 
6. turning more generally to u.s.-armenian relations, 
shugaryan spoke of the need to restore the "openness and 
mutual trust" which characterized our ties in 1994 and 
early 1995. yerevan was pleased with american involvement 
in the karabakh process, which was essential to armenia's 
balancing off the russians. yerevan was very pleased with 
the "formal side of negotiations," shugaryan stressed- he 
had recently been back to yerevan for consultations with 
president her-petrossian. they had discussed the idea of 
arranging for a one-time unofficial emissary to yerevan 
(but not baku) to discuss u.s.-armenian relations in their 
"global" context. he suggested retired FSO Ed Djerejian 
as one possibility. this mission would serve as a 
communication channel, not a negotiating channel. 
shugaryan solicited talbott's opinion on this idea. 
7. talbott responded by indicating he would consider 
meeting the emissary upon his return from yerevan, so long 
as the proposal was an armenian initiative. but there 
could be no official U.S. status to this person's trip. 
we have a talented and energetic ambassador in yerevan 
already, as well as a dedicated team of competent people 
in the administration to work the nk lssue. 
8. talbott then expressed concern over the connotation of 
the shugaryan remarks, especially the reference to a loss 
of mutual trust. shugaryan cut in to stress his 
government's high regard for the u.s. team, from 
ambassador tomsen to ambassador presel and others. he 
referred, however, to armenian concern over the 1995 human 
rights report, which had been critical of armenia, and to 
"some statements" (he later elaborated that this was a 
particular reference to the june 12 statement on the 
porter amendment). some in yerevan had seen the 1995 
human rights report as an administration attempt to create 
a lever for use with armenia on nagorno-karabakh. 
shugaryan concluded that his leadership also understood 
the importance of caspian oil to the u.s., but worried 
about this new tendency. 
9. talbott disagreed strenuously with the armenian 
characterization of u.s.-armenian relations. he was 
particularly troubled because both he and deputy national 
security advisor berger had gotten very engaged in the 
relationship in the past year. stressing the special 
importance of u.s.-armenian relations, talbott said he was 
very distressed to hear the armenian perception of an 
american "tilt" toward azerbaijan. he asked shugaryan to 
report to his president that there was no "tilt" and that 
we regretted that perception.

10. as to our reference in the june 12 statement to 
territorial integrity, this was indeed a very important 
american principle, which we have applied equally to the 
trans-caucasus, the former soviet union generally, and to 
the former yugoslavia. therefore he cautioned armenia not 
to ask us to drop the term "territorial integrity" from 
our vocabulary. in fact, the u.s. would like to see 
armenia use the term in its own dialogue on 
nagorno-karabakh. 
11. talbott wanted to send the clearest possible message 
to president her-petrossian on this issue. he asked that 
shugaryan report that the deputy secretary, speaking for 
himself and the leaders of the U.S.G. as well, also took 
offense at the insinuation that american policy would be 
dictated solely by oil. in fact, we need peace in the 
region so that energy resources can be developed, a 
strategy which will benefit both Armenia and Azerbaijan 
and their economic partners.
