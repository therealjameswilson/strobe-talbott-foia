2. summary. in his meeting with deputy secretary 
talbott, fm kracun argued that slovenia is ready for nato 
membership. talbott agreed that slovenia bad made great 
progress, but said it was too early to say which countries 
would be invited to join the alliance. he added that the 
first tranche of new nato members would not be the last. 
on bosnia, kracun offered to discuss slovenia's 
contribution to any ifor follow-on force. talbott took 
under advisement kracun's request for u.s. assistance in 
resolving slovenia's maritime border dispute with 
croatia. he then stressed the seriousness with which the 
u.s. views reports that slovenian companies continue to,
negotiate sales of arms-related equipment to iran. in 
response to kracun's question, talbott noted that nato 
enlargement and improvements in nato russia relations are 
,on parallel tracks, the goal of both being a more secure 
europe. in closing kracun made a bid for a non-permanent 
seat on the security council, asked about slovenia's 
inclusion in the visa waiver pilot program, and urged 
completion of a double-taxation treaty. end summary. 
3. deputy secretary t:albott met with slovenian foreign 
minister kracun in the department on september 19. also 
present were eur das marshall adair, eur/nce director 
jonathan rickert, slovenian ambassador ernest petric, dcm 
igor kerstein, mssrs. bozo cerar and gregor zore of the 
slcivenian mfa, d special assistant phil goldberg, and 
eur/nce notetaker brad freden. 
NATO 
4. fm kracun began by noting that slovenia's primary 
foreign policy concern at the moment is to be included 
among the first group of'central european countries 
invited to join nato. he said that slovenia is looking to 
present its case in terms of both what nato can offer to 
slovenia and what slovenia can offer to nato. "there is 
no question about the basics," he said, referring to the 
criteria of democracy, a market economy, good relations 
with neighbors, and civilian control of the military. he 
singled out improved relations with italy as one example

5. the deputy secretary replied that he was impressed by 
what he had seen so far, adding that slovenia should "keep 
up the good work." he outlined the timetable laid out in 
the secretary's stuttgart speech: the nac ministerial in 
'december, followed by a nato summit in the first half of 
1997. he said the decision as to which partners would be 
invited to join the alliance would be made at the summit, 
based on the circumstances regarding each country at that 
time. "what happens between now and then is important," 
he added; "potential new members must continue moving in 
the right direction." talbott told kracun that it is 
impossible to say at this time whether slovenia would be 
included in the first group of new members, but the 
process will be ongoing and open. "the first tranche will 
not be the last," he emphasized. 
6. talbott said that nato would take into account not 
only a potential new member's internal situation, but its 
position on the map. slovenia's status as part of the 
former yugoslavia is not an unambiguously positive factor, 
nor is it an unambiguously negative factor. he noted that 
it would send an important signal to the other republiCs 
of the former yugoslavia if, on the basis of merit alone, 
slovenia were to join one of the major institutions of the 
west. At the same time, all of the republics had 
contributed in their own way to the terrible tragedy that 
engulfed the former yugoslavia after 1991. talbott said 
that he hoped slovenia was doing what it could to further 
the process of peace. 
bosnia/ifor 
7. kracun replied that slovenia was not,directly involved 
in the recently-ended fighting to the south, but that it 
had supported the deployment of ifor and is intent on 
having good relations with its erstwhile sister 
republics. he noted, however, that slovenia does not have 
diplomatic relations with the federal republic of 
yugoslavia. in response to talbott's question, ambassador 
petric said the main impediment to better relations 
between slovenia and the fry is the question of 
succession: the fry considers itself the sole successor 
to the former yugoslavia, a position slovenia opposes. 
talbott repeated the u.s. view that there are five equal 
successor states.

8. when asked for his views on the recent bosnian 
elections, kracun said that the results were "similar to 
1990." he added that the presence of ifor was decisive 
for peace, and called for croatia and the fry to begin 
disarming. talbott replied it is important for ifor to 
complete its mission on time, but added that the west will 
not abandon bosnia. the bosnians, however, must take 
advantage of the opportunity presented by the recent 
elections. in response, kracun said that slovenia was 
ready to discuss its contribution to any ifor follow-on 
force. 
croatia 
9. kracun raised the issue of slovenia's relations with 
croatia. there are number of unresolved bilateral issues 
arising out of the break-up of yugoslavia, he said, all of 
which are under negotiation. referring to the unresolved 
border disputes, he noted that "these are not questions of 
security, but of geometry." he pointed to the maritime 
boundary in the bay of piran as one particularly 
nettlesome question, noting that--unlike on land--there 
had been no maritime boundaries between the former 
yugoslav republics. for slovenia, with its short 
coastline, "sovereign access" to the open sea is an 
important issue. the "simple" solution to the maritime 
question cuts slovenia off from the open sea, he said. 
10. responding to talbott's question, kracun said croatia 
was willing to give. slovenia free access to the open sea, 
but not "sovereign" access. resolution of this question, 
kracun added, is a high priority for slovenia, but not for 
croatia. kracun•said slovenia would appreciate any 
friendly advice the u.s. might offer to both sides, and 
would not rule out arbitration. talbott said he would 
look into the matter, and took the slovenian request under 
consideration: 
arms to iran 
11. turning to the issue of slovenian companies' sales of 
arms-related equipment to iran, talbott noted that he had 
discussed this issue with prime minister drnovsek when the 
latter was in washington last may, he stressed the 
importance of keeping a close watch on companies that are 
potential arms-suppliers. "there is still room for 
improvement," he said, asking kracun to pass this message

russia 
13. in response to a question about developments in 
russia, talbott said the alliance is determined to forge 
ahead with enlargement, but will work at the same time to 
create a new nato-russia relationship. the goal of this 
relationship should be to enhance the security of the 
states lying between nato and russia, talbott said. he 
pointed to the secretary's proposal in Stuttgart for an 
Atlantic charter with russia that would codify the 
principles of the relationship. asimiliar process is 
needed'between the alliance and ukraine, as well as other 
states that do not intend to join nato. russian policy, 
he added, is unpredictable. however, one way to encourage 
good nato-russia relations is to support reform within 
russia. the july election results were heartening because 
the russian electorate rejected the candidate of the past. 
italy, unsc, visa waiver, double-taltation 
14. das adair reminded kracun of the importance of 
implementing the agreement with italy and the eu which 
will bring slovenia's property ownership laws into 
conforMity with those of the eu. (n.b. this so-called 
"spanish proposalll broke the logjam between italy and 
slovenia over property restitution earlier this year, 
thereby allowing slovenia to sign an association agreement 
with the eu.) kracun replied that implementation of the 
agreement would be the government's first task after the 
november 10 election. "we will prepare to ratify the 
agreement and change the constitution," he said.
