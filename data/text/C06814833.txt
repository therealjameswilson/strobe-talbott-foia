Summary 
1. t% During their May 11 meeting in Maputo, Deputy 
Secretary Talbott reaffirmed USG recognition of 
President Chissano's re-election and the fairness of 
the December elections with RENAMO President Afonso 
Dhlakama. The Deputy Secretary also praised and 
thanked Dhlakama for the pivotal role he played in the 
democratization of Mozambique, underscoring the need 
for Dhlakama's continued positive engagement in the 
proceSs. Dhlakama said he was "not Jonas Savimbi" and 
would not return his country to war. He asked about 
the USG strategy for democracy building in sub-Saharan 
Africa. The Deputy Secretary proposed continued USG 
support for Mozambican democratization and assistance 
to provide an economic basis for its development. End 
Summary. 
Meeting with RENAMO President Dhlakama 
2. -(41) In a forty-minute meeting on May 11, Deputy 
Secretary Talbott expressed the USG's admiration for 
the country's progress toward democratization and 
commended opposition leader Dhlakama on his pivotal 
role and contribution to the process over the last 
eight years. He told Dhlakama that Mozambique's 
eight-year experience of democracy had seen more 
accomplished than the United States had managed in 
decades. Mozambique had achieved tranquility in just 
a few short years after its civil war and should be 
praised for such an achievement. 
3. (U) RENAMO deputies David Aloni and Vicente Ululu, 
along with Dhlakama's special assistant Agostinho 
Ussore, DAS Schneidman, Executive Assistant Bass, 
Special Assistant Doherty, Charge Loftis and POLOFF 
Ferguson-Augustus (notetaker) also participated in the 
meeting. 
4. '&6.1- Dhlakama thanked the Deputy Secretary for his 
visit to Mozambique, saying that the country needed 
the United States' help in the democratization 
process. The RENAMO president commended the efforts 
of the United States for its support of the African 
continent and expressed his pride that Mozambique was 
touted as a successful example of a young democracy. 
He also thanked the United States for its support to 
the Mozambican people during the recent floods and 
expressed his satisfaction with its pledged support 
for the country's reconstruction.

Moving Past the Elections 
5. The RENAMO leader turned to tensions between 
the FR LIMO ruling party and the opposition. 
Referring to a recent altercation involving the police 
and RENAMO sympathizers in Angoche, Nampula province, 
Dhlakama reiterated the necessity of political 
stability in a democracy, saying that without it there 
could be no economic stability. Coming to the 
December elections, Dhlakama told the Deputy Seeretary 
that while the voting had gone well, there had been 
irregularities in the tabulation process. Renamo 
supporters, he said, did not agree with the Government 
and the Supreme Court decision that President Chissano 
had won re-election and they would not accept him as 
their leader.. Dhlakama, however, did say "we (RENAMO) 
are a peaceful party. I am not a Savimbi." He said 
the opposition was doing everything it could to reach 
a political settlement with ruling party FRELIMO. 
6. tel The Deputy Secretary conveyed the admiration of 
President Clinton and Secretary Albright for the role 
Dhlakamá had played in ending the civil war and his 
active participation in the democratization process. 
He said he was aware of the disagreement between 
RENAMO and President Chissano and the Supreme Court . 
and the opposition's discontent. He again reiterated 
the USG position, as conveyed by Ambassador Curran and 
Charge Loftis, that the United States recognized 
President Chissano as having won re-election in a fair 
election. The Deputy Secretary went on to say that he 
respected the opposition's opinion concerning the 
election outcome and knew that Dhlakama would equally 
respect the U.S. government's opinion as well. 
Dhlakama nodded. (Comment: Dhlakama's reaction to 
the Deputy Secretary's words was noteworthy for his 
acceptance. On May 10, the Charge and POLOFF had 
visited Dhlakama at his request. Taking a somewhat 
bombastic tone, he referred again to the need for a 
political solution for the current impasse between the 
opposition and the government, saying he could not 
stand by passively while his supporters were being 
attacked, a reference to the incident in Nampula. In 
that meeting as well, however, he also eschewed 
violence. End Comment.) 
RENAMO's Role in the Democratization Prpcess 
7. -4.C.1.Returning to Dhlakama's earlier words, the 
Deputy Secretary emphasized that the USG and RENAMO 
agreed on the concept of "peaceful" when discussing 
participatory democracy. He underscored the 
importance of the opposition's role in the democratic 
process, saying that it often was the more important 
one. He recalled for Dhlakama the example of the 
Democratic party, which had waited 12 years to return 
to the White House, and then lost control of the 
Congress two years later. He urged Dhlakama to 
continue his vital participation in the Mozambican 
democratization process, and to seek a peaceful 
political solution to the current impasse.

THE USG Strategy for Democracy in Africa 
8. 1.Q.4sThe opposition leader also queried the Deputy 
Secretary as to the USG strategy for establishing 
democracy in Africa. 
9. -.(064- Dhlakama asked, that given the preponderance 
of dictators and Marxists in the region, how could 
leaders in the region be made to accept democratic 
reforms? Deputy Secretary Talbott responded that the 
best way was to use peaceful democratic struggle to 
achieve a democratic end. The Deputy Secretary 
proceeded to outline possible U.S. support to assist 
nascent democracies in Africa: 
1) Clarify the differences between a democracy 
and dictatorship; 
2) Provide pro-active assistance for the 
creation of the democratic process; and 
3) Provide as much substantive assistance as 
possible to create an economic basis so as to 
provide the people hope for the future. 
10. 1.64-Finally, he highlighted the value of sub-
regional organizations that would support the 
development of democracy and guard against threats to 
it, citing the Organization of American States as an 
example. The U.S.-Southern African Development 
Community (SADC) Forum in Maputo, May 10-11, 
demonstrated that SADC was an organ that could be such 
a tool and the USG would lend its continuihg support 
to it. 
11. /t4. Deputy Assistant Secretary Schneidman also 
emphasized that the primary USG goal was to accelerate 
Africa's entry into the global economy. This could 
only be done, he said, when a country had its 
democratic reforms in place. He called Mozambique in 
the forefront of successful democracies and 
accentuated the important role of the opposition in 
keeping Mozambique there. "A country's development is 
only as good as its democratic processes," Schneidman 
said. He assured Dhlakama that the dialogue on 
democracy would continue on a local level through the 
Ambassador and Charge. 
12.'/.84.. The Deputy Secretary closed the meeting by 
sharing with Dhlakama the importance he attached to 
their meeting, saying that whenever U.S. officials 
visit a democratic country they always call on the 
opposition leader. "And then," he said, "we wish both 
the government and the opposition good luck and good 
results in resolving their problems, as it ultimately 
will be up to a country's people to cast the final 
votes as to their political future."
