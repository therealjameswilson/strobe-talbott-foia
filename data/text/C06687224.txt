RELEASE IN FULL 
RE: Update on Eximbank Activity in Russia 
During the visit of Prime Minister Chernomyrdin to Vice 
President Gore at the end of this month, we are looking forward to 
signing the Russian oil and Gas Framework Agreement under which we 
may provide as much as $2 billion or more to finance rehabilitation 
of the Russian energy sector. A final text has been agreed upon 
this week, and the signatories will be the Ministry of Fuel and 
Energy, Ministry of Finance, Central Bank and Eximbank. We would 
like the signing to take place during the visit, preferably as a 
separate occasion at which we could emphasize the benefits the 
Agreement will generate for both sides. For the Russians, the 
result should be to assist the sector that will produce the foreign 
exchange export earnings, on which the general recovery of Russia 
depends; for.us, it will be to emphasize the thousands of American 
jobs that will be generated throughout the country. 
In addition, we have once again raised with the Russians 
the need for a Project Incentive Agreement containing the 
assurances of legal and governmental stability that will enable us 
to support transactions involving limited recourse project 
financing in Russia. In such transactions, the projects themselves 
generate the foreign exchange needed to repay the financing. This 
type of financing can be used not only in the Russian energy 
sector, but also in such other sectors as power, mining, 
telecommunications and transportation. A draft protocol was given 
to the Russian side earlier this week, and we have urged that it 
be signed during the Chernomyrdin/ Gore meeting.

I understand that the Administration is also considering 
possible financing sources for other elements of the President's 
cooperation programs with Russia, including the nuclear power 
safety program. Please keep in mind that with the need to leverage 
Eximbank's limited budgetary resources, we may only be able to play 
a minimal role in supporting these programs on the basis of high 
cost Russian government risk. Instead, we will want to look at 
ways of structuring our support to reduce risk and budget costs by 
using the collateralized financing approach described above. 
Please let us know if we can help your staff prepare any talking 
points which may be needed on possible Eximbank support for these 
programs. 
I am also attaching a copy of a memorandum Eximbank sent 
to Nick Burns which provides more information on both the Framework 
Agreement and the Project Incentive Agreement.
