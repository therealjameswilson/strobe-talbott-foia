THE DEFENSE ASSESSMENT 
4. Hei- EAST DESCRIBED THE BACKGROUND BEHIND THE SOON-TO-
BE-RELEASED WHITE PAPER/DEFENSE ASSESSMENT AND PROVIDED 
SOME HISTORICAL CONTEXT FOR THE DECISIONS WHICH IT 
CONTAINED. HE WAS QUICK TO GIVE FORMIN DON MCKINNON 'CREDIT 
FOR WORKING HARD SINCE 1990 TO REESTABLISH SPENDING 
PRIORITIES FOR'DEFENSE. HE STATED THAT SINCE 1990, FISCAL 
CONSTRAINTS HAD BEEN APPLIED TO BRING THE ECONOMY BACK INTO 
"BALANCE." SINCE 1989 DEFENSE SPENDING HAD SKIDDED 30 
PERCENT WHILE ACTIVITIES IN SEVERAL AREAS, FOR INSTANCE 
PEACEKEEPING, HAD INCREASED DRAMATICALLY. EAST FURTHER 
INDICATED THAT IT HAD TO BE ACKNOWLEDGED THAT NZ MAY "NOT 
HAVE BEEN PULLING ITS WEIGHT" (ABOUT WHICH AUSTRALIA HAD 
"NOT BEEN SHY") AND THAT THE ASSESSMENT PROCESS WAS NEEDED. 
HE MENTIONED THAT SENDING A COMPANY OF SOLDIERS TO BOSNIA 
FOR UNPROFOR HAD STRETCHED THE DEFENSE FORCE'S CAPABILITIES 
(DUE TO OLD EQUIPMENT), HAD PROVED FISCALLY DRAINING, AND 
HAD DRIVEN THE NEED FOR A FRESH LOOK AT DEFENSE.

RESTATED HIS BELIEF THAT ANY INCREASE IN THE DEFENSE EFFORT

ROLE IN THE REGION. 
10. -+C-4--.EAST ALSO SAID THE ISSUE OF EXERCISING-WITH U.S.

CAUTION AS THE U.S. DID NOT WANT TO PERPETUATE, OR 
RESURRECT, THE IDEA THAT THE WHOLE RELATIONSHIP WAS BASED' 
ON THE ISSUE OF EXERCISES. NOR DID THE U.S. WANT TO GIVE 
THE IMPRESSION THAT THE "UNFINISHED BUSINESS"-IS 
UNIMPORTANT. LODAL ADDED THAT, ABSENT CHANGE BY NZ, 
THERE WOULD BE NO CHANGE TO THE U.S. POLICY ON EXERCISES. 
EAST REGISTERED HIS OPINION THAT THE U.S. AND NZ ARE IN A 
"CUL-DE-SAC" AND THAT, ALTHOUGH NZ-WOULD LIKE'TO SEE THE 
POLICY CHANGE, IT WAS CLEARLY A MATTER FOR THE U.S. TO 
DECIDE. TALBOTT ALSO EXPLAINED THAT THE U.S. DID-NOT-WANT 
TO DO ANYTHING WHICH WOULD INDICATE IT SEEMED LAX IN ITS 
EXPECTATIONS OF ALLIES, PARTICULARLY IN LIGHT,OF NATO 
ENLARGEMENT, AND THUS THE STANCE WITH RESPECT TO NEW 
ZEALAND HAD TO BE CONSISTENT.
