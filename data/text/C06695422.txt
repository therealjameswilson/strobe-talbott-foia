2. IN HIS MEETING WITH THE DEPUTY SECRETARY ON JULY 25, 
PRIME MINISTER CHUAN FORMALLY EXTENDED AN INVITATION TO 
THE PRESIDENT TO VISIT THAILAND FOLLOWING THE APEC 
LEADERS MEETING IN INDONESIA. I HOPE THE PRESIDENT CAN 
DO IT. A PRESIDENTIAL VISIT WILL BOLSTER AS NOTHING 
ELSE CAN A RELATIONSHIP AS IMPORTANT AS ANY IN THIS PART 
OF THE WORLD, SOLID AT THE CORE BUT BESET BY SOME 
DIFFICULT ISSUES. FOR A VARIETY OF REASONS, SOME 
UNAVOIDABLE, WE HAVE GIVEN IT RELATIVELY LITTLE 
HIGH-LEVEL ATTENTION OVER MANY YEARS. 
3. THAILAND IS AS CONSISTENT A FRIEND AS WE HAVE IN 
ASIA. ACTING OUT OF ITS OWN PERCEIVED SELF-INTEREST IT 
HAS BEEN A QUIETLY RELIABLE ALLY THROUGHOUT THE POST-WAR 
PERIOD, HOSTING UPWARDS OF 50,000 U.S. TROOPS DURING THE 
VIETNAM WAR AND SENDING ITS OWN FORCES BOTH TO VIETNAM 
AND LAOS (AS IT DID TO KOREA), COOPERATING WITH US 
DURING THE VIETNAMESE OCCUPATION OF CAMBODIA, JOINING 
WITH US IN OUR LARGEST BILATERAL MILITARY EXERCISE 
PROGRAM IN ASIA, AND PERMITTING VIRTUALLY UNRESTRICTED 
ACCESS TO ITS MILITARY FACILITIES FOR EVERYTHING FROM 
POW/MIA OPERATIONS TO MILITARY RESUPPLY DURING THE GULF 
WAR. A PRESIDENTIAL VISIT WOULD BE A FITTING 
RECOGNITION OF THAT REALITY, AND WOULD ADVANCE OUR 
EFFORTS TO BUILD UPON IT. 
4. THAILAND COMES AS CLOSE AS ANY COUNTRY IN THE REGION 
TO SHARING, AND ACTING UPON, VALUES CONSISTENT WITH OUR 
OWN. ALONG WITH THE PHILIPPINES, THAILAND IS THE ONLY 
OTHER SOUTHEAST ASIAN COUNTRY THAT CAN BOAST A DEMOCRACY 
IN THE SENSE WE UNDERSTAND THAT TERM, INCLUDING AN 
EXTRAORDINARILY FREE PRESS.
