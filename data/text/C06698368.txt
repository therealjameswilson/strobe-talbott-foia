half-hour with Romanian President Constantinescu in Lisbon 
on December 3. Constantinescu emphasized the high 
priority he accords Romania °S relationship with the U.S. 
He asked for U.S. assistance that would enable his 
government to put its economic reform package in place in 
another five months. The Deputy Secretary, reiterating 
U.S. support for Romania's political stability and 
economic growth, advised Constantinescu to work to create 
a hospitable environment for U.S. private investment and 
for international development bank lending. The Deputy 
Secretary also noted that the NATO enlargement process had 
already brought democratization and regional stability 
dividends. He told Constantinescu that whatever the 
decisions taken at next July's NATO summit, the 
longer-term process should be inclusive, in general and 
with respect to Romania. Finally, Constantinescu said he 
was encouraged,following his meeting with President 
Kuchma, about prospects for Romania's bilateral treatY 
with Ukraine. The Deputy Secretary stated that the U.S. 
would play a supportive role, if it was requested to do so 
by both Bucharest and Kiev. End summary,.
3. _AR') Deputy Secretary Talbott met December 3 with 
Romanian President Constantinescu in Lisbon, where both 
were attending the OSCE Summit. The half-hour meeting 
followed Constantinescu's meeting,the previous day with 
Vice President Gore. The meetings with the Vice President 
and Deputy Secretary were the first with U.S. or foreign 
officials since his election on November 17. They spoke 
through an interpreter.

5. .(-E1 President Constantinescu replied that he was 
pleased that a senior U.S. government official was 
familiar with the balkans -- now and then. For his part, 
he had spent one year at Duke University, an experience 
that had helped him understand American ways of thinking. 
Also, all of his chief assistants, who would soon be 
occupying important positions in his government, hold at 
least MA's from U.S. universities. Having just 
participated in a national campaign, they should be able 
to find a common language with the opposition, based on an 
accurate understanding of their points of agreement and 
disagreement. This would contrast sharply with the years 
after the revolution. It would be a pleasure to welcome 
the deputy secretary to Romania and to show him the 
changes now underway. 
6. 4E1 Constantinescu then got to what he called the 
"essence of my message." U.S. support is needed to help 
Romania translate its political stability into economic 
growth. The time is over for unrealistic policies that 
result in unfulfilled promises. His government must now 
provide the means for the poorest romanians to survive 
through and the next five months, when economic reforms 
will be put in place. If Romania receives the necessary 
assistance now, it would be possible to build a region of 
stability. This would be in the interest of both his 
country and the U.S. Romania will remain a faithful ally 
to the U.S.; this would be to the benefit of all 
romanians. Within five months, romania will no longer 
require aid. From next Spring, the "rules of profit" will 
be in place. U.S. investors will be offered privileged 
positions, because they bring a long-range outlook. Also, 
if the U.S. has a continuing economic interest in Romania, 
it will also have a political interest in his country. 
This will also serve stability in the region. 
7. (.e) Responding, the Deputy Secretary stated that the 
connection between economic well-being and security, 
between political and economic factors, is clear. 
However, as the Romanian President surely knows, there are 
limits to what the U.S. and others can do for Romania. 
While the U.S. administration would like to persuade 
Congress of the need to increase U.S. economic aid, there 
is little likelihood new funds would be available during 
the next five months. Romania must instead look to the 
U.S. private sector and to its leadership role in the 
international financial institutions. Success in finding

8. Cr.+ Turning to security issues, the Deputy Secretary 
stated that an important factor behind the decision to 
enlarge NATO was the conviction that this process would 
support democratization and regional stability in Central 
Europe. Positive results were already to be seen 
including the improvement in Romania's ties with Ukraine. 
The Deputy Secretary stated that he did not know what NATO 
would decide when it hold its Summit in July. However the 
process should be inclusive, in general and specifically 
with respect to Romania. 
9. Del Constantinescu replied that dialogue with the U.S. 
must continue. The romanian government would take an 
honest approach; it would cease to make promises it could 
not keep. It would concentrate on results. He commented 
that on the positive outcome of his meeting with Ukrainian 
president Kuchma. The presidents had decided to instruct 
their foreign ministers to finalize their bilateral 
treaty. Romania does not contest it border with Ukraine, 
said Constantinescu. The Romanian people that had elected 
him would surely be able to support such a treaty. They 
know that the tragic past must be left behind. Both he 
and Kuchma agree that the U.S. can play an important role 
in this respect. The Deputy Secretary replied that the 
U.S. was prepared to play such a role, if both parties 
desired and requested it. He added that in the months 
ahead he would be relying in particular on Ambassador 
Moses, in whom he had the utmost confidence.
