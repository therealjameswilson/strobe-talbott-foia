Let's start from the premise that Russian politics 
are a mess, and they'll continue to be so for a very 
long time. It is very easy for card-carrying 
supporters of Russian reform to get as discouraged as 
the Russian reformers themselves are. The most 
conspicuous trends, after all, are not good. The local 
elections continue to favor conservative, even 
retrograde elements (much more than the Zhirinovskyite 
neo-fascists). There's murmuring in the streets, in 
the backrooms of Moscow, and therefore in the 
intelligence reports about the dark forces making a 
third attempt at, in effect, restoring a Soviet system 
(the first twb having been the August '91 coup and the 
October '93 White House putsch). 
Two points about such apocalyptic scenarios.

it. We're not so heavily invested in support for 
reform that we couldn't turn on a dime and deal with 
the overnight reversion of the Russian government to 
Soviet type. We don't want to dwell publicly on our 
ability to do that, but I'm sure it's true. The
