SUBJECT: THE MOOD IN DELHI AFTER ROUND EIGHT: STRATEGIC 
THINKERS EXPRESS OPTIMISM WITH SOME LINGERING CONCERNS 
1. CLASSIFIED BY E. ASHLEY WILLS, DEPUTY CHIEF OF 
MISSION. )REASON 1.5 (B) AND (D) 
2. TC.1- SUMMARY: INDIAN STRATEGIC THINKERS ARE MORE 
POSITIVE ABOUT THE U.S.-INDO DIALOGUE NOW THAN AT ANY 
POINT SINCE INDIA CONDUCTED NUCLEAR TESTS IN MAY 1998. 
ON THE HEELS OF ROUND EIGHT OF THE TALBOTT/SINGH TALKS, 
THERE HAS BEEN A NOTICEABLE CHANGE IN THE TONE AND 
SUBSTANCE OF THEIR COMMENTARY. MANY BELIEVE PROGRESS ON 
MOST OF THE FOUR BENCHMARKS IS FEASIBLE WITHIN THE SHORT 
TERM. FOLLOWING U.S.-PAK TALKS IN ISLAMABAD, SOUTH ASIA 
SENIOR ADVISOR MATTHEW DALEY RETURNED TO NEW DELHI BOTH 
TO EXPAND PUBLIC OUTREACH EFFORTS WITH KEY INDIAN 
SECURITY AND DEFENSE INTERLOCUTORS AS WELL AS TO ASSESS 
HOW THIS INFLUENTIAL GROUP VIEWS PROGRESS IN U.S.-INDO 
TALKS TO DATE AND, EQUALLY AS IMPORTANT, HOW THEY SEE THE 
PROCESS EVOLVING IN THE SHORT AND MEDIUM TERM. INDIANS 
EXPRESSED CONCERN ABOUT CHINA, PAKISTAN, THE OPTICS OF 
THE "SYNCHRONIZATION" OF U.S.-INDO STEPS, SANCTIONS AND 
OTHER ISSUES. IN SPITE OF THESE CONCERNS, HOWEVER, 
INTERLOCUTORS WERE POSITIVE AND UPBEAT THAT U.S.-INDO 
RELATIONS WOULD BE BACK ON TRACK SOON. END SUMMARY.

4. ---(4g4.. ACROSS THE BOARD, INDIANS APPEAR TO BE 
PREOCCUPIED WITH THEIR OWN EFFORTS TO IDENTIFY WHAT 
INDIA'S REAL SECURITY THREATS ARE AND DETERMINE HOW BEST 
TO RESPOND TO THEM. DISCUSSION ABOUT CREDIBLE MINIMUM 
DETERRENCE IS STILL UNDERWAY AND THERE IS, AS YET, NO 
CLEAR INDICATION OF THE PRECISE DIRECTION INDIA WILL 
TAKE. MEETINGS WITH INDIANS FOLLOWED A STANDARD PATTERN 
WHICH INCLUDED A SUMMARY DEBRIEF BY SA DALEY ON THE 
STATUS OF THE TALKS BETWEEN THE U.S. AND INDIA AS WELL AS 
THE U.S. AND PAKISTAN. OUR INDIAN INTERLOCUTORS RAISED 
THE SAME ISSUES TIME AND TIME AGAIN. EACH OF THE KEY 
ISSUES RAISED IS OUTLINED BELOW: 
--PAKISTAN: INDIANS AGREED WITH DALEY'S ASSESSMENT THAT 
INTERNATIONAL ECONOMIC ASSISTANCE TO PAKISTAN HAS 
PROVIDED PRIME MINISTER NAWAZ SHARIF A WINDOW OF 
OPPORTUNITY TO MOVE THE INDO-PAK DIALOGUE FORWARD, AND 
INDIA SHOULD CAPITALIZE ON THIS. INDIANS UNIVERSALLY 
ENDORSED PRIME MINISTER ATAL BIHARI VAJPAYEE'S 
ANNOUNCEMENT THAT HE PLANNED TO RIDE THE DELHI-LAHORE BUS 
TO MEET HIS PAKISTANI COUNTERPART. THERE WAS KEEN 
INTEREST IN THE STATUS OF PAKISTAN'S NUCLEAR PROGRAM AS 
WELL AS THE STATUS OF U.S.-PAK TALKS. MOST INTERLOCUTORS 
JUDGED THAT PAKISTAN DOES NOT POSE A SERIOUS STRATEGIC 
THREAT TO INDIA, NOTING THAT A STABLE PAKISTAN IS 
ESSENTIAL FOR A STABLE INDIA. ACCORDING TO OUR 
INTERLOCUTORS, THE PRIMARY THREAT TO INDIA IS NOT 
PAKISTAN BUT CHINA. 
--CHINA: INDIAN FEARS ABOUT CHINA HAVE BEEN EXACERBATED 
BY A LACK OF ACTIONABLE INFORMATION ON CHINESE NUCLEAR 
CAPABILITIES AND INTENT. SEVERAL INTERLOCUTORS SAID IT 
WOULD BE IN THE INTEREST OF BOTH THE U.S. AND INDIA TO 
SHARE INFORMATION ON CHINA. AS ONE INTERLOCUTOR PUT IT, 
"WE HAVE LIMITED INFORMATION, BUT IT DOESN'T GEL BECAUSE 
WE DON'T HAVE ENOUGH OF A SPIN ON CHINESE NUMBERS (OF 
WARHEADS)." WHEN DALEY NOTED THE UPCOMING VISIT OF EAP 
DAS SUSAN SHIRK, IDSA DEPUTY DIRECTOR UDAI BHASKAR 
PROMPTLY OFFERED TO HOST A ROUNDTABLE ON CHINA DURING HER 
VISIT. SEVERAL INDIAN INTERLOCUTORS WORRY THAT THE U.S. 
IS UNABLE OR UNWILLING TO "FETTER" OR CONTROL CHINA, 
LEAVING CHINESE EFFORTS AGAINST INDIA UNCHECKED. 
--FMCT: ON FMCT, CONSIDERED TO BE THE MOST DIFFICULT

ISSUE IN THE BILATERAL DIALOGUE, OUR INTERLOCUTORS LINKED 
;NDIA'S INITIAL HESITATION TO ENTER A DIALOGUE ON THIS 
ISSUE TO DELHI'S CONCERNS ABOUT THE SCOPE OF.THE TREATY 
(EXISTING OR FUTURE STOCKPILES), THE VERIFICATION PROCESS 
IN THE CONTEXT OF A MORATORIUM AND UNCERTAINTY ABOUT U.S. 
INQTIONS. IN PARTICULAR, SOME EXPRESSED THE CONCERN 
THAT THE U.S. MIGHT CHANGE ITS POSITION WITH REGARD TO 
EXISTING STOCKPILES MAKING THE INDIAN AND PAKISTANI 
STOCKPILES SUBJECT TO RESTRICTIONS WHILE LEAVING THOSE OF 
THE P-5 INTACT. WITH REGARD TO VERIFICATION, DALEY 
STATED THAT THE U.S. WAS PREPARED TO DISCUSS THIS ISSUE 
BILATERALLY WITH INDIA. DALEY EXPLAINED THAT WE ASKED 
INDIA AND PAKISTAN TO ACCEPT A MULTILATERAL MORATORIUM ON 
FISSILE MATERIAL PRODUCTION IF ALL OF THE OTHER STATES 
THAT ACKNOWLEDGED HAVING CONDUCTED NUCLEAR WEAPONS TESTS 
DID THE SAME. THIS ASSUAGED INDIAN FEARS ABOUT BEING OUT 
IN FRONTON THIS ISSUE. DALEY POINTED OUT THAT EARLY 
ACTION ON THIS ISSUE BY INDIA WOULD SERVE A STRATEGIC 
PURPOSE BY ALLOWING INDIA TO LOCK IN ITS CURRENT 
STOCKPILE STATUS VIS-A-VIS PAKISTAN AS WELL AS BY 
PREVENTING CHINA FROM RESUMING PRODUCTION. 
--ROLE OF THE MILITARY: OUR INTERLOCUTORS INDICATED THAT 
THE U.S. DECISION TO INCLUDE GENERAL RALSTON IN THE 
TALBOTT DELEGATION SENT A POWERFUL AND IMPORTANT SIGNAL 
TO THE GOVERNMENT OF INDIA WITH REGARD TO THE ROLE OF THE 
MILITARY ON SECURITY AND DEFENSE ISSUES. MOST 
INTERLOCUTORS OPINED THAT THE GOI WOULD NOT BE ABLE TO 
KEEP THE MILITARY OUT OF THE LOOP FOR MUCH LONGER, ADDING 
THAT THE HIGH VISIBILITY OF GENERAL RALSTON IQOVIDED A MUCH-
NEEDED BOOST TO INDIA'S ARMED 
FORCES WHO ARE LOOKING FOR A PLACE AT THE TABLE WITHIN 
THEIR OWN GOVERNMENT AS SECURITY AND DEFENSE POLICY IS 
DISCUSSED AND DEVELOPED. OUR INTERLOCUTORS WELCOMED THE 
NEWS THAT IMET PROGRAMS WERE BEING RESUMED AS WELL AS THE 
FACT THAT WE HAD INVITED ARMY CHIEF GENERAL MALIK TO A 
CHANGE OF COMMAND CEREMONY AT CINCPAC LATER THIS MONTH. 
--U.S. OUTREACH: INTERLOCUTORS ASKED FOR ADDITIONAL U.S. 
OUTREACH TO CLARIFY ISSUES FOR THE INDIAN STRATEGIC AND 
DEFENSE ESTABLISHMENTS. AT THE SAME TIME, HOWEVER, MANY 
WERE CRITICAL-OF USG STATEMENTS ON KASHMIR THAT At 
REGULAR INTERVALS REITERATED U.S. WILLINGNESS TO MEDIATE 
IF ONLY INDIA WOULD AGREE. THEY NOTED THAT THES&PROVOKE 
STRONG EMOTIONAL RESPONSES HERE, EFFECTIVELY DRAWING 
ATTENTION AWAY FROM THE POSITIVE DEVELOPMENTS IN OTHER 
AREAS. C. RAJA MOHAN, THE WELL-PLUGGED IN STRATEGIC 
AFFAIRS EDITOR OF THE HINDU, WARNED THAT THE USG NEEDS TO 
CALIBRATE ITS PUBLIC REMARKS IN A WAY WHICH BOLSTERS THE 
MIDDLE (READ MODERATE) POSITION. HE CLAIMED THAT SOME 
REMARKS, INCLUDING OUR ASSESSMENT THAT THE MAY 1998 TESTS 
HAD NOT BEEN AS SUCCESSFUL AS REPORTED, PLAYED INTO THE 
HANDS OF THE HARD LINERS. 
--CREDIBLE MINIMUM DETERRENCE: NO CONSENSUS HAS BEEN 
REACHED ON THIS ISSUE. IT IS STILL UNDER DEBATE IN 
INDIA. THERE,IS NO TIME LIMIT FOR THE DISCUSSION 
ACCORDING TO OUR INTERLOCUTORS. DALEY RESTATED THE U.S. 
POSITION THAT THE USG IS NOT ASKING INDIA TO PROVIDE 
NUMBERS BUT RATHER AN ORIENTATION OF THE DIRECTION IN 
WHICH INDIA IS HEADED.

--CTBT: INTERLOCUTORS GENERALLY AGREED THAT INDIA WOULD 
SIGN THE CTBT, BUT A FEW FELT THE BJP-LED COALITION WOULD 
'HAVE A HARD TIME DOING THIS IN LIGHT OF DOMESTQ GREATER 
INTEREST TO THEM WAS WHETHER THE 
U.S. SENATE WOULD RATIFY THE CTBT BY SEPTEMBER 1999. 
DALEY NOTED THAT THE U.S. PUBLIC OVERWHELMINGLY SUPPORTS 
THE CTBT. HE ADDED THAT THE U.S. AND PREVAILING 
INTERNATIONAL PRACTICE MAKES A DISTINCTION BETWEEN 
SIGNING, RATIFYING AND DEPOSIT OF THE INSTRUMENT OF 
RATIFICATION/ACCESSION. ALL THREE STEPS ARE NECESSARY TO 
COMPLETE THE PROCESS OF ADHERENCE. AT THIS POINT, WE ARE 
ONLY ASKING INDIA TO SIGN THE TREATY. THE OTHER TWO • 
STEPS IN THE PROGRESS ARE DISTINCT AND SEPARATE FROM 
SIGNING AND CAN FOLLOW IN THE MONTHS AFTER SIGNATURE. 
--SANCTIONS: INDIANS CONSIDER THE USG DECISION NOT TO 
BLOCK AN UPCOMING WORLD BANK LOAN FOR AN INDIAN 
INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECT IN THE STATE OF ANDHRA PRADESH AS 
A POSITIVE STEP. ON THE BROADER SANCTIONS ISSUE, OUR 
INTERLOCUTORS SAID LIFTING POST-MAY 1998 SANCTIONS WOULD 
SEND AN IMPORTANT SIGNAL. IN REPLY TO A QUESTION ABOUT 
TECHNOLOGY TRANSFER RESTRICTIONS WHICH PREDATE MAY 1998, 
DALEY INDICATED THAT THE USG WOULD CONSIDER REVIEWING THE 
1984 MOU. INDIANS WELCOMED THE NEWS THAT, AS ADDITIONAL 
PROGRESS IS MADE, THE USG WOULD ALSO CONSIDER NARROWING 
THE ENTITIES LIST TO REMOVE THOSE ENTITIES RELATED TO 
CONVENTIONAL MILITARY ACTIVITIES. 
--GOI MISHANDLING OF THE NUCLEAR TESTS: THERE WAS 
UNANIMOUS AGREEMENT AMONG INDIAN INTERLOCUTORS THATJ THE 
BJP-LED COALITION HAD MISHANDLED THE TESTS FROM START TO 
FINISH. PARTICULARLY UNFORTUNATE AT THE OUTSET WAS THE 
DECISION TO USE THE CHINA THREAT AS A JUSTIFICATION FOR 
THE TESTS. MORE RECENTLY, OUR INTERLOCUTORS SAID THE GOI 
HAS NOT DONE A GOOD JOB ON CONSULTATIONS WITH THE 
OPPOSITION. WHILE PUBLICLY CLAIMING A DESIRE. TO BUILD A 
CONSENSUS ON SIGNING THE CTBT, GOI ACTIONS HAVE TENDED TO 
UNDERMINE THIS OBJECTIVE WITH THE UNFORTUNATE RESULT OF 
POLITICIZING THE ENTIRE DEBATE. AGAINST A BACKDROP OF 
DOMESTIC POLITICAL INSTABILITY, THE OPPOSITION HAS TRIED 
TO USE THE POOR HANDLING OF THE ISSUE TO ITS ADVANTAGE 
DOMESTICALLY. DESPITE THE BJP-LED•COALITION'S INEPT 
HANDLING OF THE NUCLEAR TESTS, MOST PREDICTED THAT THE 
OPPOSITION WOULD BE BROUGHT ON BOARD SOON. 
--NAVAL PRITHVI: ONE INTERLOCUTOR SAID THAT A LIQUID-
FUELED MISSILE THE SIZE OF THE PRITHVI WAS SINGULARLY 
INAPPROPRIATE FOR THE INDIAN NAVY. ANOTHER, (CURRENTLY 
SERVING AS A COMMODORE IN THE INDIAN NAVY) OBSERVED THAT 
THE NAVY WAS NOT INTERESTED IN THE PRITHVI BUT, DEPENDING 
ON FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS, COULD BE SERIOUSLY INTERESTED IN 
SHIP-LAUNCHED CRUISE MISSILES. 
-- CIVILIAN NUCLEAR'POWER: MANY INTERLOCUTORS STRESSED

THE SAME DEAL TO INDIA, I.E., PEACEFUL NUCLEAR

NON-NUCLEAR WEAPON STATE. DALEY EXPLAINED THAT NUCLEAR 
COOPERATION WAS THE MOST DIFFICULT AREA FOR THE U.S.
