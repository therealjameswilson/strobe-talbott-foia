1. SUMMARY: THE HEMISPHERIC FOREIGN MINISTERS MET ON 
SUMMIT IMPLEMENTATION IN PANAMA JUNE 2, UNDER THE 
CHAIRMANSHIP OF DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT, AND 
ACCOMPLISHED TWO MAJOR OBJECTIVES of (a) assessing the 
positive progress in Summit implementation during the 
past year, and (b) determining the place (Chile) and 
timing (late 1997 - early 1998) of the next Summit. THE 
SUMMIT IMPLEMENTATION REVIEW GROUP (SIRG) WAS COMMENDED 
FOR ITS WORK IN PROMOTING SUMMIT IMPLEMENTATION, AND WAS 
CHARGED WITH SERVING AS THE MECHANISM TO BEGIN DEVELOPING 
THE AGENDA FOR THE NEXT SUMMIT OF THE AMERICAS. 
END SUMMARY. 
2. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT, AS CHAIR OF THE 
MINISTERIAL MEETING ON SUMMIT IMPLEMENTATION, CONVEYED 
SECRETARY CHRISTOPHER'S GREETING. TALBOTT REFERRED TO 
THE NOW-SHARED STRATEGIC VISION IN THE HEMISPHERE. HE 
NOTED THE CONSENSUS THAT HAS DEVELOPED ON THE TIMEFRAME 
AND LOCATION OF THE NEXT §UMMIT AND THE DESIRABILITY OF 
ANNOUNCING IT AT THE CURRENT MEETING. 
3. TALBOTT REFERRED TO THE SECRETARY'S STATEMENT ON 
PROGRESS ACHIEVED AND REMAINING CHALLENGES OF 
IMPLEMENTATION FOR THE SUMMIT, AND THAT ALL SUMMIT 
GOVERNMENTS HAD SEEN AND HAD THE OPPORTUNITY, TO COMMENT 
ON THE TEXT OF THE STATEMENT. (RELEASED TO THE PRESS 
FOLLOWING THE MEETING, this statement also made clear 
that there was agreement that the SIRG would be the 
mechanism through which the next Summit's agenda would be 
developed.) 
4. COUNSELOR TO THE PRESIDENT MACK MCLARTY REVIEWED 
THE PAST YEAR'S PROGRESS AND OBSERVED THAT THE SUMMIT 
ARCHITECTURE IS NOW IN PLACE TO ALLOW US TO ADDRESS THE 
INEVITABLE DIFFICULTIES WHICH WILL ARISE ACROSS THE 
HEMISPHERE. HE SAID THAT ALTHOUGH THERE HAD BEEN A 
CRISIS IN MEXICO'S ECONOMY,"BORDER PROBLEMS BETWEEN 
ECUADOR AND PERU, A FRAGILE AND VOLATILE DEMOCRACY IN 
HAITI, AND A CONSTITUTIONAL CRISIS IN PARAGUAY, THE 
HEMISPHERE HAD RESPONDED DECISIVELY TO BOLSTER 
CONSTITUTIONAL ORDER AND DEMOCRATIC CONTINUITY SINCE THE 
LAST MEETING OF FOREIGN MINISTERS IN HAITI LAST YEAR. HE 
OBSERVED THAT DRAMATIC PROGRESS HAS BEEN MADE SINCE MIAMI

5. REPRESENTATIVES OF FOUR GOVERNMENTS THAT HAD 
VOLUNTEERED-AS RESPONSIBLE COORDINATORS SPOKE ON PROGRESS 
MADE IN THEIR PARTICULAR ACTION INITIATIVES. MEXICAN 
FOREIGN MINISTER ANGEL GURRIA OUTLINED MEXICAN EFFORTS ON

EDUCATION BE BASED ON EQUALITY OF ACCESS, QUALITY, AND 
RELEVANCE. UNIVERSAL ACCESS TO QUALITY EDUCATION, HE 
SAID, DEPENDS ON DECENTRALIZATION OF EDUCATIONAL SYSTEMS. 
6. JAMAICAN MINISTER OF STATE BENJAMIN CLARE 
HIGHLIGHTED ADVANCES MADE IN IMPLEMENTATION OF THE CIVIL 
SOCIETY INITIATIVE. JAMAICA AND FELLOW RESPONSIBLE 
COORDINATOR URUGUAY ARE FOCUSSING THEIR EFFORTS ON THE 
COMPILATION OF A PROGRESS REPORT ON CIVIL SOCIETY TO BE 
PRESENTED AT THE BOLIVIA SUMMIT CONFERENCE ON SUSTAINABLE 
DEVELOPMENT IN DECEMBER. TO HELP COMPILE THE WEALTH OF 
INFORMATION BEING PRODUCED AT THE 1DB AND BY OTHER 
SOURCES, CLARE NOTED THAT USAID HAS PROVIDED A CONSULTANT 
TO WORK WITH BOTH GOVERNMENTS. 
7. NICARAGUAN FOREIGN MINISTER ERNESTO SANCHEZ LEAL 
DISCUSSED NICARAGUA'S INTEREST IN THE ROLE OF WOMEN IN 
DEVELOPMENT AND ITS ACTIONS AS RESPONSIBLE COORDINATOR. 
HE REPORTED THAT CO-COORDINATOR ARGENTINA WILL HOST A 
TECHNICAL MEETING TO DEFINE NEXT STEPS IN THIS ACTION 
ITEM AND THAT HIS GOVERNMENT PROPOSED A SECOND MEETING IN 
NICARAGUA BEFORE THE NEXT SUMMIT OF THE AMERICAS TO 
PREPARE A PROGRESS REPORT.

in the human rights area, and to come up with concrete

to elections, but also encompassed social advancement and

9. ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES OF PROGRESS. SEVERAL 
MINISTERS PROVIDED SPECIFIC EXAMPLES OF PROGRESS IN 
IMPLEMENTING THE SUMMIT ACTION PLAN: 
FONMIN Miguel Angel Burelli of Venezuela stated 
that with the conclusion of the Inter-American Treaty 
against Corruption in March, the anti-corruption 
"initiative" of the SOA Plan of Action had become a 
commitment of the hemisphere. He welcomed the U.S. 
signature by Deputy Secretary Talbott of the treaty, 
which occurred in a ceremony following the meeting of the 
Foreign Ministers, and urged signators to ratify the 
convention as quickly as possible. 
- ARGENTINE VICE FOREIGN MINISTER PETRELLA 
SUMMARIZED THE ACCOMPLISHMENTS OF THE BUENOS AIRES 
MINISTERIAL ON MONEY LAUNDERING, EMPHASIZING SUCH 
PRIORITIES AS THE CRIMINALIZATION OF MONEY LAUNDERING AND 
THE RESPONSIBLE BANKING PRACTICE OF "KNOW YOUR 
CUSTOMER." MONEY LAUNDERING IS A PROBLEM, HE SAID, THAT 
TRANSCENDS THOSE OF ILLICIT NARCOTICS AND JEOPARDIZES THE 
STABILITY OF OUR SOCIETIES. 
- CHILEAN FOREIGN MINISTER JOSE MIGUEL INSULZA 
REPORTED ON THE RESULTS OF THE ANTI-POVERTY AND 
DISCRIMINATION MEETING HELD IN SANTIAGO IN JANUARY, 
1996. INSULZA STRESSED THAT EDUCATION AND TRAINING MUST 
BE A PRIORITY IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THIS ACTION ITEM. 
GREATER PRIVATE SECTOR PARTICIPATION, DECENTRALIZATION OF 
DECISION-MAKING, MORE EFFICIENT ANTI-POVERTY STRATEGIES, 
AND THE NEED TO TAKE MEASURES TO COMBAT RURAL POVERTY 
SHOULD BE THE FOCUS OF HEMISPHERIC IMPLEMENTATION EFFORTS. 
FONMIN RODRIGO PARDO OF COLOMBIA DESCRIBED THE 
ACTION PLAN FOR SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY ADOPTED AT THE 
SCl/TECH MINISTERIAL IN CARTAGENA, AS WELL AS THE 
IMPORTANT PROGRESS ON THE FREE TRADE INITIATIVE RECORDED 
AT THE DENVER AND CARTAGENA TRADE MINISTERIALS. HE 
CALLED ATTENTION TO THE ENTHUSIASTIC PARTICIPATION OF THE 
PRIVATE SECTOR VIA THE PARALLEL BUSINESS FORUM MEETINGS. 
- FOREIGN MINISTER CASTILLO OF THE DOMINICAN 
REPUBLIC COMMENTED ON POSITIVE PROGRESS TOWARD FREER 
TRADE, BUT LAMENTED THE INABILITY OF SMALL ECONOMIES TO 
MAKE ADJUSTMENTS WITHOUT SPECIAL CONSIDERATION ON THE 
PART OF THE LARGER ECONOMIES. HE CALLED ON THE U.S. TO 
PUSH FOR SPECIAL LEGISLATION TO ASSIST SMALLER COUNTRIES

10. SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT SUMMIT: BOLIVIAN FOREIGN 
MINISTER ARANIBAR BRIEFED THE GROUP ON THE STATUS OF 
PLANNING FOR THE SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT SUMMIT IN SANTA 
CRUZ, BOLIVIA DECEMBER 7-8. HE SAID A PRESIDENTIAL LEVEL 
MEETING IS NEEDED IN ORDER TO ENSURE SUSTAINABLE 
DEVELOPMENT IN THE NEXT CENTURY. THE FOREIGN MINISTER 
SAID THAT THE NEXT STEP IN THE PREPARATIONS WILL BE THE 
DEVELOPMENT OF AN AGENDA PROPOSAL REFLECTING THE REACTION 
OF THE SUMMIT COUNTRIES TO THE BOLIVIAN DRAFT DOCUMENTS. 
ARANIBAR LAID OUT A SERIES OF TECHNICAL MEETINGS WHICH 
WILL LEAD TO A MINISTERIAL IN SAN JOSE, COSTA RICA TO 
DISCUSS THE AGENDA. THE OAS PERMANENT COUNCIL WILL THEN 
APPROVE THE DECLARATION AND PLAN OF ACTION IN LATE 
OCTOBER. THE FINAL PREPARATORY MEETING WILL OCCUR IN 
NOVEMBER. ARANIBAR CLOSED HIS PRESENTATION BY STRESSING 
THE IMPORTANCE OF COOPERATION BETWEEN FOREIGN MINISTRIES 
AND TECHNICAL AGENCIES. 
11. OAS SECGEN GAVIRIA'S REMARKS: SECGEN CESAR GAVIRIA 
SAID THAT AS A RESULT OF THE MIAMI SUMMIT A NEW 
RELATIONSHIP BASED ON SHARED VALUES AND PURPOSES AND A 
COMMITMENT TO COLLECTIVE ACTION EXISTS AMONG THE 
COUNTRIES OF THE AMERICAS. AS A RESULT THE OAS AGENDA 
WAS TRANSFORMED LAST YEAR AT THE OASGA TO COINCIDE WITH 
THE NEW OBJECTIVES. HE IDENTIFIED MANY SPECIFIC AREAS 
WHERE THE OAS HAS BEEN ABLE TO SUPPORT THE SUMMIT PROCESS. 
12. LOCATION AND TIMEFRAME OF THE NEXT SUMMIT: There 
were two principal objectives of this meeting -- to 
review progress in implementing the Summit's Plan of 
Action and to obtain agreement on the timing and place of 
the next Summit of the Americas. Both goals were 
achieved. Hemisperic governments and inter-American 
institutions reported significant progress in achieving 
Summit goals, and a consensus emerged that the next SOA 
should be held in late 1997 or early 1998. (We noted, 
however, that the exact date of the next SOA would be 
announced at the Sustainable Development Summit in Santa 
Cruz this December.) Chilean F.M. Insulza praised the 
U.S. and the SIRG for leadership in Summit implementation 
and formally extended his governments's invitation to 
host the next SOA in Santiago, confirming a decision that 
had emerged at the Jamaica SIRG and later had been 
endorsed by the Rio Group.
