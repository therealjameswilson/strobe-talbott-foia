Dear Strobe, 
RELEASE IN PART 
B6 
I've been thinking of you a lot in the run-up to and aftermath of the summit. I was never quite 
sure when the confirmation took place, but I do want, even if belatedly, to congratulate you 
both the on the book, which is terrific, and on your new post, and, more importantly, to wish 
you the best on the critical assignment ahead for you. 
It's hard to think of a more important question than the one in which you're now at the center 
of, nor can one think of one better prepared than you. You mentioned when we talked last 
autumn about the "trajectory being dear" almost twenty-five years ago. Your own trajectory --
and commitment -- over an even longer time has been in my mind: I can remember the note 
you wrote me in Russian between our freshman and sophomore years, and so many other 
things, from the Khrushchev memoirs onward. But who would have thought that Soviet 
communism itself would simply collapse inward and that all your experience and commitment 
and judgement would come to bear at this sensitive.point in history. 
You know better than anybody both the opportunities and the risks. So much will be 
determined in the next few years. And there seem to be at least four different fronts on which . 
you must contend at the same time -- the Russians themselves, of course, but also the 
Congress, public opinion, and the allies. In short, a pretty tall order. It's very good for all 
parties concerned that your own "trajectory," in ways that you may not have expected, has 
nevertheless made it possible for you to move from the writing of history to helping make 
history. It means a great deal to me, as someone who has known you so long and admired your 
discipline and contribution, to see you in this role. 
Let Inc wish you, in the most heartful way, the best, 
B6
