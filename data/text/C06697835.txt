3. Acting Secretary Talbott met April 24 with Singapore 
Foreign Minister Jayakumar for a 30-minute meeting. The 
Acting Secretary led off the discussion by stressing our 
recognition of Singapore's important role in Southeast 
Asia. In particular, we appreciate Singapore's 
contribution in providing access to U.S. military forces. 
Acting Secretary Talbott stressed that Senior Minister Lee 
Kuan Yew's speech on the Taiwan Strait was particularly 
useful in underscoring the need for restraint during a 
difficult period.. 
4. FM Jayakumar observed that Lee's speech was the only 
major statement to come out of Southeast Asia during the 
tension in the Strait. Singapore had told Beijing it 
should give Taiwan more international space while 
preserving the concept of one China, emphasizing that 
countries in the region would not understand the use of 
force against Taiwan. At the same time, Singapore pointed 
out to Taipei that it should not unsettle the PRC, and 
suggested that Taiwan review its campaign for UN 
membership. In his Bangkok meeting with Lee Kuan Yew, Li 
Peng had been particularly concerned with the UN issue. 
5. Assistant Secretary Lord remarked that it is difficult 
for Taipei to drop its bid for a UN seat as long as the 
PRC continues to demand that it do so. The Acting 
Secretary observed that tensions in the Taiwan Strait are 
merely one part of our broad relationship with the PRC; 
unfortunately, the interaction of this problem with others 
involving economic, commercial and proliferation issues is 
not conducive to the tone we seek in the relationship.

6. A/S Lord said that the U.S. has an upside-down 
relationship with Singapore. With many countries, the 
appearance of relations is very good, but there is little 
substance. With Singapore, we have excellent substantive 
cooperation, but a few issues keep the relationship from 
looking as good as it should. Jayakumar responded that 
differences arising from diverse systems of jurisprudence 
and differing perceptions are inevitable. We must manage 
these issues in such a way that they do not affect 
underlying cooperation. 
7. Noting that he had recently met with Martin Lee of 
Hong Kong, Acting Secretary Talbott asked for Jayakumar's 
assessment of that situation. He added that Lee believes 
Hong Kong is paying the price for Beijing's irritation 
with Taiwan. Jayakumar said that contrary to what many 
say, the diminishment of Hong Kong is not in Singapore's 
interest. Singapore would like to see Hong Kong continue 
as a vibrant commercial center. This is also in Beijing's 
interest, as none of China's other cities can replace Hong 
Kong in this regard. 
8. Jayakumar continued that the PRC is suspicious of 
moves toward democracy which have arisen late in the day, 
inasmuch as the UK made no effort in this direction until 
it began preparing for Chinese control of Hong Kong. 
Beijing fears that democracy in Hong Kong will spread to 
other parts of China. While it is not clear that Beijing 
fully understands Hong Kong, the PRC does want to maintain 
its economic dynamism. 
9. The Foreign Minister was accompanied to the meeting by 
MFA North American and European Affairs Director Ong, 
Singapore Embassy DCM Selverajah, and MFA American Desk 
Officer Goh. A/S Lord, Acting DAS Owens, and Singapore 
desk officer Wayman (notetaker) also attended.
