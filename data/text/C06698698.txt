SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S MEETING WITH ROMANIAN 
FOREIGN MINISTER TEODOR MELESCANU 
1. Classified by EUR DAS Marshall Adair, reason 1.5 (B) 
2. Summary: during a meeting with Romanian foreign 
Minister Melescanu in Washington on October 3, Deputy 
Secretary Talbott said the U.S. supports Romania's 
aspirations for NATO membership, but cannot commit to 
Romania being in the first tranche. He urged Melescanu to 
avoid an all or_nothingmentalityandsaidtheUSCwouldbe 
straightforward withRomania as our decisionmaking 
progresses. End Summary. 
Melescanu s Message: NATO 
2. Melescanu said Romania has 2 major concerns. First, 
the world is far from a stable place given difficulties in 
the Balkans, Russia, Turkey, central Asia and the Middle 
East. Second, in Central Europe, Poland, Czech Republic, 
and Hungary are clearly in the first tranche, and the 
Baltics now seemed to have a special arrangement. That 
would leave out Romania and Slovenia (note: Slovakia 
never came up). To avoid being left out, Romania believed 
it important to be included in the first tranche. 
3. Romania is encouraged by strong support from other 
members. France openly supports its inclusion in the 
first tranche. Germany says it will support that, but 
France must be the godfather. Greece and Turkey can't 
conceive of any NATO Enlargement without Romania because 
it would undermine the flank. 
4. Romania is working closely with other candidates. 
Melescanu stressed NATO membership for both Poland and 
Romania would have a stablizing effect on Ukraine. 
Including Romania, Hungary and Slovenia would'be good for 
the Balkans. Romania had just ratified the,treaty with 
Hungary by an extraordinary vote in the lower house of 
159-1. However, it would be extremely dangerous if NATO 
were to artificially separate the two countries in the 
months to come. 
TALBOTT'S MESSAGE: NATO, RUSSIA, UKRAINE 
5. Talbott first recommended establishing a direct 
channel of communication between Melescanu and him, noting 
the SeCretary had charged him with responsibility for NATO

6. Talbott said the USG fully supports Romania's 
aspirations; it is in the U.S. interest to have Romania 
integrated into the west. However, we cannot say we are 
committed to Romania being in the first tranche, because 
this decision has not been made. That did not mean it 
could not happen. Among other things it would depend on 
developments between now and next Spring. The U.S. is. 
very impressed by the progress Romania has made to date. 
But Talbott also noted some are concerned about the state 
of economic reform. 
7. Talbott then stressed to Melescanu the importance of 
avoiding an all or nothing mentality by defining anything 
short of being in the first tranche as disaster. This 
could have a counterproductive impact on NATO's 
deliberations. 
8. In this regard, Talbott noted the Secretary's 
commitment that the first tranche will not be the last. 
He added his own comment that neither the second or the 
third would be the last. He said Romania was well 
situated and very much ahead of many others who eventually 
will join. 
9. On Russia, Talbott referred to difficult meetings with 
Primakov in New York and said Russia faces a dilemma. It 
doesn't like NATO enlargement, but is beginning to accept 
it as a reality. Russia will simultaneously try to 
prevent it and find ways to be reconciled with it. 
10. On Ukraine, Talbott stressed the importance of 
working closely with Ukraine to prevent it from feeling 
disadvantaged. He was confident Ukraine would eventually 
be a member of NATO -- now the challenge was to find ways 
to move forward without threatening the legitimate 
security concerns of neighbors.
