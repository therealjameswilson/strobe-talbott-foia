PARTICIPANTS:THE DEPUTY SECRETARY AND FM LINDH 
4.-1(-S-)— THE DEPUTY SECRETARY THANKED LINDH FOR THE ROLE 
SWEDEN PLAYED AS A PROTECTING POWER IN BELGRADE AND ADDED 
HE HAD THE DUTY OF ORDERING THE U.S. EMBASSY STAFF AND THE 
CHARGE TO LEAVE BELGRADE. HE TOLD FM LINDH THE AIR 
OPERATION WAS IMMINENT AND THAT IT WAS AN OPERATION NATO 
UNDERTOOK WITH REGRET. HE STRESSED THAT, WITH THE 
INABILITY OF AMBASSADOR HOLBROOKE TO CONVINCE MILOSEVIC TO 
COME TO HIS SENSES AND WITH THE CONTINUING CARNAGE, NATO 
DID NOT HAVE ANY OTHER CHOICE. 
5.48+ THE DEPUTY SECRETARY THEN REVIEWED THE FOUR GOALS 
OF NATO REGARDING KOSOVO. THESE WERE:, 1) TO DETER THE 
OFFENSIVE THAT BELGRADE HAS CARRIED OUT AGAINST CIVILIANS 
IN KOSOVO, 2) TO BLUNT THE SERBIAN MILITARY CAPACITY, 3) 
TO PREVENT THE SPREAD OF THE CONFLICT TO NEIGHBORING 
COUNTRIES, AND 4) TO GIVE BELGRADE REASON TO RECALCULATE 
THE WISDOM OF ENTERING INTO SERIOUS NEGOTIATIONS. HE 
ACKNOWLEDGED THAT FM LINDH HAD SERIOUS CONCERNS ABOUT THE 
HUMANITARIAN SITUATION IN KOSOVO, AND SAID THE U.S. WOULD 
WORK CLOSELY WITH UNHCR AND THE NGO COMMUNITY TO DEAL WITH 
THE REFUGEE CRISIS. 
6.1-S-} THE DEPUTY StCRETARY MENTIONED THE MEETING THAT THE 
RUSSIAN AMBASSADOR HAD WITH THE SWEDISH POLITICAL DIRECTOR 
IN STOCKHOLM ON MARCH 24. HE SAID THE RUSSIANS MADE A 
PUZZLING HINT OF RETALIATORY STEPS THEY MIGHT TAKE IF 
MILITARY ACTION OCCURED. HE NOTED THE SWEDISH FOREIGN 
MINISTRY HAD BEEN.PROMPT AT RELAYING THIS INFORMATION TO 
THE U.S., AND EXPRESSED HIS GRATITUDE FOR THIS CLOSE 
WORKING RELATIONSHIP. 
7. 44 FM LINDH RESPONDED BY STRESSING THE IMPORTANCE OF 
WORKING TOGETHER AFTER THE MILITARY ACTION CONCLUDED. SHE 
ADDED THAT SWEDEN WOULD BE MORE UNDERSTANDING THIS TIME 
THAN THEY WERE DURING OTHER RECENT SECURITY COUNCIL CASES 
INVOLVING MILITARY ACTION. LINDH SAID THAT SWEDEN 
UNDERSTOOD THAT MILITARY ACTION WAS UNAVOIDABLE, BUT WOULD 
NOT EXPRESS DIRECT SUPPORT FOR THE ACTION. FM LINDH ADDED 
SWEDEN HAD NO REASON TO EXPRESS DIRECT SUPPORT BECAUSE IT 
WAS NOT A MEMBER OF NATO, BUT NOTED SWEDEN UNDERSTOOD THE 
U.S. POSITION. 
8. +EH THE DEPUTY SECRETARY SAID SWEDEN'S POSITION WAS AS 
MUCH SUPPORT AS THE U.S. COULD HOPE FOR, AND HE COMMENTED 
THAT A STRONG STATEMENT FROM THE EU WOULD BE HELPFUL. FM

LINDH NOTED THE EU MADE A STATEMENT CONCERNING ITS 
POSITION ON KOSOVO EARLIER ON MARCH 24. THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY SAID HE LOOKED FORWARD TO READING THE STATEMENT, 
AND COMMENTED THAT THE COMMUNITY OF NATIONS MUST PRESENT A 
STRONG MESSAGE TO BELGRADE .IN ORDER TO RESUME DIPLOMACY 
AND DISCONTINUE MILITARY ACTION. 
9. FM LINDH ASKED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY WHAT HE FORESAW 
IN THE FUTURE REGARDING KOSOVO. HE OPINED THAT MILITARY 
ACTION WOULD CONTINUE, BUT THAT A LOT WOULD DEPEND ON WHAT 
MILOSEVIC DID. HE SAID IF MILOSEVIC DID THE WISE THING, HE 
WOULD MAKE IT POSSIBLE FOR THE U.S. TO RESUME DIPLOMACY, 
BUT IF HE ATTACKED CIVILIANS OR NATO FORCES ELSEWHERE, THE 
MILITARY ACTION WOULD CONTINUE AND INTENSIFY. FM LINDH 
ASKED THE DEPUTY SECRETARY HOW HE THOUGHT MILOSEVIC WOULD 
REACT. HE SAID HE DID NOT KNOW BUT WE WOULD FIND OUT SOON. 
HE CONCLUDED BY SAYING HE LOOKED FORWARD TO THE STRONG 
STATEMENT THE EU ISSUED MARCH 24. 
10. (U) THE CALL ENDED AT 1345 EST.
