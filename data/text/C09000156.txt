Friday, August 5, 1994 
Chris: 
Some parting thoughts about Haiti... 
I understand entirely your decision that you,
cannot recommend to the President an invasion, risking 
American lives, to restore Aristide as long as there is 
a significant chance that he will be indicted. 
My own attempt to grapple with this damnable 
situation brought me to the point I laid out for you in 
my last memo. I see the imperfection if not the 
inadequacy of the approach I suggested there. I 
realize we must all face up to the consequenes ofrwhat 
we recommend, and I see that there is a very real risk 
in proceeding with a date certain for intervention as 
long as the Justice matter unresolved. 
At the same time, there is also a risk if we put 
everything on hold, .which is what I fear we're now 
about to do, willy-nilly. The fact that the Président 
has not decided whether he is in fact prepared to use 
force has become a fixture in the public consciousness. 
Confirmation of that is the real damage done by the 
NYTimes piece of yesterday: whatevér its stupidities 
and mischievously inspired mistakes, it was accurate 
(almost by accident) in characterizing the PC as 
inconclusive and indecisive. That revelation 
exacerbates our problem with the Haitian dictators, 
with the international community (on a whole range of 
issues), and with our own public.

You, Bill Perry and Tony are all now lined up 
behind the proposition that we must resolve the Justice 
matter first, and only then decide on an internal 
deadline. My concern is that, whatever we might say to 
the contrary, the machinery of preparing for 
intervention will go into neutral. We may, as a 
result, be even more likely to end up having to jump-
start the machinery in response to events we cannot 
control, having to intervene ill-prepared, perhaps 
unilaterally. We also risk losing the momentum and 
cohesiveness of our multilateral effort, as Madeleine 
warned in the PC. 
For that reason, I would urge in the strongest 
terms that we proceed with preparations for an 
intervention as though we had an internal deadline. I 
recognize that .the PC more or less decided something 
along those lines, but it was less rather than more --
and there will not be adequate follow-through unless 
you and Bill and Tony,agree, and order, that there must 
be. 
There is another difficulty -- a more serious one. 
Even if events cooperate and we are not forced to act, 
or react, before we learn of Justice's intention, we 
must be prepared for Justice to decide that Aristide is 
indictable. If that proves to be the case, then we 
must be in a posture that allows us to proceed with the 
removal of the dictators and the restoration of 
democracy -- but without Aristide himself. 
That consideration argues for a political 
strategy, accompanying the military preparations, 
rather different from the one on which we are now 
embarked. We are now slipping, I fear, into a
