Telephone Conversation Between Acting Secretary Talbott 
and Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Mamedov 
RELEASE IN FULL 
Friday, September 6, 9:15 a.m. 
DECLASSIFIED 
Deputy Foreign Minister Mamedov opened the call by noting 
that he had "a message from Yevgeniy Maksimovich" (Primakov) 
for the Acting Secretary: "sorry, full stop." (Comment: This 
was an apology for Primakov's quoting the text of Secretary 
Christopher's Stuttgart speech, prior to delivery, to German 
Foreign Minister Kinkel. End Comment) Mamedov said that he 
also had an important message to convey regarding 
Russian-American relations. He could not "overestimate the' 
importance" of persuading the UK to withdraw from UNSC 
consideration its current draft resolution on Iraq. Otherwise, 
Mamedov said, the U.S. and Russia would be at loggerheads --
Russia would be forced veto the resolution -- and everything 
that the two countries were doing together would be at risk. 
Acting Secretary Talbott said that he took seriously not 
just that Russia and the U.S. would be at loggerheads, but also 
that we might run into a Russian veto. Mamedov said that, 
especially with U.S. elections coming up, both the U.S. and 
Russia needed to exercise restraint. But in New York, the 
Russian side had been told that additional changes in the text 
of the draft resolution were out of the question. In that 
case, Russia would be forced to veto it. 
Talbott asked from whom the Russians had heard that no more 
changes were possible. Mamedov said that this was the position 
taken by U.S. diplomats in New York. Talbott responded that 
Ambassador Albright was still in negotiations with Russian 
Ambassador Lavrov on this subject. 
Mamedov reiterated that, as things stood, the GOR "would be 
forced" to veto the resolution. Moscow wanted to avoid that. 
It would be bad for events in, the region (i.e., the Gulf) and 
for the bilateral relationship. 
Talbott remarked that he appreciated Primakov's apology 
regarding the Stuttgart speech, but Mamedov should understand 
that "significant repair work needed to be done at the 
ministerial level." Talbott said that he understood Mamedov's 
message about the UNSC resolutions; but Mamedov should 
understand the importance of the UNSC, and especially Russia 
and the U.S., making clear that Baghdad's military operation in 
northern Iraq was an unacceptable violation of UNSC 
resolutions. Talbott also recognized Mamedov's "contextual
