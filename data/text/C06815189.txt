4.~SUMMARY. THE ACTING SECRETARY AND FOREIGN
MINISTER GOFF DISCUSSED THE SITUATION IN INDONESIA AND
EAST TIMOR AFTER THE KILLING OF THE THREE UNHCR WORKERS IN
WEST TIMOR. GOFF SAID NEW ZEALAND'S TROOPS WOULD NOT
WITHDRAW FROM EAST TIMOR NEXT MAY IF THERE WERE STILL A
MAJOR SECURITY PROBLEM. NEW ZEALAND AND AUSTRALIA WOULD
PRESSURE THE EUROPEANS IN NEW YORK TO END ARMS SALES TO
INDONESIA. ON FIJI,GOFF INDICATED, NEW ZEALAND WAS
PRESSING THE GOVERNMENT TO HOLD EARLIER ELECTIONS, JAIL
GEORGE SPEIGHT FOR'TREASON, AND RETURN TO THE 1997
CONSTITUTION. REGARDING THE DPRK, GOFF REPORTED NEW
ZEALAND WAS SENDING A TEAM TO PYONGYANG THE THIRD WEEK OF
SEPTEMBER TO DISCUSS MUTUAL DIPLOMATIC RECOGNITION BY THE
END OF THE YEAR. NEW ZEALAND WOULD RAISE LONG-RANGE
MISSILES, WEAPONS OF MASS DESTRUCTION AND HUMAN RIGHTS IN
ANY TALKS WITH THE NORTH. FINALLY, GOFF NOTED THE PEACE
TALKS IN BOUGAINVILLE WERE PROGRESSING WHILE THOSE IN THE
SOLOMONS WERE MOVING MORE SLOWLY. END SUMMARY.
INDONESIA AND EAST TIMOR
5,~GOFF SAID HE HAD BEEN CONCERNED WITH THE SITUATION
ON THE WEST TIMOR BORDER SINCE JULY. IF THERE WERE STILL
A MAJOR SECURITY PROBLEM IN EAST TIMOR NEXT MAY, NEW
ZEALAND WOULD NOT WITHDRAW ITS TROOPS AS PLANNED. THE
REFUGEE CAMPS IN WEST TIMOR WERE THE KEY ISSUE. THE
INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY COULD HELP BUT INDONESIA HAD TO
TAKE THE FIRST STEP. OTHERWISE, THE CAMPS WOULD CONTINUE
TO BE A BREEDING GROUND FOR MILITIA AND A BASE FOR CROSS
BORDER INCURSIONS. GOFF STATED INCIDENTS LIKE THE DEATH
OF THE UNHCR WORKERS IN ATAMBUA WOULD CONTINUE. HE
WONDERED IF THIS ATTACK HAD. BEEN SPONTANEOUS OR PERHAPS

6.~GOFF NOTED U.S. AMBASSADOR TO INDONESIA ROBERT
GELBARD HAS SAID THE INDONESIAN ARMY (TNI) WAS INVOLVED
WITH WHAT WAS HAPPENING IN WEST TIMOR: IT APPEARED THERE
WERE FORCES TRYING TO BRING WAHID DOWN. GOFF COMMENTED
THAT WHAT INDONESIAN MINISTERS SAID AND WHAT ACTUALLY
HAPPENED WERE TWO DIFFERENT THINGS. THE TWO EXTRA
BATTALIONS SENT BY WAHID WOULD NOT CHANGE ANYTHING. WAHID
AND FOREIGN MINISTER SHIHAB CLAIM THEY WANT TO RESOLVE THE
SITUATION IN WEST TIMOR, GOFF SAID, BUT THE POPULAR
SENTIMENT IN JAKARTA WAS ONE OF NATIONALISTIC RESENTMENT
AT THE LOSS OF EAST TIMOR.
7.~ THE ACTING SECRETARY SAID HE AGREED WITH FM GOFF'S
ASSESSMENT. THE U.S. HAD THOUGHT TWICE ABOUT DEFENSE
SECRETARY COHEN'S VISIT TO JAKARTA BUT HAD DECIDED IT WAS
BETTER TO ENGAGE INDONESIA. ASSISTANT SECRETARY ROTH
OBSERVED THAT IT WAS IMPORTANT TO MAKE SURE THE GOVERNMENT
OF'NDONESIA UNDERSTANDS IT RISKS THE CUT-OFF OF
MULTILATERAL AID UNLESS IT GETS THE SITUATION IN WEST
TIMOR UNDER CONTROL. GOFF AGREED, NOTING IT WAS IMPORTANT
TO MAKE THE COST TO INDONESIA OF ITS NOT DOING ANYTHING
TOO HIGH. THE U.S. AND NEW ZEALAND HAD ENDED MILITARY
COOPERATION AND EQUIPMENT SALES BUT THE EUROPEANS HAD NOT.
GOFF SAID HE AND AUSTRALIAN FOREIGN MINISTER DOWNER WOULD
RAISE THIS ISSUE WITH UK FOREIGN SECRETARY COOK AND OTHER
EUROPEAN FOREIGN MINISTERS DURING THE UNGA IN NEW YORK.
8. ~ GOFF ASKED WHAT THE U.S. THOUGHT ABOUT AN
INDEPENDENT DEFENSE FORCE FOR EAST TIMOR. ROTH NOTED
XANANA GUSMAO, PRESIDENT OF THE NATIONAL COUNCIL OF THE
TIMORESE RESISTANCE MOVEMENT, HAD CHOSEN THE SMALLEST
OPTION. GOFF WONDERED IF 1,500 SOLDIERS WOULD BE ENOUGH.
HE WAS ALSO CONCERNED DECEMBER 2001 MIGHT BE TOO SOON FOR
EAST TIMORESE INDEPENDENCE BUT NOTED IT WAS HARD TO
POSTPONE SOMEONE ELSE'S INDEPENDENCE. GOFF SAID THE
REPORTS HE RECEIVED NOTED UNTAET ADMINISTRATION OF AST
TIMOR WAS IMPROVING.
FIJI
9.~THE NEW FIJI GOVERNMENT, WITH ONLY ONE INDO-FIJIAN
AND TWO OFFICIALS FROM THE FORMER GOVERNMENT, WAS NOT
REPRESENTATIVE, GOFF STATED. NEW ZEALAND WAS PRESSURING
THE GOVERNMENT TO SPEED UP THE ELECTION TIMETABLE. GOFF
SAID HE HAD STOPPED IN FIJI ON HIS WAY TO WASHINGTON AND
HAD SEEN FORMER PEOPLE'S COALITION FOREIGN MINISTER TUPENI
BABA, FOREIGN MINISTER TAVOLA AND ATTORNEY GENERAL QATAKI.
TAVOLA WAS THE KEY PLAYER: A MODERATE, A PROFESSIONAL
CIVIL SERVANT, AND MARRIED TO A NEW ZEALAND CITIZEN. IT
WAS UNCLEAR, HOWEVER, WHETHER HE SPOKE FOR THE REST OF THE
GOVERNMENT. GOFF SAID PRIME MINISTER QARASE AND FORMER
PRIME MINISTER CHAUDHRY WOULD BE SPEAKING TO THE
COMMONWEALTH MINISTERS ACTION GROUP IN NEW YORK. PREVIOUS
PRESSURE FROM THE COMMONWEALTH HAD SHORTENED THE
GOVERNMENT'S ELECTION TIMETABLE TO JULY 2002.
10. ~~OFF SAID NEW ZEALAND WAS PRESSING THE FIJI
GOVERNMENT TO KEEP THE 1997 CONSTITUTION. HE WOULD TRY
AND HAVE THE COMMONWEALTH PRESSURE THE GOVERNMENT AS WELL.
GOFF NOTED HE HAD CONVINCED THE AUGUST SOUTH PACIFIC FORUM
MINISTERS MEETING IN SAMOA TO AFFIRM THE COMMONWEALTH'S
HARARE DECLARATION OF PRINCIPLES, UNDERTAKE TO RESPOND
EXPEDITIOUSLY TO A FUTURE CRISIS, AND CONDEMN THE
OVERTHROW OF THE GOVERNMENT IN FIJI.GOFF REPORTED NEW

NORTH KOREA AND KEDO
11. ~ THE ACTING SECRETARY THANKED GOFF FOR NEW
ZEALAND'S PARTICIPATION IN KEDO AND ASKED NEW ZEALAND TO
ENCOURAGE BRUNEI AND SINGAPORE TO CONTRIBUTE MORE. HE SAID
THE U.S. WAS READY TO CONSULT WITH NEW ZEALAND ON THE
DPRK. GOFF REPORTED HE WAS SENDING A TEAM TO PYONGYANG
THE THIRD. WEEK IN SEPTEMBER. NEW ZEALAND PLANNED TO MOVE
TOWARD MUTUAL DIPLOMATIC RECOGNITION WITH THE DPRK BY THE
END OF THE YEAR, WITH ITS AMBASSADOR IN SEOUL ACCREDITED
TO PYONGYANG. IT WOULD DO SO NOT OUT OF EUPHORIA BUT
BECAUSE IT WAS BETTER TO ENGAGE THAN ISOLATE NORTH KOREA.
NEW ZEALAND WOULD RAISE LONG-RANGE MISSILES, WEAPONS OF
MASS DESTRUCTION, AND HUMAN RIGHTS IN ITS DISCUSSIONS WITH
THE NORTH KOREANS.
12.~GOFF NOTED DPRK FOREIGN MINISTER PAEK SEEMED
INFLEXIBLE IN MEETINGS. IT WAS CLEAR HE HAD NO
NEGOTIATING AUTHORITY. GOFF BELIEVED THE DPRK'S RE-
ENGAGEMENT WITH THE WEST WOULD FORCE CHANGES IN ITS
POLITICAL SYSTEM. GOFF SAID CHINESE FOREIGN MINISTER
TANG HAD TOLD HIM THE DPRK WOULD EVOLVE I IKE CHINA, WITH
ECONOMIC LIBERALIZATION BUT TIGHT POLITICAL CONTROL. DPRK
LEADER KIM JONG IL WAS BEGINNING THIS RAPPROCHEMENT NOW,
TANG THOUGHT, BECAUSE HE WAS COMPLETELY IN CONTROL.
SOIOMONS AND BOUGAINVILLE
13.~GOFF REPORTED THE TWO PARTIES IN THE SOLOMON
ISLANDS DISPUTE WERE MAKING PAINFUL PROGRESS
TOWARD'"SOLVING
THEIR DIFFERENCES. THE PROBLEM WAS TOO MUCH
FACTIONALIZATION WITHIN EACH SIDE TO THE DISPUTE. GOFF
BELIEVED THE VENUE OF THE MEETINGS WOULD SHIFT FROM THE
NEW ZEALAND FRIGATE OFF HONIARA TO CAIRNS, AUSTRALIA. ON
BOUGAINV1LLE, GOFF INDICATED THE PEACE TALKS WERE GOING
WELL AND DISCUSSIONS WERE MORE ADVANCED. IT APPEARED
THERE WOULD BE A CONSTITUTIONAL SOLUTION.
