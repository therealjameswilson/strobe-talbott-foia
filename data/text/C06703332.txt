2. IN A MEETING WITH PRESIDENTIAL SENIOR DIPLOMATIC 
COUNSELOR JEAN-DAVID LEVITTE IN PARIS MARCH 4 (OTHER 
TOPICS SEPTELS), DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT URGED THAT 
FRANCE FIND A WAY TO KEEP ITS GENDARMES ON THE GROUND IN 
HAITI BEYOND THE 31 JULY SCHEDULED PULL-OUT DATE. TALBOTT 
SAID FRANCE'S DECISION WOULD SEND AN IMPORTANT SIGNAL TO 
THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY. THE U.S., HE NOTED, PLANNED 
TO KEEP ITS CIVPOL CONTRIBUTION IN PLACE AT LEAST UNTIL 
THE END OF THE YEAR. THE CANADIANS HAVE MADE A SIMILAR 
COMMITMENT. THE HAITIAN GOVERNMENT NEEDS AND WELCOMES THE 
CONTINUATION OF THESE FORCES, INCLUDIgG THE 77-MAN'FRENCH 
CONTINGENT. 
3. -Tt%).. LEVITTE SAID THE GOF FULLY UNDERSTOOD THE 
POLITICAL IMPORTANCE OF WHAT TALBOTT WAS SAYING. HAITI 
A "SUCCESS STORY" -- ALBEIT A "FRAGILE" ONE -- AND IT 
WOULD BE A PITY IF THE FRIENDS OF HAITI PULLED OUT TOO 
SOON. FOR THAT REASON, LEVITTE SAID, THE GOF WAS 
CONSIDERING OPTIONS FOR A "POSITIVE ANSWER" TO TALBOTT'S 
REQUEST. 
4.-(4).... TALBOTT SAID HE HAD JUST RETURNED FROM HAITI AND 
HAD BEEN DEEPLY IMPRESSED BY THE WORK CIVPOL WAS 
PERFORMING. HE INVITED LEVITTE TO ACCOMPANY HIM TO HAITI 
TO JUDGE FOR HIMSELF, POSSIBLY AS A ONE-DAY ADD-ON TO A 
FUTURE LEVITTE TRIP TO WASHINGTON. LEVITTE AGREED TO 
CONSIDER THIS OFFER.
