SUMMARY 
4. E.Q.4.....SUIvIMARY. DURING A MEETING WITH THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY FEBRUARY 22, MOSCOW MAYOR LUZHKOV URGED THE 
WEST TO MAKE A "GESTURE OF SUPPORT" FOR RUSSIA'S FUTURE 
AND FORGIVE THE BULK OF RUSSIA'S FOREIGN DEBTS WHICH, 
HE SAID, FEW RUSSIANS BELIEVE THEIR COUNTRY WILL EVER 
BE ABLE TO REPAY. HE SAID THE U.S. SHOULD ALSO 
APPRECIATE RUSSIA'S DIVERSITY AND RECOGNIZE THAT MANY 
REGIONS, SUCH AS MOSCOW, ARE PURSUING PROGRESSIVE, 
MARKET-ORIENTED ECONOMIC POLICIES RECEPTIVE TO OUTSIDE 
INVESTMENT. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REPLIED THAT MANY 
'AMERICAN FIRMS LOOKING TO INVEST IN RUSSIA REPORT 
DIFFICULTIES STEMMING FROM CRIME AND CORRUPTION. HE 
ALSO SAID THAT THE U.S. CONTINUES TO WORK TO ALLEVIATE. 
RUSSIA'S DEBT BURDEN BUT SEEKS TO ENSURE THAT THE 
RUSSIAN ECONOMY CAN GROW BEFORE NEW DEBTS ARE ASSUMED. 
END SUMMARY.

RUSSIA AS "A NEGATIVE MOMENT FOR THE STATE." THE 
COUNTRY WAS NOT BUILDING STABILITY. PRIME MINISTER

6. te4--TALBOTT EXPLAINED THAT HE WAS VISITING MOSCOW 
FOR THE STRATEGIC STABILITY TALKS, A SIX-YEAR-OLD 
TRADITION IN THE BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP. THE USG 
REMAINS CONCERNED, HE SAID, THAT SOME RUSSIANS BELIEVE 
THE U.S. SEEKS A WEAK AND DIVIDED RUSSIA. THE TRUTH IS 
"EXACTLY THE OPPOSITE." HOWEVER, THE TASK AHEAD IS TO 
APPLY NOBLE AND POSITIVE SENTIMENTS IN PRACTICAL WAYS. 
TALBOTT LISTED THREE KEY AREAS: A) ECONOMIC 
COOPERATION; B) RESOLVING REGIONAL CONFLICTS; C) 
EFFORTS ON NONPROLIFERATION. HE ASKED FOR LUZHKOV'S 
SPECIFIC ADVICE. 
FORGIVE RUSSIA'S DEBT 
7. LUZHKOV REPLIED THAT HE HAS NOTICED A 
DISCERNIBLE COOLING IN THE BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP, 
DESPITE MANY WELL-MEANING DECLARATIONS FROM BOTH SIDES. 
THE WEST NEEDS TO UNDERSTAND THE IMPACT OF ECONOMIC 
SUFFERING IN RUSSIA, HE SAID. THE ECONOMIC HARDSHIP 
HAS PRODUCED AN ATMOSPHERE WHICH ALLOWS EXTREMISM TO 
FLOURISH, BECAUSE MANY PEOPLE FIND THEIR BASIC NEEDS 
NOT BEING MET. THE WEST COULD TAKE A STEP TO ADDRESS 
THIS HARDSHIP BY FORGIVING THE HUGE DEBT -- PERHAPS AS 
MUCH AS 100 BILLION DOLLARS -- NOW FACING RUSSIA. MUCH 
OF THIS DEBT WAS COMPILED BY THE SOVIET UNION, LUZHKOV 
SAID. TO FORGIVE THIS DEBT WOULD BE A "MORAL BOOST" TO 
RUSSIA, AND A MAJOR GESTURE OF SUPPORT FOR RUSSIA'S 
FUTURE. 
8. -17E4-LUZHKOV NOTED THAT HE WAS A REALIST, AND THE 
REALITY IS THAT FEW RUSSIANS REGARD THIS DEBT AS 
SOMETHING THEIR COUNTRY WILL EVER BE ABLE TO REPAY. 
SOME RUSSIANS WERE EVEN CALLING FOR THE DEBT TO BE 
RENOUNCED. IT WOULD BE PREFERABLE IF THE WEST WOULD 
SIMPLY ACT FIRST AND SET ASIDE THE DEBT, HE SAID. 
9. 4C.,1__LUZHKOV SAID THAT THE USG ALSO NEEDS TO 
"ADJUST" ITS PERCEPTION OF RUSSIA BY RECOGNIZING THE 
COUNTRY'S DIVERSITY. MANY REGIONS -- MOSCOW, ST. 
PETERSBURG AND SAMARA, FOR EXAMPLE -- ARE PURSUING 
PROGRESSIVE AND MARKET-ORIENTED ECONOMIC POLICIES. 
WESTERN INVESTORS SHOULD APPRECIATE THAT THEIR 
INVESTMENTS IN SUCH REGIONS WILL BE PROTECTED. THIS IS 
A-MAJOR OPPORTUNITY TO INFLUENCE RUSSIA'S FUTURE 
DIRECTION. IN THIS CONTEXT, LUZHKOV CONTINUED, THE 
WEST ALSO SHOULD ENCOURAGE THE DEVELOPMENT OF LIGHT 
INDUSTRIES IN RUSSIA TO AUGMENT THE COUNTRY'S RELIANCE 
ON ITS RAW MATERIALS.
