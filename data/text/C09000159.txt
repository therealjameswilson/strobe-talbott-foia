RELEASE IN FULL 
April 14, 1994 
Chris: 
I've just returned from Brussels and Points East. 
I have gone straight home but will check in with Liz by 
phone before the end of the day. I will be in the 
office tomorrow. 
I wanted to report to you on a one-on-one dinner I 
had last night with Yuri Mamedov. We spent over three 
hours together and covered virtually the whole 
waterfront of the relationship. Overall, I was, as I 
usually am after a session with Yuri, hopeful that we 
can keep on course. I must continually remind myself 
that precisely because he is so reasonable, solution-
oriented, and easy to work with, Yuri is not entirely 
representative of his government (or of his Minister).
Nonetheless, heAlas established an extremely good traCk 
record in several respects. First, hp plays straight 
with me. He neither exaggerates nor downplays problems 
for tactical purposes. Second, his early-warning radar 
is good. When he has alerted me to a difficulty in 
advance, it has almost always materialized -- unless, 
of course, we've figured out some way of heading it 
off. Third, he delivers -- and he has a pretty 
accurate sense of where, when and what he can deliver. 
Fourth, he's sometimes breathtakingly candid about 
what's going on in within his government, and his 
Ministry -- in ways that ring true to what I know 
independently and what I sense intuitively. That's one 
reason we dO these sessions one-on-one. 
That said, he has an extremely good working 
relationship with Jim Collins and Tom Pickering. He

I took pains to assure him that in the Gorazde case,

therefore our government wasn't in a position to notify 
the Russians. To fortify this point, I left him with a

discussion over authority. I told him very bluntly, we

appearance or the reality of a Russian veto; nor did we 
want to do anything that compromised the security of 
the operation. 
On the first point, he was more or less mollified. 
He took detailed notes, welcomed the nonpaper, said 
he'd use it to good effect in spinning down colleagues 
back in Moscow. He said that his government ended up 
with a double portion of egg on its face: first, 
because Yeltsin was embarrassed in the eyes of his 
parliamentary opposition at having been caught by 
surprise; second, because the Serbs didn't --,and still 
don't -- believe that the Russians were surprised at 
all; they're convinced that the U.S. did tip Moscow off
