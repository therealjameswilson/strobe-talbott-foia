1. (-SI SUMMARY: ON FEBRUARY 1, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT 
OUTLINED TO G-8 AMBASSADORS IN NEW DELHI A TWO-PHASE 
PROPOSAL TO RELAX IFI SANCTIONS IN EXCHANGE FOR INDIAN 
SIGNATURE ON CTBT. IN THE FIRST PHASE, THE INTERNATIONAL 
COMMUNITY WOULD ALLOW THE ANDHRA PRADESH WORLD BANK LOAN 
TO GO THROUGH AS AN ACT OF GOOD FAITH BUT DEFER ALL OTHER' 
NON-BASIC HUMAN NEEDS (BHN) LOANS. IN THE SECOND PHASE, 
COORDINATED WITH INDIAN SIGNATURE ON THE CTBT, THE 
INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY WOULD CEASE ITS EFFORTS TO BLOCK 
OR POSTPONE THE REMAINING NON-BHN LOANS TO INDIA. 
TALBOTT ARGUED THAT THIS APPROACH WAS LIKELY TO GIVE 
INDIA THE INCENTIVES AND POLITICAL COVER IT NEEDS TO SIGN 
THE CTBT BY MAY. TALBOTT ALSO BRIEFED THE G-8 ON 
PROGRESS ON THE OTHER G-8 BENCHMARKS. END SUMMARY. 
2. 4s4.—THE DEPUTY SECRETARY TOLD THE AMBASSADORS THAT OF 
THE EIGHT ROUNDS SINCE MAY THIS LAST ROUND OF TALKS HAD 
BEEN THE LONGEST AND MOST INTENSE. HE CHARACTERIZED THE 
TALKS AS THE "MOST PRODUCTIVE AND PROMISING" SINCE PRIME 
MINISTER VAJPAYEE'S SEPTEMBER ANNOUNCEMENT THAT HE 
INTENDED TO ADHERE TO THE CTBT BY SEPTEMBER 1999. HE 
STRESSED THAT THIS HAD BEEN STRICTLY A BILATERAL DIALOGUE 
IN WHICH THE U.S. HAS NEVER CLAIMED TO REPRESENT OTHER 
GOVERNMENTS, THE G-8, P-5 OR THE SOUTH ASIA TASK FORCE. 
HE DID POINT. OUT, HOWEVER, THAT THE U.S. HAS ADHERED IN 
THOSE DISCUSSIONS TO THE BASIC ELEMENTS OF THE LONDON G-8 
PRINCIPLES AND THAT HE HAD CONTINUALLY BRIEFED THE G-8 IN 
WASHINGTON. 
THE PROPOSAL 
3. -1;81 ON FEBRUARY 1, DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT BRIEFED 
THE G-8 AMBASSADORS IN NEW DELHI ON A PROPOSAL TO RELAX 
MULTILATERAL SANCTIONS ON INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL 
INSTITUTION (IFI) LOANS TO INDIA IN EXCHANGE FOR . 
SIGNATURE ON CTBT. THIS PHASED PLAN WOULD INVOLVE A GOOD 
FAITH GESTURE BY THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY, CONTINUED 
DEFERRAL OF OTHER NON-BHN IFI LOANS AND FINALLY A BROADER 
RELAXATION OF IFI SANCTIONS WHEN INDIA SIGNS THE CTBT IN 
MAY. THE GOOD FAITH GESTURE WOULD BE TO ALLOW THE 
PENDING WORLD BANK LOAN TO ANDHRA PRADESH (NOT CONSIDERED 
BHN BY THE U.S.) TO BE APPROVED. THE PURPOSE OF THIS 
GESTURE WOULD BE TO PROVIDE CONCRETE EVIDENCE OF IFI 
SANCTION ALLEVIATION FOR THE GOI TO USE DOMESTICALLY IN 
ITS EFFORTS TO BUILD CONSENSUS ON SIGNING THE CTBT.

THE RESPONSE 
6. —44-THE JAPANESE AMBASSADOR ASKED A NUMBER OF PROBING 
QUESTIONS. HE SAID THAT THIS PLAN WOULD DIVERGE FROM 
CURRENT JAPANESE POLICY WHICH ONLY SUPPORTS RESUMPTION OF 
IFI LOANS AFTER BOTH SIGNATURE AND RATIFICATION OF CTBT. 
TALBOTT RECOGNIZED THE DIFFERENCES IN POLICY AMONGST THE 
G-8 ON THIS ISSUE. HE SAID THAT HE BELIEVES THAT 
FLEXIBILITY ON THE SEQUENCING OF THESE STEPS WOULD HELP 
MOVE THE PROCESS IN THE RIGHT DIRECTION. HE ALSO POINTED 
OUT THAT THE INDIAN CONSTITUTIONAL PROCESS FOR 
RATIFICATION WAS SIMPLER THAN IN MANY OTHER COUNTRIES AND 
THAT SIGNATURE CROSSED AN. IMPORTANT POLITICAL THRESHOLD. 
A/S INDERFURTH ADDED THAT THE GOI ON SEVERAL OCCASIONS
HAD TOLD SENIOR U.S. OFFICIALS THAT INDIAN SIGNATURE ON 
CTBT IS AN IRREVOCABLE ACT. THE JAPANESE AMBASSADOR 
ASKED WHETHER THE EXPECTED AGNI MISSILE TEST WOULD ALSO 
BE LINKED TO THE RELAXATION OF IFI LOANS. DAS EINHORN 
RESPONDED THAT WHILE IT HAD NOT BEEN EXPLICITLY LINKED, 
THE U.S. HAS INDICATED TO THE INDIANS THAT IT COULD 
AFFECT THE CLIMATE IN WHICH A DECISION IS TAKEN ON IFI 
LOANS.

THE OTHER THREE BENCHMARKS 
8. tSr-THE ITALIAN AMBASSADOR ASKED ABOUT THE OTHER 
THREE CENTRAL ISSUES IN THE TALKS. ON FMCT, THE DEPUTY 
SECRETARY SAID THAT THE U.S. OBJECTIVE HAS BEEN TO HAVE 
INDIA SEE IT AS IN ITS OWN INTERESTS TO MAKE A 
CONDITIONED STATEMENT SUPPORTING A MULTILATERAL 
THE COMPLETION OF THE FMCT. FOR INDIA TO MOVE IN THIS 
DIRECTION WOULD REQUIRE THAT THE OTHER SIX NATIONS WHO 
ACKNOWLEDGED HAVING TESTED ADOPT THE SAME MORATORIUM. HE 
REPORTED THAT THE INDIAN POSITION HAS EVOLVED FROM 
REFLEXIVE REJECTION TO CAREFUL CONSIDERATION. TALBOTT 
INDICATED THAT THE INDIANS HAD TOLD HIM, HOWEVER, THAT 
MOVING FORWARD ON BOTH CTBT AND THE FISSILE MATERIAL 
MORATORIUM AT THE SAME TIME MIGHT OVERLOAD THE CIRCUITS. 
HE HOPED THAT REAL PROGRESS COULD BE MADE ON THIS ISSUE A 
LITTLE LATER IN THE SPRING. 
9. -44- ON EXPORT CONTROLS, THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REMARKED 
THAT DURING THIS ROUND THE INDIANS HAD GIVEN MORE 
SPECIFIC RESPONSES THAN IN THE PAST ON HOW AND WHEN IT 
WOULD BRING ITS EXPORT CONTROLS INTO CONFORMITY WITH 
INTERNATIONAL STANDARDS. ON DEPLOYMENT (AKA DEFENSE 
POSTURE), TALBOTT SAID THAT THIS WAS IN MANY WAYS THE 
MOST DIFFICULT ISSUE. HIS OPINION, ECHOED BY VICE 
CHAIRMAN JCS RALSTON, WAS THAT THE INDIANS HAVE NOT YET 
MADE UP THEIR MINDS ON WHAT THEY MEAN BY MINIMAL 
DETERRENCE. DAS EINHORN SAID THAT THE U.S. OBJECTIVE IS 
TO ENCOURAGE THE INDIANS TO MAKE COMMITMENTS WHICH GIVE 
SUBSTANCE TO THEIR STATEMENTS THAT THEY WISH TO MAINTAIN 
THE LOWEST POSSIBLE LEVEL THAT WILL MEET THEIR SECURITY 
NEEDS. TALBOTT CAUTIONED THAT THE U.S. POSITION REMAINS 
THAT THE RIGHT LEVEL IS ZERO AND, THEREFORE, THE U.S. HAS 
NOT DISCUSSED OTHER POSSIBLE NUMERICAL LEVELS BEHIND
