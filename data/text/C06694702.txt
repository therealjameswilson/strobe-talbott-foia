6. PRIME MINISTER DUNCAN AFFIRMED THAT U.S./IVORIAN 
BILATERAL RELATIONS WERE VERY GOOD, BUT HE SAID THAT 
EVEN SUCH GOOD RELATIONSHIPS COULD BE IMPROVED. DUNCAN 
SAID THAT THE REAL FOCUS OF OUR BILATERAL ECONOMIC 
RELATIONSHIP SHOULD BE ON PRIVATE SECTOR COOPERATION, 
AND IN PARTICULAR ON AMERICAN INVESTMENT IN COTE 
D'IVOIRE. THE IVORIAN INVESTMENT CLIMATE•SUBSEQUENT TO 
THE JANUARY 1994 DEVALUATION OF THE CFA FRANC WAS MUCH 
MORE FAVORABLE, HE NOTED. DUNCAN ALSO CITED THE 
IMPORTANCE OF THE UNITED MERIDIAN (UMIC) ENGAGEMENT IN 
IVORIAN OFFSHORE OIL AND GAS DEVELOPMENT, AND ENCOURAGED 
ADDITIONAL U.S. INVESTMENT IN EXTRACTIVE AND 
AGRICULTURE-RELATED,INDUSTRIES. ALMOST AS AN ASIDE, THE 
PRIME MINISTER SAID THAT OUR PL-480 PROGRAMS SEEMED TO 
BE WORKING VERY WELL. 
7. TURNING TO THE PROBLEM OF THE COUNTRY'S IMMENSE 
FOREIGN DEBT, THE PRIME MINISTER NOTED THAT IN LAST 
SPRING'S PARIS CLUB MEETING ON COTE D'IVOIRE'S PUBLIC 
DEBT, THE UNITED STATES WAS THE ONLY CREDITOR COUNTRY 
WHICH CHOSE DEBT RESCHEDULING INSTEAD OF DEBT 
FORGIVENESS. DUNCAN SAID THAT HE AND ASSISTANT 
SECRETARY MOOSE HAD DISCUSSED THIS ISSUE AT LENGTH LAST 
JULY, AND THAT HE UNDERSTOOD THERE COULD BE NO CHANGE IN 
THE USG STANCE ON DEBT FORGIVENESS "FOR THE TIME 
BEING." REGARDING IVORIAN FOREIGN PRIVATE DEBT, THE 
PRIME MINISTER SAID THAT HE ANTICIPATED A LONDON CLUB 
MEETING WITH CREDITOR BANKS TO BE SCHEDULED AT THE END 
OF THE YEAR OR EARLY IN 1995. HE SAID THAT HE HOPED FOR 
AMERICAN "COOPERATION" IN THIS /MPORTANT ENDEAVOR AS

U.S. RESPONSES AND POINTS OF EMPHASIS 
9. DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT REPLIED THAT: THE UNITED 
STATES GOVERNMENT LOOKS TO COTE D'IVOIRE TO PROVIDE 
ADDITIONAL ECONOMIC LEADERSHIP IN THIS REGION PRECISELY 
BECAUSE OF ITS EXCELLENT TRACK RECORD AND ITS 
ENLIGHTENED ECONOMIC POLICIES. ASSISTANT SECRETARY 
MOOSE THEN NOTED THAT HE PERCEIVED SOME HESITATION ON 
THE PART OF WEST AFRICAN ANGLOPHONE COUNTRIES AS REGARDS 
REGIONAL ECONOMIC COOPERATION WITH A DOMINANT FRENCH 
FRANC ZONE MONETARY UNION. HE SUGGESTED THAT THE 
IVORIANS TAKE THE LEAD AND ENGAGE WITH THEIR 
ENGLISH-SPEAKING REGIONAL PARTNERS TO DEVELOP IDEAS ON 
HOW WIDER COOPERATION, ALLOWING ROLES FOR BOTH ECOWAS 
AND UEMOA, COULD BE BOTH EFFICIENT AND OF BENEFIT TO 
ALL. (DUNCAN INTERJECTED THAT UEMOA SHOULD SERVE 
ECOWAS.) ASSISTANT AID ADMINISTRATOR HICKS THEN ADDED 
THAT THE U.S. GOVERNMENT WOULD BE PREPARED TO PROVIDE 
ASSISTANCE IN REGIONAL ECONOMIC INTEGRATION, FOR EXAMPLE 
BY HELPING WITH A STUDY ON HOW TO BRING FRANCOPHONE AND 
ANGLOPHONE COUNTRIES CLOSER TOGETHER. HE NOTED THAT
USAID ALREADY HAD DONE WORK ON REGIONAL ECONOMIC 
INTEGRATION EFFORTS IN BOTH EAST AND SOUTHERN AFRICA; WE 
WOULD BE WILLING TO LOOK AT WEST AFRICA, TOO.
