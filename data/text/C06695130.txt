SUBJECT: Requesting Use of Costa Rican Territory for a 
Haitian Refugee Safe Haven 
Acting Secretary Talbott telephoned President Figueres to convey an 
urgent request from President Clinton that Costa Rica agree to permit 
the temporary establishment of a safe haven facility on its territory for 
Haitian political refugees. The President was travelling to Naples for 
the G-7 or he would have made the call himself, but the Acting 
Secretary explained that he was chosen to convey the message due 
to his recent, very cordial meeting with Figueres. 
The Acting Secretary emphasized that Costa Rica was not being 
asked to assimilate Haitians, but simply to serve as hosts for a 
notional period of six months. The U.S. would set up and administer 
the camp in conjunction with UNHCR and the GOCR. The Acting 
Secretary told Figueres that the U.S. was requesting Costa Rica's 
assistance, mindful of the unique concem for refugees and human 
rights historically demonstrated by the GOCR. 
Continuing, the Acting Secretary reminded Figueres that the U.S. 
changed its Haiti refugee policy May 8 because the President felt it 
was morally and politically untenable. Picking up fleeing Haitians on 
the high seas and taking them to processing centers only increased 
the numbers of those leaving Haiti, compelling the U.S. to request the 
assistance of Costa Rica and other countries in the region. Noting the 
hemispheric support for Haiti sanctions and the transition to 
democracy, the Acting Secretary suggested that establishing-
temporary safe havens would allow time for the sanctions to take 
effect on the de facto Haitian government. 
The Acting Secretary agreed with Figueres' characterization of the 
request as "a tall order," adding that the U.S. would do what it could to 
make the request palatable, including sending a special envoy to 
Costa Rica as soon as possible to work out the details.

Figueres then reminded the Acting Secretary of the problems

Figueres said that he would welcome the McNeil party in San Jose on

acceptance of any facility. He explained that Costa Rica was

tradition of concem for human rights, and Costa Rica's excellent 
relationship with the U.S., he agreed to give the matter further 
consideration by accepting the proposed U.S. exploratory party.
