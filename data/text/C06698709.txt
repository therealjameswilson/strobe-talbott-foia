3. .s.se- on october 8, lithuanian foreign minister gylys, 
in the U.S. for the unga, called on acting secretary 
talbott to discuss lithuania's thoughts on the nato 
enlargement process. gylys said lithuania preferred to 
keep its thoughts on the nato enlargement process in 
diplomatic channels. however, secretary perry's recent 
statement that the baltics would not be included in the 
first group of countries had caused a public reaction in 
the region that led to the september 28 joint statement on 
security by the baltic presidents. 
4. ye gylys noted that as we approach the december nac 
and the mid-1997 nato summit it appears that three or four 
countries will be named in the first group to join. 
lithuania believes it should be designated in that group 
of central european countries considered to be "in the 
process" of joining nato. he said the baltic countries 
and others intend to use Partnership for peace (pfp) as 
the channel to nato. gylys stressed that it is important 
that all nato candidate countries be declared as such and 
that the accession process should begin for all at the 
same time -- even if states should be admitted to the 
allianceat differenttimesastheymeetthe membership 
criteria. If instead nato proceeds with a "limited" 
enlargement involving only a few countries, lithuania believes

5. J4.11'-gylys said lithuania welcomed the development of

noted that lithuania wants cooperative security in europe 
and the council of baltic sea states helps this. but a 
limited nato enlargement will leave lithuania isolated and 
force it to assume too great a burden of responsibility. 
"if a big neighboring country turns clumsily, it leaves 
bruises at its border, even in a good mood. but if the 
mood turns bad, it could be most painful for us," gylys 
added. "We can't neglect russia, but they are playing 
their own game." gylys said according to recent polls, 40 
percent of russiana believe that if nato cooperates with 
russia they do not consider nato an enemy; clearly, the 
russian leadership is exaggerating fear of nato 
enlargement for its own purposes. 
6. ce) gylys noted that lithuania has invested heavily 
in westernization and modernization, and "we have little 
to learn from the east." any turn away from this western 
orientation would be a loss -- "not today, not next year, 
but in five years." he said lithuania is prepared for the 
scenario of a limited nato enlargement, where favored 
countries would be treated as members of a first group. 
"we know nato wants to keep its door open for others, but 
we know from experience that the crosswinds of history can 
bang doors shut." 
7. the acting secretary responded by reassuring gylys 
that secretary perry's comments had been misconstrued and 
that lithuania has no cause for concern. he said the 
president, vice-president, the secretary and secretary 
perry believe fervently that if nato enlargement serves 
the larger cause of enhancing common security, the doors 
must remain open, including to the baltics. talbott noted 
gylys' frequent use of the term "limited nato 
enlargement." he assured him that the first, second and 
third enlargements will not be the last. lithuania is 
making substantial progress toward the west, and deserves 
to be part of it.

8: (Ae'on russia, talbott said the u.s. has worked on a 
nato-russia dialogue for over a year, but the russians 
only began to get serious after their elections. 
simultaneously they are trying to 1) stop the process, (2) 
limit enlargement to the first tranche, and (3) seek the 
most advantageous position if (1) and (2) fail. in this 
context the russian deputy foreign minister set forth 
propositions to guide the nato/russia relationship, one of 
which is that the baltic states and ukraine will never 
join. the usg has said categorically and repeatedly that 
it would never accept the exclusion of the baltic 
countries and ukraine from nato. 
9. (5), talbott noted that the baltic action plan was 
intended to address some of lithuania's concerns. the 
purpose of the plan was to keep the nato door open and to 
help states meet nato's accession standards. gylys 
responded that the presence and activity of the u.s. in 
central europe and the baltics was welcome. he 
appreciated the plan's bilateral political and economic 
initiatives, and the elements of regional cooperation. he 
noted the plan's offer to help in lithuania's dialogue 
with the russians "when we need it." lithuania accepts 
U.S. assurances that the baltic action plan is not an 
alternative or compensation for nato membership, but gylys 
feared that the public perceived it that way. 
10. gylys warned that kaliningrad should not be 
forgotten, since the enclave will be part of any dialogue 
involving lithuania's integration with the west. talbott 
commended gylys for the lithuanian/polish relationship, 
and urged him to discuss issues on kaliningrad with the 
russians as well.

were Ambassador Alfonsas Eidintas, MFA Americas A/S Audra

sakalauskas. the u.s. side included d staff phil 
goldberg, eur/nb director van voorst, and baltic desk 
officer evans.
