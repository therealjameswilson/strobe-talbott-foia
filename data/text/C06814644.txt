SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY'S MEETING WTH FINNISH DEP STATE 
SECRETARY BLOMBERG JULY 21, 1999 
Classified by Deputy Secretary Strobe Talbott. Reasons: 
para. 1.5 (b) and (d). 
1. Summary: In his July 22 meeting with Finnish 
Depu State Secretary Jaakko Blomberg, the Deputy 
Secretary raised USG concerns about the format of the July 
30 Sarajevo Conference, suggested that Front Line State 
leaders/be included as speakers, and asked that the USG be 
able to provide advance input to the communique. BloMberg 
agreed to continue to work closely with the USG on the 
conference and to take our communique suggestions. The 
Deputy Secretary briefed Blomberg on.the Baltic Partnership 
Commission Meeting July 19 and encouraged further U.S.-EU 
cooperation in Northeast Europe. Both agreed on the 
importance of keeping Russia informed and bringing it along 
on regional issues. Other participants included Finnish 
Amb. Jaakko Laajava, EUR Acting A/S E. Anthony Wayne, D

Blomberg discussed the Stability Pact, the proposal for a 
US-EU-Russia summit at the OSCE Summit in Istanbul, and the 
European Security and Defense Initiative. End Summary. 
2.8.),The Deputy Secretary thanked Finland for agreeing 
to modify the proposed "everyone speaks" format for the 
Sarajevo summit. He encouraged Finland to provide a 
greater speaking role for the regional states (FLS), rather 
than just having Bosnia speak. While the U.S. agrees 
Bosnia should have a strong role, we should also consider • 
ways to incorporate more fully the FLS. Blomberg replied 
that he was not sure where the discussion stood at that 
moment but said the USG proposal made sense. EUR Acting 
A/S Wayne thanked Blomberg for sharing the draft communique 
with us, and said we would like to offer comments before 
the draft was re-circulated. Blomberg said he did not want 
to open the draft to a wider discussion, but that he would 
take our comments into consideration on the next draft, if 
they were received soon. EUR Wayne promised to transmit 
them immediately. 
3. .),,The Deputy Secretary briefed Blomberg on the U.S.-
Baltic Partnership Commission meeting July 16, which had 
allowed the U.S. to resume a broad dialogue with the Baltic 
region, and demonstrate that we can engage in the Balkans 
and the Baltics at the same time. The Baltic states were 
generally doing the right things on security, social 
integration and regional cooperation with Russia. It wouLd 
be perverse, he said, to penalize them regarding Euro-
integration broadly or NATO enlargement specifically in 
favor of Southeastern Europe because of their relative 
stability and prosperity. The Deputy Secretary said we are 
refocusing our attention to the North, including Baltic 
relations with Russia. The Partnership Commission 
addressed a range of tasks and objectives. The U.S. now 
looks to Finland for leadership in regional cooperation 
with Russia and intends to cooperate with the Finnish 
Presidency and the EU on its Northern Dimension. The 
Deputy Secretary indicated we would continue to consider 
the possibility of a Trilateral (U.S.-EU-Russia) Summit as 
recently suggested by Russian Deputy FM Gusarov; the 
Istanbul Summit provided the logical timing window. 
4.W..),Blomberg agreed that the U.S. and the EU needs to 
work together both on Kosovo, Northern Europe, and Russia. 
Moscow wants to work with the EU on all levels, and Finland 
will be working with Russia in the fall to put more 
substance into EU-Russia relations. After the events in 
Kosovo, Blomberg said, it is important not to marginalize 
Russia, but rather to try to work with it on a range of 
issues. In the case of Kaliningrad, it is striking that 
the local authorities can now work with foreign entities, 
and he added that he hoped Russia would use the opportunity 
of a trilateral summit to increase cooperation, and that he 
hoped cooperation with NATO will be back on track by then. 
Russia wants to work with the West, Blomberg said, but it 
has serious domestic problems. A trilateral meeting could 
influence Russia positively, especially if the agenda were 
specific and limited. 
5. The Deputy Secretary agreed, and said we need to 
work on the substance of specific cooperative projects. 
The problems in Russia are serious and sobering and extend 
far beyond disagreements over Kosovo, he said. There is a 
sense of drift and disillusionment among Russians, both 
with themselves and with the West, and we need to try to 
bring them along. He encouraged Blomberg to keep trying to

Russia. He also said he hoped Belarus could also be a

6. N. On the Balts, the Deputy Secretary said he had 
been concerned by the passage of the Latvian language law, 
but that the controversy had a silver lining. President 
Vike-Freiberga made the difficult but correct decision to 
• send the law back to the Parliament, and the OSCE, EU, and 
U.S. played constructive roles. The Deputy Secretary said 
the Russian government had asked the U.S. to use the 
Partnership Commission to get the law fixed. That was a 
breakthrough, the Deputy Secretary said, because previously 
the Russians had seen the Commission solely as a vehicle to 
bring the Balts into NATO. Now, they were beginning to see 
its possibilities in other areas. BloMberg said Russian 
Deputy FM Gusarov had also raised the language law with 
Finnish FM Tarja Halonen and appealed to her to speak with 
Vike-Freiberga, which she did. The Deputy Secretary 
encouraged Finland to get credit from the Russians for 
that, and said EUR DAS Ronald Asmus would make this point 
to Gusarov when they met in Vienna July 23. The Deputy 
Secretary asked Blomberg to tell FM Halonen of his 
appreciation, as well. 
7.-('e) Blomberg said he was concerned by developments in 
the Russian military. It is difficult to know what is 
happening, he said, and asked to keep in touch about these 
issues. He also asked to compare notes 'on Baltic-NATO 
relations, especially whether the Russians were alarmed by 
U.S. statements on the Baltics and NATO enlargement. The 
Deputy Secretary said we had tOld Moscow that we are not 
accelerating or slowing down our commitment to integrating 
the Balts into the West, including NATO, and the Russians 
made no remonstrance. The Deputy Secretary concluded that 
he wanted to work with Finland on these issues, and not 
exclusively in its EU Presidency context.
8. /S) In his meeting with Acting A/S Wayne, Blomberg 
discussed the final modalities for the Sarajevo Conference, 
including the importance of fully including regional states 
in all Stability Pact activities. Wayne pressed for 
final communiquE that emphasizes the critical importance of 
achieving democracy and political and press freedoms in 
Serbia. Blomberg pointed out that the Sarajevo Conference 
message must be carefully crafted because of the Russian 
presence. Wayne also underscored the importance of 
building a solid investment and commercial foundation for 
the Stability Pact. He stressed the need to include 
regional companies, promote transparency in assistance 
efforts, and allow open international competition for 
reconstruction work in Kosovo. They briefly discussed the 
future status of Montenegro. Wayne voiced strong support 
for Djukanovic and the Montenegrins, but added that the 
creation of a Balkan mini-state will not help to advance 
political stability or economic integration in the long 
run. The U.S. and EU, Blomberg and Wayne agreed, stand 
ready to support democratic forces within Serbia, as they 
become more united and coherent in their opposition to 
Milosevic. 
9.-(6)-Blomberg favored a US-EU-Russia summit on the 
margins of the November OSCE Istanbul Summit as a means to 
signal that relations between Russia and the West are back 
on track and of reinforcing the hand of the moderates 
before the Duma and Presidential elections. Blomberg took 
pride in the evolution of EU policy toward Russia. He 
pointed to the EU's common strategy, Northern Dimension,

10. Acting A/S Wayne warned that the EU must not allow 
ESDI fo become a "PoteMkin village" for European security. 
The crux of ESDI, he stressed, is military capability. 
Without adequate defense capability, the EU will be 
unprepared to meet the range of future security challenges. 
Wayne reiterated the need for good will, transparency, hard 
work, and a genuine commitment by the EU to getting ESDI 
right. BloMberg replied that Finland, as a non-NATO 
member, is very pragmatic and intends to move cautiously 
and cooperatively to strengthen the EU without weakening 
NATO. He hoped that NATO SYG Solana's assumption of his 
new duties as the EU's High Representative for Common 
Security and Foreign Policy in October would facilitate 
this process.
