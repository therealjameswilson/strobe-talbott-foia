SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT'S JANUARY 27 MEETING WITH 
FRENCH AMBASSADOR BUJON 
1. CLASSIFIED BY DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT. REASONS 
1.5 (B) AND (D). 
2. 444 DEPUTY SECRETARY STROBE TALBOTT MET WITH FRENCH 
AMBASSADOR FRANCOIS BUJON ON JANUARY 27 TO DISCUSS NATO 
SUMMIT ISSUES, KOSOVO DEVELOPMENTS, SOUTH ASIAN NON-
PROLIFERATION AND UNITED STATES-GOF CONSULTATIONS ON IRAQ. 
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY UNDERSCORED WASHINGTON'S INSISTENCE ON 
CLOSE CONSULTATIONS WITH PARIS AND LONDON ON ESDI 
DEVELOPMENTS AND STRESSED THAT PREDESIGNATION OF FUTURE 
MEMBERS WAS UNACCEPTABLE. ON KOSOVO, BUJON ADVISED THE GOF 
CONCURRED WITH WASHINGTON'S SEQUENCING STRATEGY AND 
OUTLINED GOF PRELIMINARY THINKING ON FRENCH GROUND TROOP 
CONTRIBUTIONS. THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REVIEWED PROSPECTS FOR 
HIS UPCOMING TRIP TO SOUTH ASIA AND REITERATED THE NEED FOR 
P-S UNITY ON SOUTH ASIAN NON-PROLIFERATION ISSUES. END 
SUMMARY. 
3. -E-ei DEPUTY SECRETARY TALBOTT AND EUR ASSISTANT 
SECRETARY MARC GROSSMAN MET WITH FRENCH AMBASSADOR FRANCOIS 
BUJON ON JANUARY 27. DISCUSSION CENTERED ON OUTSTANDING 
NATO SUMMIT ISSUES, KOSOVO DEVELOPMENTS, SOUTH ASIAN NON-
PROLIFERATION; AND TOUCHED BRIEFLY ON UNITED STATES-GOP 
BILATERAL CONSULTATIONS ON IRAQ. ALSO PRESENT AT THE 
MEETING WERE SPECIAL ASSISTANT JOHN BASS, FRENCH EMBASSY 
COUNSELOR OLIVIER CARON, AND FRENCH DESK OFFICER MARGOT 
SULLIVAN - 
4. +e) THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REVIEWED BOTH ESDI AND 
CHARATERIZATION OF NATO'S OPEN DOOR, NOTING HE EXPECTED TO 
FOCUS ON BOTH TOPICS DURING HIS UPCOMING VISIT TO PARIS. 
ON ESDI, TALBOTT SAID HE WANTED THE FRENCH TO HEAR THE SAME 
MESSAGE HE GAVE SENIOR UK OFFICIALS DURING A RECENT VISIT: 
THE U.S. WAS FRUSTRATED AND DISAPPOINTED BOTH WITH THE 
OUTCOME AT ST. MALO AND THE LACK OF GENUINE CONSULTATIONS 
AND TRANSPARENCY BOTH BEFORE AND AFTER THE FRANCO-BRITISH 
SUMMIT. HE UNDERSCORED THAT WASHINGTON STRONGLY SUPPORTED 
ESDI AND WANTED IT TO SUCCEED: BUT SUCCESS HAD TO BE 
DEFINED AS ENSURING,THERE WAS NO DIMINUTION OF EITHER THE 
TRANSATLANTIC LINK NOR THE SPIRIT AND MEANING OF THE 
APPROACH TAKEN AT THE BERLIN MINISTERIAL. THE "THREE D'S" 
REMAINED U.S. REDLINES: DISCUSSION WITH SENIOR NORWEGIAN 
OFFICIALS THE DAY BEFORE HAD UNDERSCORED THE IMPORTANCE OF 
"NO DISCRIMINATION" AGAINST NON-EU NATO ALLIES. BUJON 
REPLIED "BUT NORWAY IS NOT A MEMBER OF THE EU," TO WHICH 
THE DEPUTY SECRETARY REJOINED "THAT'S PRECISELY THE POINT." 
CONCLUDING, TALBOTT STRESSED THAT THE U.S. WANTED TO ENSURE 
THAT, WE NEEDED TO IDENTIFY SOON THOSE ASPECTS OF "BERLIN-
PLUS" WHICH COULD BE TEED UP BY THE SUMMIT. MUCH MORE

5. tet ON THE OPEN DOOR ISSUE, HE SAID THAT THE LANGUAGE IN 
THE SUMMIT COMMUNIQUE SHOULD NEITHER MAKE PRE-JUDGEMENTS ON 
ASPIRANTS, NOR PRE-COMMIT THE ALLIANCE TO A SCHEDULE. HE 
.ADDED THAT THE USG DID NOT WANT THE WASHINGTON SUMMIT TO 
DEFLATE ROMANIAN EXPECTATIONS, BUT NEITHER WANTED TO EXTEND 
UNREALISTIC EXPECTATIONS. BUJON SAID THAT.PARIS WAS MOST 
CONCERNED THAT THE WASHINGTON SUMMIT REFLECT A MADRID-PLUS 
APPROACH TO THIS ISSUE. TALBOTT AGREED BUT REITERATED THAT 
THE LANGUAGE COULD NOT PREDESIGNATE WHO WOULD BE INCLUDED 
IN THE NEXT ROUND OR WHEN IT WOULD OCCUR. 
6. -KRi ON KOSOVO, TALBOTT OBSERVED THAT RECENT DEVELOPMENTS 
COULD PROVIDE THE EUROPEANS WITH AN OPPORTUNITY TO 
DEMONSTRATE THEIR COMMITMENT TO ESDI. BUJON TOOK THE POINT 
AND WENT ON TO NOTE THAT THE EUROPEANS HOPED TO DO JUST 
THAT. BOTH BUJON AND ASSISTANT SECRETARY GROSSMAN AGREED 
THAT AFTER MUCH WORK, PARIS AND WASHINGTON HAD IRONED OUT 
DIFFERENCES ON THE PROPOSED SEQUENCING SCENARIO. GROSSMAN 
THEN DEBRIEFED ON RUSSIAN VIEWS REGARDING SEQUENCING. HE 
SAID THAT IN HIS DISCUSSION WITH THE RUSSIAN CHARGE EARLIER 
THAT MORNING, RUSSIAN CONCERNS WERE LIMITED TO THE SHORT 
TIME ALLOTTED FOR THE KOSOVO NEGOTIATIONS. GROSSMAN 
EXPLAINED THE NEED FOR A SHORT PERIOD OF NEGOTIATIONS TO 
PUSH THE TWO PARTIES TOWARDS AN AGREEMENT. GROSSMAN ALSO 
NOTED THAT CHRIS HILL AND PIETRICH BELIEVED THAT IF THE 
NEGOTIATIONS TOOK PLACE, THEY WOULD BE ABLE TO IDENTIFY A 
GROUP OF ALBANIANS TO SPEAK TO AND, HOPEFULLY, MAKE 
"PROGRESS." 
7. -442+ BUJON ASKED ABOUT WASHINGTON'S POSITION ON 
CONTRIBUTING GROUND TROOPS TO KOSOVO OPERATIONS. TALBOTT 
REPLIED THAT THE U.S. HAD MADE CLEAR IT WOULD NOT PROVIDE 
GROUND FORCES AS PART OF CURRENT NATO OPERATIONS IN AND 
AROUND KOSOVO: HE DECLINED TO SPECULATE ABOUT CONTRIBUTIONS 
TO ANY POST-SETTLEMENT IMPLEMENTATION FORCE. BUJON 
CHARACTERIZED THE PRESENCE OF AMERICAN TROOPS IN MACEDONIA 
AS 11VERY USEFUL." HE STRESSED THAT THE GOF WOULD NOT 
SIMPLY ALLOW ITS FORCES TO BE PLACED IN AN UNCERTAIN 
SITUATION -- OR AN "ADVENTURE" AS HE PUT IT, IN KOSOVO. HE. 
SAID THAT LONDON AND PARIS WOULD CONSULT CLOSELY ON THIS 
ISSUE AND WOULD WANT TO KNOW WHAT THE SPECIFIC GOALS WOULD 
BE FOR A GROUND OPERATION.

PROGRESS ON THE P-5 BENCHMARKS NOW, BUT HOPED TO CONCLUDE A 
TIMETABLE THAT WOULD YIELD PROGRESS LATER IN THE YEAR. 
REGARDING HIS VISIT TO PAKISTAN TALBOTT OBSERVED THAT THE

9. .ele} BUJON INQUIRED ABOUT CONGRESS' VIEWS REGARDING 
"UNITED STATES' SANCTIONS." TALBOTT RESPONDED THAT IF THE 
INDIANS REFUSED TO MAKE TANGIBLE PROGRESS TOWARD MEETING 
THE BENCHMARKS, SANCTIONS WOULD REMAIN IN PLACE. THE 
DEPUTY SECRETARY ALSO REMINDED BUJON THAT IN ADDITION TO 
WASHINGTON'S SANCTIONS, INTERNATIONAL SANCTIONS WERE IN 
EFFECT. HE NOTED POINTEDLY THAT ALL OF THE P-5 MEMBERS HAD 
AGREED TO THESE SANCTIONS. TALBOTT ALSO SAID THAT THERE 
WAS INCREASING CONSTERNATION AT THE UN AND AMONG SEVERAL 
NON-NUCLEAR STATES REGARDING POSSIBLE P-5 WILLINGNESS TO 
ALLOW INDIA AND PAKISTAN TO BECOME DE FACTO NUCLEAR STATES. 
TALBOTT EMPHASIZED THAT THIS WOULD BE "DISASTROUS." HE 
ADDED THAT THERE WERE SIGNS WITHIN THE G-8 THAT SOME 
MEMBERS WERE RAISING GO1 EXPECTATIONS REGARDING ITS ABILITY 
TO OBTAIN IFI FINANCING. TALBOTT SAID THAT THIS WAS 
CONTRARY TO THE AGREEMENTS MADE BY P-5 FOREIGN MINISTERS IN 
GENEVA, AND G-8 LEADERS IN LONDON. HE REITERATED THAT 
UNITY ON THIS ISSUE WAS CRITICAL AND STRESSED THAT HE 
WANTED TO CONDUCT A FRANK ASSESSMENT WITH ERRERA ABOUT THIS 
ISSUE. TALBOTT ALSO NOTED THAT UNSYG ANNAN WAS SCHEDULED 
TO TRAVEL TO INDIA OSTENSIBLY TO DISCUSS THIS ISSUE. BUJON 
SAID THAT THE SITUATION REMAINED DIFFICULT, BUT ONE COULD 
NOT EXPECT INDIA AND PAKISTAN TO RETURN TO "WHERE THEY WERE 
BEFORE." TALBOTT UNDERSCORED THAT THE P-5 WAS NOT ASKING 
FOR THAT, BUT FOR THE GDP'S AND GOI'S COMPLIANCE WITH NON-
PROLIFERATION BENCHMARKS. 
10. (C) BUJON DEBRIEFED ON THE JANUARY 17-18 VISIT TO 
PARIS OF GOI OFFICIAL MISHRA. MISHRA'S VISIT RECIPROCATED 
THE VISIT OF GOF OFFICIAL ERRERA TO INDIA LAST YEAR. BUJON 
SAID THAT ERRERA THOUGHT MISHRA APPEARED MUCH MORE FLEXIBLE 
DURING HIS VISIT TO PARIS. ACCORDING TO ERRERA, THE 
INDIANS WANT A "MINIMAL CREDIBLE DEFENSE SYSTEM TO RESPOND 
TO CREDIBLE NUCLEAR AGGRESSION." BUJON CONCEDED, HOWEVER, 
THAT MISHRA DID NOT ADDRESS MOST OF THE P-5 BENCHMARK 
REQUESTS. BUJON ALSO DEBRIEFED ON THE RECENT VISIT TO 
PARIS OF GO1 DEFENSE MINISTER FERNANDES. ACCORDING TO 
BUJON, FERNANDES DID NOT ADDRESS NON-PROLIFERATION ISSUES. 
THE FRENCH AMBASSADOR ALSO NOTED THAT THE PAKISTANI FOREIGN 
SECRETARY WAS SCHEDULED TO VISIT PARIS IN THE NEAR FUTURE.
