SUBJECT: DEPUTY SECRETARY'S SEPTEMBER 20 MEETING WITH 
HUNGARIAN FOREIGN MINISTER MARTONYI 
Classified by the Deputy Secretary, Reasons 1.5 (B) and 
1. Summary. In his September 20 meeting with the 
Deputy Secretary, Hungarin FM Martonyi expressed pessimism 
about the prospects for removing Milosevic. He asked for . 
more international pressure on Belgrade to meet its 
obligation to keep the Danube clear, and warned there could 
be serious flooding if nothing is done before winter. The 
Deputy Secretary noted that some aspects of the proposal 
Hungary supports for Vojvodina autonomy are problematic for 
us, and said it is premature to try to decide now what form 
of autonomy there will be for minorities in a free and 
democratic Serbia. The Deputy Secretary strongly urged 
that Hungary support the current initiative among the three 
new NATO members to produce a joint pro-Atlanticist 
statement on ESDI. Martonyi assured the Deputy Secretary 
that Hungary agrees with U.S. thinking on ESDI and believes 
there is no safe future for Europe without America. 
Martonyi briefly raised Hungary's desire to be included in 
the U.S. visa waiver program. End Summary. 
2. eputy Secretary Talbott met with Hungarian FM 
Martonyi September 20 in New York, on the margins of the 
UNGA. Martonyi was accompanied by MFA Chief of Staff 
Laszlo Szoke, MFA Press Spokesman Gabor Horvath, and 
Hungarian UN Mission Third Secretary Kinga Simon. 
Participating on the U.S. side were EUR Deputy Assistant.
Secretary Jim Swigert, D Special Assistant John Bass, and 
.EUR/NCE Acting Director Jim WojtasiewicZ (notetaker). 
3. 'The Deputy Secretary asked how preparations are 
going for the Szeged conference for Serb opposition mayors 
October 7. Martonyi said he hoped that all of the invited 
Serbs would attend but suspected some might not because of 
differences among the,opposition groups. Because of these 
differences, he was pessimistic about prospects for the 
removal of Milosevic, though there are some signs of 
dissatisfaction in the army and police. Swigert said we 
share this frustration with the inability of Serb 
opposition groups to work together, though most of them do 
seem to agree that the goal should be removal of Milosevic 
before holding efections. All Serb opposition groups also 
seem to agree that if elections are held before Milosevic 
departs there should be strict preconditions, including an 
intrusive role for the international community. Swigert 
expressed the hope that the rolling demonstrations due to 
begin the next day will create more momentum for the 
removal of Milosevic, but said we are not optimistic that 
will happen. 
4. (13.) Martonyi said he personally was skeptical about the 
effectiveness of these demonstrations. If turnout is low 
and does not grow, it will appear that support for 
democracy is waning. He strongly concurred that there 
should be preconditions for any elections held while 
Milosevic is still in power. Martonyi expressed concern 
that Montenegro President Djukanovic (who had just visited 
Budapest) has shifted his attention away from support for 
democracy in Serbia and is now thinking more about 
detaching Montenegro from the Serbia. The Deputy Secretary 
asked whether Montenegran talk about secession might not

6.-(e)-The Deputy Secretary asked whether our views were 
understood on the subject of clearing the Danube. Martonyi 
said yes: the river can be cleared, but no bridges should 
be reconstructed. However, Hungary would like the 
international community to apply more pressure on Belgrade 
to meet its international obligation to keep this waterway 
clear for shipping. It is difficult for Hungary to 
pressure Belgrade because it has no contacts with 
Milosevic's government. He also noted that EU countries 
had been falsely arguing that the United States opposes 
clearing the Danube, using this as an excuse for inaction. 
It is not enough for EU countries to say they do not oppose 
clearing the Danube -- they need to actively support it. 
If nothing is done before winter there could be serious 
floods, which Milosevic of course would blame on NATO. He 
wondered whether the U.S. position could shift from not 
opposing to more actively supporting the clearing of the 
river. Swigert responded that it would helpful for us to 
do that if the countries of the region could present a 
concrete proposal that had EU support and that made clear 
it would include no bridge reconstruction. However, we 
remain concerned that some may be prepared to give in to 
Milosevic's blackmail. 
7.-(61-The discussion then turned to the subject of 
Vojvodina. Swigert briefed Martonyi on his recent contacts 
with Vojvodina Hungarians. Martonyi described the outlines 
of Tibor Varadly's concept of three layers of autonomy: 
cultural, local and (if possible) provincial. The Deputy 
Secretary noted that the third layer is most problematic 
for us. Swigert added that there are also problems with 
the second layer, local autonomy, because Serb opposition 
groups perceive this effort to gain territorial autonomy 
within Vojvodina as giving Milosevic an easy propaganda 
tool to play on Serb secessionist fears. In our contacts 
with Serb opposition groups, as we urge greater unity we 
are also stressing the importance of addressing minority 
concerns. Decentralization and local control could be an 
important part of a future restructuring of a democratic 
Serbia and democratic Yugoslavia. However, we are also 
stressing to Vojvodina Hungarians the importance of 
unifying behind the main objective of getting rid of 
Milosevic.

decentralization clearly must be part of the overall 
process of dernocratization. He denied that Vojvodina 
Hungarians want to negotiate with Milosevic for regional

9.1'S.),The Deputy Secretary then turned to ESDI, noting 
that we understand that the three new NATO members are 
considering issuing a joint pro-Atlanticist statement on 
this subject. He strongly urged Hungary to support this 
initiative. This would be very healthy in generating the 
kind of healthy debate we need to have within the Alliance 
on the key issue of how to accommodate in ESDI the 
interests of non-EU NATO members. He outlined the three 
main elements of U.S. thinking on ESDI: we want ESDI to 
work, we do not want ESDI to be used to split the European 
pillar off from NATO, and we do not want ESDI to 
discriminate against any Europeans. He noted that this 
concept is not new, but the road from Berlin in 1996 has 
recently taken some winding turns through San MaLo and 
Cologne. We would like to straighten it out. 
10.-W) Martonyi assured the Deputy Secretary that Hungary 
supports the initiative to produce a joint pro-Atlanticist 
statement, and concurs with all three elements of U.S. 
thinking which the Deputy Secretary outlined. Having only 
recently become a member of NATO, Hungary does not want to 
see its status in the Alliance undercut. Furthermore, in 
Hungary's view there is no safe future for Europe without 
America. Hungary has made this point with other European 
countries so strongly that it and the other new members are 
suspected of trying to tilt the Alliance in a more 
pro-Atlanticist direction, an accusation they readily admit 
is true. The Deputy Secretary commented that indeed, the
three new members not only have proven they can contribute 
to the Alliance's collective defense, but they are 
contributing a refreshingly pro-Atlanticist element to 
Alliance thinking. 
11. In closing, Martonyi reiterated Hungary's desire to 
be included in the U.S. visa waiver program. He complained 
that Hungarians are required to wait in long lines and,pay 
for telephone calls to request information. The Deputy 
Secretary said we would look into these questions with our 
Embassy in Budapest.
