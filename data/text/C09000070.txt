January 6, 1994 
I was delighted to read of your nomination to be Deputy 
Secretary of State. Congratulations! Its wonderful to see one's 
contemporaries rise so high in the service of their country, and 
I am sure you'll have great itpact. 
In your new position, I hope you will be able to help 
President Clinton - whom I otherwise admire - develop a more 
coherent, credible and consistent foreign policy. From a 
strictly parochial point of view, I hope that you can help patch 
up relations between the U.S. and India, which are deteriorating 
steadily. Historically, Indian governments have got on better 
with Democratic than Republican Administrations. So I was rather 
startled when Prime Minister Narasimha Rao told me he regretted 
George Bush's defeat. Since then there have been a series .of 
urlfortunat4phrased comments requiring clarifications that never 
quite erase the initial uproar. 
'The absence of d U.S. amba5sador to India for almost 
year has certainly not helped matters. Stephen Solarz is a' 
superb choice for the job because of his personal popularity in 
India, but he will have his job cut out for him. I haven't heard 
this level of resentment towards the U.S. government since Henry 
Kissinger convinced Nixon to send the Enterprise battle group 
into the Bay of Bengal during the Bangladesh War. It is 
particularly unfortunate at a time when the Narasimha Rao 
government is engaged in a radical liberalization of the Indian 
economy, opening up a middle class market half again the size of 
Russia. Thus far, U.S. companies have been the major 
beneficiaries of the policy changes.
