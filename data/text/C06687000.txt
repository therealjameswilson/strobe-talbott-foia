Dear Ms. Bennet, 
Thank you for your letter of June 17 that asked for names 
of possible NIS participants for the Institute's NIS security 
conference this Fall. We think the Institute's efforts to help 
build a security dialogue among members of the NIS as well as 
between NIS and Western experts can play ah important role in 
the challenges NIS states face. 
We requested that our embassies in NIS capitals recommend 
possible candidates for the Conference. The result of this 
request is listed below. While not a complete representation 
of all the NIS, we hope that the list will be of some aid in 
selecting participants. In requesting names from embassies, we 
stated a preference for officials who have influence on the 
policy makers of the NIS and are fluent in English. The 
preference for officials fluent in English limited somewhat the 
potentiaJ list of candidates. 
AZERBAIJAN. Government official: Araz Azimov, MFA 
International Organizations Director. Academic official: 
Leyla Aliyeva, Institute for Strategic and International 
Studies. 
BELARUS. Government official: Serguei Martynov, Charge 
d'Affaire, Embassy of Belarus in Washington, D.C. Academic 
official: Anatoliy Mikhaylov, philosopher (Ph.D.) and top aide 
to Supreme Soviet Chairman Shushkevich. 
GEORGIA. Government official: National Security Advisor 
Tedo Japaridze. Academic official: Gia Nodia, Head of the 
Caucasian Institute for Peace. 
KAZAKHSTAN. Government official: MFA Political-Military 
Counselor Nurgaliyev, Deputy Foreign Minister Tokayev, or 
Deputy State Counselor Agybay Smagulov. Academic official: 
Umirserik Kasenov of the Strategic Studies Institute. 
KYRGZYSTAN. Government official: Member of the 
Presidency's International Relations Department Yevgeniy 
Kablukov, or Foreign Affairs Commentator for the weekly 
newspaper "Res Publica" Mikhail Ozmitel.
